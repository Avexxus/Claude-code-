## 2. The saga in one page (current canon)

**Two layers.** The world layer (physics, magic, cosmology) is rigid. The story layer (characters, plot, books, names) is clay. Every name is a placeholder.

**Reality.** Matter came first and has no author. The One is the origin of *consciousness* only: it came apart into every mind. Every being is flesh plus a fragment of the One, its **Remnant**, which is its soul and its self. **The regress:** every plane is a being that came apart. At our level, the piece that let go completely is our One, the pieces that let go partly are the **sibling worlds**, and the pieces that held on are the **Unified Ones**, who built the **barrier**. *Keeper, consumer and Blight* are titles found at every level, and each holder has their own motives. The level above is varied. Our **keeper** (the "puppeteer") answered the portal and lends the two men their time powers. Its motive is left open on purpose.

**The breach.** Jon and Derrek's portal opened the shield. **Every reset does two things: it widens the breach and it sweeps the invaders back out.** Rifts leak power from the level above. They are reservoirs, never the source of anyone's magic. Each dimension also holds **three worlds**: ours, plus two lifeless copies the elves call heaven and hell, which they reach with a rift device. "The elves built heaven" is what the world believes, not established fact.

**Magic.** Your Remnant extends an **appendage** that grips reality. There are **three dials**: Remnant (strength), control (skill) and the **inner sense** (unexplained). The cost is **fatigue**. **Remnant can never be burned, spent or transferred.** There's no physics audit and no "fire mages." Force words describe an act after the fact. **Soul magic** is rare, forbidden and two-way, and its limit is risk to yourself. **Pacts** teach technique, never Remnant. **Enchantment** is a soul held in matter, and that soul feels dread. **In-betweeners** predate the One and run **the Rule** (Void, Chain) instead of Remnant. Machines can copy magic's effects but never the act.

**Beings.** The lifespan ladder: orcs < humans < dwarves < elves < dragons < giants < great gods. Bigger souls resist aging but are never immune. True immortals exist only by mechanism: Jon, Derrek and the rule-beasts. Only a god with power from beyond the regress who also outsmarts Derrek can threaten him. **A changeling is a crime, not a race:** a self-made soul thief, a possessor rather than a shapeshifter, mostly monsters now. Angels and demons are one kind of being, the same kind as changelings. Every god was born of flesh. Humans become gods only by time-fusion or theft.

**History.** The age of giants: total law, forced protection, later erased. **Betrayal day:** the giant emperor's closest advisor, a dragon, struck with a prepared stone. **The Tower** rose from the emperor's Convergence, which trapped the city's souls (exact identity: L7). Then the Age of Dragons, which everyone else calls the Darkest Age, a name that's unfair to the dragons. Then humans rose. The city never moved. Derrek took the Tower from the dragon empire. Jon led Mika and the elves away on the seedship. The modern day is a human technostate. The **far future** is mastery and still brutal: a galaxy with no aliens, the Galactic Council on Earth, cathedral ships, the organ trade, clones with souls.

**Jon and Derrek.** At the portal, Derrek *demanded* power and was cursed. Jon collapsed in awe and was given control. **Derrek sees time; Jon jumps; Derrek is tethered to Jon's jumps; only Jon can reset.** Each run lasts about 10,000 years, and there have been 1,000 or more. **Derrek** chose to be the greatest evil out of love and guilt: Jon's hatred keeps him resetting, and the resets hold back the Blight. He is humanity's open, faceless Emperor. He planned **a son** as his lever for the day Jon stops jumping, and he is gathering Lirael's soul fragments in the Tower. **Jon** is the true villain: warm, sincere, the elves' hidden god, breeding a warrior people. His one secret is that **he kills Lirael on purpose, run after run**, to keep her memory of him loving. He stops because he learns he's being manipulated and he's tired of killing her. "The final run" is his lie to himself. His arc is a fall.

**Books.** Book numbers and eras are placeholders. **Book 1:** Derrek's nameless son flees, Jon scoops him up, the son rises and gathers a crew, a giant space war breaks out, and the Tower sequence follows (Void and the master's black hole → the queen against the apprentice → Lirael's Collapse turned inside out → the changeling's barrage → Jon defeats Derrek) → **everyone dies** → the window, with no plea and no time travel shown. **A later book** returns to that window with the pleas. **Another later book** is Jon's. **Every book ends at the window, with only two men there. Jon never answers; the reader decides.** The thesis: *"it doesn't matter in the grand scale, but everything matters to someone."*

## 3. Twenty rules every agent must follow (merged from the nine must-know lists)

1. **Only Patrick makes canon.** Label all output 🌊 Claude proposal. Never use 🔒 or ✅. Nothing goes into the codex unless Patrick calls for that pass.
2. **Every line of prose is Patrick's.** Give beats, options and consequences only. Claude-drafted passages in 13/SCENES are records, not material to build on (L34).
3. **World is rigid, story is clay.** Never bend a world law to make a story idea fit.
4. **Remnant is never burned, spent, fuel or transferred** (W10). Only wholesale absorption moves it. "Stealing production" has an open mechanism (L2), so don't invent one as fact.
5. **Three dials plus fatigue; no physics audit, no math, no "users", types, element classes, grids, triads or formulas.** Everyone is "just a skilled user." Build the person first and add the magic's flavor last.
6. **Everything is graded.** There's no god/mortal divide, no monoliths, no racial sins, and no group is THE anything. Conditions shape people, never blood. Don't use "mortal" as the opposite of "god."
7. **Matter does no magic; a soul held in matter does.** Whether a vessel can hold a soul without someone holding it (W14) is **unconfirmed** (L11).
8. **Changelings are possessors, never shapeshifters.** Their transfers are **never witnessed on the page**, each taking kills the host, and they're limited to 1–2 souls at a sustainable pace. They're mostly monsters, and there are no changeling children.
9. **Pacts teach technique, never Remnant.** The tether fades only on the holder's side, and taught abilities remain.
10. **Dragons are a capable feudal empire of builders, never solitary takers.** Breaths are wielded, not a species trait. No single being beats a great ancient dragon.
11. **The elven bond doesn't track individuals and doesn't cross between pocket worlds.** Only the bond-spoof chip cracks it. Elven society is authoritarian.
12. **Derrek is the open Emperor; Jon is the hidden god.** Never swap them back. Derrek engineered the forge-world strike.
13. **Every book ends at the window with only two men. Jon never answers or "refuses."** Book 1 has no plea and no visible time travel. Book numbers are placeholders.
14. **"The final run" is Jon's lie.** Show only one run per book. Earlier loops "never happened."
15. **Everyone in Book 1's party dies, and there's no redemption.** The tone is grimdark: cost and characterize, never titillate.
16. **Madness is subtext only. No clinical labels.**
17. **Don't invent names.** Claude-coined names (the Returned, drow, Serath, warden-object, forgetting horizon, Rule-engines) are placeholders. "Drow" is banned.
18. **No real-world racial or ethnic analogies.**
19. **Check every idea** against 02, the 🚫 lists (combined in notes A §4, C §5, D §6, F §7, G §8) and 12. Name the law or ruling an idea touches.
20. **Prefer closing open items over opening new ones.** Say so whenever an idea opens a new question. Give Patrick small numbered options, and expect answers like "c, both."

## 5. Where to attack first

Taken from register §6 (Claude's own ranking, 🌊). Clusters 1–3 are one sitting, because they're all one question: how soul-power works when it's held in something or taken from someone.

1. **L1 + L2 + L11 — the soul-power sitting.** Covers how stored or stolen soul-power acts without being burned, how stealing production works, and whether a vessel needs a holder. Settling these unblocks about a dozen lines across 12 files and three Tower deaths, and also NP#27, NP#28 and L18.
2. **L7 — the Tower's identity.** This feeds L8, since the apprentice's "latent pools" could be the Tower's trapped Remnant.
3. **L3 — where the Lirael line lands.** This is the ending of Book 1 and of the later window book.
4. **L5 + NP#3 — the series shape and Jon's memory.** These are one question.
5. **L4 / NP#12 / NP#15 — where time-god power comes from**, which also settles who can kill whom.
6. **L6 — whether Jon's feelings are felt or autopilot.**
7. **L34 — prose is Patrick's.** Decides how agents work from here on.
8. **L15 + L16 — restating gods and pacts** after W30–W32.

Cheap wins worth doing next: **NP#10, the lock pass** (one sitting that tidies law status everywhere), NP#5 (hell), NP#6 (the tether), and L24 (the gnome changeling).
