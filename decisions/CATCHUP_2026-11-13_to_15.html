1. SERIES STRUCTURE

  • Three books, three heroes. [P]
  • Book 1: Eli. Naive. Raised outside the harsh world, thanks to his father's influence.
  • Book 2: a more experienced, rugged hero.
  • Book 3: a tired veteran, broken by the shape of war, on foot, a decent man who did what the age required. His arc is SOFTENING: he learns to bend instead of using brute force.
  • Reading-order vs. chronology: Book 3 is chronologically earliest, then Book 2 (space/wild-west era), then Book 1 (cold, clinical, far-future empire). [P] (Book 3 is set in the traditional-fantasy age after the Tower exists and after the dragons overthrew the giants; Derrek is already emperor.)
  • Jon runs the OPPOSITE curve through reading order. [P]
  • Book 1: hollow.
  • Book 2: warmer.
  • Book 3: warm, cheerful, attentive, before he lost too many people.
  • Backstory not shown on the page: the Tower's birth, Doc's first soul theft, Mika's rise to godhood by subjugating his people. [P]

Book 3 specifics [P]

  • Human protagonist in the human world. Humans have grown in the dragons' shadow.
  • Three-way war on Earth: elves (with a grown empire), dragons, humans. This shows WHY the elves later left. The exodus happens long after Book 3 and before Book 2.
  • Jon in Book 3: already cursed and ageless but early in his span. NOT yet obsessed with killing Derrek. A chance encounter. He is exploring, enjoying life, making friends. More present and patient. The "relax man, it's ok" guy who helps the hero soften. More of him on the page.
  • Younger Derrek in Book 3: clumsier methods, bolder, takes failure worse, less quick to harness entire races. A public figure and emperor who CAN'T hide (in the far future he hides behind robots and screens and orders by psychic thought over the chip). He rules by words and hides his true power because using it openly would unite the world against him. He uses magic in measured bursts to punish traitors (Palpatine comparison).

3. BOOK 2 ERA: NEAR-FUTURE SPACE "WILD WEST" [P]

  • Colonies, space stations, von Neumann probes, early Dyson swarms, massive warships, long-term colony ships.
  • Expansion, but nowhere near Book 1's coldly efficient, clinical, mass-produced, muted, grey world (pessimism by force; luxury and immortality for the rich).
  • Book 2 is rugged, untrained, free, fun, bloody. Pirates, rebels, black markets, constant war, people fleeing and founding homes. The most diversity of rule.

Race positions

  • Dragons: hold seats of power in planet-spanning industries (giant = scale, NOT the giant race). Dragon-captained fleets with mixed crews. They keep companies, own planets, humans and clones, are rich, and have dragon-sized yachts on beach worlds.
  • Dwarves: deep-flung mine worlds with missile cruisers and old trade networks.
  • Elves: the most competitive fleet. First to seek exodus from Eterna (new name for the homeworld).
  • Giants: quietly perfect ships toward cathedral ships crewed by whole countries. Sell living ships from the forge world to fund revenge.
  • Humans: win by creeping growth over thousands of years, no war needed. Neither dragons nor giants can threaten humanity: they can kill worlds and smash a fraction of a fleet, but humans replace it.

Slavery, clones, orcs, chips

  • Book 1 era: slavery hidden behind the "clone" label amid quadrillions of voices. Meaning is lost; humans are AI/VR-brainwashed and apathetic; even clones and slaves barely register.
  • Book 2 era: slavery more open. The human federation of planetary republics cannot publicly endorse slavery, so the military is steered at scapegoats (rebels, pirates) under ordinary charges (treason, espionage, damage, smuggling, piracy) and the government gains subjects. No state-justified slavery outside orcs (robots, AI and abundant food mean no labor need), but the government quietly dabbles anyway.
  • Clones in Book 2: expensive, government-lab only, used as soldiers. Cheap meat/organs/raw Remnant source comes MUCH later.
  • REJECTED: [C] idea that raids feed freed people into the clone trade.
  • Orcs: no one thinks of them as slaves any more than a dog. The lie is that orcs are animals; they are people who can do magic and have traditions, heroes and tales.
  • Chips: ONE universal chip sold in different boxes for different classes. It works like a SHOCK COLLAR (prods, shocks, subtle rewiring, chemical signaling). Full control only when the wearer is unruly. Can control anyone chipped. A premium chip with control removed gives Remnant-network access; overload destroys the body (the soul is not spent, consistent with the "Remnant can't be burned" ruling).

5. JON AND DERREK PSYCHOLOGY (private craft lens; keep clinical labels OUT of prose)

Jon [P]

  • Id/ego model: hot emotions are ego interpretations; drives (fear, dread, lust, anger) are body-level. ONLY anger and lust actually move him.
  • When he sees Lirael's look in the Queen, the smile drops from a body-level desire/tension jolt, NOT tenderness. He is blank, at most slightly surprised. (He is not "enamored.")
  • He smiled while Doc and Teo died, a tear rolling. The tear is PRIDE in Eli's team (like watching a favorite show deliver). It is not grief, and Doc was not a chosen tool.
  • He reads cues skillfully but doesn't understand motives like love or honor; acts for personal gain. "I love ya," not "I love you."
  • Lovable, charming, gangsterish, warm smile that never slips, hollow. "Never lies" is his self-image (he prefers silence/omission and lies when needed).
  • He picks MANY heroes he can manipulate who have potential. Eli: light that draws people, determination, kindness, able to convince an army to march to death; naive but smart, controllable, arrogant, impulsive. Eli is the one who succeeded.
  • Jon is always gone, maybe leading simultaneous teams of all kinds of beings; he stays absent so people don't see the cracks; he acts only when required.
  • Once he quit resetting, the choice became his wherever he was: pick his hero and watch; it ends the same in his mind.
  • Jon doesn't know what will happen (no script). "Rehearsed" = skilled against rule-beasts.

Derrek [P]

  • "Superman." Every hit he takes is ALLOWED; choreographed so Jon feels it was worth it; each crew member gets a moment.
  • Sight, chip, enhanced flesh, god power, skill. Never misses or slips lines (he has seen how it could go, not lived it step by step, and adjusts).
  • Weakness is emotional/ego: obsession with Jon, who barely looks. The show worked because Jon was subtly moved.
  • Has fathered many sons over the ages; Jon watched many emperors' sons.
  • Pre-curse Derrek was Jon's teacher (greatest master of humanity); equals after the curse.
  • [OPEN] source of Derrek's obsession (Derrek-as-teacher gives Jon's approval-seeking a native source).
  • "Faceless" Derrek = nobody in the Empire knows his face (empty oval seal). He IS on the page in Book 1's Tower. Do not use the "faceless until Book 3" line from the non-canon Oct 31 exploration file.
