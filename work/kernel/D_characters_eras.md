## A. HARD RULES

### The master timeline (oldest first; "B1" means Book 1's present)
- ✅ The world is already ancient before any book: the age of giants, the giants as lawgivers holding the other races in forced protection, the orcs born into bondage, the elves always outside giant control. (`WORLD_OVERVIEW` "ages"; `07`)
- ✅ **Betrayal day and the coup were one event.** The giant emperor's closest dragon advisor detonated his "fisile" stone in the heart of the giant capital. The coup wasn't coordinated entirely by dragons: a few were warned to be in the right place, and the rest was opportunism once the capital lay dead. Because it was uneven, some giants survived. (R091, R086)
- ✅ The advisor dies in the blast on purpose, "called the king in memory" but never the ruler of dragons. The old emperor "didnt have enough." (R079, R138; `SCENES` canon duel t262–268)
- ✅ **The Tower rises the same day.** By the sacred ritual the emperor drew his whole city into himself; everything outside was vaporized, and he dissolved into it. The Tower is the converged city: mindless, the largest Remnant source on Earth, a giant channeling device and amplifier. (R136, R111, R026)
- ✅ The elves took no part in the coup. A giant who says "Elves too" is showing his own bias. (R092)
- ✅ The dragons stole the title of empire from the giants that day. Giants were exiled and are unwelcome near dragon land. The blast land stayed radioactive, and dragons and enslaved orcs went back first. (R042, R078, R137)
- ✅ The Age of Dragons, which everyone else calls "the Darkest Age," was long; humans didn't beat dragons quickly. (`09` t496; `WORLD_OVERVIEW`)
- ✅ **The city never moved.** It went from giant capital to dragon seat to human capital, with the Tower at its heart. Derrek took the Tower from the heart of the dragon empire by uniting and arming humans; he didn't just show up. (`09` t64, t494)
- ✅ **Book 3 (about 10k+ years before B1): the late Darkest Age.** Swords and magic, a three-way war on Earth (elves, dragons, humans), and Derrek already emperor. (R089)
- ✅ **The exodus** comes long after Book 3 and before Book 2. Mika alone ordered it, as a top-down directive, and it went on one seedship. (R094; `09` exodus, jul23 t132; CU §1)
- ✅ **Book 2 (about 5k+ years before B1): the near-future space "wild west."** The elves leave first and build an empire, the giants quietly build their own ships and settle the forge world, and humans fill Earth. Pirates and rebels hold the far reaches, and flesh metal (and opposition to it) begins. (R089, R140)
- ✅ **Book 1 (B1): the far future, a bureaucratic stalemate.** The worlds are packed, the pirates "were smashed," and a galaxy-spanning AI reads every citizen's biometrics. The rift is at its widest. (R088, R089; `09` t334)
- ✅ Book 1's catalyst: a dragon overdrives a cannon and annihilates the heart of the giants' forge world, and the giants go to open war. **Derrek engineered the strike** (how is not ruled). The road to the Tower is a giant space war. (`09` t410–422; wk W64)
- ✅ Book 1 ends at the Tower window with the universe collapsing. (wk W53)
- ✅ **Book 4 is outside the run: the multiverse.** The cosmic book, undecided. Jon and Derrek go "to the multiverse station up the regress and back in time," and Jon's own book folds into it. (R100; `09` Book 4)
- 🔒 The cycle is a closed loop: once started, it has always been. The Tower is fused into all eras ("It was always that spot and derrek always chose it"). (`02` Law 19; `09` t68, t102)

### Time numbers and loop rules
- ✅ Each run lasts about **10,000 years**, and there have been **a thousand or more** runs. "About 5k times before" is a floor, not a count. (wk W42; `09` t328)
- ✅ The book gaps are **about 5k years or more each**, and Book 3 to Book 1 is about one run. (R089; `09` SERIES)
- ✅ **Books 1–3 sit inside the current run**, and each ends at the window. (R100; `02` Law 19)
- 🔒 **Only the final run is shown.** Earlier loops "literally never happened once he reset" and appear only as residue (lines about "5k times before," scars, foreknowledge). (`02` Law 19; t328, t330)
- ✅ "The final run" is **Jon's lie to himself**, not a fact; nothing makes the cycle's end real. (wk W42)
- ✅ **Books 1–3 show no jumps and no resets on the page.** Both men move straight forward this run: Derrek because he can't jump without Jon, Jon because he has stopped. When Jon "leaves" mid-book he is elsewhere, not jumping. (`09` How Jon & Derrek exist; wk W53)
- 🔒 **The tether:** whenever Jon jumps, Derrek is pulled with him, every time. "the tether does not care why they jumped. derrek goes with jon. end of story." (`02` Law 17; jul08 t369)
- ✅ **Only Jon can reset. Derrek sees time; Jon jumps.** Derrek's craft is aiming Jon's jumps. (`09` t713; wk W39)
- ✅ A reset rewinds from outside, like a recording, and nobody inside time-travels. Jon's soul is carried while his body and brain are rewound; Derrek's sight is the vantage. (R012)
- ✅ What carries across a reset: Derrek's memory, the widened breach, and the rift scars. Jon wakes blank and his memory returns over the run. (R118; `09` single timeline)
- ✅ Each reset widens the breach **and** sweeps the Blight's invaders back out. The window's "reset or reality ends" is literally true. (`09` window line; `08` two-function reset)
- ✅ Rifts form wherever Jon jumps. The Tower's rift is the most torn and leaks "next dimensions size chunks of raw floating remnant." (R013, R014)
- ⚖️ Butterfly effect: a jump-to-save unmakes other people upstream (the "never-were"). Every race loses never-were people, but only the elves feel the holes. (`08` finale recast; how the elves feel them is open, A-32)

### The window
- ✅ **Every book (1–3) ends at the Tower window.** (`09` t456; R100)
- ✅ "Only two men at the window" is **a literary device, not a headcount**: whoever else is there, it comes down to Jon and Derrek. (R121)
- ✅ At the window **Derrek is always beaten**, "a man on deaths door. Each time." (`09` t368)
- ✅ **Jon never answers.** He doesn't "refuse"; the reader decides whether he resets, and a reread means he did. (wk W50, W55)
- ✅ **Book 1's window has no plea and shows no time travel.** A later book returns to the same window and reveals the jumps and pleas: Derrek begs and brings his son, the son pleads, and the Historian is present. (wk W53, W54)
- ⚖️ The window is a rehearsed play aimed at the son. The confession ("I broke the world when we were young and arrogant…" + "and ive told you infinite times and your cursed to always forget") is new only to him and to the reader. (`09` t731–736)

### The eras: what is locked
**Book 3: the late Darkest Age**
- ✅ It's an age of swords and magic: dragons in towers, dwarven mines, elves hidden in their fortresses. (R089, BANK 11-16 [SERIES])
- ✅ The three-way war (elves, dragons, humans) is why the elves later leave. Derrek is already emperor here. (R089; CU §1)
- 🔒 It's the medieval register: magic is named by sensation (fire, water, earth, air) and nobody names the forces. (`02` Law 7)
- ✅ The Tower city is the dragons' official capital and the place where humans come to learn from dragons. "almost Noone here is nobody." (R071)
- ✅ No Returned exist yet; they belong "way later." (R147)
- ✅ The elves man a crude, hand-opened portal to their prison/heaven world, guarded on both sides. (R015)

**Book 2: the space wild west**
- ✅ Probes, colonies, stations and early Dyson swarms. The elves have gone, the giants are on the forge world, the dwarves tinker, and dragons are "in charge technically." (R089, R140)
- ✅ Earth feels like a slow, declining apocalypse, "More steam punk edge runner than modern," smoggy and polluted, "while the elite continue pumping out new devices." (R089)
- ✅ The far reaches are "rugged, untrained, free, fun, bloody. Pirates, rebels, black markets, constant war." (`09` tone, CU §3)
- ✅ The human federation of planetary republics can't openly endorse slavery. (R085)
- ✅ This is the age where flesh metal first appears and the first opposition to it rises. (R089)
- 🚫 Never set it in "the modern day" or a magic post-apocalypse as an era; that survives only as the feel of Earth. (R089)

**Book 1: the far future**
- ✅ A bureaucratic stalemate: "Noone really wants war when they are extracting what they want so efficiently." The brutality is extraction. (R088, R089)
- ✅ Slavery hides behind the "clone" label "amid quadrillions of voices." Humans are AI/VR-brainwashed and apathetic. (R088)
- 🔒 Forces are fully understood and wielded directly at monumental scale. (`02` Law 7)
- ✅ Humanity faces three crises: the war the dragons started, the Orc King's plague army, and the elves, sworn to end the Emperor. (`09` t532)
- ✅ The pirates are gone; "These pirates no longer exist. They were smashed." (R088)
- 🚫 Never call Book 1's galaxy "in constant chaos." (R089)

**Book 4: the cosmic book**
- ✅ It's undecided, it strips "back the cosmic curtain," and Jon's book folds into it. (R100; BANK 11-16 [SERIES])
- ✅ The time-god society's Citadel stands in the outer region, between realities. (R127)
- ✅ Breaks are local and vary by universe; not every universe has a pair. (R103)

### Jon (placeholder name)
- ✅ He is **human** and a **god**, and the author's oldest character. (`08` t370, t530)
- 🔒 **Time is his only given power.** He can rewind and reset (fast-forward is 🌊). Every other force and expression he **learned** across the loops; he has no innate affinity. (`02` Law 17; R109)
- ✅ He is one with time, "in control of it and inextricably linked to it," through the keeper's pact. Never call it "time-fusion." (R108)
- ✅ His control of time is **the shape of his sentence**: "The gifts are a CURSE and a punishment. Nobody is rewarded." (R117)
- ✅ He keeps a permanent portal that he opens and closes at will and "barely pays attention" to. (R020)
- ✅ **Memory:** he wakes blank each reset and memory returns over the run; "by this point he remembers things." (R118)
- ✅ **There is no script.** He knows patterns and is skilled, but he never knows outcomes; "rehearsed" means skilled at fighting rule-beasts. (R119)
- ✅ He was Derrek's top apprentice, the one Derrek saw as his equal, and he is still uncertain underneath. (`08` t554; jul08 t65)
- ✅ **He is the true villain, the worst person in the series,** and he is openly warm, honest and sincere. His only hidden thing is the Lirael secret. (wk W44)
- ✅ He killed Lirael (Mika's sister) **on purpose, again and again across loops**, because "Mika required a dead sister." Only Derrek knows. (wk W45)
- ✅ He stops jumping because he learns he's being manipulated and because he's tired of killing Lirael. (wk W40)
- ✅ He is just Mika's friend: not the elves' leader, god, or gardener. (R094)
- ⚖️ The charm is his autopilot. He **says** charming things and **does** selfish or cruel ones with no change in tone (a hand on the shoulder while the other hand finishes something terrible). Never confirm it through a narrator. (`08` t249, t746–749)
- ⚖️ Only Derrek ever moved him to hatred; everyone else gets "slight disgust or pity." Lirael is the exception: "he did care and he does care for her." (`08` t243, t301)
- ⚖️ He won't push anyone to die, but he builds the altar, arranges the conditions and lets the willing climb it. (`08` t340, t554–555)
- ⚖️ His signature atrocity is the cold pre-emptive killing of a liked character for things they did in earlier loops, saying only "trust me." He tortures, always in service of the mission. (`08` t709–710)
- ⚖️ He is indifferent to orcs: not malicious, just indifferent. (`08` t705)
- ⚖️ His arc is a fall, not a redemption. "This was never jons redemption. This was always his collapse." (`08` t214)
- ✅ **Fate:** he beats Derrek at the window every loop. His body can be destroyed in the world, but **the story never allows Jon to die.** (R105; `08` t368)
- 🚫 Never: Jon as the elves' (hidden) god, breeding a warrior people, or leading the exodus; "time-fusion"; Jon "refusing" at the window; "gardening" as a mask he wears; Jon "NOT a user of people." (R094, R108; wk W50, W44; `08` RETIRED)

## B. FLAVOR / AURA

**The shape of the whole**
- Two men and a door. "it was always supposed to be jon and derrek… I lost sight of the real war." (t326) Everything else is weather: the hero lives in "the world they created." (t332)
- The thesis cuts both ways: **"it doesnt matter in the grand scale but everything matters to someone."** (t466)
- Duality is the name versus the act, and a single subject holds two true opposites. Jon's victory is also the start of his infinite cycle. (`09` theme; wk W29)
- It's grimdark by Patrick's own label: "my work truly fits the label of grimdark." (t658) The ruling register as of 11-21: **"haunting evil world with a kind hearted light we follow."** "A light in a dark world." (BANK 11-21)
- The light is the protagonists, and then they're taken: "They all die. There can be nothing left to cheer for by the end." (t380) Tenderness is the setup and the violence the punchline. (`09` grimdark)
- Levity is wanted: "dark jokes and they can be punctuated by more violence." (jul08 t24) Simultaneity: a small funny human moment while millions die offstage.
- Unforgivable is about intensity, not body count: "Its the intensity and coldness of the killings. How useless the killing was." (t708)
- The book is a loop: "It can only begin on page 1 and it always ends at the tower." (t718) Every reread is a reset.
- "We are humans reading about the history of the humans." (t302) Every age is named by whoever holds the pen.

**The eras' feel**
- **Book 3: wonder and myth.** The cosmology is invisible and the time-gods' intrusions look like fate and monsters. It's Dark-Souls-scale giant architecture, "grown, not built," with no chisel marks, too big for those who came after: dragons "physically sitting inside the remains of the giant family who built those halls." (SCENES t22–34, t300) A giant caravan has an orchestra "who plays constantly so they make enough noise to not be crushed." (R040)
- **Book 2: grit and frontier.** Smog, megacities that "glow like small suns" (jul08 t293), flying traffic lanes, implants, the elite "pumping out new devices," and out past it "rugged, untrained, free, fun, bloody." (R089; CU §3) It's (inferred) the age of the edge, the last time anyone can run.
- **Book 1: mastery as horror.** "The brightness is the horror… we fixed everything except what beings are." (`09` tone) It's bureaucratic and efficient: biometric readers on every stair landing, a flesh market hosing down its slabs, cores that twitch "the way cut meat twitches on a block." (story texture, Book 1 v3 ch1–2; inferred as the target feel) The stalemate is quiet because extraction works. (R088)
- **Book 4: the curtain stripped back.** "this is happening infinitely and were only 1 level of this happening infinitely." (t464)

**Jon**
- He's warm, charming and fun-loving, and it's real on the page and empty underneath: "Only we know he doesnt feel anything." (t744) The disconnect is the tell: "him comforting the boy as he casually does something brutal." (t748)
- "Warm and fun loving are not mutually exclusive from detached." (t239) "To jon he really is the tragic victim and a tired hero." (t293)
- Pause too long at a statue: "just a moment to long. Not just showing respect to a fallen hero of old but something more." (t392–396)
- He's the most stubborn man alive: "when he decided he was done he meant it." (t656)
- What makes him Patrick's (inferred): the villain is the mentor everyone trusts, never unmasked by a narrator. You learn him the way the son does, too late.

**Derrek**
- He's a faceless emperor, smoke-thick presence, yawning while his apprentice burns. "Proud? Proud of what exactly?" (t768) He's never slingy in a fight: "these are wizards. Big and tough doesnt mean throws punches." (R038)
- He sighs "about the waste" (t342) and kills to make the stakes believable: "would you believe how series the stakes here are if I didnt?" (t738)
- His tragedy is love warped into tyranny: "everything he does is out of a warped sense of love or loyalty." (t694)
- The image: a man on death's door at the window, every time, still the most capable person in the series. (t368; jul23 t24)

**The hero (and the light)**
- "The softness is the point." "Hes a good man who was raised to see his words as the most important words in the world. Everyone since he was a boy were bent beneath him so he never had to learn how to listen." (BANK 11-21) Show the gap between his warm *idea* of a friend and the actual person, and never state it. (BANK 11-18)
- He's "a man of equal resolve as either if the 2 gods" (t754), and a pawn with no name: "His name will come last." (t201)
