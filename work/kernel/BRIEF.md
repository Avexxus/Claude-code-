# Brief for kernel extractors (2026-11-21)
Patrick wants two things built from one full read of the codex:
1. **The World Kernel**: the hard rules a story writer must never break, so agents read about 6 pages instead of 230k words.
2. **The Flavor sheet**: the *aura* of the world, meaning how it feels to live in it, plus Patrick's taste. This gives agents his flavor, not just his facts.

## Read first (they override the codex)
`decisions/BANK.md`, then `decisions/RULINGS.md`. Precedence: BANK > RULINGS (R###) > `v5-cc` > `v5-wk2` > `v5-wk` > `v5` > `v5-jul` > untagged v4. The Retired Archive (`archive/`) is NOT canon; don't use it. `FLAGGED_ADDITIONS.md` and `NEEDS_PATRICK.md` are NOT canon: they are open questions.

## What to write
Write your output file in two sections.

### A. HARD RULES
Rules only, one line each, in this format:
`- [marker] rule, stated plainly and concretely. (cite)`
- marker = 🔒 / ✅ / ⚖️ (a flexible rule still counts as the default) / 🚫 (retired tripwire: "never X").
- cite = the codex way, e.g. `04 v5-cc`, `R112`, `BANK 11-17`.
- Group the lines under short headings.
- Write so that a writer can act on each line in a scene. "Burnout = the body breaks: bleeding, organ failure, death; never a mana bar" beats "burnout is a cost".
- Include numbers wherever canon gives them (lifespans, dates, distances, era gaps).
- Add a subsection **OPEN: don't decide in a story**. List each open world question from your files as one line (cite the NP#/L# if known). The point is that writers write *around* these, not invent answers.
- Don't include story-level (🟡) details as rules unless they are locked. Don't add your own ideas.

### B. FLAVOR / AURA
Short bullets on how this part of the world *feels*: textures, smells, sounds, the attitude, the emotional register. Also cover what makes it Patrick's rather than generic fantasy or sci-fi, and the images and contrasts he keeps returning to. **Quote Patrick's own words** wherever the files record them (BANK lines, t### turns, his phrasings), with cites. Mark anything you infer as `(inferred)`.

## Constraints
- Be dense and exact, not exhaustive prose. Aim for about 1,500–3,000 words per file.
- No real-world racial or ethnic analogies, and no clinical labels.
- Don't edit any file except your own output.
