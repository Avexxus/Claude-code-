## 1. Settled already

Each race has its own road (`07`, t438–444): humans traverse hell, which is the one elven prison realm (bank NP#5), and that route is how breakouts happen. Dwarves use a dimensional squeeze. Elves shrink their own pocket (heaven), cross, then expand it, and the bond is cut while they're inside (t600). Dragons burst-jump on rotating chamber crews, with the master as the final lever (t442). Dwarves build the drives and focusing chambers everyone uses (t538). Giants build the great hulls. Cathedral ships are converged dead, and later bought orcs (t96, t276–280). Ship power by race: dragon burst (about 5 beams, never on the page), elven sustained bond-pool, human gun crews. Both mechanical and Remnant-augmented modes exist at every scale (`07`). Under the new tech rules (bank 2026-11-02), simple machines run on electricity, and only advanced-AI machines carry a flesh soul-core. Machines can't sense souls. Fast messaging exists without the bond. The elven homeworld shines like a beacon to every elf (bank L9). Open: giant fold, Returned stutter, dragon exotic fuel (F74 / R-M9), and how the hell route works (R-C1).

## 2. The catalog

### A. The FTL roads (how each actually works)

1. **Mechanical squeeze drive**
   - *Convention:* warp bubble.
   - *Here:* A dwarven field engine that copies the squeeze effect by machinery, never the act (jul08 t339–340). It needs no soul-core, so it's the galaxy's workhorse that anyone can buy. It's slowest of the roads.
   - *Closes:* R-M9 (treat FTL as craft, not as the cut domain).
   - **TWIST** (touches R-M9, the space-folding cut)

2. **Ensouled squeeze drive**
   - *Convention:* premium jump core.
   - *Here:* The same engine with a soul-core steering the field: faster and tighter, and expensive. Dwarves sell the mechanical drive to everyone and the cored drive to whoever pays most (t538 neutrality).
   - **FITS** (layered tech)

3. **Transmuted drive mass**
   - *Convention:* exotic matter fuel.
   - *Here:* The squeeze field needs heavy, unstable elements that only the dragons who study transmutation can make. This is the stone-making industry (bank 2026-11-01) selling to dwarven drive-makers. It's fuel made of matter, never of Remnant.
   - *Closes:* F74's "dragon exotic fuel" (t442 "It does fit").
   - **FITS**

4. **The hell-gate engine**
   - *Convention:* realm-shortcut drive.
   - *Here:* A human copy of the elven rift device, pushing the ship into hell and back out. It needs a large soul-core to hold the opening. How humans got it stays open (bank NP#5: a traitor elf or their own research).
   - **FITS**

5. **The shrunken prison**
   - *Convention:* compressed subspace.
   - *Here:* Hell is a shortcut because the elves keep it shrunk as part of the prison's design: cramped, dark and starving. Humans exploit the shape of the elves' own cage.
   - *Opens:* why don't the elves expand it and strand human shipping? (See Q-A.)
   - **TWIST** (touches W27–29 clone-pockets)

6. **The heaven crossing**
   - *Convention:* private hyperspace lane.
   - *Here:* Elven ships enter heaven, it's shrunk under them, they cross, then it's expanded. The crew's bond-pool powers the shrink (amplifier, never fuel). Crews go silent to the collective in transit (t600).
   - **FITS**

7. **Chamber-rotation burst-jump**
   - *Convention:* jump drive with a cooldown.
   - *Here:* Dragon crews take turns in focusing chambers, and fatigue is the cooldown. The master's overdrive burst (t442) risks burnout, the folk word for overuse wrecking the body (bank NP#2). Chamber veterans carry visible burnout scars as rank.
   - **FITS**

8. **Returned stutter**
   - *Convention:* phase-skip.
   - *Here:* The Returned's own learned phasing magic (t148), worked by crews in Returned-built hulls: short, ugly hops. It's the only craft that can slip into the elven pocket uninvited, but it still needs the traitor elf to know where and when (t602 ambush).
   - *Closes:* F74 "stutter", and gives t602 a mechanism.
   - **TWIST** (touches Law 8: their own magic, not Rule)

9. **Retire the giant fold**
   - *Convention:* space-folding.
   - *Here:* Giants build hulls, they don't drive them. Their own war fleets (t416) fly on dwarven squeeze drives.
   - *Closes:* F74 "fold", and keeps the cut clean.
   - **FITS** (recommend 🚫)

10. **Riders**
    - *Convention:* stowaway races.
    - *Here:* Changelings and orcs have no road of their own. Changelings ride inside other people's ships; orcs ride as cargo, crew, or literally as the hull (t280–281).
    - *Closes:* the F74 riders item.
    - **FITS**

### B. Hell-crossing specifics

11. **Fraying the seal**
    - *Convention:* each jump weakens the barrier.
    - *Here:* Every human crossing thins the seal between the realities a little (F75, t439). The busy lanes are where breakouts cluster, so the elves hate human traffic.
    - *Closes:* F75 under the one-hell ruling.
    - **FITS**

12. **Exit pickets**
    - *Convention:* customs blockade.
    - *Here:* Inquisition ships wait at the busy hell exits. Up close, elves feel any dark elf aboard as "wrong and warped" (t228, bank L9: close range works). The human Legion escorts convoys through in open defiance (t490), and that's a standing flashpoint.
    - **FITS**

13. **The long road to the lanes**
    - *Convention:* refugee trail.
    - *Here:* The 50–200-year escape with companions (t610) is the trek across hell to reach a human shipping lane. Hell's societies cluster near the lanes.
    - **FITS**

14. **Lane trade in hell**
    - *Convention:* frontier toll.
    - *Here:* Human crews quietly trade with hell's societies: supplies inward, passage outward. The changeling farms (W26) sell to the same crews.
    - **TWIST** (touches W25–26)

15. **Running dark**
    - *Convention:* silent running.
    - *Here:* Hell is total darkness (t233), and its people don't run on machines that sense. Crews cut lights and emissions to cross unnoticed, and the crossing's dread is the quiet, not monsters. Hell has no native beings (bank NP#5).
    - **FITS**

### C. Navigation and messaging

16. **Beacon bearing**
    - *Convention:* pulsar navigation.
    - *Here:* Coming out of heaven, an elven crew takes its bearing from the homeworld's bond-glow, which every elf feels (bank L9). Lone elven ships are pinpricks and nearly impossible for other elves to find.
    - **FITS**

17. **Hell charts and dead reckoning**
    - *Convention:* blind astrogation.
    - *Here:* Hell is a lifeless copy of our dimension (W27), so its layout is chartable. There's no light, so ships rely on charts, timing and lane markers.
    - **FITS**

18. **Wrecker beacons**
    - *Convention:* false lights.
    - *Here:* Dark-elf raiders move or fake hell's lane markers to strand ships, then strip them.
    - **FITS**

19. **Construct astrogators**
    - *Convention:* nav computer.
    - *Here:* Plain soulless digital constructs do the math. They're cheap, perfect inside the digital, and blind to souls (`07` ASI line).
    - **FITS**

20. **Squeeze-packet relays**
    - *Convention:* courier drones and FTL comms.
    - *Here:* Chains of mechanical relays squeeze message packets from station to station. This is the fast messaging that doesn't need the bond (bank 2026-11-02). Cutting a chain isolates a sector.
    - **FITS**

### D. Ship classes

21. **Convoy haulers**
    - *Convention:* bulk freighter.
    - *Here:* Earth's food convoys (`07`, no farmland) on mechanical squeeze, run by drones and orc deckhands. They're the slow artery that keeps the capital fed.
    - **FITS**

22. **Cathedral ships as moving Towers**
    - *Convention:* living flagship.
    - *Here:* A converged hull is a centerless mass of trapped Remnant, like the Tower (bank L7), so it's an amplifier. That's why giant hulls with dwarven chambers are the supreme works (`07`), and why Derrek keeps one (t222–223) even though he rarely leaves the Tower.
    - *Closes:* the "rarely leaves" vs owning a ship tension.
    - **TWIST**

23. **Orc-hull ships**
    - *Convention:* cheap mass-produced hull.
    - *Here:* Converged bought orcs (t280–281) amplify exactly as well, but the market prices them as "tainted" (bank tainted cores). Shrewd buyers get real amplification at a discount.
    - **TWIST** (touches "souls are all the same")

24. **Dragon home-brew**
    - *Convention:* local refit.
    - *Here:* Dwarven chambers in dragon-built frames with no converged hull (t444), so they get no amplification. That gap is why cathedral hulls are the prestige prize.
    - **FITS**

25. **Seed ships**
    - *Convention:* colony ark.
    - *Here:* Clone vats and grown flesh in the hold. Those who die on the voyage get the funerary Convergence, which releases the soul (`07`), and their bodies are grown into the colony's first tower.
    - **TWIST**

26. **Palace yachts**
    - *Convention:* luxury liner.
    - *Here:* The far-colony rich (W59) on ensouled drone-crewed yachts, with hidden fleets behind them.
    - **FITS**

27. **Remnant tankers**
    - *Convention:* fuel tanker.
    - *Here:* They haul pool Remnant from the wells held in flesh vats, because only flesh holds it well. A breach doesn't explode; the Remnant just drifts loose, and salvagers swarm the spill.
    - **TWIST** (touches W10, nothing burned)

### E. Docking, stations, orbital infrastructure

28. **Grown elevators and spires**
    - *Convention:* space elevator.
    - *Here:* Convergence-grown spires (bank 2026-11-02). Funerary-grown ones are plain material. Soul-trapping ones faintly feel and amplify, and the public just calls them "the better grade."
    - **TWIST**

29. **A dwarven dock in every port**
    - *Convention:* neutral free port.
    - *Here:* This mirrors the dwarven district in every city (jul08 t223). They refit anyone's ship, shown and never told.
    - **FITS**

30. **Forge worlds as yards**
    - *Convention:* shipyard world.
    - *Here:* Giant forge worlds (t98) and asteroid forges (t51). The "drained slave planet" (t54) is reread as bound slaves amplifying the forges, not batteries.
    - *Closes:* t54's 🌊 W10 note.
    - **FITS**

31. **Hell-gate stations**
    - *Convention:* jump gate.
    - *Here:* Big fixed gates for bulk human traffic, beside onboard engines on warships. Gates are chokepoints and the pickets' main target. (See Q-B.)
    - **FITS**

### F. Ship AI and life support

32. **Three grades of ship-mind**
    - *Convention:* ship AI.
    - *Here:* A held mind, an infused core, or a plain construct (bank). Crews say a cored ship "comes alive." Each grade is a market tier.
    - **FITS**

33. **Core moods**
    - *Convention:* haunted ship.
    - *Here:* Soul-cores feel only vague strangeness (bank Q-A). Spacers' lore says cores go "off" in hell, and that's only superstition.
    - **FITS**

34. **Flesh aces and chrome pilots**
    - *Convention:* cyborg pilot.
    - *Here:* Metal augments dull magic (bank), so Remnant-augmented aces stay flesh or flesh-grown, while mechanical-fighter pilots chrome up freely. That makes two pilot cultures (`07` two fighter modes).
    - **FITS**

35. **Grown life support**
    - *Convention:* bio-recycler.
    - *Here:* Plain machinery by default. Converged and grown ships add flesh-grown scrubbing organs supplied by the clone-organ trade.
    - **FITS**

### G. Piracy and smuggling

36. **Core-rippers**
    - *Convention:* chop shop.
    - *Here:* Pirates cut the soul-core out of a ship, which is "a thing with a soul bound in it" and so sellable (bank money). A gutted ship flies dumb.
    - **FITS**

37. **Counterfeit cores**
    - *Convention:* knock-off parts.
    - *Here:* Orc-soul cores relabeled as pool cores (bank) in second-hand ships.
    - **FITS**

38. **Chip-runners**
    - *Convention:* forged papers.
    - *Here:* The bond-spoof chip gets smugglers past elven pickets and ports. It's rare and costly (jul23 t150).
    - **RISKY** (touches the chip's timing, R-PL2)

39. **Sale hulls**
    - *Convention:* slaver ship.
    - *Here:* Dwarven traffickers move captured dark elves from the Inquisition to rich humans (W68), often back through hell itself.
    - **FITS**

### H. Ship combat (systems level)

40. **Blind until it fires**
    - *Convention:* fog of war.
    - *Here:* Machines can't sense souls, so a Remnant-augmented ship looks the same as a mechanical one until it acts. Only rare soul-reading-chip sensors tell them apart.
    - **FITS** (bank ruling 3)

41. **Gunner rigs**
    - *Convention:* power armor gunner.
    - *Here:* Human gun crews (`07`) wear amplifier rigs, and burned-out gunners are the veterans' tragedy.
    - **FITS**

42. **Cored munitions**
    - *Convention:* smart missiles.
    - *Here:* Ensouled drones and missiles (bank). When they detonate, the Remnant isn't destroyed (W10); it drifts, so old battlefields become shallow wells.
    - **TWIST**

43. **The digital arena**
    - *Convention:* electronic warfare.
    - *Here:* Plain constructs are gods in the digital, and ensouled minds fight there and in the real world (`07` ASI cage-fight).
    - **FITS**

44. **Orc-crew kill switch**
    - *Convention:* mutiny lock.
    - *Here:* Orc chips (t624) fitted to orc crews: drain, puppet, kill.
    - **FITS**

45. **Ship-mage**
    - *Convention:* ship with a soul.
    - *Here:* An infused AI given real receptors could grip reality (bank Q-B). A ship-scale mage is rare and feared.
    - **RISKY** (could swamp Law 1 scale; see Q-F)

46. **Hell-step booths**
    - *Convention:* teleporter.
    - *Here:* A short hop in and out of hell: the same road at room scale, not space-folding. Derrek's Tower "teleprompter rooms" (t62) may be this.
    - **RISKY** (touches the cut, R-M9; see Q-G)

## 3. Avoid

- A bloodline of hell-pilots. That's blood rather than conditions, and it's too close to Warhammer 40k.
- Warding fields against realm-monsters. Hell has no native beings (bank NP#5).
- Any drive that burns or spends Remnant (W10).
- Giant space-folding, or any wieldable fold or teleport magic (the cut).
- General soul-radar on warships. That takes the elves' gift (bank ruling 3).
- Skull-and-cable cyborg crews or mind-wiped crew-meat (Warhammer 40k look).
- Aliens, or "first contact" derelicts. The galaxy has none.
- Dragons flying fighters, or being ridden (t444, bank).
