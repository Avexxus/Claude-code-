# Trope flavor bank: orcs, goblins, trolls, changelings, in-betweeners, dark elves

🌊 **Claude proposal, all of it.** Nothing here is canon. Not yet run through canon-checker.
Generic genre conventions only, nothing copied from any named work. No invented names; roles only.
Tags: **FITS** drops straight in · **TWIST** trope inverted to fit canon · **RISKY** needs Patrick to bend something (named).
Cites: codex style; "bank 11-01" = `decisions/BANK.md` entries dated 2026-11-01 (not yet passed).

## 1. ORCS

**Already has:** three masters; grievance without archive; HATE as their own word; pact-craft as "everyone's religion with a poorer patron"; shortest lives, overcrowded; fracture inside each orc; far-future cattle and ship stock; orc chips; the Orc King as a symbolic plague-pact title; never organized (`10`, t520–526, W51).

**Reader-trap note:** most details below show the behavior first and leave the condition under it for later. The slide to "maybe they deserve it" is the indictment (jul08 t181).

### Daily life and customs
- **Live fast, die young** → nobody glorifies it. Orcs just can't plan past it: food is eaten when it's found, debts are never called in, promises cover days. Outsiders call it appetite. **TWIST** (clock; "short-horizoned, not stupid", `10`)
- **Clans and tribes** → there are none. Pens, work gangs and prison blocks are the only units. Loyalty forms to the gang you were sorted into and dies when the gang is re-sorted. **TWIST** (t520 never organized; t524)
- **Crowded warrens** → fights over a bunk or a ration are the daily texture, and the reader sees them first. **FITS** (🔒 nature: overcrowded, fight among themselves most)

### Rites
- **Blood-oath scarring** → the chip scar from birth is the one rite every orc shares, and it was done *to* them. Some gangs add a second cut beside it after the fact, as the child's "naming." **TWIST** (orc device, `01`; W51)
- **Coming of age by first blood** → a "first-hate" rite instead: an occult leader gives the child its grievance orally. It's a wrong with no names in it, only "the ones above," and the child repeats it back. **FITS** (grievance without archive; oral tradition, `10`)
- **Warrior's pyre** → orcs rarely keep their dead, since bodies go to processing. Mourning happens over an empty space, or over a chip that was cut out. **FITS** (t524 "food or luggage or space craft")

### Craft and art
- **Crude brutal iron** → orcs make nothing they get to keep. Their craft is repairing their masters' gear. Their art is what can't be confiscated: spoken grievance, chant, and marks on the body. **TWIST** (the wound transmits, the history doesn't)
- **Trophy-taking** → small things stolen from masters and hoarded, punished when found. A private ledger of restitution. **FITS** (hunger as restitution, `10`)
- **Bone-and-hide gear** → inverted: orcs *are* the material. The galaxy's cheapest flesh weapons and hulls are made from bought orcs. An orc handed a flesh blade may be holding kin. **FITS** (bank 11-01 flesh weapons ✅; t278–280; t524)

### War and arms
- **The endless horde** → there has never been an orc army. Every legion belonged to someone else (the dragons' coalition, human penal units), and the Orc King's "army" is empty animated flesh. **TWIST** (t520, t522)
- **Berserker rage** → pact fighters cast past what their bodies can take. Burnout, the body ruined while the Remnant stays whole, is a common orc death and counts as a proud one in orc talk. **FITS** (bank 10-31 burnout; pact resonance, `06`)
- **War chant and drums** → the first-hate words, used as a battle cry. Masters ban them in the pens. **FITS**

### Law, crime and punishment
- **Might makes right** → strength sets the pecking order, but it's the masters' rule copied: the strongest orc is made overseer and punishes on the masters' behalf. The same replication orcs run on goblins. **FITS** (trauma-replication, `10`)
- **Brutal summary justice** → it's remote and instant, through the chip's kill switch. Orcs keep folk rules about "what wakes the chip," part real and part superstition. **FITS** (orc device kill switch; "executed for trifles," `07`)

### Religion and superstition
- **Shaman with a demon patron** → pact priests whose patrons are small, cheap and cruel. A priest who can really cast is rare and burns out young. **FITS** (`10` poorer patron; `06` technique-not-Remnant)
- **Curses and omens** → curses aimed at an unnamed "above" are believed to land slowly, over generations. Many orcs read the Orc King as a curse finally landing, with awe and with dread. **FITS** (t522 awe and pride, makes orcs' image worse)

### What others say about them
- **"Born brutes / they earned it"** → this is the world's voice only. Humans feel nothing, the way you feel nothing about livestock, and they cite orc-on-orc violence as proof. **FITS** (jul08 t183; "earned it" is purely the world's lie)
- **"All orcs are alike"** → the galaxy calls goblins and trolls "orcs" too. Orcs resent being lumped in with the races they once lumped. **FITS** (t566; jul23 t64 blanket title)

### Small texture
- **The rare grey elder** → an old orc is a curiosity, and grey hair unsettles other orcs. Elders become occult leaders mostly because they survived. **FITS** (shortest-lived)
- **Names** → orcs answer to a ledger designation. Their personal names are spoken only in the first-hate rite. **TWIST**
- **On the ship** → orcs crewing cathedral or orc ships know the hull may be kin, and some won't sleep on the deck. **FITS** (t281 "the floor is orcs" 🌊; t276–280)

### Tropes to avoid
- Born evil, or "they earned it" as the author's view: it breaks conditions-not-blood and the reader-trap (`10`).
- An orc horde or kingdom under a past warlord: orcs never had a king (t520; 🚫 Orc King's past setting).
- Orcs made in pits by a dark lord: every race coalesced in the shatter (`07` generations).
- Half-orcs as a trope: crossbreeding isn't established, and it leans on blood.
- "Noble savage" honor culture or dialect speech: real-world coding.
- Liberation by a kind outsider hero: the B3 rhyme is 🚫 retired. Rebellion spreads as contagion (t526).
- Orcs as stupid: they're "short-horizoned, not stupid" (`10`).

### Top 5 (orcs)
1. **The first-hate rite.** It turns "grievance without archive" into a scene you can stage: a wrong with no names.
2. **Flesh weapons made from orcs.** It joins the new ✅ ruling to t524 and the cathedral ships.
3. **The chip scar as their only rite.** It ties orc culture to Derrek's device.
4. **Burnout as the proud orc death.** It joins the burnout ruling to pact-craft and the Orc King.
5. **"All orcs are alike."** The blanket-title irony, aimed back at the orcs.

## 2. GOBLINS

**Already has:** the orcs' underclass, scarred until pain is their core memory; they lost coherence and act as orc "pets"; they are the same as any being, just weaker; no history and no city, ever; in the far future, counted and called "small orcs" (`10`, jul23 t64–68, t566). Thin by ruling, so these are small details only.

### Daily life and customs
- **Swarming numbers** → kept in numbers by orcs as labor and fodder. They never organize themselves. **FITS** (`10`)
- **Warrens** → no goblin home exists. They sleep in their owners' spaces: under bunks, in pen corners, in ducts. **TWIST** (no goblin city, jul23 t64)
- **Scavengers** → they eat what orcs leave. The only things a goblin owns are things it found. **FITS**

### Rites
- **Goblin ceremonies** → none they remember. They copy orc rites without the words, like a mimed first-hate rite. The chain of pain, one rung lower. **TWIST** (trauma-replication, `10`)
- **Naming** → owners name them for a task or a trait. **FITS**

### Craft and art
- **Tinkerers** → small hands, trained for crawlspaces, machinery and ship ducts. It's a skill, not invention, and the owner takes the credit. **TWIST**
- **Trap-makers** → snares for vermin and food. The one craft passed goblin to goblin by watching, not telling. **FITS**

### War and arms
- **Expendable fodder** → sent ahead of orc gangs to trip defenses. **FITS** (`10` cannon fodder)
- **Cowardly** → a flinch learned from pain. They scatter because standing was always punished. **TWIST**
- **Beast-riders** → instead they tend the masters' animals, because they're treated as animals themselves. **TWIST**

### Law, crime and punishment
- **Thieves** → orcs punish goblin theft the way humans punish orc theft: the same punishments, scaled down. **FITS** (cast-of-pain chain, jul23 t64)
- **Counted as orcs** → so they're chipped at birth like orcs. The kill switch doesn't know the difference. **FITS** (t566 + orc device). *Opens:* goblin chips aren't stated anywhere.

### Religion and superstition
- **Crude idols** → secondhand faith. They revere their owners' pact patrons *through* the owners, praying toward whoever holds the leash. **TWIST** (`10` orc religion)
- **Superstitious dread** → rumors of being "processed." **FITS** (F136, Claude's, 🌊)

### What others say about them
- **"Pests"** → the galaxy calls them "small orcs" and doesn't bother naming them, and blames them for carrying the Orc King's plague. **FITS** (t566; F136 🌊)
- **Orcs' view** → pets. Some orcs genuinely love their goblins the way one loves an animal: warmth inside the cruelty. **FITS** (grading, no monoliths)

### Small texture
- **Cackling** → appeasement laughter. Goblins laugh when struck, because it shortened beatings. **TWIST**
- **Hoarding shiny things** → a few kept objects as the only proof of a self, with no story attached. **FITS**
- **No history** → each goblin remembers its own life, and nobody records it. **FITS** (orc "priority, not incapacity" carried down)

### Tropes to avoid
- A hidden rich goblin culture or city reveal: banned, nothing left to recover (jul23 t64).
- A goblin–troll alliance: 🚫 retired (jul23 t66).
- Goblins as comic vermin with no cost: that's the wrong register.
- Goblin genius inventors, bankers or markets: a specific genre package, and it breaks "lost coherence."
- Goblins as evil or cruel by nature: no racial sins.

### Top 5 (goblins)
1. **The mimed first-hate rite.** Trauma-replication made visible, tied to the orc top pick.
2. **Chipped because they're counted as orcs.** Dark irony from t566 plus the orc device.
3. **Secondhand faith toward the leash.** Gives goblins a religion (chat t331) without inventing a culture.
4. **Appeasement laughter.** One line that inverts the trope and costs something.
5. **Orcs who love their goblins.** Grades the cruelty and keeps orcs from being a monolith.

## 3. TROLLS

**Already has:** a subordinate latecomer race; the orc tragedy repeated "to an even more ruthlessly complete degree"; "a few members secretly whisper to one another while in their ghettos"; no troll fortress ever (`10`, jul23 t64, t66). Small details only, by ruling.

### Daily life and customs
- **Under the bridge** → trolls live in ghettos under and inside other people's infrastructure: undercities, beneath transit spans. **TWIST** (ghettos, jul23 t66)
- **Solitary hermits** → they live crowded, not alone, but they don't talk openly. They're silent together. **TWIST**

### Rites
- **Riddles at the bridge** → the whisper itself is the rite: a few words passed mouth to ear. Outsiders never hear what's said. **FITS** (jul23 t66)
- **Mourning** → done in silence, because loud grief drew punishment. **FITS**

### Craft and art
- **Club and boulder** → heavy labor; whatever craft they had is gone. **FITS**
- **Turned to stone by sunlight** → others' folk tale. Its root: masters once used troll dead as cheap wall material through the basic Convergence that survived. **RISKY**: adds troll history and needs Patrick to accept troll bodies used as building stock (W15; chat t377 "only the most basic use to forge walls"; bank 11-01 flesh weapons).

### War and arms
- **Brutal heavy hitters** → used as haulers and shock labor in other people's wars, never as a troll force. **FITS**
- **Regeneration** → a folk exaggeration. Trolls are worked while injured, so people believe they heal. There's no racial magic. **TWIST** (Law 8)

### Law, crime and punishment
- **Toll-keeper** → trolls are made to staff checkpoints and toll gates for their masters. They're hated as the face of the toll and keep none of it. **TWIST**
- **Ghetto rule** → curfews, and whispering in groups punished as conspiracy. **FITS**

### Religion and superstition
- **Troll beliefs** → something is whispered that outsiders never hear, and the reader never learns it. No reveal. **FITS** (small details, no fortress)

### What others say about them
- **Dumb brutes** → their silence is read as stupidity. **TWIST**
- **Man-eaters** → humans tell children trolls eat people, while trolls are the ones being consumed. **TWIST**
- **Lumped in** → the galaxy files them under "orcs." **FITS** (jul23 t64)

### Small texture
- **Huge bodies** → **RISKY**: a big body over a latecomer-sized soul sits against the size spectrum (fragment size → body durability, `07`). Patrick would need to say trolls are large-bodied but small-souled, or keep them human-scale.

### Tropes to avoid
- Trolls as monsters or man-eaters as fact: they're victims of the churn.
- A troll fortress or hidden kingdom: banned (jul23 t64).
- Regeneration or turning to stone as literal racial magic: matter does no magic, and races have no magic lean (Law 8).
- A troll–goblin alliance: 🚫 retired.
- Dumb comic muscle: no racial deficiency.

### Top 5 (trolls)
1. **The whisper as rite.** It builds only on Patrick's one line and keeps the mystery.
2. **The toll-keeper inversion.** The classic image turned into a forced job.
3. **"Man-eaters" while being consumed.** The reader-trap at small scale.
4. **Regeneration as folk exaggeration.** Keeps Law 8 clean.
5. **The stone myth** (RISKY). Strong if Patrick wants it, since it joins trolls to Convergence.

## 4. CHANGELINGS (soul thieves: a crime, not a race)

**Already has:** possessors, never shapeshifters; self-made from any race; every taking kills the host and is never witnessed; a pace of 1–2 souls; decay and rupture past the pace; largely monsters, with a few clever ones at court; institutions and serial bodies; the knowledge-gap tell and the soul–body mismatch; everyone knows they exist; two roads (transfer, consume); the gnome as the trusted village healer (`07`, W16–W22, t676, t680, bank 11-01).

### Daily life and customs
- **Testing kin** → families keep private questions only kin could answer, like a shared childhood detail, and ask them after an illness or a long absence. **FITS** (knowledge-gap tell, t282)
- **The patriarch who never dies** → the old man dies "naturally," and the grandson grows suddenly wise. The transfer road, old body into young. Off the page only. **FITS** (t676, t112)
- **Distrust of healers** → after tales like the gnome's, folk wariness of healers who are "too strong." The sick are brought in pairs, with a witness. **FITS** (bank 11-01 gnome healer)

### Rites
- **Burning the dead quickly** → swift cremation denies corpses to necromancers and ghost-binders, and denies a changeling its "burned mask" corpse. **FITS** (F90 🌊; t500 ghosts; jul23 t161 pt1)
- **Exorcism** → none exists. Every taking kills the host. Folk exorcists are frauds or worse, and they make a favorite cover. **TWIST** (t120)

### Craft and art
- **Soul-jar** → there isn't one. The crowd lives in the living body. Kill the holder and the held souls are freed or destroyed. **TWIST** (binding = concentration, t504–505)
- **A signature in hosts** → taste in bodies is a changeling's fingerprint. The gnome loves gnomish bodies; others hop to bigger ones to hide the mismatch. **FITS** (t772; W18)

### War and arms
- **Body-hop at the killing blow** → the moment is never shown. Only the aftermath: the killer finds a fresh corpse beside the old one. **FITS** (no-witness 🔒🔒; t120 a dead body left behind)
- **The sorcerer cornered** → leverage first, then instant elder-tier force when cornered. **FITS** (t544, `07`)

### Law, crime and punishment
- **Witch hunts** → mobs hunt changelings and mostly catch eccentrics and the grieving. "Soul thief" is also a handy cover charge, which is exactly the Inquisition's rot. **FITS** (W68; t680)
- **Punishment** → elves send soul thieves to hell, and everyone else improvises. **FITS** (t682)

### Religion and superstition
- **Demons** → demons are changelings far along the continuum, the ones who offer deals. Includes the con of a fake pact. **FITS** (t666, t548). *Feeds* NP#89.
- **Haunted houses** → every true ghost is a crime scene. Folk mistake echoes for ghosts and the reverse. **FITS** (t498–502)

### What others say about them
- **"The cold one"** → folk suspect the cold. But warmth is a performance, so the warmest get suspected too. **FITS** (t629c; jul23 t161)
- **Monsters in the wilds** → the decayed and the ruptured as folklore bogeymen: house-of-horrors tales. **FITS** (W19, W20)

### Small texture
- **A held voice surfaces** → a cold king says a dead child's phrase. The soul surfaces but doesn't drive. **FITS** (jul08 t291)
- **Vampire feeding hunger** → inverted: the pace is discipline, not hunger. The monsters are the ones who gorged. **TWIST** (W16, W22)
- **Rivals** → two changelings can work the same mark unknowingly. They don't form covens. **FITS** (jul08 t281)

### Tropes to avoid
- Shapeshifting, a face change as the reveal, or a doppelgänger meeting the original: possession only (🚫 #23).
- A witnessed transfer: 🔒🔒 pre-write check.
- Changeling children, or the folk cradle-swap: "no more babies" (🚫 #27).
- Changelings as a race, a secret society or a kingdom: a crime, not a people (🚫 #4, #32).
- "Infiltrate every delegation": 🚫 W19.
- Burning or spending souls as fuel: W10, and souls are amplifiers only (bank 10-31).
- Glowing eyes or any physical tell: the tell is knowledge gaps and mismatch.

### Top 5 (changelings)
1. **The patriarch who never dies.** The transfer road, made domestic and grim, never on page.
2. **Kin questions.** Turns the knowledge-gap tell into a custom.
3. **Distrust of healers.** Ripples from the new gnome ruling into folk life.
4. **No exorcism, no soul-jar.** Two inversions that teach the reader the rules.
5. **Witch hunts as cover.** Joins folk paranoia to the Inquisition's rot (W68).

## 5. IN-BETWEENERS / RULE-BEASTS (the fae and trickster slot)

**Already has:** older than the One and not descended from it; their own bubble between worlds; obey physics; run the Rule, not Remnant; Void, Chain, two states, splitting; kit, honor code and Cascade (⚖️); Afflicted and Victor; maze-land; the dwarves' Sale; the Tower rule-beast; alien "remakers"; transition is their food (`06`, `07`, W37).

### Daily life and customs
- **Fairy rings and mounds** → folk avoid land where roads loop back to a castle on a hill. The maze is just land. **FITS** (t152–158)
- **Never say their name** → folk speak of them indirectly, partly because nobody knows what they are. **FITS**
- **Offerings at the door** → useless. They don't want milk or gold, they want a good contest. Leaving gifts is courting attention. **TWIST** (honor code)

### Rites
- **Fair bargains bind both sides** → the Rule traps the beast too. The folk habit: listen to the exact wording. **FITS** (`06` the Rule)
- **The tithe to the otherworld** → the dwarves' Sale. Told in dwarven halls as a ledger entry, not a myth. **FITS** (t146–148, t622)
- **The fae champion** → mortals given Rules (the Afflicted) are their tests, set loose to fight. **FITS** (`06` cultivating opponents)

### Craft and art
- **Fae gifts with a price** → captured trinkets that obey physics but behave like rules. Folk fear them; the Returned take them apart. **FITS** (W37). *Touches* R-R5.
- **Glamour** → the Void laid over a real room, so those inside don't notice at first. **FITS** (t430)

### War and arms
- **The wild hunt** → they don't hunt the weak. They hunt matched duels, and their ultimate quarry is Jon and Derrek. **TWIST** (honor code; threshold-sense)
- **Cold iron** → folk iron charms do nothing. The real counters are pinning a manifested body (a gravity anchor) and solving the condition. **TWIST** (`06` Unfixing; Law 0c)
- **They cheat when losing** → Cascade, signaled when the beast goes still. **FITS** (`06`)

### Law, crime and punishment
- **The fae can't lie** → they don't need to. The trap is the Rule's wording, and the fight is deducing it. **TWIST** (`06` puzzle-prison)
- **Win the game, earn the gift** → the Victor is awarded the Rule. **FITS** (⚖️; R-R2 open)
- **The honor code vs the spider** → their fracture: the ideal is fair, uncertain contests, but many only take games they're sure of. **TWIST**. *Closes* R-R3 by making the clash the point.

### Religion and superstition
- **Worshipped as old powers** → some faiths do worship them, and get nothing back: no Remnant means no pacts. **TWIST** (`10` W57: in-betweeners are among the worshipped)
- **Reverence for the maze** → some Returned keep maze habits and quietly honor the web that made them. **FITS**. *Feeds* t623 (open).

### What others say about them
- **"Timeless"** → wrong. They're truly immortal by mechanism (4b) but not outside time. Coming home from the maze is unremarkable, with no lost centuries. **TWIST** (t154, t163)

### Small texture
- **Shapeless, many-bodied** → a piece split too far wanders, not knowing it was ever part of something: a ghost story with no ghost in it. **FITS** (t164)
- **Spirited away** → Chain drags you into the Void, and witnesses only see someone gone. **FITS** (t126)

### Tropes to avoid
- A fae realm "outside time and space": that's Jon and Derrek (t163; R-C2 open).
- Fae time-slip, a night that costs a century: same reason.
- Iron, salt or true names as literal magic: matter does no magic, and there's no name magic.
- Fae courts paired in light and dark: monolith politics, and the light/dark halves are 🚫.
- The cradle-swap: changelings are possessors, and no children.
- Calling them "fae": the fae is a separate, undeveloped otherworld kept out of this (`07` L272).
- In-betweeners granting pacts or Remnant: they run the Rule, and pacts are soul-magic (`06`).
- Returned stealing Rule magic: Law 8, t148.
- Seductive fae: never titillate.
- Rules of three: no forced triads (W33).

### Top 5 (in-betweeners)
1. **The honor code vs the spider as their fracture.** Closes R-R3.
2. **The Sale as the otherworld's tithe.** The genre's oldest fae beat, already canon.
3. **Cold iron does nothing; pinning does.** Teaches the real counter.
4. **"They can't lie": they don't need to.** The puzzle-prison as folk wisdom.
5. **Offerings are useless; contests aren't.** Sets them apart from fae in one line.

## 6. DARK ELVES (hell-born exiles; the name is a placeholder)

**Already has:** hell is a lifeless clone-pocket of total darkness, drought and famine; the crime was soul theft (drifting toward dissidents, 🌊); societies inside; farms run by changelings; "a god-forge full of elf-haters"; a large population, many born outside; escapes take 50–200 years with companions; the martial god; the companion; the rupture changeling; hunted and sold by the Inquisition; one hell, and humans cross it for FTL (`07`, W25–W26, W33, W68, chat t401, bank 11-01).

### Daily life and customs
- **The dark underworld** → hell is a dead copy of our world: the same coastlines and mountains, all lifeless and black. Dark elves know Earth's shape by touch and have never seen it lit. **TWIST** (W27 clone-pocket)
- **Light-blind** → the hell-born find the outside's light painful. New escapees keep to the shade. **FITS** (total darkness, t228)
- **Scarcity** → water as wealth, and strict sharing or hoarding norms. **FITS** (drought, famine, t228)
- **Bond noise** → in a full hell the bond is constant crowd-noise. Outside, a lone dark elf feels nothing for the first time, and the silence terrifies. **FITS** (bank 11-01 bond scaling; t602–604)

### Rites
- **The trial passage** → the escape road is the rite: decades of walking with companions, and whoever arrives has been changed. **FITS** (t610)
- **An oath to a dark goddess** → inverted: an oath *never* to take a soul, sworn under the martial god. Descendants of soul thieves become the people who hate soul thieves most. **TWIST** (W33; t682; W26 farms)

### Craft and art
- **Poisons and webs** → none. Craft comes from a lifeless world, so the only organic material is their own dead. Bone tools carry lineage. **TWIST**

### War and arms
- **Raiders from below** → the hell-lanes. Humans cross hell for FTL, and dark elves stow away on, raid or hijack the transits. That's where breakouts happen. **FITS** (bank 11-01 NP#5; t438)
- **Martial discipline** → a cult of the body. The martial god despises soul magic, so they fight with self-made magic only. **FITS** (W33)
- **Hunters of their jailers** → escaped bands hunt inquisitors. **FITS** (W33)

### Law, crime and punishment
- **Treacherous noble houses** → power in hell is the changeling-run farms against the free settlements, not houses. **TWIST** (W26; t606)
- **Slavers** → they're the ones enslaved: sold by the Inquisition through dwarven traffickers into the organ trade. **TWIST** (W68; t351)

### Religion and superstition
- **Demon worship** → "demon" is a name earned by the strong, not a race. Some powers in hell are called demons, and the monk fuses with dark elves. **FITS** (bank 11-01; W24)
- **Hatred of surface kin** → the beacon: escapees feel the elven homeworld of trillions shining, the people who shut them away, always visible. **RISKY**: needs Patrick to confirm the homeworld beacon reaches exiles once they're back in our world (bank 11-01 bond; t230 applies only across realities).

### What others say about them
- **"Twisted kin"** → elves feel them as wrong and warped, and the galaxy stays on alert. **FITS** (t228)
- **"Soul thieves"** → the human buyers' label, used as cover. **FITS** (W68)

### Small texture
- **Born outside** → the second generation in human slums inherits hell's habits: sleeping in total dark, rationing water, hearing the crowd. **FITS** (chat t401)
- **Heaven-empty irony** → hell-born joke about why heaven is empty. **FITS** (t602–604)

### Tropes to avoid
- The name "drow": banned (t233).
- Skin color as moral coding: real-world racial coding.
- A spider-goddess matriarchy: a specific genre package.
- Innate cruelty: conditions, not blood. Drought, famine and the farms shaped them.
- A separate demon home plane: banned (bank 11-01; too close to an existing setting).
- Underground cities beneath Earth: hell is a pocket copy, not a cave system.
- Settling the medieval-sect question by accident: R-RA5 stays open.
- "Elves built hell" as fact: in-world belief only (W28–29).

### Top 5 (dark elves)
1. **A dead copy of Earth, known by touch.** Turns W27 into a haunting detail.
2. **The hell-lanes.** Joins the new one-hell ruling, human FTL and breakouts.
3. **An oath never to take a soul.** The irony of descent from soul thieves, linked to the martial god and the farms.
4. **Silence outside.** Uses the new bond-scaling ruling to make exile felt.
5. **Slavers become the enslaved.** The drow-trope inversion through W68.
