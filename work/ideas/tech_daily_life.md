# Far-future tech: daily life and cities (2026-11-02)

🌊 **Claude proposal throughout.** Nothing here is canon. Not yet canon-checked. Brief: `work/ideas/_tech_brief.md`.

---

## 1. Settled already

Earth is one planet-wide city with the Tower at its heart. Its weather has long been controlled, its food comes in on huge convoys, and factions of humans, dragon houses and a few clever changelings run it. Orcs and the lower races are cattle there (`07` far-future Earth ⚖️). The rich live on far colonies where drones do the labor (wk W58–W60). Simple machines run on electricity. Only complex machines with an advanced AI need a flesh soul-core (BANK 11-02). AI comes in three kinds: held minds, Remnant-infused AI and soulless constructs, and only the ensouled kinds can cross into the physical world. Soulless constructs are sovereign inside the digital world and powerless outside it (`07` ASI). Machines can't sense souls, except for rare devices built on the spoof or reading chips. Fast interstellar messaging exists without the bond. Metal augments hurt your magic and flesh-grown ones don't. A soul can't be sold, but a being, or a thing with a soul bound into it, can be. The trusted currencies are an imperial coin, a dwarven mark and an elven silver crown (placeholders). The public thinks Remnant is a mined "battery" material. Clones are sold as "real". Tainted orc cores are sold as pool cores. The Tower is Earth's largest amplifier (BANK 11-01).

## 2. The catalog

### A. Personal devices and communication

1. **Plain handset**
   - Convention: the pocket phone or slate.
   - Here: plain electric machinery with no core and a soulless assistant. Everyone has one, the poor included. FITS.
2. **Ensouled personal assistant**
   - Convention: the AI companion.
   - Here: a thumb-sized soul-core in a sleek case. People call it more "alive" and quicker than a plain assistant, and owning one marks wealth. It feels only vague things and has no senses of its own (BANK 11-02 Q-A). FITS.
3. **Relay messaging**
   - Convention: FTL comms (fast messages between stars).
   - Here: chains of ensouled relay satellites carry fast interstellar messages with no bond involved, so elves hold no comms monopoly. Message cost rises with distance and priority, so the poor send text and the rich send presence. FITS.
4. **Broadcast walls and the faceless liturgy**
   - Convention: city-wide screens and propaganda feeds.
   - Here: plain screens on every surface. The Emperor appears only as a faceless figure or a voice (t364), and human zealotry (t488) turns his broadcasts into worship. FITS.
5. **Flesh-grown interface**
   - Convention: the neural jack (a brain-to-machine plug).
   - Here: a metal jack is cheap but dulls magic (BANK 11-02). Mages who want direct net access pay for a grown-flesh interface that keeps their grip. TWIST (it makes interface choice a class line).

### B. The net and the digital world

6. **Two-tier net**
   - Convention: AIs ruling cyberspace.
   - Here: soulless constructs are gods inside the net and helpless outside it (`07` ASI). Held minds live there too, and they are the only net-beings that can leave. Inside the net, the soulless rule. FITS.
7. **Crossing out**
   - Convention: the AI downloads into a body.
   - Here: a held mind rides out into an ensouled drone or robot. With real receptors it could likely do magic (BANK 11-02 Q-B). The public reads this as "an unusually smart drone." FITS.
8. **Arena duels in the net**
   - Convention: hacker battles shown as spectacle.
   - Here: constructs fight caged duels in the digital arena (`07` t-line "cell-fight"), with mass betting. Real mages are blind there. FITS.
9. **Mind leasing**
   - Convention: hired AI labor.
   - Here: firms rent out held minds by the hour to run machines. This is legal because a thing with a soul bound into it can be sold (BANK 11-02 money). FITS.

### C. Transport inside a planet-wide city

10. **Vertical spine lifts and deck rail**
    - Convention: arcology lifts and maglev.
    - Here: plain electric machinery that hauls millions up and down the stacked decks. The cheapest and slowest transit. FITS.
11. **Sky lanes with traffic minds**
    - Convention: flying-car traffic.
    - Here: the aircars are plain. Each lane's routing is run by one ensouled traffic mind (complex machinery, so it needs a core). When a core fails, its lane jams for kilometers. FITS.
12. **Dragon-kin mounts**
    - Convention: exotic riding beasts.
    - Here: rich humans ride captured lesser dragon-kin through the lanes as status. No great dragon would ever be ridden (BANK 11-01 riders; dragon umbrella). FITS.
13. **Food convoys and orbital lifts**
    - Convention: space elevators and freight fleets.
    - Here: Earth has no farmland, so it lives on imported food (`07`). The convoys are plain mechanical freighters (`07` humans: "needs no magic"). A late convoy means a hungry deck. FITS.
14. **Teleporter option A: squeeze gates**
    - Convention: the fixed teleport pad.
    - Here: a short-range version of the dwarven dimensional-squeeze drive (t438), built in fixed pairs, dwarven-run and very expensive. It is a machine copying an effect, not an act (jul08 t339). Nobody can do it as magic, so it stays outside the space-folding cut (QUESTIONS 5.7b). TWIST (rides on 5.7b).
15. **Teleporter option B: hell-step booths**
    - Convention: the cheap jump booth.
    - Here: the human FTL route through hell, the elven prison realm (t438, BANK NP#5), at a scale of seconds. It's cheap, it's for the masses, and every booth is a door into a full prison. Breakouts happen (BANK NP#5), and some travelers never arrive. RISKY (it puts hell's door in every neighborhood and touches L19 and hell's societies, t606).
16. **Teleporter option C: none**
    - Convention: none.
    - Here: "porting" is just street slang for the express tube, and real jumps happen only ship-scale on the racial drives. This keeps the seam closed entirely. FITS.

### D. Housing and structures

17. **Grown towers**
    - Convention: organic architecture.
    - Here: modern towers are Convergence, the Tower's own process, shaped small (BANK 11-02 tech 7). They are grown from bought lives, the way cathedral ships are (t278–t280). Only the rich live in grown housing. TWIST (it says what feeds them).
18. **Little Towers**
    - Convention: places of power.
    - Here: if a grown tower trapped its source souls the way the Tower did (BANK 11-01 L7), every grown tower is a small amplifier. Mages pay premiums to live "where you feel stronger," and nobody publicly knows why. RISKY (depends on Q2).
19. **Living tops, grey bottoms**
    - Convention: the stratified megacity.
    - Here: the upper decks are lit, weather-tuned and grown. The lower decks are poured plain stacks that never see the controlled sky. "Grey ruined Earth" and "living seat" are both true, by altitude. FITS. **Closes NP#60 / R-RA21.**
20. **The living house**
    - Convention: the smart home.
    - Here: a rich home runs on one ensouled house-mind, and "How alive is your house?" is a status line. The poor run plain timers. FITS.

### E. Food, weather, utilities

21. **Vat protein**
    - Convention: synthetic food.
    - Here: plain bioreactors grow cell protein with no brain and no core, the standard ration of the middle decks. FITS.
22. **Bottom-deck protein**
    - Convention: the grim secret of what's in the food.
    - Here: orc bodies are processed into food (t524). It isn't a secret. Most people feel nothing about it, the way people don't think about animals used for food (jul08 t183). Show the cost, never linger. FITS (grimdark register, Q5).
23. **Weather satellites**
    - Convention: climate control.
    - Here: satellites are ensouled machines (BANK 11-02 list), so Earth's controlled weather is run from orbit by soul-cores. Districts pay for sun; the poor get runoff and leftover cloud. FITS.
24. **Plain grid with grid-minds**
    - Convention: the fusion or power grid.
    - Here: ordinary energy powers everything, and Remnant never does (W10, amplifier-only ruling). A few ensouled grid-minds balance the load. FITS.
25. **"Charged core" scams**
    - Convention: snake-oil power cells.
    - Here: because the public believes Remnant is a battery (BANK 11-02 public view), shops sell "recharge" services and "full-charge" cores. Nothing drains, so the scam can never be caught by failure. FITS (it puts the in-world misconception on the page).

### F. Household robots and labor drones

26. **Plain labor drones**
    - Convention: worker bots.
    - Here: scripted electric machines on simple tasks. They are the bulk of drone labor everywhere. FITS.
27. **Ensouled work drones**
    - Convention: smart autonomous robots.
    - Here: cored drones handle judgment work: surgery, deep-site mining, the colonies' estates (wk W58–W60). Core size sets the drone's grade. FITS.
28. **Cheap bots on tainted cores**
    - Convention: knockoff electronics.
    - Here: bargain household robots carry orc-soul cores labeled as pool cores (BANK 11-02 tech 6). Assay shops grade cores by size, and some quietly know their source. FITS.
29. **The drone-or-orc ledger**
    - Convention: automation replacing workers.
    - Here: in many jobs an orc is cheaper than a drone. Owners run the math case by case, and the orc who loses the job doesn't get a severance (t280, t524). FITS (grimdark register).

### G. Money, banking, retail

30. **Counting houses and ledger minds**
    - Convention: AI banking.
    - Here: dwarven houses keep their books in ensouled ledger-minds. The dwarven mark is trusted because the houses are too entangled to fail (t540). The elven silver crown circulates on Earth as a reserve coin, though few elves live there. FITS.
31. **Cored collateral and indenture**
    - Convention: debt slavery.
    - Here: loans are secured on cored goods. A defaulter can be sold as a being, which is legal (BANK 11-02 money). This is the same road the gnomes walked (t568), and humans walk it too. FITS (conditions, not blood).
32. **Real-flesh certificates**
    - Convention: authentic goods versus fakes.
    - Here: clone organs pass as "real" (BANK 11-02). Clinics sell certificates, and forgers sell the same certificates. The legal market sits above the black one (chat t349). FITS.
33. **Augment clinics, two counters**
    - Convention: the cyber-clinic.
    - Here: metal parts are cheap and fast but dull your grip. Flesh-grown parts are dear and slow but keep your magic. Gifted poor take metal anyway and lose what made them rare. FITS.

### H. Education, law, surveillance

34. **Dragon academies**
    - Convention: elite schools.
    - Here: the oldest schools on Earth are dragon-run, which is why humans say "science" (wk W63). Derrek studied at one (wk W64). Their tuition is a dragon house's quiet leverage. FITS.
35. **Body-only surveillance**
    - Convention: the total-surveillance city.
    - Here: cameras, gait, face and gene readers everywhere, but none sense souls (BANK 11-02 rejection). A changeling in a body is invisible to the whole system. That fits "nobody guards against it" (BANK changeling paranoia). FITS.
36. **Reading-chip gates**
    - Convention: the security checkpoint.
    - Here: a handful of soul-reading gates built on the spoof or reading chips. They stand at the Tower and the Council halls, and a few stolen ones sit in private hands. FITS.
37. **Clone identity fraud**
    - Convention: identity theft.
    - Here: a clone is a perfect biometric match, so body-ID can't tell a clone from its original. The law treats clones as soulless objects (W66 pretense), which leaves a legal hole. TWIST.
38. **Legion patrols with rigs**
    - Convention: enforcer police.
    - Here: the Emperor's zealot Legion (t490) patrols in amplifier rigs. Burnout is the line officer's death (BANK burnout), and burnout wards fill the lower decks. FITS.

### I. Religion's tech and entertainment

39. **Reliquary amplifiers**
    - Convention: holy relics with power.
    - Here: a temple's reliquary holds pooled Remnant behind the altar and extends the priest's reach, so "miracles" are amplifier magic. That fits religion as a network of pact champions and name versus act (wk W57). FITS.
40. **Tower pilgrimage**
    - Convention: the holy city.
    - Here: pilgrims walk the decks inward toward the Tower. The folk belief is that "machines think better near the Tower," and real estate is priced by that belief. TWIST (depends on Q6).
41. **Rig-fight arenas**
    - Convention: blood-sport broadcasts.
    - Here: fighters in amplifier rigs fight on live feeds. Burnout on camera is part of the draw and the cost. Keep the register: cost, never relish. FITS.

## 3. Avoid

- **Remnant as grid power or a true battery.** It breaks W10 and the amplifier-only ruling (BANK 10-31). It is allowed only as the public's misconception.
- **Soul scanners as common gear.** Patrick rejected it: that gift belongs to the elves (BANK 11-02).
- **Scan-and-print teleport** (destroy the traveler, rebuild them elsewhere). It drags in where a printed body's soul comes from, it touches clones having souls (W66) and the rule that the soul leaves at death as an event (jul08 t342), and it opens a large new question.
- **Wieldable teleport or folding magic.** The domain is cut (C §8.14).
- **Mind upload as an escape from death.** Moving a soul is wholesale absorption, which makes it a changeling crime, not a consumer product.
- **Clone-body swapping as a rich man's immortality.** Same reason: it is the possessor road (t680).
- **A mass class of ASI-bodied slaves.** That's too close to Warhammer 40k's servitors. Keep the ASI-body as a rare horror (jul08 t343).
- **Skulls-and-cables looks.** Keep flesh cores hidden inside clean casings.
- **Souls or Remnant as money.** Ruled out (BANK 11-02).
- **One race owning a technology outright.** "Ideas spread" (BANK 11-02).
- **Naming Earth after a famous fictional city.** `07` already uses a famous sci-fi city-planet as its comparison. Keep it to "planet-wide city" (NP#9 / L23).

## 4. Questions for Patrick

**Q1. Teleporters?**
- a) none: "porting" is only slang
- b) dwarven squeeze gates only, for the rich
- c) hell-step booths only, for the masses
- d) both: squeeze gates for the rich, hell-step booths for the poor

Claude's pick: **d**. Both are ship drives shrunk down, machines copying an effect, so they sit outside the cut (QUESTIONS 5.7b). The poor stepping through hell is pure grimdark.

**Q2. Do grown towers trap their source souls?**
- a) no: plain grown flesh
- b) yes: every grown tower is a little Tower and a small amplifier
- c) only the oldest and biggest ones

Claude's pick: **b**. It ties the city's skyline to the Tower ruling.

**Q3. Is Earth grey or living (NP#60)?**
- a) living throughout
- b) living above, grey below
- c) grey throughout

Claude's pick: **b**. This closes NP#60.

**Q4. "Tainted" orc cores, when the public doesn't know Remnant is souls?**
- a) the public reads "orc-grade" as a poor ore from orc worlds
- b) only insiders know where the cores come from
- c) both

Claude's pick: **c**. It squares the tainted-core ruling with the public-knowledge ruling (the follow-up flagged in BANK 11-02).

**Q5. Orc-derived food?**
- a) an open fact most people shrug at
- b) hidden in cheap protein
- c) rumor only

Claude's pick: **a** (t524, jul08 t183).

**Q6. Can ensouled machines draw on the Tower?**
- a) no: only wielders can
- b) yes: AI with receptors can
- c) belief only

Claude's pick: **c**. It keeps the Tower a wielder's peak.

**Q7. Metal augments and magic?**
- a) graded: more metal means a weaker grip
- b) the metal part is a dead zone
- c) both

Claude's pick: **c**.

**Q8. Soul-reading gates?**
- a) the Tower and the Inquisition only
- b) also the Council halls and the black market

Claude's pick: **b**.
