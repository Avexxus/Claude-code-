# Trope flavor bank: the elder races (giants, dragons, elves)

*🌊 Claude proposal, every line. Written 2026-11-01 by idea-scout. Not canon. Nothing here enters `codex_v5/` without a ruling.*

*Sources read: `CLAUDE.md`, `notes/00_MASTER_BRIEF.md`, `decisions/BANK.md`, `notes/D_beings.md`, `notes/E_world.md`. `RULINGS.md` has no elder-race entries yet. BANK items are cited as "BANK + date + topic" because they aren't R-numbered yet.*

*How to read this: **Trope** is the genre-wide convention (no specific work). **Here** is how it would look in this world. **Tag:** FITS (drops straight in), TWIST (the trope inverted to fit canon), RISKY (only if Patrick bends something, which is named). **Faction** says which part of the race it belongs to, because no race is a monolith (t596).*

*Not yet canon-checked. Run `canon-checker` on any survivors before showing them to Patrick.*

## GIANTS

**Already has:** longest-lived and most potent; grown seamless architecture through true transmutation; Convergence (funerary rite releases the soul; the god-forging unison rite is lost); total law and love-led tyranny; betrayal day and the Tower as a centerless soul-mass (BANK 11-01 L7); the age erased by dragons; stoicism as scar tissue over a war of attrition; three far-future camps; cathedral ships; the last friendly giant.

### Flavor bank

**Daily life and customs**
1. **Trope: giants as slow, patient deliberators.** Here: a giant household or council may take a human lifetime to answer a letter. "Hasty" is their sharpest insult. This is the custom behind the secret faction out-planning dragons (t44). **FITS.** All factions.
2. **Trope: a giant's home is a mountain or hill.** Here: the faction that chose isolation (jul23 t34–40) grows its homes as hills. Human villages farm the slopes of "hills" that are really inhabited halls and never know it. **TWIST** (the landscape is a dwelling, not a sleeping giant). The isolationist faction.
3. **Trope: giants live with their ancestors (barrows, halls of the fathers).** Here: your walls are literally your grandparents (t34, t36). The funerary rite releases the soul (W15), so the walls are empty, but families still greet the lintel at meals out of habit. It's memory, not worship, and it contrasts with the Tower, where the souls were never released. **FITS.** Strongest in the ethically-sourced camp.

**Rites**
4. **Birth. Trope: rare births are a clan-wide event.** Here: slow births and huge upkeep (`07`) mean a birth gets a planning commission, the same way a king's funeral does (t42). Among the old-fashioned, an elder may choose a consensual Convergence to grow the child's first room: "a death for a birth." **TWIST.** Leans on the deliberate consent ambiguity (t40). Traditionalist families.
5. **Coming of age. Trope: a trial of strength.** Here: the trial is not lifting but *shaping*: the youth's first seamless work, judged for no chisel marks and for the grain of its bonds. **FITS** as long as it stays silent on whether the work must stand once the youth stops holding it. Saying either way picks a side on the open "fired vs held" question (t35, t42).
6. **Marriage. Trope: marriage as an alliance of clans.** Here: two families must agree *whose dead build the new hall*. Feuds over whose grandmother goes in the doorframe. A marriage is literally the merging of two ancestries in stone. **FITS.** All factions.
7. **Death. Trope: cairns and barrows, and grave-robbers.** Here: giants who distrust Convergence since it was monetized (t52, t54) bury their dead whole and deep so nobody can build with them. The any-tactic camp and dwarven suppliers rob those graves, because the supply of dead giants is the bottleneck (t44) and flesh can be shaped into weapons (BANK 11-01, flesh weapons). **FITS.** Sets the ethically-sourced and refuser families against the any-tactic league. Grimdark without needing anything on screen.

**Craft and art**
8. **Trope: giant runes and carved stone.** Here: giant work carries no marks at all. The art lives in the grain, a visible sheen where the chain bonds run (t34). A giant reads which family grew a wall the way others read handwriting. **FITS.**
9. **Trope: giant-forged weapons of legendary size.** Here: giants carry no weapons and raise them from the terrain (t81). So the flesh-weapon craft (BANK 11-01), bending flesh into soul-holding weapons by a Convergence-like ritual, is plausibly a giant art that giants themselves won't wield. The ethically-sourced camp calls it obscene; the any-tactic league sells it. **TWIST.** Opens a question: who originated flesh weapons?

**War and arms**
10. **Trope: giants hurl boulders and fight with clubs.** Here: carrying a weapon is a mark of a giant too weak or too young to raise one. The battlefield itself is the weapon: walls, pits, a valley closing. "The giant takes away the sky" (t81). **TWIST.** All factions.
11. **Trope: champions and single combat.** Here: since betrayal day, a giant never grants a dragon a duel, because a duel is how the emperor died. Refusing single combat with a dragon is giant law. Giants answer with patience and cathedral ships instead (t56). **FITS.**

**Law, crime and punishment**
12. **Trope: an ancient and unbending ancestral law.** Here: in the old age, crime "effectively did not exist" (t472). The ultimate sentence was **denial of Convergence**: no wall, no place in any descendant's house, erased from the family stone. Today the ethically-sourced camp's gravest crime is building with a giant who didn't consent. **FITS.**
13. **Trope: a giant's oath binds forever.** Here: a giant's word is a legal instrument because giant memory outlasts the debtor. Human families inherit debts their ancestors signed, which feeds the long-memory enmity cycle (`10` 🔒). **FITS.**

**Religion and superstition**
14. **Trope: giants as the old gods of lesser races.** Here: human hill-cults leave offerings at grown hills (see 2) that are inhabited. The giants inside find it useful or embarrassing, and the faction decides which. Giants' own faith turns on the lost unison rite (W17): "soul mortar" as holy lost knowledge. Some revere the million-soul god; to the ethically-sourced he is the great horror of stolen consent (chat t395). **FITS.** Feeds "every race needs a religion" (chat t331).
15. **Trope: a cursed or haunted ancestral ruin.** Here: the Tower is their emperor and their whole dead city, merged into one centerless soul-mass (BANK 11-01). Giants use kin words for it. A traditionalist strand holds that the Tower's dead were never given the funerary release and wants it performed. That would end the largest amplifier on Earth (BANK 11-01), which pits them against Derrek and against everyone who draws on it. **TWIST.** Opens new questions: can a mass that size be released at all, and who tries?

**What other races say about them**
16. **Trope: "the gentle giant" and "the giant tyrant", both at once.** Here: humans see giants as scenery (`07`). Dragons say "they'd have kept us as pets forever." Elves call that age *their* golden age and remember the giants only as the ones they watched from outside (jul08 t225). Orc oral memory keeps the giants as the first master, the one whose cage was softest: "the ache, not the blow" (`10`). **FITS.**

**Small texture**
17. **Trope: deep voices and long silences.** Here: giant speech is slow and low, felt through the floor. A pause in a giant's conversation can run for minutes, and short-lived listeners interrupt without knowing it. Halls smell of cold stone and old bone-dust. **FITS.**
18. **Trope: the elder who measures time strangely.** Here: giants date things by other races' generations ("three human kings ago") and call a human by a grandparent's name out of carelessness. **FITS.**
19. **Trope: the grudge kept forever.** Here: the hatred gradient (t104) is taught to children as a ranked list: the dragons' breath first, human sieges and the dwarven-armored gold raids lower down. The forge-world giant's dry coldness (t99) is what a ledger-keeper looks like. **FITS.**

### Tropes to avoid
- **Giants as dumb brutes.** "A true giant is smart, cunning and focused" (t76), and the most potent race (`07`).
- **Fire, frost and storm giants (elemental subtypes).** Elementals were split out of giants (t78, t102 r3); the symbolic element is cultural only (Law 8); no types.
- **Size charts and square-cube logic.** 🚫 "no math" (t72).
- **Giants as cosmological titans or gods by birth.** They are mortal titans (`07`). Godhood is earned or stolen, never racial.
- **Man-eating ogre giants.** It cheapens a race defined by duty, grief and craft, and their real horror (building with the dead) is already worse.
- **The fading, melancholy, solitary last-of-their-kind.** 🚫 "mostly solitary" and "withdrew, leaving halls empty" (retired 8, 13); they rebuilt quietly (t418). "The last friendly giant" is one man, not the race.
- **Gentle-giant protectors as the whole picture.** Love-led *and* cruel tyrants (t40, t472); duality held.
- **Giants hating elves.** Elves were always outside giant control, so giants don't hate them (jul08 t225).
- **Pride as the giant racial sin.** Pride-as-duty is a mask, not a sin (retired 37; jul23 t52–54).
- **Stone giants turning to stone or sleeping as landmarks.** Giants are active users, never passive landmarks (`07`); the Tower is a one-off act.

### Top 5 (giants)
1. **#7 Burial refusal and grave-robbing.** It turns the supply cap (t44), monetized Convergence and flesh weapons into one fracture you can stage in a scene.
2. **#15 The Tower-release strand.** It ties giant faith to the BANK Tower ruling and gives giants a reason to oppose Derrek. It does open a question.
3. **#6 Marriage over whose dead build the hall.** A small, grim, very specific custom built on t34/t36.
4. **#12 Denial of Convergence as the ultimate sentence.** It gives giant law (t472) a concrete punishment.
5. **#9 Flesh weapons as a giant art giants won't wield.** It links BANK flesh weapons to "carry none" (t81) and to the camps.

## DRAGONS

**Already has:** a capable feudal empire of builders; humanity's first teachers and premier historians, who erased the record; four legs with the Remnant grip as hands, plus servants; breaths that are wielded, several per dragon; burst power with drain over a long fight (🔒); betrayal day and the prepared stone as life's masterwork (BANK 11-01); raised houses vs dispossessed; living in giant palaces; focusing-chamber crews; conglomerates; the dragon god of rigorous study.

### Flavor bank

**Daily life and customs**
1. **Trope: the dragon's hoard.** Here: the hoard is an **archive**. A house's wealth is its records, including private true accounts of the giants' age that dragons erased from everyone else (W61, W63). Raised houses keep full archives. Dispossessed houses lost theirs, and they know their own history only through the usurper's copy. **TWIST.** It turns "greed" into scholarship and makes erasure a monopoly.
2. **Trope: the dragon's mountain lair.** Here: dragons live inside giant-grown palaces (t32, t300). They can't grow walls, so they *inscribe* them, cutting house heraldry into seamless giant work. Giants see it as defacing their grandmothers. **TWIST.** It connects the two races' crafts and grudges.
3. **Trope: the dragon slumbering for centuries.** Here: there is no long sleep, but there is the rest after a burst, because a dragon drags in a long fight (🔒). Guarding a resting lord is the highest vassal honor, and the hour when a lord is most easily killed in house wars. The cost is fatigue, never Remnant spent (W10). **TWIST.** Raised and dispossessed houses alike.

**Rites**
4. **Birth. Trope: egg clutches guarded in the lair.** Here: births are rare and familial, and announced to the liege, because only family or appeasing dragons are allowed near (t260). Dispossessed children are born beneath the usurper's roof. **RISKY** if written as *eggs*: dragon reproduction isn't ruled. Keep it as "births" unless Patrick wants eggs.
5. **Coming of age. Trope: the first flight or the first flame.** Here: breaths are learned and wielded, not a trait, so a young dragon comes of age by examination at a dragon school (W64): demonstrate a breath and defend a thesis. Failing shames the house. **TWIST.** Scholar houses most of all.
6. **Marriage. Trope: dynastic matches between great houses.** Here: houses marry to heal or hide the schism (t270). A usurper line taking a dispossessed bride launders legitimacy, and the dispossessed call those children "the paper heirs." **FITS.**
7. **Death. Trope: the dragon graveyard.** Here: a broken, dying elder is scavengeable by changelings (t112, t543), and a dying dragon may self-detonate (`07`). So deathbeds are guarded by kin, and the honorable end is to fly somewhere remote and detonate. Dragon graveyards are craters. Bodies that don't get there are coveted by giants for cathedral flagships (`07`) and by the organ trade. **TWIST.** Grimdark, and built entirely on canon.

**Craft and art**
8. **Trope: dragons as alchemists and arcane scholars.** Here: every great dragon spends a lifetime on one **stone**, the most unstable element they can transmute, as their masterwork (BANK 11-01; "weak force" is flavor wording only). A stone that is never used is the highest prestige. A dragon who uses theirs is a martyr or a fool, depending on the house. **FITS.**
9. **Trope: dragons with a taste for beauty and treasure.** Here: they collect *materials*, because transmuters sense materials (`07`). A dragon's gallery is a shelf of impossible elements, each labeled with who made it. **TWIST.**

**War and arms**
10. **Trope: dragon riders.** Here: there are no bonded riders. Humans served dragons as indentured war-users (`07`), and in the far future crews ride *inside* dragon ships while the master is the final lever (t442). "Dragon-rider" is a human boast with nothing behind it. **TWIST.**
11. **Trope: aerial duels for dominance.** Here: the empire has never been at peace since the coup (t298), so formal breath-duels between houses have heavy etiquette. "Never land" (t81) is a point of pride. The far-future version is cathedral ships murdering each other (t274). **FITS.**
12. **Trope: the dragon's one weak scale.** Here: the real weakness is endurance (🔒). Human folk wisdom says "keep it talking, keep it flying." A human proverb about outlasting a dragon fits humanity's numbers-not-duels lesson (jul08 t239). **TWIST.**

**Law, crime and punishment**
13. **Trope: dragons bound by bargains, contracts and riddles.** Here: dragons *legislate* (`10`). A dragon contract is always technically kept, and the loophole counts as a virtue. That is how young dragons build conglomerates "legally hidden through loopholes" (jul08 t273–275), and why elder-race law hands humans a functional win (W60). **FITS.**
14. **Trope: exile from the flight.** Here: the punishment is not exile but **dispossession**: the house serves beneath the one that usurped it (t270). Humiliation by proximity, for generations. **FITS.**

**Religion and superstition**
15. **Trope: dragon cults and self-worship.** Here: there are two dragon faiths. (a) The scholar's faith around the god of rigorous study (W33): the mind is what slips, so dragons fear senility more than death. (b) The martyr's faith around the advisor remembered as "king" (t268), strongest in houses descended from him. **FITS.** Feeds "every race needs a religion."
16. **Trope: the dragon's riddle game.** Here: it's a **greeting against changelings**. Dragons test each other with a question only the real one could answer, after the advisor caught the gnome changeling that way (t282). Among old dragons it's etiquette; to young ones it's paranoia. **TWIST.** Fits the "knowledge-gap tell" canon.

**What other races say about them**
17. **Trope: the dragon as tyrant and dread.** Here: humans name it the Darkest Age, while dragons call it the Age of Dragons and say they freed everyone from the giants (t448). Giants call them betrayers and occupiers. Elves call them the only ones who stand toe to toe (t239). Dwarves call them the best customers. Humans also say "they taught us, then owned us" (W63). **FITS.**

**Small texture**
18. **Trope: smoke, sulfur, the smell of the lair.** Here: dragon halls smell of ozone and hot metal. Objects move around a dragon unhandled, carried by its invisible grip (W63), and human servants learn to keep still when things float past. **FITS.**
19. **Trope: the sardonic, witty dragon.** Here: dragons talk like scholars. They cite sources, correct other people's history (which they wrote), and say "dragons see more" (t286). **FITS.**
20. **Trope: dragons laired in blighted, dangerous land.** Here: dragons were the first to live in the blast zone (t88, t496). Some houses still seat themselves in hot ground as a show of mastery, and their human servants sicken. **TWIST.**

### Tropes to avoid
- **The gold hoard as racial greed.** No racial sins (jul23 t52); dragons are builders, not takers (retired 16, 17).
- **The solitary dragon in a cave.** Retired 14–16; they are a feudal empire with families (t260).
- **The lone hero slaying a great ancient dragon.** 🔒 beatability: no single being beats a great ancient; only groups, bad matchups or augmented humans.
- **Color-coded breath kinds or chromatic "types".** "A drake who wields corrosion," never "a corrosion drake"; no types or force labels (`07` 🚫).
- **Metallic-good vs chromatic-evil moral sorting.** A monolith split by blood; the real fracture is houses (t270).
- **Dragons shapeshifting into humans.** No shapeshifting exists; changelings possess (retired 23).
- **Dragon heart or blood granting power.** Remnant can't be transferred or spent (W10); taking power needs soul theft.
- **True names and a dragon tongue as spell language.** Force words describe after the fact; no word magic (`04`).
- **God-king sorcerer-tyrants who slowly recede into proxies.** Retired 14.
- **Bumbling, pride-blind dragons.** Pride is a character tool, never a system rule (`07`); "we were being racist against dragons" (t446).

### Top 5 (dragons)
1. **#1 The hoard as archive.** It inverts the core trope and links scholarship (W63) with erasure (W61) and the house schism (t270).
2. **#7 The deathbed and detonation custom.** It ties changeling scavenging, self-detonation, cathedral flagships and the organ trade into one rite.
3. **#16 The question-greeting.** A small daily habit grown from the gnome-changeling canon (t282).
4. **#8 The stone as life's masterwork.** It turns the BANK ruling into a whole artistic tradition.
5. **#2 Heraldry cut into giant walls.** It makes the occupier relationship visible in every hall.

## ELVES

**Already has:** ancient but mortal; the bond (innate, collective, scaling with Remnant and distance, BANK 11-01); authoritarian, with reunion as the mask; advance fastest; never under the giants; feel resets as grief for the unnamed; heaven empty and hell full (one hell, the elven prison, which humans travel through, BANK 11-01); dark elves; the Inquisition and its rot; exodus by Mika alone (BANK 11-01); Mika as god-emperor (Jon is NOT their god, BANK 11-01); dwarven-built ships; organ-trade victims.

### Flavor bank

**Daily life and customs**
1. **Trope: elves at one with the forest.** Here: wood elves are a sect (`10`), and the grove is permitted distance, not freedom: "you can build a grove in the mountains but you are always part of the collective" (t598). Groves are licensed. **TWIST.** Wood-elf sect vs the high/imperial realm.
2. **Trope: elves disdain cities and machines.** Here: elves advance fastest (🔒, frictionless cooperation). Elven cities are the most advanced on any world, with a dwarven district in each (jul08 t223). They are not woodland traditionalists. **TWIST.**
3. **Trope: elves aloof toward short-lived races.** Here: much of the aloofness is that other races are present but not *bonded*. Up close, elves feel them (BANK 11-01 bond), like a stranger in the house who never joins the room. Humans read that as arrogance, and the discrimination is also real (jul23 t132). **TWIST.** It grows from a condition, not blood. Touches L9 only lightly: presence, never thoughts.
4. **Trope: communal, musical elves.** Here: elves seek crowds. Solitude feels like sickness, and the homeworld, a beacon of trillions (BANK 11-01), is where elves are calmest. Elven festivals are about density, not song. **FITS.**

**Rites**
5. **Birth. Trope: elven births are rare and cherished.** Here: a newborn is a faint new presence. It's carried through a crowd to be "felt in." Midwives and mothers also carry the reset grief, for children who after a reset never were (`07`). **FITS.**
6. **Coming of age. Trope: the elven wandering or journey.** Here: distance is allowed, separation is not (t598). The rite is a youth's first trip off-world, where the beacon fades to a pinprick, and the test is enduring near-silence. Many come back changed, and a few come back dangerous. **TWIST.** Grows from BANK bond scaling.
7. **Marriage. Trope: elves bond for life, or for centuries.** Here: in the imperial realm a union needs state sanction and is weighed for the collective. Marrying outside the race is forbidden there (jul23 t132). Wood groves may be looser and are watched for it. **FITS.** Fracture: realm vs groves.
8. **Death. Trope: elves sail west to a paradise.** Here: heaven is empty, and entering it cuts you from the collective (t600–604). An elf who dies far away dies *alone* (W08), which is the worst fate. So the dying are brought home to die inside the crowd, and the far-flung dead are mourned as lost twice. **TWIST.** Steps around 🚫 40 (no Undying-Lands melancholy).

**Craft and art**
9. **Trope: exquisite, timeless elven craft.** Here: elven works are made by many hands and never signed. Signing a work is vanity, faintly seditious. Ships are the exception: dwarves build them (`07`), a dependence elves resent. **FITS.**
10. **Trope: elven healers and medicine.** Here: humans took elven medicine and called it their own (`10`). Elven prisons are long rehabilitation: "live long enough to try to fix a murderer" (t228). **FITS.**

**War and arms**
11. **Trope: precise elven archers and skirmishers.** Here: the sustained mirror of dragons. Spread out, bait the dragon ship's beams, then pour sustained pooled fire (t220–223). Ground units fight the long fight, as many presences acting close together. **FITS.**
12. **Trope: the elf–dwarf rivalry.** Here: it's darker than rivalry. Elves depend on dwarves for ships, while dwarves made the bond-spoof chip by dissecting elves (jul23 t154) and help run the organ trade (`10`). Trade partners who butcher them. **TWIST.**

**Law, crime and punishment**
13. **Trope: high elven justice and eternal exile.** Here: hell is only for stealing a fellow elf's soul or body (t682). Otherwise there are ordinary prisons, and dissent is met with "pressure" from neighbors and inquisitors. Exile into hell is the one sentence elves won't speak aloud. **FITS.** Note: the drift to dissidents (W25, L19) is still open, so don't pick a side.

**Religion and superstition**
14. **Trope: elves revere stars and nature spirits.** Here: elven faith centers on Mika as the center of the bond (`07` W33) and on reunion: elves as the One's surviving unity (`07`). "Elves built heaven" is held as belief, not fact (W29). The Inquisition doubles as clergy-police. **FITS.** Feeds the thin elven religion slot (`10` §10). With Jon retired as their god (BANK 11-01), this needs a replacement anyway.
15. **Trope: elves mourn what others forget.** Here: after a reset-tremor, elves set an unnamed marker or an empty place for "the ones who never were" (`07` grief; BANK 11-01 hint 2). Humans call it madness or theater. The Inquisition reads it as evidence (t490). **FITS.** It makes BANK's time-travel hint (2) a visible custom.
16. **Trope: the forbidden place in elven lands.** Here: "the weak place," a door where people vanish, which feels like the Tower (t382). Elves won't speak of it. Dark elves whisper that it's a way out that never works. **FITS.**

**What other races say about them**
17. **Trope: elves as arrogant, beautiful and cold.** Here: humans call them racist, and they're right (jul23 t132). Dragons call them the only equals in a fight (t239). Giants remember the ones who watched and never stepped in (jul08 t225). Dwarves say "good customers, better parts." Dark elves call the empire the jailers who sell them (W68). **FITS.**

**Small texture**
18. **Trope: elven grace and stillness.** Here: off the homeworld, elves look strained, and in human cities they keep touching kin to confirm presence. They talk about "the near" and "the many" rather than friends and nation, in plain words. **FITS.**
19. **Trope: the underground dark elves.** Here: hell is a prison realm of darkness, drought and famine (t228). Dark elves born there keep its habits outside: rationing water, flinching at strong light, speaking low. It's a prison culture, not a cave race. **TWIST.** Dark-elf population, many born outside (chat t401). The name stays open, and never "Drow."
20. **Trope: elves fear death because they live so long.** Here: elves are mortal and shorter-lived than dragons and giants (t272). They don't fear death itself. They fear the silence, dying beyond the beacon. **TWIST.** Ties to #8.

### Tropes to avoid
- **Elves as pure and good.** Authoritarian, extreme discrimination (jul23 t132); the Inquisition's rot (W68).
- **Immortal or awakening elves.** Mortal (4a); 🚫 "elves awaken instead of reproducing."
- **Sailing to an undying paradise.** 🚫 retired 40; heaven is beautiful and empty and cuts the bond.
- **Elves as fae or tricksters bound by truth.** The fae are a separate, undeveloped otherworld (`07`).
- **Dark elves as an evil skin-colored subrace.** A real-world racial overtone, and dark elves are a condition (prison exiles), not blood. "Drow" is banned (t233).
- **The telepathic hive mind that reads thoughts.** The bond carries presence scaled by Remnant and distance, not thoughts (W08, BANK 11-01; L9 is open, so don't harden it).
- **The fading race in twilight.** "Elves were NEVER the underdog or weak" (jul08 t299).
- **Elves as Jon's people, devotees or breeding project.** Retired by BANK 11-01; Mika rules his own people.
- **Elven ears, blood or organs as magic reagents.** The organ trade is real, but Remnant can't be transferred (W10), so organs carry no power. Don't imply they do.
- **Exotic elven beauty played for allure.** Cost and characterize, never titillate (12, grimdark register).
- **All elves leaving together by consensus.** 🚫 retired 35; it was a top-down directive, Mika alone.

### Top 5 (elves)
1. **#15 The empty place for the never-were.** It turns BANK time-travel hint (2) into a visible, scene-ready custom and gives the Inquisition evidence.
2. **#8 Dying at home inside the crowd.** It inverts the paradise trope using W08 and the empty heaven.
3. **#6 The silence trip as coming of age.** Built straight on the BANK bond-scaling ruling.
4. **#3 Others present but unbonded.** Explains the aloofness by condition, not blood, and stays clear of L9.
5. **#19 Dark elves' prison habits.** Gives the large dark-elf population (chat t401) texture without any cave-race trope.
