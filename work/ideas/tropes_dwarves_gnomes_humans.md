# Trope flavor bank: dwarves (and the Returned), gnomes, humans

🌊 **Claude proposal, 2026-11-01. Not canon.** Genre-wide conventions only, adapted to the saga. Nothing has been canon-checked yet; run `canon-checker` before showing any of it to Patrick as a choice.

**Sources read:** `notes/00_MASTER_BRIEF.md`, `decisions/BANK.md`, `notes/D_beings.md` (races, §6 retired list), `notes/E_world.md` (§3 per race, §5 systems, §7 retired list).

**Tags:** **FITS** drops straight in · **TWIST** the trope turned to fit canon · **RISKY** only if Patrick bends something (named).
**Faction tag** in brackets says who a detail belongs to. No detail applies to a whole race.

## DWARVES

**Already has:** independence as the root and greed as its learned strategy; neutrality bought by being too costly to conquer; craft learned from giants (the mini-age); magic fused into metal leading to the first digital apparatus; district in every human city; the far-future trade empire with monopolies and private armies; slavers; gnome indenture; the bond-spoof chip; focusing chambers; the organ trade; the sale to the maze; the fracture of integrationists, isolationists and the Returned (`10`; `07`; t54, t144–148, t540, t568, t594, jul08 t299).

### Flavor bank

**Daily life and customs**
1. **Trope: the clan hold under the mountain.** Here, every family is run as a firm, and the hold's great hall is its counting house: meals are taken where the accounts are kept. *[all, strongest among isolationists]* Grows from the trade empire and family-first as the professed face (t594). **FITS**
2. **Trope: the dwarven quarter in the human city.** Here, the district wall faces inward as well as outward. It's an embassy, warehouse and fortress in one, and it can be sealed off within the hour when the city burns. *[integrationists]* Grows from "district in every city" (`10` politics by era) and "indispensable, not inconquerable". **FITS**

**Rites**
3. **Trope: a birth recorded in the clan book.** Here, a newborn is entered in the family ledger with an opening debt: what the family will spend raising them. Paying it off is adulthood. *[old holds; integrationists may treat it as a quaint formality]* Grows from "bottom of the ledger" (t622). **TWIST** (the honor book becomes an account book)
4. **Trope: coming of age by forging a first masterwork.** Here, the first contract signed in your own name, under your own maker's mark, is your coming of age. A piece good enough to carry the family seal outranks any age. **FITS**
5. **Trope: the marriage alliance between clans.** Here, a marriage is a merger, and the dowry is audited before anyone talks about love. That is why a Returned who arrives with a dowry gets taken seriously (t622). **FITS**
6. **Trope: solemn stone tombs for the dead.** Here, the funeral is an audit. The ledger is read aloud, debts and credits pass to the heirs, and the line is closed. A dwarf who dies owing is buried with the debt in the record, and that shames the heirs until they pay it. **FITS**

**Craft and art**
7. **Trope: the maker's mark and dwarven-made as a seal.** Here, forging a maker's mark is the one crime every hold punishes alike, because the mark is what the whole neutrality rests on (seal of mastery, `07` far future). **FITS**
8. **Trope: the ancestral heirloom weapon.** Here, the old holds' greatest heirlooms are flesh weapons (BANK 2026-11-01, ✅): an ancestor's own flesh shaped by a Convergence-like ritual, still holding them. The soul inside feels dread (W13). Families call that devotion. Some went willingly, some didn't, and the ledger records which. *[old holds; integrationists sell the method]* Touches W12–W13 and the flesh-weapons ruling. **TWIST**
9. **Trope: dwarves mistrust wizards and magic.** Here, dwarves draw no line between magic and craft. Infusion is a shop skill taught at the bench. That's a real-life case of the codex's "one act under different names" (W57). **TWIST**

**War and arms**
10. **Trope: the stubborn shield wall of dwarven warriors.** Here, dwarves rarely fight their own wars. They arm both sides (Darkest Age stance) and field private armies of hired others. When a hold does fight, the doctrine is to make conquest cost more than it's worth: halls rigged to collapse, passes rigged to fall. *[isolationists hold this hardest]* Grows from "too costly to conquer" (t148). **TWIST**
11. **Trope: dwarven armor as the best in the world.** Here, the armor humans wore to raid the giants' gold reserves was dwarven (t104), and so was the rust-proof shield a transmuting dragon could sense. Dwarves sell the armor and stay out of the raid. **FITS**

**Law, crime and punishment**
12. **Trope: the oath that can't be broken.** Here, the contract is the oath, and the "sophisticated series of laws" (`10` systems) is mostly contract law. Breaking a contract is worse than violence. **FITS**
13. **Trope: exile with a shaved beard, or a disgraced warrior seeking death.** Here, the worst punishment is being *struck from the ledger*: no family, no protection, fair game for any slaver. A struck dwarf's way back is debt-diving into the dangerous element caves, which the far future already has dwarves exploring (`07`). **TWIST**

**Religion and superstition**
14. **Trope: the smith-father god.** Here, a flesh-born founder-smith from the mini-age who learned craft at a giant's forge and grew into a god (BANK: any being can become a god; W30 all gods flesh-born). Their faith is a portrait of a god who never took a side. Feeds the open dwarven-faith item (t560/t631) and the clay slate of two gods per race (W33). **FITS** (but opens the dwarven gods slot)
15. **Trope: you don't dig too deep, or something wakes.** Here, it's nursery superstition, not fact: "be the weakest and the maze keeper comes for you." Children are told the in-betweener took the slow and the lazy. Ordinary dwarves never ask who did the choosing. Grows from the sale (t144–148). **FITS**

**What others say about them**
16. Here, humans say that after every war, someone asks who armed both sides, and the answer is always the same. Elves, the old customers who later became the specimens for the chip (jul23 t154–158), don't joke about dwarves at all. Grows from "missing party in every crisis" (t538). **FITS**

**Small texture**
17. Here, a dwarf names a price before the greeting. Forge smoke, lamp oil and the tang of coin metal hang in every hall. Hammer strikes mark the hours. Sayings are plain: everyone can be bought, and the trick is being too expensive. **FITS**
18. **Trope: the patriarch who outlives his sons.** Here, the dwarf-changeling dynasty that moves into cloned "sons" (W23) hides behind normal inheritance law. An old house where the heir always looks exactly like the father raises talk, and nobody acts on it. **FITS** (it rests on clay W23)

### The Returned (sub-bank)

19. **Trope: the lost kin who return from the deep, changed and twisted.** Here, they're changed by where they were raised, not by evil. They were born inside the Void (t516) and use new Remnant magic learned there, not stolen Rule magic (Law 8; t148). Nobody hunts them (t622). **TWIST**
20. **Trope: the escaped slave who swears never again.** Here, they keep only short contracts paid in cash and refuse open-ended terms, ranks and oaths of service. That's "never bound again" (t624) turned into business manners: the most unscrupulous traders in the galaxy, with the cleanest terms. *[the trader and slaver faction; the bandits refuse even that]* **FITS**
21. **Trope: the coming-of-age trial.** Here, their rite is winning the maze keeper's game, which means release (t622). Children born outside the maze to Returned parents have no rite at all, and some feel the lack. **FITS** (the second half opens a small question: second-generation Returned)
22. **Trope: a race that has never seen the sun.** Here, the first sky a freed Returned sees is texture. Some never get used to open space and work best in sealed chambers, which is one reason they're the core driving the focusing chambers (t146). **FITS**
23. **Trope: bitter exiles plotting revenge on their homeland.** Here, the feeling is resentful but grateful (t622). Some Returned keep a counter-ledger of which families sold whose ancestors, as leverage, not for vengeance. Bringing a dowry into one of those families is the sweetest deal. **TWIST** (opens a question: do they ever cash in that leverage?)

### Tropes to avoid
- **Dwarves are greedy by nature.** That's the retired racial sin (E §7 #13, jul23 t54). Greed is a strategy serving independence.
- **Dwarves are neutral by nature.** That's an inline 🚫. Neutrality is bought and learned (t148).
- **Real-world national metaphors for the holds** (the mountain-bank country). Retired, D §6 #33. The study notes still carry a "Switzerland metaphor" line, so flag it at the next pass.
- **Accents or customs mapped to a real ethnicity.** That breaks the no-real-world-analogy rule.
- **An ancestral blood feud with goblins, orcs or elves.** It makes enmity innate. Here the relationship is inventory or specimen, which is conditions, not blood.
- **Dwarves can't use magic or resist it.** That conflicts with "small, enormously powerful" (t562) and the enchanter-race candidacy.
- **Rune magic as a separate system.** That's a new type. Marks are signatures, not a magic school.
- **The comic drunk, or bearded-women jokes.** Comic relief breaks the grimdark register.
- **Dwarves as the honorable ones.** No clean hands (🔒). They sold their own.
- **Returned as corrupt dark kin, or as wielders of stolen Rule magic.** That breaks Law 8 and t622.
- **One unified dwarven king.** The one-empire vs several-emperors question is open (#57), so don't pick it here.

### Top 5, dwarves
1. **#3 + #6, the ledger from birth to funeral.** It turns the existing "bottom of the ledger" line into a whole life cycle and makes the sale read as precedent, not aberration.
2. **#8, ancestor flesh weapons.** It ties the brand-new flesh-weapons ruling to the dwarves as an enchanter people, and it's grimdark without titillating.
3. **#15, the maze keeper as a bogeyman.** One line of nursery talk makes the sale part of daily culture and shows the self-deception.
4. **#13, struck from the ledger, then debt-diving.** It links punishment, the slaver economy and the far-future element caves.
5. **#20, Returned contract manners.** It makes "never bound again" visible in every scene where a Returned does business.

## GNOMES

**Already has:** cheerful bright towns that "smell good"; care little for wealth or a boss; happiness read as weakness; self-deceived about their safety; the schism between fighting for rights and being content with balance; no golden age, "securely an average race"; Remnant similar in size to a dwarf's (🌊); good control through education and free thinking; second only to changelings at assassination; medieval forest villages, gone by the end; indentured to the dwarves in the far future; the gnome changeling as shaman and healer, and as the gnome god (jul23 t50–62; t284, t568, t772–778; BANK L24).

### Flavor bank

**Daily life and customs**
1. **Trope: the cozy village of burrows and gardens.** Here, it's medieval and remote, and it doesn't last. In the far future, indentured gnomes keep window-box herbs in dwarven barracks, and the quarters still "smell good" (t60). It's the one habit indenture never broke. *[content faction]* **FITS**
2. **Trope: gnomes as curious scholars and tinkerers.** Here, every village teaches every child to read, which is where their control comes from (jul23 t60). Under the dwarves, literate gnomes do the fine assembly and bookkeeping. They're valued for their hands and minds and owned anyway. **TWIST** (tinkerer becomes indentured technician)

**Rites**
3. **Trope: a birth feast with the whole village.** Here, it's the medieval village custom. Far-future version: a gnome child's indenture papers are drawn up at birth. The feast still happens, and nobody mentions the papers. **TWIST**
4. **Trope: coming of age by apprenticing to a craftsman.** Here, coming of age is the year a gnome's inherited indenture starts. Families celebrate it like a wedding because it's the only party the masters allow. *[far future]* **FITS**
5. **Trope: the merry funeral with songs and jokes.** Here, gnomes laugh at funerals and tell the dead person's worst jokes. After the shaman atrocity, a family keeps the dying at home and someone stays with the body until it's buried. Nobody gets left alone with a healer. Grows from BANK L24. **TWIST**

**Craft and art**
6. **Trope: gem-cutters and fine artisans.** Here, gnomes do the delicate finishing work in dwarven chip and chamber workshops, including on the bond-spoof chip (jul23 t154). Gnome hands have touched the galaxy's most dangerous device, and gnomes get no credit for it. **FITS**
7. **Trope: gnomish music and comic songs.** Here, the songs are about villages none of the singers have seen. Content gnomes sing them as nostalgia, rights gnomes as indictment, and the tune is the same. *[schism]* **FITS**

**War and arms**
8. **Trope: the illusionist or trickster.** Here, there's no illusion school. Small Remnant plus fine control (jul23 t60) suits quiet, precise work: misdirection, the small grip in the right place. That's how "second only to changelings at assassination" works in practice. Touches the no-types rule. **TWIST**
9. **Trope: the underestimated little fighter.** Here, the indentured servant is in every rich house, which is the perfect assassin's position. It mirrors the changelings and the dwarves: inside every party, unseen (t540). *[rights faction; also hired out]* **FITS**

**Law, crime and punishment**
10. **Trope: a peaceful village with no ruler.** Here, villages decide by talking it out, with no boss (t60). Punishment is shaming and laughter. It's gentle until it fails, and against a strong healer it failed (BANK L24). Under indenture, dwarven contract law governs them and gnome custom has no standing. **FITS**

**Religion and superstition**
11. **Trope: the kindly folk healer or shaman.** Here, that's the deepest wound in gnome culture. Never leave your sick alone with someone strong. Strong healers are looked on with fear and gratitude at once. Grows from BANK L24 and t778. **TWIST**
12. **Trope: gnomes revere a trickster god.** Here, the gnome god is the gnome changeling (W23, W33). Some gnomes may venerate a god of jokes and ease without knowing he's the soul thief who enslaved a village. Others curse his name. **RISKY:** this needs Patrick to rule on whether gnomes know who their god is. It opens a question.

**What others say about them**
13. Here, dwarves say gnomes have good hands. Humans say "happy little people." Elder races don't count them at all, which is the "lesser races" lie (t562–564). Assassins' circles say the opposite of all three. **FITS**

**Small texture**
14. Here, gnomes greet each other with a joke, and a gnome who stops joking is a warning sign. Their places smell of baking and herbs and sound of chatter, even in the barracks. A common saying: if they're laughing, they think they're safe (the world's reading of t62, said back with an edge). **FITS**
15. **Trope: forest gnomes who befriend animals.** Here, the medieval remote-forest villages survive in later eras only as stories. A rights-faction gnome may keep a single plant or animal as a deliberate act of memory. **FITS**

### Tropes to avoid
- **Comic-relief gnomes.** The jokes are survival and character (the changeling's "inappropriate jokes", t762), never slapstick.
- **The explosive inventor gag.** It's slapstick, and it steals the craft-master role from dwarves.
- **An illusion school or "gnome magic".** No magic types (rule 5).
- **Gnomes as nature spirits.** They're latecomers with a Remnant like everyone else.
- **A lost gnome golden age or great gnome city.** Canon says "no golden age"; the physics-chat scale beats the July city.
- **Innocent, sinless gnomes.** Every race carries the full vice set (E §7 #12).
- **A fixed gnome lifespan.** That rung is unplaced (#31) and shouldn't be decided through flavor.
- **Garden-ornament cuteness.** It titillates by sweetness and breaks the register.

### Top 5, gnomes
1. **#11 + #5, never alone with the healer.** It makes the BANK L24 atrocity a living custom and ties the gnome changeling to the whole people.
2. **#9, the servant in every house as assassin.** It explains "second only to changelings" through indenture, with no magic type needed.
3. **#6, gnome hands on the bond-spoof chip.** One line ties gnomes to the galaxy's biggest plot device.
4. **#4, indenture as coming of age.** Grimdark and specific, and it shows the far-future era change.
5. **#7, one song, two factions.** It shows the schism with no new lore.

## HUMANS

**Already has:** the hunger for everything (want vs necessity, open); supremacy as the mask ("anointed children of the One"); zealotry around the puppeteer and Derrek; generalism as a symptom; science and magic driven by war; dragons as first teachers, hence "science"; parasites on others' gifts; lords enslaving their own peasants; the fracture of true believers and realists; the third age named for humans while they ruled the actual darkest age; the Legion; hell as the FTL route; the organ trade; augmented humans (`10`; t480, t488, t552, W57, W63; BANK NP#5).

### Flavor bank

**Daily life and customs**
1. **Trope: the dating of years from a king's reign.** Here, human calendars count from the Emperor's taking of the Tower (t494). Realists use it out of habit, believers as a creed. *[all eras after Derrek]* **FITS**
2. **Trope: the bustling human city.** Here, it's always the same city: the capital that "never moved", with only the ruler changing (t64). Human streets run through giant foundations, dragon palaces too big to live in, and a dwarven quarter. **FITS**

**Rites**
3. **Trope: a newborn blessed by the church.** Here, a newborn is presented under the Emperor's sign, so the child is placed in the hierarchy before it's named ("humans always obey some level of hierarchy", jul23 t24). **FITS**
4. **Trope: knighthood or an oath of fealty at coming of age.** Here, the coming-of-age oath is sworn to the Emperor. True believers mean it. Realists swear it to keep their papers. *[fracture]* **FITS**
5. **Trope: burial in sacred ground.** Here, the far-future death rite is a tithe. The government's legal organ market (W67) is framed as a final service, and the poor give everything usable. The black market underneath takes the rest. **TWIST**

**Craft and art**
6. **Trope: humans as the second-best at everything.** Here, humans are the second-best metalworkers (`07`) and the bulk factory of the galaxy. "Human-made" means cheap and everywhere, and that is exactly how humans win functionally (W60). **FITS**
7. **Trope: wizard academies and colleges.** Here, the oldest human schools of "science" were founded on dragon tutelage (W63; Derrek studied at a dragon school, W64). Human scholars teach the curriculum and drop the teachers' names, which is "take the gift, deny the giver." *[realists more honest about it]* **TWIST**

**War and arms**
8. **Trope: the mass army that overwhelms by numbers.** Here, massed gun-crews and war-users, and later augmented soldiers, are how "humans in number can fight a dragon" (jul08 t239, t261). Human war doctrine is built around one question: how many of us does it cost? **FITS**
9. **Trope: battle mages who burn out.** Here, "burnout" is the soldiers' word for overuse destroying the body while the Remnant stays whole (BANK NP#2). Old war-users show it in tremors and wasted bodies, and veterans name it with dread. Touches W10. **FITS**
10. **Trope: the peacekeeping force that's really an invasion.** Here, colonies spread by law, custom and business, with the occasional "peace keeping mission little more than a disguised invasion" (chat t211). **FITS**

**Law, crime and punishment**
11. **Trope: witch hunts in the village.** Here, changeling hunts: "it's your father, your uncle, the priest" (t680). Villagers question suspects about shared memories to catch the knowledge gaps (t282). Innocent people with poor memories die too. **FITS**
12. **Trope: the worst sentence is the dungeon or execution.** Here, the Emperor binds souls as punishment (`10` soul theft). The worst human sentence is binding into an object, and with flesh weapons (BANK ✅) the condemned may end up as a soldier's weapon, feeling dread (W13). **TWIST**

**Religion and superstition**
13. **Trope: pilgrimage to the holy city.** Here, the faithful go to the Tower, and close to it they *feel* something: the merged, senseless soul-mass of the giants' city (BANK L7, L8). Believers call it the god's presence. It's real, and it isn't what they think. **TWIST** (strong link between zealotry and the Tower)
14. **Trope: saints' relics.** Here, some relics really do hold a soul (W12), and it feels dread. Zealots venerate a suffering they can't hear. Realists sell fakes. *[fracture]* **TWIST**
15. **Trope: sailors' superstitions before a voyage.** Here, human crews have rites before crossing hell, which is the elven prison realm where breakouts happen (BANK NP#5). There are stories about warped things seen in transit, and those stories are about dark elves. **FITS**

**What others say about them**
16. Here, elder races call humanity a "recurring organism" (the long-memory cycle, 🔒). Dragons call humans the students who stole the library. Giants haven't forgotten man's role (t420). Dwarves call them the best customers. Orcs call them the third master (`10` orcs). **FITS**

**Small texture**
17. **Trope: everyday idioms about fate.** Here, a common idiom is "the Emperor already knew," for anything that turns out to have been foreseen. It's ordinary speech built on Derrek's impossible foreknowledge, one of the Book 1 hints (BANK time-travel item 3). **FITS**
18. Here, the human world smells of incense, engine oil and crowds, and sounds of prayer bells and sirens. Humans call every act of magic "science" in plain speech. Clones are spoken of as "it", which is the world's pretense that clones have no souls (W66). **FITS**

### Tropes to avoid
- **Humans as the bland default race.** Generalism is a symptom of insecurity, not an identity (`10` nature).
- **Humans as uniquely adaptable or chosen, stated as fact.** That's their mask (supremacy) and it must read as the world's lie.
- **Humans as plucky underdogs or the good guys.** They ruled the actual darkest age (jul08 t271).
- **Humans as the only race that can become gods.** BANK revises W31: any being can.
- **A god-emperor on a corpse throne, power-armored zealot knights, a heresy machine.** Too close to Warhammer 40k, which Patrick already steered away from (BANK NP#5).
- **Half-breeds or blood-mixing tropes.** That's blood thinking, and there's no hybrid canon.
- **Named or detailed human kingdoms.** Polities are deliberately unbuilt (t528, t630).
- **Copying real-world empires** (legions as a stand-in for a real empire, and the like). No real-world analogies.
- **Settling want vs necessity through flavor.** That's NEEDS_PATRICK #11, still open.

### Top 5, humans
1. **#13, pilgrims feel the Tower.** It ties human zealotry straight to the newest ruling (the Tower's soul-mass) and makes the faith rest on something real but misread.
2. **#11, changeling hunts as witch hunts.** It uses t680 and t282 directly and gives every era a village-level grimdark scene.
3. **#9, burnout as a soldiers' word.** It puts the BANK folk term into living speech.
4. **#17, "the Emperor already knew."** An idiom that quietly plants a Book 1 time-travel hint.
5. **#12 + #14, souls bound in relics and sentences.** They tie the flesh-weapon ruling to human law and faith.
