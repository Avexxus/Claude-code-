# Far-future tech: minds, industry and energy

🌊 **Everything here is a Claude proposal (2026-11-02).** Nothing is canon until Patrick rules. Names are descriptive labels only.

---

## 1. Settled already

These come from BANK 2026-11-02 unless cited otherwise. Simple machines run on electricity. Complex machines need an advanced AI, and that AI's Remnant is held in flesh. There are three kinds of AI: held minds, Remnant-infused AI and plain constructs. Only ensouled AI can cross into the physical world, and with real receptors it can likely do magic.

Soul-cores are flesh grown around a measured draw of pool-Remnant, and a bigger core means a more capable machine. A core feels only vague things. Pools are mined like oil, with prospectors, wildcat wells and field wars, and rift sites are the richest. Orc-soul cores are sold as pool cores. Convergence grows the modern towers and hulls, and only the basic wall-forging use of the old rite survives (chat t377).

Machines can't sense souls; only devices built on the chips can. The public thinks Remnant is a "battery". It's really an amplifier only, and burnout wrecks the body, never the Remnant. You can sell a thing with a soul bound into it, but not a soul. Computing has dwarven roots: humans took the knowledge by coercion, and an arms race produced the first ASI (`07` ASI). In the ASI-body case the brain is not the soul, and the original self stays inside (jul08 t331–t343). Dwarves brave deadly caves for the best elements (`07` race forms).

## 2. The catalog

### A. The three minds: making them, telling them apart, their status

**1. Held mind (a soul in the data)**
- *Convention:* the digital ghost or emergent AI.
- *How:* a pool draw sits in a flesh core that is wired into compute. The mind has no self of its own beyond what Remnant gives, and it lives entirely in the digital. It can reach the physical only if it's installed in a body or a robot with receptors.
- *Grows from:* BANK ruling set 1 and Q-B.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**2. Infused AI (a weapon-like focus)**
- *Convention:* the sentient ship or smart weapon.
- *How:* a plain construct does the thinking, and a chunk of Remnant is held "in focus" in its flesh core, the way a living weapon holds one. It has the same split as the ASI-body man: a digital brain plus a held soul in a vague state (jul08 t341).
- *Tag:* FITS.

**3. Plain construct**
- *Convention:* the classic AI.
- *How:* authored mastery with no soul. It is sovereign in the digital arena and near-powerless outside it (`07` ASI). It's the cheapest and most common kind, and it runs the ordinary networks.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**4. The crossing test**
- *Convention:* the Turing test.
- *How:* no machine can detect a soul, so people tell the kinds apart by what they can do. Only an ensouled mind can drive a body's receptors to act in the physical world. "Can it cross?" is the trade's working proof. Hiring an elf to stand next to the core also works (BANK L9: you feel souls up close), but it's costly and politically awkward.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**5. The label that can't be checked**
- *Convention:* counterfeit goods.
- *How:* all souls are the same, so no device, elf or test can tell an orc core from a pool core. "Tainted" is a paperwork grade backed only by provenance documents. Forged documents are the whole fraud.
- *Closes:* the tainted-cores vs public-ignorance check (BANK 2026-11-02 #62). The public would call it something like "living-stock draw" and believe it's a lesser *material*, never a soul.
- *Tag:* TWIST.

**6. Machine status law**
- *Convention:* AI personhood debates.
- *How:* Council law treats Remnant as a mineral, so an ensouled machine is legally "a thing with material bound in it." That makes it sellable property. The worst-crime laws on soul theft never touch the industry, because the law doesn't know what Remnant is.
- *Grows from:* BANK money and public-knowledge rulings.
- *Opens:* do blocs differ? Do elves, who can feel cores up close, quietly know better?
- *Tag:* FITS.

**7. Machine undead**
- *Convention:* the haunted or rogue AI.
- *How:* "souls in machines" is already an undead type (W69). Here the folk word "undead" is what people call an ensouled machine that has slipped its function, such as a held mind loose in a network or a core acting with no orders. Nobody admits it's the same thing as a working core.
- *Closes:* gives W69's machine undead a mechanism.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**8. Machine burnout**
- *Convention:* the overclocked system.
- *How:* overdriving an amplifier-core wrecks its flesh housing, just as burnout wrecks a soldier's body. The Remnant is untouched and is salvaged into new flesh.
- *Grows from:* BANK burnout ruling.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### B. Robots and androids

**9. Plain labor drones**
- *Convention:* the robot worker.
- *How:* metal, electric, no Remnant. These are the drones of the rich colonies (W58) and most of what moves boxes in the galaxy.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**10. Overseer networks**
- *Convention:* the hive or swarm AI.
- *How:* one ensouled mind runs thousands of plain drones. That matches the ruling's "the network for them to control it": the soul sits at the top, and everything below it is plain machinery.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**11. Receptor-grade android**
- *Convention:* the synthetic human.
- *How:* an infused AI in a body with flesh-grown sense organs. It can likely do magic (Q-B). It's rare and costly, mostly dwarven make, and feared because it is a mage that can be owned.
- *Opens:* how strong can one get, given that the core's size is its ceiling?
- *Tag:* TWIST.

**12. Metal-bodied ensouled robot**
- *Convention:* the war robot.
- *How:* a soul-core in a metal frame. It's a capable mind, but its grip is crippled, by the same logic as metal augments dulling a person's magic. That makes it smart and strong but magically weak, and it's the common military model.
- *Grows from:* BANK augmentation ruling.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### C. Soul-core manufacturing and grading

**13. Core vats**
- *Convention:* the clone tank.
- *How:* core flesh is grown from clone tissue and then given its measured draw. This is where the clone-flesh trade feeds the machine trade.
- *Grows from:* BANK clone flesh, W66.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**14. Core grades**
- *Convention:* processor tiers.
- *How:* a core is graded twice: by the size of its draw, which sets its ceiling, and by its flesh, cloned or harvested. Harvested flesh is sold as premium even though it's identical, the same irony as clone bodies.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**15. The binder's bottleneck**
- *Convention:* the skilled-artisan choke point.
- *How:* "machines copy the effect, never the act" (jul08 t339), so the step that fixes a draw into a core needs a living hand. Every advanced machine passes through a licensed binder. In all but name the binder is doing soul magic, the worst crime there is, and no one knows it.
- *Closes:* gives the core trade its irony engine (BANK #62).
- *Touches:* Law 18 and soul magic's rarity. See Q-B.
- *Tag:* TWIST.

**16. Core salvage**
- *Convention:* recycling.
- *How:* Remnant is never spent (W10), so a core outlives every machine it serves. Old warship cores pass from hull to hull for centuries, and an ancient core is valued the way a vintage blade is.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**17. Dwarven master cores vs human bulk cores**
- *Convention:* luxury vs mass market.
- *How:* this is the existing craft split (`07`: dwarven make as mastery, humans as "the factory") carried over to cores.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### D. Remnant wells

**18. Where pools pool**
- *Convention:* geology as plot.
- *How:* latent pools gather in two kinds of place. The first is rifts, where Remnant leaks from above (t188). The second is sites of great mass death, such as old killing-grounds, buried cities and giant halls.
- *Grows from:* geologic scars (`07` places) and BANK rifts as places of constant war.
- *Opens:* pool origin (Q-A). Patrick said Remnant is "randomly floating around."
- *Tag:* TWIST.

**19. Prospecting by history**
- *Convention:* the prospector and the wildcatter.
- *How:* machines can't sense pools, so prospectors read the past (battle records, ruin maps, scar sites), hire elf dowsers, or rent rare chip-based reading rigs. Archaeology is a mining trade.
- *Tag:* FITS (if 18 holds).

**20. Flesh wicks**
- *Convention:* drilling and pumping.
- *How:* grown tissue is lowered into a well, and Remnant settles into the body-like matter (W12: the more body-like a material, the better it holds a soul). No soul mage is needed at the well; the binder comes later, at portioning.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**21. Living tankers**
- *Convention:* the oil tanker.
- *How:* Remnant can't sit in a steel tank, so bulk transport is grown flesh in life-support holds. Tanker crews hate the hum of the holds.
- *Touches:* W13 dread, reduced to "vague" in BANK Q-A.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**22. Spill and blowout**
- *Convention:* the industrial disaster.
- *How:* a breach frees Remnant, which drifts into the nearest body-like matter: corpses, meat stores, livestock. The result is a sudden outbreak of "souls bound to corpses" (W69).
- *Touches:* L11/W14, the unconfirmed holder rule. See Q-E.
- *Tag:* RISKY (it bends W14 either way).

**23. Field wars**
- *Convention:* resource wars.
- *How:* a pool field is a giant amplifier, like a small Tower (BANK L8). Whoever holds the field fights at a higher peak, so field wars draw the most powerful and end in burnout.
- *Grows from:* BANK rifts as places "where the most powerful congregate."
- *Tag:* FITS.

**24. Well labor**
- *Convention:* company towns.
- *How:* orcs and indentured gnomes work the wellheads (`07` race forms) because bodies cost less than rated drones. Grimdark texture: the galaxy's worst-paid hands handle its most precious material.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### E. Power for plain machines

**25. The ordinary grid**
- *Convention:* the fusion economy.
- *How:* fission, fusion and solar power everything. Remnant appears nowhere on a power bill.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**26. Made-to-order fuel**
- *Convention:* the exotic fuel trade.
- *How:* the dragon transmutation industry (BANK "maybe" #54) also has a civilian side: elements made to order for reactors, beside the weapon stones.
- *Tag:* TWIST (it builds on a "maybe").

**27. "Remnant cell" scams**
- *Convention:* snake-oil batteries.
- *How:* the public believes Remnant stores power, so crooks sell "charged" Remnant cells for household gear. They do nothing, because Remnant only amplifies.
- *Grows from:* BANK public misconception #63.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### F. Factories and automation

**28. Dark factories**
- *Convention:* lights-out automation.
- *How:* human bulk industry is plain drones under one overseer core, with a binder line as the only human station that can't be cut.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**29. Cheaper than drones**
- *Convention:* the labor underclass.
- *How:* where cores are scarce, orc and gnome hands replace overseer networks. Automation is a luxury, and slavery is the budget option.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### G. Convergence as construction

**30. Wall-grade Convergence**
- *Convention:* growing buildings biologically.
- *How:* the only surviving use of the old rite, forging walls (t377), industrialized. The god-forging unison stays lost.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**31. Feedstock grades**
- *Convention:* construction materials.
- *How:* clone mass for cheap towers, bought orcs for hulls (t278–t280), and the funerary dead (W15) for prestige builds.
- *Opens:* does a funerary build release the soul or keep it (W15)?
- *Tag:* FITS.

**32. Every tower a little Tower**
- *Convention:* the arcology.
- *How:* grown towers hold souls, so each one is a faint amplifier. Downtowns are where mages fight best, and old giant halls are strongest of all.
- *Grows from:* BANK L8, the Tower as the largest amplifier.
- *Tag:* TWIST.

**33. Grown hulls as crew amplifiers**
- *Convention:* the living ship.
- *How:* a converged hull lifts its crew's reach. That explains why Remnant-augmented ships beat machine-only ones (`07` ship-power), without anyone spending anything.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### H. Materials science

**34. The holding scale**
- *Convention:* the materials hierarchy.
- *How:* materials are ranked by how well they hold a soul: flesh best, then living plant (the rose, BANK), bone and wood, with metal worst (W12). The industry's holy grail is a non-flesh substrate that holds, and nobody has made one.
- *Tag:* FITS.

**35. Rift-cave elements**
- *Convention:* the exotic mineral.
- *How:* the rarest elements form only in caves near rifts and pools, where power leaking from above warps the rock. The caves are deadly because every beast and bandit in them fights amplified. This is why dwarves pay "great personal cost" (`07`).
- *Tag:* TWIST.

### I. Terraforming

**36. Slow terraforming**
- *Convention:* the atmosphere processor.
- *How:* plain machinery working over centuries on dead worlds, with orc labor and a high death toll.
- *Grim link:* under idea 18, the mass graves of a terraforming project become a well field a few centuries later.
- *Tag:* FITS.

### J. Data and computing

**37. Living server halls**
- *Convention:* the data center.
- *How:* held minds need flesh, so their halls are climate-held tissue racks. Plain networks run beside them, and fast messaging needs no bond (BANK).
- *Tag:* FITS.

**38. The dwarven root**
- *Convention:* the patent empire.
- *How:* all computing descends from dwarven magic-infused metal (`07` ASI). The dwarves hold no office but collect on every chip (t540).
- *Tag:* FITS.

**39. Licensed soul-readers**
- *Convention:* the scanner.
- *How:* the only devices that sense souls are rare chip-derived rigs. Inquisition auditors and black-market core forgers both want them.
- *Grows from:* BANK ruling set 2.
- *Tag:* FITS.

## 3. Avoid

- **Servitor look.** No lobotomized people mass-converted into machines, and no skulls and cables. Keep the ASI-body man a one-off (t343).
- **Remnant power cells or fuel.** That breaks W10 and the amplifier-only ruling.
- **Machines that sense souls.** That's the elves' gift (BANK ruling set 2).
- **Mind upload of a living person.** Transfer is impossible (jul23 t108). A held mind is pool Remnant, not an uploaded person.
- **A machine race as aliens.** The galaxy has no alien minds (`07` far future).
- **Robot mages without flesh receptors.** That breaks Q-B and "matter does no magic."
- **Teleporting freight.** Space-folding is cut. Any freight jump must use a race's existing drive.
- **Entropy engines or probability computers.** Both are cut domains.

## 5. Top 10

1. **The label that can't be checked (5).** It turns tainted cores into pure prejudice and fixes the public-knowledge tension.
2. **The binder's bottleneck (15).** It makes "never the act" into economics, and it hides soul magic inside a legal trade.
3. **Where pools pool and prospecting by history (18, 19).** War makes future wells, which ties rifts, scars and battlefields to the oil economy.
4. **Every tower a little Tower (32).** It scales BANK L8 down into every city.
5. **Flesh wicks and living tankers (20, 21).** Extraction and shipping that need no soul mage at the well.
6. **Machine undead (7).** It gives W69's "souls in machines" a mechanism.
7. **Rift-cave elements (35).** It explains why the dwarves' caves are deadly.
8. **Machine status law (6).** Soul-theft law can't see the industry.
9. **Metal vs receptor-grade bodies (11, 12).** The augmentation ruling applied to robots.
10. **Core salvage (16).** Ageless cores as heirlooms, a direct consequence of W10.

**Top 3 and why:** 5, 15 and 18/19. Each one connects two rulings that already exist: tainted cores with public ignorance, "never the act" with soul-crime law, and rifts and scars with the well economy. None of them adds a new force.
