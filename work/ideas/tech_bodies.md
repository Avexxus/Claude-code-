# Far-future tech: BODIES (2026-11-02)

🌊 **Claude proposal throughout.** Nothing here is canon. Not yet canon-checked. Names are plain labels, never proper names.

---

## 1. Settled already

Clones have souls and magic, and everyone pretends otherwise (`10` wk W66). Clone organs and flesh exist; clone bodies are seen as lower quality but are identical, so they're often sold as "real" (BANK 2026-11-02). The organ trade is a legal government market with a black market beneath (t349), a dwarven and human enterprise (t341–347). Orcs are excluded (their organs "would be wrong"), and captured dark elves are part of it (t351, W68). Metal augments hurt your magic, because you can't fully control your Remnant without your whole body, while flesh-grown augments keep it (BANK 11-02). Every birth draws diffuse unclaimed Remnant into the child at conception (🔒 Birth, B §190). Lifespan follows Remnant size, giving resistance to age but never immunity (🔒 4a). The brain belongs to the body and the soul is Remnant (jul08 t331–332; BANK 11-01 time mechanism). Changelings transfer by soul theft, and every taking kills the host (t676, t127). Resurrection is near-impossible (W51). Focusing-chamber implants exist (jul08 t261–263). The ASI cyborg exists (jul08 t339–345). Burnout is the body wrecked by overuse (BANK 10-31).

## 2. The catalog

### A. Cloning

**1. Whole-body growth vat**
- *Convention:* cloning vats.
- *Here:* plain biotech machinery around a grown womb-lining. The vat starts a whole organism from a cell sample, so at that "conception" it draws diffuse Remnant like any birth. That's why every clone has a soul (🔒 Birth; W66).
- **FITS**

**2. Organ-only culture**
- *Convention:* lab-grown organs.
- *Here:* a single organ grown on a scaffold. A part isn't an organism, so it gathers no soul. Simple machinery, no core needed.
- **TWIST:** it gives a clean rule, "a whole organism gathers a soul, a part never does."

**3. Why the industry grows whole bodies anyway**
- *Convention:* organ farms.
- *Here:* scaffold organs come out weaker because they never grew inside a living body. The best organs come from whole clones raised and then harvested. So the trade *needs* ensouled clones, and the pretense (W66) exists to cover that.
- **FITS** (it is W66's motive)

**4. Forced growth**
- *Convention:* accelerated aging in the tank.
- *Here:* the body reaches adult size in months, but the brain comes out blank. The flesh is truly identical (BANK 11-02), so the "lower quality" stigma is prejudice. Cheap black-market rushes do carry real flaws, and those feed the stigma.
- **FITS**

**5. Imprinting**
- *Convention:* flash-education, memory implants.
- *Here:* plain brain-tech writes skills and memories onto a clone's brain. That's possible because memory lives in the brain, which belongs to the body. The clone's own soul does the thinking, so it is a person who has been handed a past.
- **FITS** (brain/soul split)

**6. Provenance papers ("sold as real")**
- *Convention:* certification of origin.
- *Here:* no physical test can tell clone from born, because they're identical. "Real" is pure paperwork: chains of custody and birth registries. Forgery is a whole profession. The elite pay a premium for provenance on organs, skin and servants.
- **FITS**

**7. Elder clones**
- *Convention:* cloning anything.
- *Here:* growing a dragon, giant or elf body. That raises whether the vat-birth draws a soul of elder size.
- **RISKY:** it touches 4a and the race ladder by fragment size (D §20). See Q-B.

**8. Memorial clones**
- *Convention:* replacing the dead.
- *Here:* a grieving family buys a clone of the dead, imprinted from a brain record. W51 makes true return near-impossible, so this is a forgery with its own new soul, a stranger wearing a loved one's past. Everyone pretends.
- **FITS** (grimdark, cost-driven)

**9. Cloning elves as body theft**
- *Convention:* clone law.
- *Here:* elven justice already sends to hell anyone who steals a fellow elf's *soul or body* (D §440). Cloning an elf without consent counts as body theft. So vat elves are a black-market good only, and the organ trade's dark-elf supply (t351) gains a clone sideline.
- **TWIST**

**10. Clones as legal property**
- *Convention:* clone rights.
- *Here:* clones are titled to an owner, by government (human-run markets) or by contract (dwarven). Their status varies by power, with no single galactic law. Since the Council's laws were built by elder races (W58–60), patchwork is expected.
- **FITS**

### B. Body swap and continuity

**11. There are no empty bodies**
- *Convention:* blank bodies waiting for a mind.
- *Here:* any grown whole body already has its own soul (idea 1). So every "fresh body" on the market is an occupied one, and every body swap is a killing (t127).
- **FITS.** This is the domain's hinge rule.

**12. Continuity clinics**
- *Convention:* mind transfer, resleeving.
- *Here:* the client's brain is recorded and imprinted onto their own clone, then the original is quietly euthanized. The clone wakes believing it is the client. It's legal, popular with the rich, and a lie. 4a holds, because each body ages on its own soul's clock.
- **FITS**

**13. The true swap is a crime**
- *Convention:* body-swap markets.
- *Here:* really moving a soul takes forbidden soul magic. It's a changeling transfer (t676) that kills the clone's soul. Most clients lack the power ("all changelings are incredibly powerful off rip", t544–545). So the high-end "true transfer" clinics are fronts where a changeling operator takes the *client's* body and fortune instead.
- **TWIST**

**14. Clones as changeling hosts**
- *Convention:* the infiltrator body.
- *Here:* the dwarf dynasty moving into cloned "sons" (W23) becomes the model case. Clones have no family and no records, and they come with provenance papers. The soul–body mismatch is still the tell, and the smart ones shield it (W18).
- **FITS**

**15. Brain-record cross-check**
- *Convention:* mind scan interrogation.
- *Here:* a changeling's host brain still holds the host's memories, while the driver knows gaps (t282). Comparing a brain record against live answers could flag a mismatch.
- **RISKY:** it bends forensic invisibility (t161 pt2) and Patrick's "nobody guards against it" (BANK 11-01). If kept at all, it should be rare and one-scene only.

### C. Organ trade machinery

**16. The legal organ exchange**
- *Convention:* organ registries.
- *Here:* a government market (t349) built from plain-machine logistics: registries, matching and stasis freight. Clone organs make up the bulk of its legal stock, which partly answers L32 (Patrick has ruled clone organs exist).
- **FITS**

**17. Stasis freight**
- *Convention:* cryo-shipping.
- *Here:* plain refrigeration and stasis that holds living stock and organs across star lanes. Whole clones ship asleep. Remnant is ageless (✅ 4a), so a frozen soul just waits in its body.
- **FITS**

**18. The "real organ" black market**
- *Convention:* black-market organs.
- *Here:* it sells provenance-true organs from born donors, convicts and captured dark elves (W68). Its whole premium rests on idea 6's paperwork.
- **FITS**

**19. The longevity-organ myth**
- *Convention:* elixir organs.
- *Here:* buyers believe an elder's organs extend life. Under 4a, lifespan follows the *soul*, so a transplant doesn't extend it. The myth mirrors the public's "battery" error (BANK 11-02) and keeps demand for elf parts high.
- **TWIST** (a belief, not a racial trait)

### D. Augmentation and prosthetics

**20. Metal prosthetics**
- *Convention:* cybernetic limbs.
- *Here:* cheap and tough, running on plain electricity. They dull the wearer's control because the appendage can't run through what isn't body (BANK 11-02). Soldiers without magic and laborers wear metal.
- **FITS**

**21. Flesh-grown augments**
- *Convention:* bio-mods.
- *Here:* grown from your own cells as organ-only cultures, so no soul is involved. They keep full control and cost far more. Mages and the rich wear flesh.
- **FITS**

**22. The augment class line**
- *Convention:* the chrome underclass.
- *Here:* metal marks the poor and the expendable, flesh marks status. A body's material shows its owner's rank at a glance.
- **FITS** (conditions, not blood)

**23. Focusing-chamber implant, regrown**
- *Convention:* combat implants.
- *Here:* the dwarven chamber-in-small (jul08 t261) gets sheathed in grown flesh so it stops costing control. Its self-destructive one-shot is *burnout*: the body wrecked, the Remnant intact. That settles the open ambiguity in C §226.
- **TWIST**

**24. Metal as a restraint**
- *Convention:* inhibitor collars.
- *Here:* metal fitted on purpose to prisoners and captive mages to cut their control. It's surgery as a lock, and it pairs with the orc device's kill switch (B §114).
- **FITS**

**25. Amplifier limb**
- *Convention:* a weapon arm.
- *Here:* a grown limb shaped by the Convergence-like flesh-weapon rite to hold a bound soul, worn as a built-in amplifier rig. The bound soul feels only vague things (BANK 11-02). Burnout risk rides with it.
- **TWIST:** it joins flesh weapons (BANK 11-01), rigs and prosthetics.

**26. Burnout rebuilds**
- *Convention:* veteran reconstruction.
- *Here:* soldiers wrecked by rigs get rebuilt with clone organs and go back to the front. Each rebuild sits nearer the next burnout. The Remnant was never the casualty.
- **FITS**

**27. Augmented human against a dragon**
- *Convention:* the super-soldier.
- *Here:* this survives (jul08 t261) only with flesh augments plus a rig. A metal-heavy human loses the control needed. A group or a bad matchup still beats a dragon; no single being beats a great ancient one (🔒).
- **FITS**

### E. Medicine and healing

**28. Regeneration tanks**
- *Convention:* healing pods.
- *Here:* plain biotech that regrows tissue from your own cells. It's slow. The brain can be patched, never replaced.
- **FITS**

**29. Ensouled surgeon**
- *Convention:* robot surgeons.
- *Here:* a soul-core machine with grown hands and real receptors. By BANK 11-02 (B), it could likely do the act itself, not just copy the effect.
- **RISKY:** it presses "machines copy effects, never the act" (jul08 t339). It holds only because it's a soul doing the act, not the machine.

**30. Hospice pipeline**
- *Convention:* body harvesting.
- *Here:* dying patients' bodies flow into the organ exchange under consent forms nobody reads. The gnome shaman's pattern (BANK 11-01) can recur in one clinic as a *story* seed, never as a custom (Patrick rejected distrust of healers, BANK 11-01).
- **FITS**

**31. Bio-plague**
- *Convention:* engineered plague.
- *Here:* the biotech plague undead (W69) and the Orc King's plague get their lab side here: vats and cultures turned into weapons. His entropy pact stays his own, not tech.
- **FITS**

### F. Life extension and stasis

**32. Organ-cycling**
- *Convention:* the rich living forever.
- *Here:* the rich on far colonies swap out every part but the brain. The brain is the ceiling, because replacing it means a blank stranger. That's why 4a holds: they live long, then die anyway.
- **FITS**

**33. Stasis-skipping**
- *Convention:* cryosleep.
- *Here:* plain machinery pauses the flesh while the ageless Remnant waits. The rich sleep through decades to outlast rivals or debts. It costs time, not life.
- **FITS**

**34. Tailored bodies**
- *Convention:* gene-editing.
- *Here:* flesh can be tuned for strength, endurance or looks. The soul is untouched, so the three dials don't move. Editing changes conditions, never "blood" or worth.
- **FITS**

### G. Mind and brain

**35. Brain records**
- *Convention:* memory backups.
- *Here:* plain construct tech copies the brain's patterns. That feeds imprinting, continuity clinics and memorial clones.
- **FITS**

**36. Uploads**
- *Convention:* mind uploading.
- *Here:* a legal upload is a brain record running as a plain digital construct, with no soul. The public believes it's you. A *true* upload would mean binding your own soul into a core, which is the soul-binding crime (ghost rule, t500).
- **TWIST**

**37. The ASI cyborg, reread**
- *Convention:* the cyborg.
- *Here:* the man's soul is locked in a metal body, and the ASI chip is a plain construct that stops the Remnant rebinding (jul08 t343–345). Metal also dulls whatever control he has left (BANK 11-02). Placement stays open (R-B8).
- **FITS**

**38. Memory wipes**
- *Convention:* mind-wipes.
- *Here:* plain brain-tech scrubs prisoners and resold clones. It mirrors Jon waking blank, since the brain is rewound with the body (BANK 11-01).
- **FITS**

### H. Body fashion

**39. Grown ornament**
- *Convention:* body mods as fashion.
- *Here:* cultured horns, colored skin, grown jewelry, all organ-only cultures with no soul. Flesh-grown pieces signal wealth, visible metal signals labor, and "real" provenance skin sits at the top.
- **FITS**

## 3. Avoid

- **Soulless clones as author's truth:** that breaks W66.
- **Copying or splitting a soul by tech:** that breaks W10 and Birth.
- **Tech-only resleeving (a mind stack moving the self):** that's soul transfer without the crime, and it breaks t676.
- **Organs that carry a piece of the donor's soul:** that breaks W10.
- **Tech that grants true immortality, or replaceable brains that keep the self:** that breaks 🔒 4a and 4b.
- **Machines that detect souls in a clone or a changeling:** that breaks BANK 11-02.
- **Lobotomized cyborg laborers as a class, cables in skulls:** it's too close to 40k, and drones do the labor.
- **Healing as a magic "type" or a healer class:** that breaks Law 0a.
- **Engineered "superior" bloodlines:** that breaks conditions-not-blood.
- **Destroy-and-rebuild teleporters:** they belong to the travel batch. If used, the copy is a new soul (idea 1), and they sit on the cut space-folding seam.
