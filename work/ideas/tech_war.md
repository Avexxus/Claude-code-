# Far-future tech: WAR & SECURITY (2026-11-02)

🌊 Claude proposal throughout. Not canon. Beats and facts only, no prose. Every name is a plain label.
Cites: BANK = `decisions/BANK.md` entry date. Codex cites as in the codex.

---

## 1. Settled already

Simple war machines run on electricity and need no Remnant; only advanced-AI machines carry a flesh soul-core (BANK 11-02). Ensouled machines include weapons, satellites and drones (BANK 11-02). Magic weapons are living flesh shaped by a Convergence-like ritual (BANK 11-01, ✅). Held souls are amplifiers only, or are grabbed and forced to act; nothing drains (BANK 10-31). Burnout is the body wrecked by overuse (BANK 10-31). Amplifier rigs extend a soldier's reach (BANK 11-02). Machines can't sense souls, except rare chip-based devices (BANK 11-02). Metal augments hurt magic; flesh-grown ones don't (BANK 11-02). Dragons sense materials, and transmutation defeats the dwarven rust-proof shield (`07`). The focusing-chamber implant lets a human one-shot a dragon at self-destructive cost (`06`, jul08 t261–265). The orc device has a kill switch, a drain and a puppet function, run from the Tower (`01`). The dragon fissile stone is Patrick's "maybe" industry (BANK 11-01). Dragon ships get about 5 beams and can be swarmed (`07`, D §13).

## 2. The catalog

### A. Personal weapons

1. **Plain slugthrower and coil rifle**
   - Convention: the standard-issue rifle.
   - Here: metal, chemistry or electricity, with no core. It's what nearly every soldier carries, because it's cheap and it's the same in anyone's hands.
   - Grows from: layered tech (BANK 11-02).
   - Tag: **FITS**

2. **Flesh blade (the true magic weapon)**
   - Convention: the legendary living or sentient sword.
   - Here: flesh bent by a Convergence-like ritual around a held soul. It raises the wielder's reach (amplifier), or the held soul is forced to strike by itself. Rare, costly, and grown, not forged.
   - Grows from: BANK 10-31 and 11-01 (flesh weapons ✅); W12 (body-like substrate holds best).
   - Closes: feeds R-M5/L11 (vessels), since flesh holds a soul on its own.
   - Tag: **FITS**

3. **Clone-flesh weapons, mass grade**
   - Convention: the cheap knock-off of the elite gun.
   - Here: flesh weapons grown from vat clone tissue around small pool-Remnant cores. They're called lower quality, but they're identical, so they get sold as "ancestral" or "true-grown".
   - Grows from: the clone ruling (BANK 11-02: "sold as real").
   - Tag: **FITS**

4. **The tainted-core sidearm**
   - Convention: the black-market gun that jams.
   - Here: a cheap ensouled weapon whose core is a whole orc soul, mislabeled as pool Remnant. When one fails, soldiers blame "the orc in it". In truth it works exactly as well, since all souls are the same.
   - Grows from: tainted cores (BANK 11-02).
   - Touches: no racial sins (the prejudice is the world's, never the fact).
   - Tag: **FITS**

5. **The heirloom held in a gun**
   - Convention: the family weapon.
   - Here: a fallen relative's soul bound into a living rifle stock. It amplifies the shooter. The held soul feels only vague strangeness.
   - Grows from: t502 (binding a loved one into armor); BANK 11-02 Q-A (cores feel vaguely).
   - Touches: W13, which this softens.
   - Tag: **TWIST**

### B. Armor and shields

6. **The maker's mark is a confession**
   - Convention: a signature master-crafted shield.
   - Here: dragons sense materials and know dwarven master-work on sight (`07` rust-proof shield). So your kit tells a dragon who made it, who paid for it, and roughly what you prepared against. Elite dwarven gear is a beacon in a dragon fight.
   - Grows from: `07` l.149.
   - Tag: **TWIST**

7. **Sacrificial armor doctrine against elders**
   - Convention: ablative plating.
   - Here: an elder's transmutation defeats any preparation (`07` difficulty ladder). So against great dragons armies wear cheap layered plates meant to be changed and shed, and save master-work for lesser breaths. "Don't bring the good shield to an elder."
   - Grows from: `07` "variety = difficulty ladder".
   - Tag: **FITS**

8. **Breath-specific kit**
   - Convention: hazard suits.
   - Here: plain physical counters to physical effects, such as rust-proofing, insulation, heat sinks and EM-field layers (jul08 t263). Magic's effects are real physics, so physical defenses work against effects. Nothing stops the act itself.
   - Grows from: "machines copy effects, never the act" (jul08 t339).
   - Closes: feeds F170 (the EM-inhibit hook) and NP#32.
   - Tag: **FITS**

9. **Grown armor (flesh-lined)**
   - Convention: organic bio-armor.
   - Here: Convergence-grown plates with a living inner layer, which lets armor hold a soul (W12). Mages favor it because metal near or in the body isn't the problem; metal *in* the body is.
   - Grows from: BANK 11-02 (grown structures = Convergence); t512 ("armor like al").
   - Tag: **TWIST**

### C. Power armor and amplifier rigs

10. **Plain exoframe**
    - Convention: the powered exoskeleton.
    - Here: electric, no core: strength, load, armor. Common infantry and dockworker kit.
    - Tag: **FITS**

11. **Amplifier rig, graded by core size**
    - Convention: the power-armor tier list.
    - Here: a rig holds pooled Remnant that extends the wearer's **reach** (range), never their ceiling. Bigger core, longer reach. Wearing it doesn't make you stronger at close range. It lets you act farther.
    - Grows from: BANK 10-31 (the "range" wording) and 11-02 (rigs).
    - Touches: W10 (nothing drains).
    - Tag: **FITS**

12. **The ensouled suit that keeps fighting**
    - Convention: the armor with its own AI.
    - Here: a suit with a Remnant-infused core is grabbed-and-forced. When the wearer goes down it can still walk, drag or fight. If it's given receptors, it may even do magic (BANK 11-02 Q-B).
    - Opens: is a suit like that a being under the law?
    - Tag: **RISKY** (bends how far ensouled AI acts on its own)

13. **Push stims**
    - Convention: combat drugs.
    - Here: issued to mage troops to override fatigue. Fatigue is the cost, so pushing past it is how burnout happens. Command knows this and issues them anyway.
    - Grows from: NP#2 burnout (BANK 10-31).
    - Closes: gives burnout an on-page military mechanic.
    - Tag: **FITS**

### D. Infantry kit and augmentation

14. **Two-tier augmentation**
    - Convention: the cyber-soldier.
    - Here: line troops get cheap metal limbs and lose fine magical control (BANK 11-02). Mage officers and the rich get flesh-grown limbs and keep it. You can read an army's class divide from its scars.
    - Touches: conditions, not blood.
    - Tag: **FITS**

15. **The focusing-chamber implant as a last-shot weapon**
    - Convention: the suicide-charge augment.
    - Here: the dwarven implant (jul08 t261) gives a human one dragon-killing shot at self-destructive cost, which is burnout by design. Doctrine treats implant carriers as single-use.
    - Closes: feeds NP#32 (the augmented era now sits in the far future too).
    - Opens: is the implant metal (and so hurts magic) or grown?
    - Tag: **FITS**

16. **Forced metal for prisoners**
    - Convention: the inhibitor collar.
    - Here: there's no anti-magic field. Instead, captured mages get metal implants seated deep, which degrade their control. It's an echo of "implant the ASI so the Remnant cannot rebind" (`08`, jul08 t343).
    - Touches: no new magic types (it uses the metal ruling).
    - Tag: **TWIST**

### E. Drones and robots

17. **Plain swarm drones**
    - Convention: the cheap drone swarm.
    - Here: no cores, so they're disposable and endless. They're the standard answer to burst power: dragon ships get ~5 beams, so you send more drones than they have beams (D §13 "swarmable").
    - Tag: **FITS**

18. **Ensouled hunter drone**
    - Convention: the smart autonomous killer.
    - Here: a flesh core lets it judge, adapt and hold focus. It still can't sense souls, so it hunts by sight, heat and signal like any machine.
    - Grows from: BANK 11-02 (no soul-sensing).
    - Tag: **FITS**

19. **War-frames with receptors**
    - Convention: the combat robot.
    - Here: a robot given sense organs and a Remnant-infused AI could likely do magic (BANK 11-02 Q-B). These are rare and frightening. Avoid the cyborg-slave look: they should be smooth, grown and quiet.
    - Opens: who builds them, and does the public know they're casting?
    - Tag: **RISKY** (the machine-casting edge)

### F. Artillery, orbital and ship weapons

20. **Kinetic orbital strike**
    - Convention: rods from orbit.
    - Here: plain mass and speed, no core. It's the cheapest way to kill a city, and so the most common atrocity.
    - Tag: **FITS**

21. **Ensouled targeting satellite**
    - Convention: the orbital weapons platform.
    - Here: plain guns, with a flesh core on the satellite for judgment and tracking. The core is grown around pool Remnant and feels only vague things.
    - Tag: **FITS**

22. **Human gun-crews as rotating mages**
    - Convention: broadside crews.

    - Grows from: D §13 "human gun-crews".
    - Tag: **TWIST**

23. **The "battery bank" misnomer**
    - Convention: the ship's power cells.
    - Here: the focusing chamber's "batteries" (t50) are held souls used as amplifiers. Engineers call them batteries because the public thinks Remnant is a battery (BANK 11-02). The captured dragon in the chamber is forced, not drained.
    - Closes: reads the t50 drain seam (06 L111) under amplifier-only.
    - Tag: **FITS**

### G. World-killers and the stone industry

24. **The three-key world-killer**
    - Convention: the planet-buster.
    - Here: killing a world takes three things: a transmuter's fissile stone, a dwarven focusing chamber, and a dragon's breath (t408; forge-world strike t412). No single race holds all three, and that interdependence is the real deterrent.
    - Grows from: BANK 11-01 (stones); `07` t408.
    - Tag: **FITS**

25. **Stone foundries**
    - Convention: the nuclear arms industry.
    - Here: transmuter dragons mass-make unstable stones and sell them to rival houses, the same arm-them-to-kill-each-other pattern as cathedral ships (t94, t274). Raised houses and dispossessed houses buy from the same foundry.
    - Grows from: Patrick's "maybe" (BANK 11-01).
    - Opens: do non-dragons buy stones?
    - Tag: **FITS**

26. **Contamination as a weapon's signature**
    - Convention: fallout.
    - Here: stone strikes leave long contamination like the land round the Tower (t88, `14` §10). Ruined worlds become "breath-scarred", and their scars still feed the hatred gradient (t104).
    - Tag: **FITS**

### H. Surveillance and intelligence

27. **The soul-blind state**
    - Convention: the total-surveillance society.
    - Here: cameras, signals and fast messaging see everything physical, but no machine sees souls. Changelings and soul thieves walk through the most watched society ever built.
    - Grows from: BANK 11-01 (changeling paranoia: "noone protects against it"); BANK 11-02 (no soul-sensing); G modern "surveillance".
    - Tag: **FITS**

28. **Reading devices (the forbidden sensor)**
    - Convention: the lie-detector or psychic scanner.
    - Here: the only machines that sense souls are built on bond-spoof or reading chips, which were perfected by dissecting elves (jul23 t154). They're rare, illegal and owned by intelligence services. The Inquisition hunts them.
    - Grows from: BANK 11-02 "very specific ones".
    - Opens: who holds them in Book 1?
    - Tag: **FITS**

29. **Elven fleet intel and the heaven blind spot**
    - Convention: telepathic early warning.
    - Here: the homeworld is a beacon to every elf, but a lone elf is a pinprick (BANK 11-01 L9). Up close, elves feel boarders. But fleets in a pocket world are cut off (t600). So the classic anti-elf ambush is set inside heaven.
    - Grows from: t600; BANK 11-01.
    - Tag: **FITS**

30. **Effect camouflage**
    - Convention: blending into the noise.
    - Here: machines copy magic's effects (jul08 t339), so a mage firing lightning amid machine arcs is invisible as a mage. Telling the act from the effect takes an elf up close or a reading device.
    - Tag: **TWIST**

### I. Anti-mage tactics (no new magic)

31. **Reach war**
    - Convention: the sniper duel.
    - Here: a mage's reach is capped by Remnant size, and rigs extend it (BANK 10-31). So doctrine is to outrange the mage with plain guns or kill the rig. Reach, not power, is the military currency.
    - Tag: **TWIST**

32. **Attrition against burst**
    - Convention: wearing down the tank.
    - Here: the 🔒 dragon weakness, generalized. Saturation fire forces a mage to keep defending until fatigue does the work. It's cheap, slow and reliable.
    - Grows from: `07` combat profile.
    - Tag: **FITS**

### K. Military medicine

36. **Clone-organ field hospitals**
    - Convention: regrowth tanks.

    - Tag: **FITS**

    - Convention: the veterans' ward.

38. **Battlefield Convergence**
    - Convention: harvesting the dead.

39. **Battlefields become wells**
    - Convention: the haunted war zone.

    - Grows from: BANK 11-02 (wells); BANK 11-01 (rifts as places of constant war).
    - Opens: are some latent pools old battlefields?
    - Tag: **TWIST**

## 3. Avoid

- **Anti-magic nullifier fields:** magic is a soul gripping reality. There's no field to cancel, only effects to block.
- **Soul scanners at checkpoints:** this takes the gift from the elves (BANK 11-02).
- **Remnant as ammo, fuel, charge or a clip that runs dry:** this breaks W10.
- **Backup bodies or resleeving into clones:** clones have souls, so this would be changeling possession.
- **Teleport strikes, fold bombs, entropy or probability weapons:** these are cut domains.
- **Servitors, skulls and cables, lobotomized cyborg slaves on the line:** this is too close to Warhammer 40k.
- **Mage ranks or classes by "type" (pyro troops, etc.):** no types (Law 0a).
- **A single super-soldier beating a great elder dragon solo:** this breaks 🔒 beatability. The implant one-shot is the only edge, and it's fatal.
- **A monolithic machine uprising:** AI comes in three kinds and is never one bloc.
