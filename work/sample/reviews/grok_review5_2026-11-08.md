## Grok's diagnosis of Claude's blind spot: unplanned pressure
Claude's revision process removes the qualities that make prose feel lived:
1. local mess that is never cleaned up (unpaid-off detail)
2. slightly wrong emotional temperature left standing
3. attention that wanders for a beat mid-crisis
4. uneven sentence pressure (Claude equalizes)
5. residual discomfort left unresolved at paragraph end (Claude closes the circuit)

## The method: change the success condition, not "more polish"
1. **The useless-detail pass:** 2–3 concrete details per high-stakes scene. Noticed by the POV, no payoff, not metaphors, not cleanly character-revealing. Later passes may NOT remove them.
2. **The wrong-temperature pass:** in 4–5 loaded moments (the first kill, the Queen's death, Doc's quiet, the floor speech, the window), the POV reaction is slightly misaligned (too flat, too practical, distracted, late) and left that way.
3. **The attention-slip pass:** a 1–2 sentence break into something private or irrelevant mid-action, with no later justification.
4. **Under-write / over-write imbalance:** some sentences deliberately thinner or clumsier, some slightly too ornate, and stop.
5. **The do-not-close-the-circuit rule:** moments of pain, shock or realization never end on resolution, understanding or a clean image.

**The brief must say:** these are NOT polish instructions. Success means the reader feels slight unease or unpredictability. Some insertions will fail; keep the ones that work.
