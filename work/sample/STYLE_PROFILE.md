# Style profile: Patrick's voice (for the sample polish pass)

> 🌊 Claude's working notes, not canon. This profile guides the polish of `SAMPLE_BOOK1_FINAL.md` and is built only from Patrick's own words: turn-cited quotes in `codex_v5/` (mostly 13, 08, 09), the "Patrick's voice" section of `notes/H_prose_scenes.md`, and every "Patrick:" line in `decisions/BANK.md`. Claude-drafted text is left out (see §0).

## 0. How much evidence there is (read this first)

**Not much, and almost none of it is finished prose.**

- **Patrick's scene narration**, meaning text that tells events in sequence as a scene would, comes to roughly **800–1,200 words in total**, spread over about ten passages. The longest are the queen vs the apprentice (t768, ~250 words, and the codex paraphrases part of it), the canon duel beats (t82–86, ~200 words), the gnome's look back (t292–294), the changeling's barrage (t774), the face-grab (t202, t762), the ship master (t442) and the window ending (t742). Even these are **scene descriptions he typed into a chat**, not drafted pages: they mix tenses, run on, and slip into planning talk ("I'm picturing…", "we see…").
- **Dialogue lines he wrote:** about 8 (Derrek's "Proud?" speech, "Would you believe how serious the stakes…", the gnome warning, "just enjoy the show old man", the confession's last clause, "this time it seems more full", and a few more).
- **Everything else** (most of BANK, most quotes in 08 and 09) is Patrick **describing** his story in chat: design talk, rough, full of typos. That shows his natural voice, his instincts and his tastes, but not how he would set down a finished sentence.
- **Authorship caution.** Patrick says the master's sacrifice and the entropy-breath scenes are *his ideas* (BANK 11-01), but the written passages in 13 (the oath "do you swear this room will not allow any energy to leave?", the ladder of deaths) come from older write-ups whose wording may be Claude's. BANK 11-01 leaves this open. Don't treat their phrasing as voice evidence. Also excluded as Claude's: the -4 laugh draft, the spectator-window wording, the "dry, cold" giant register (t99), all of SCENES Part Two, and Claude's writing notes on the queen scene.

**VOICE vs MECHANICS.** Copy his **voice**: what he wants a scene to feel like, his rhythm, his images, his habit of landing hard. **Do not copy his surface.** "noone", "your/you're", "to/too", "remnent", "tendered" for "tended", missing apostrophes, comma splices, run-ons and tense drift are artifacts of phone typing in chat. His own production plan (t246–255) is: he writes, Claude cleans the mechanics, an editor polishes. A polish pass should read like Patrick on a good day with a good editor, never like his chat transcript.

## 1. Sentence rhythm and length

- **Long run, then a short drop.** He piles action into one breathless chain joined by "and", "as" and "then", and then stops dead on a very short sentence. "…he burns it to attack Derrek. It fails." (t378). "No one really heard him. He sighs." (t768). "He had time." (t292). This is his clearest structural habit.
- **Fragments for weight.** "The first thing he sees is absolute devastation. Complete ruin." (t294). "Not exactly. A lot messier. More grotesque." (t518). Short noun-phrase sentences come in a cluster.
- **Litany, with a personal last item.** "Giants and dragons, elves and humans. Dwarves leading orcs, and fellow gnomes" then "his brother" (t774). A rhythmic list for scale, then the one name that hurts.
- **Capital letters for the turn.** "THEN out of the ruin he sees the tower emerge" (t294); "FEEL" (jul08 t127); "UNTIL a new foe loses" (t136). In finished prose, carry that turn with sentence structure (a new paragraph, a short line), not caps or italics.
- **Tense:** his drafts drift between past and present. That is mechanics, so pick one and hold it.

## 2. Diction

- **Plain and conversational, with bursts of gleeful excess.** Mostly ordinary words: "a mean man", "put down", "brushes her aside", "a quick horse ride". Then, in action, he cranks the volume all the way up: "his most fearsome soul-crushing roar, the one he reserves for truly worthy opponents" (t82–86); "the overdrive hyper dragon burst beam death wave cannon" (t442). The excess is deliberate fun, usually played for comedy or awe, not purple description.
- **Words he reaches for:** *absolute, truly, ultimate, maximum, literally, casually, cold, cruel, useless, worthy, sigh(s), brush(es) aside / brushed off, sacrifice, pawn, quiet, peaceful, warm embrace.*
- **Physics words used as verbs of action** ("lays a pure em field", "weak force", "fissile"), but in chat only. On the page, the craft law turns these into acts and sensations (13 l.82; scaffolding rule).
- **Not ornate.** No lush metaphor stacks, no archaic diction, no lyrical interior monologue. Feeling arrives through a body (yawning, kneeling, eyes) or a short plain line.

## 3. How he writes the hard things

**Violence.** Physical, fast, and **careless in a way that's meant to horrify**: "hand killed without mercy… absolute grotesque cold-blooded and careless violence. Like a mean man would put down an unruly dog" (t202). Bodies fail concretely: "feels her bones breaking under the stress of blocking" (t768), "bursts into fire from inside… reduced to cinder with a long scream" (t768), "radiation dissolving flesh from bone" (t82–86). What makes a kill land is "the intensity and coldness… How useless the killing was. How cruel were their acts" (t708), not the gore count. Cost and character, never titillation (13 grimdark).

**Dark humor.** Comes from **deadpan contempt in the middle of carnage**. Derrek's put-down to a dying man, then: "Another of his amazing one-liners ruined by idiots" (t768). The narration briefly shares the villain's vanity. Also slapstick bluster ("scream about incompetence and doing it yourself as he straps himself in despite his assistant's complaint", t442) and the gnome's "inappropriate jokes at the wrong moments to cut the tension" (t762). His rule: "levity and humor but they can be dark jokes and they can be punctuated by more violence" (jul08 t24).

**Jon.** Says charming things, does cruel things, in the **same moment** and with no change of tone: "him comforting the boy as he casually does something brutal" (t748). Warm on autopilot: "the warm but quick to switch off. Real attachment that always has a value" (BANK 11-01). He deflects rather than answers: "just enjoy the show old man" (BANK 11-01). Readers "see him feel a lot… Only we know he doesn't feel anything" (t744). Early on his power is modest: "a believable older wizard act not a crazy mind bending late game power" (BANK 11-08). Behavior only, never a clinical label.

**Derrek.** Bored, put-upon, and terrifying through **indifference**: "yawning and resting his head on his hand" (t768); "sighing about the waste" (t342); brushes attacks "aside with a jeer" (t388). His son "may as well be drywall" (BANK 11-01). He speaks in clipped, sardonic rhetorical questions ("Proud? Proud of what exactly?", "Would you believe how serious the stakes here are if I didn't?", t738). Presence as weather: his power "palpably felt pouring off him [like] thick smoke" (jul08 t83).

**Death.** Big deaths land on a **quiet, plain final beat**: "For the first time in as long as he can remember it was quiet. He found it peaceful" (t774); the dying giant "slowly loses himself to its warm embrace" (t82–86). Deaths often mean nothing to the killer and everything to someone: "it doesn't matter in the grand scale but everything matters to someone" (t466). No redemption, no comfort: "They all die. There can be nothing left to cheer for" (t380).

## 4. Images he reaches for

- **Dogs and putting down:** "put down an unruly dog" (t202); "a girl and her dog… a lot messier" (t518).
- **Smoke, fire and light from inside a body:** the apprentice "begins to shine… turns the shine into fire" (t768); Derrek's power as thick smoke (jul08 t83).
- **Ruin, then the monument rising out of it:** mushroom cloud first, "THEN… the tower emerge… the giant pearly tower sticking into the clouds" (t292–294).
- **Aging to dust:** "ages ten thousand years in minutes… turns to dust" (t774); the god who "was always flesh" (entropy idea).
- **Theater and chess:** "basically performing a play… Rehearsed lines" (t736); pawn, bishop, queen (BANK 11-07).
- **Gardens:** "tendered the garden so the conditions were right" (t688). Patrick has since retired "gardening" for Jon's role with the elves (BANK 11-01), so don't use it there.
- **Eyes as the last thing:** "a sickened look", "his brother's hate-filled eyes" (t774); the changeling eye tell (jul08 t289).
- **The window and the two men:** "It's only 2 men at a window" (t458).

## 5. What he never does (his stated rules)

- Never puts **numbers or force-type labels** on the page ("never write 'my fire 6'", 13 scaffolding; t0–5, t72); no character has a "type" (t612).
- Never writes a **fairy tale**: "Your story reads like a kids fairy tale" (jul08 t223); a dragon fight must be "menacing and a true battle not some silly wizard in the woods."
- Never uses **exposition dumps** or "weird ham fisted" trait-telling (jul08 t223); never info-dumps at the end: "I would insult people by info dumping on the last page" (BANK 11-01).
- Never **spells things out**: "stuff that just feels way to spelled out" (BANK 11-07); "Just tell the reader there's a district… instead of stating it" (jul08 t225).
- **No AI-isms**; plain, standard writing (BANK 11-07).
- Never writes **orcs as animals**: the world treats them as cattle, but the narration keeps them people, "somewhat sympathetic" (BANK 11-07). Never writes beings as unfeeling objects ("he hit the fence and he didn't act like a being with feelings", BANK 11-07, paraphrased by Patrick).
- **No corny goodness**: a sympathetic monster stays "a murderous hungry user of other people" (t338). No forgiveness (t558).
- **No rescue by the narrative**: an intervention is "the narrative saving a character as a plot point" (jul23 t126).
- **No video-game sensing** (t223, t225): reading souls is rare and master-level.
- **No clinical labels**; madness is subtext (W44, W70; t361).
- **The son is not a boy** on the page: "It's not a child. He's a man" (t752).
- **No invented names** (t630).

## 6. Scene-construction instincts

1. **Tenderness is the setup, violence the punchline.** "A very kind moment between a father and daughter just before the daughter is eaten" (jul08 t24). The warmth must be real enough to hurt, not a death flag (13 grimdark).
2. **Contrast and simultaneity.** Small funny human moments while millions die offstage. Comedy and atrocity in the same beat (t768).
3. **Escalate, then collapse.** A fighter pushes past every limit, the villain is bored, and the effort comes to nothing (t768, t774, t342). The noise ends in one quiet line.
4. **Order the shots.** He thinks in camera order: devastation first, then the tower (t294). Readers should see "both happen at once so it isn't confusing" (t296).
5. **Reveals land suddenly, never telegraphed** (jul08 t227). Misdirect the reader along with the mark (jul08 t127).
6. **Honor between killers.** The roar "he reserves for truly worthy opponents"; the changeling attacks because he "sees Derrek as a truly worthy foe" (t378).
7. **Menace through politeness.** "nice warm sunny weather for a quick horse ride out of the city" (t282–296). Understatement carries the threat.
8. **Leave the big question unanswered.** Book 1's window is deliberately opaque; the son asks "a barrage of unanswered questions" (BANK 11-01).
9. **The hero wins by voice, not by force.** Eli's final "attack" is emotional, "to sway opinion not win the battle" (BANK 11-08; t371).
10. **Keep openings tight on motion:** "focusing on the run from the city", not world exposition (BANK 11-07).

## 8. Do / don't for a writer imitating him

**Do**
- Run the action in one long, driving sentence, then cut it off with a short flat one.
- Land every big sequence on a small, plain, emotional line.
- Let villains be bored, sighing and sardonic, and let Derrek's contempt show in his body (yawn, sigh, brushing aside).
- Put the kind moment right before the cruelty, and make the warmth real.
- Play a dark joke inside the violence, then follow it with more violence.
- Write Jon kind and brutal in the same breath, without changing tone.
- Show acts and sensations: heat, light, bones, smoke, skin, eyes.
- Use plain words, and save the over-the-top phrasing for moments meant to be fun or awesome.
- Use lists for scale and end them on the one item that is personal.
- Order the images as a camera would see them.
- Leave the big questions open, and trust the reader.
- Write the orcs and the son as people. The son is a capable man.

**Don't**
- Copy his chat spelling, punctuation, run-ons or tense drift. Clean all of it.
- Explain magic, name forces, give numbers, or narrate "sensing" as a readout.
- Over-explain motives, summarise a feeling after showing it, or end a paragraph on a moral.
- Use AI-isms: "for a moment", stacked "the way…" similes, "Not X. Y." turns, emphatic italics, "the hardest thing he had ever done" superlatives (EDIT_NOTES §2, per Patrick's 11-07 ask).
- Go lyrical or ornate. He is plain until he is loud.
- Soften anyone into goodness, redeem anyone, or let the narrative rescue them.
- Turn Jon into a power fantasy early on, or Derrek into a ranting villain.
- Use clinical labels, invented names, or "boy" for the son outside the gods' view.
- Put new prose in Patrick's mouth as canon. Every line of the real book is his (t257). This profile only makes a test sample sound closer to him.
