## Book 1 canon checklist (bank wins where it differs from older text)

**Opening**
- At the edge of a human city, a young man flees a guard, and the reader wonders what he stole.
- He's the faceless Emperor's runaway son. Nobody, including him, knows the Emperor is Derrek.
- He's an **adult**, capable and powerful, raised in the seat of power. The book is his rise.
- He's a pawn. Use "Eli" (Patrick's own test name) or keep him unnamed; codex says his name comes last.

**Jon**
- Scoops him up. Eccentric and charming, comes and goes.
- His warmth is autopilot: warm, but quick to switch off. His attachments are real, but each one always carries a value. He needs to see himself as good while knowing he isn't. Behavior only; never a clinical label.
- He's an outsider who helped Mika as a boy and keeps some favor with him. He is NOT the elves' god or master. Mika fully controls the elves.
- Jon doesn't care about orcs.

**The crew** (the son gathers them; the cruel irony is that he's the recruiter delivering Jon's sacrifices to the Tower)
- his master
- Jon
- a dark-elf woman, at the end of a long escape, who becomes his love
- a friend who fled the palace with him
- an elven warrior queen Jon chose as his rival
- a cheerful gnome who is secretly an ancient changeling. He was the village healer who took the souls of the sick slowly, one or two at a time, and told families "I wasn't able to save your loved one." Never show a soul transfer anyone could witness.

**Along the way**
- An elven city where Jon lingers a moment too long at Lirael's statue.
- The last friendly giant.

**The spark**
- A dragon destroys a giant forge world. Derrek engineered the strike. Open war follows, and the road to the Tower runs through a giant space war.

**Time travel is only hinted, never shown**
- Rifts are ground sites (not the sky) where Remnant leaks through, empires are forged, war never stops and the powerful gather. Each is a scar from a reset.
- Elves grieve people no one can name.
- Derrek knows impossible things.
- Jon has decided he won't reset anymore. Nobody rewinds anything on the page.
- Maybe: in the Tower, a cage holding a rose missing a petal (Lirael's gathered soul). Jon: "this time it seems more full." Someone: "This time? You've been here before?" Jon: "Not for a long time." (Patrick's own lines; it's a "maybe" beat.)

**The Tower**
- It's the emperor-giant's converged city: one centerless, faintly conscious mass of trapped souls. It's the largest amplifier on Earth, which is why everyone in the Tower sequence is at their absolute peak, why Derrek rarely leaves, and why he tore the dimensions open there.
- Jon chose the team to **wear Derrek down and drain him, not kill him**. Derrek can't die: when he dies, the keeper instantly resets him to where he died. Jon and Derrek long ago stopped trying to kill each other because it's pointless. A true god would just cause an endless death-loop.

**The Tower sequence, in order**
1. A rule-beast that Derrek trapped (by rehearsing their encounter thousands of times) waits in its Void. The master blows himself up / becomes a star or black hole to punish the beast inside its own game. He uses only his own body, since nothing leaves the room. His death is burnout (the body destroyed); Remnant is never spent.
2. Derrek kills the friend with casual brutality as they enter the throne room (a face-grab).
3. The queen dies dueling Derrek's unloved apprentice. The apprentice taps the Tower's soul-mass as an amplifier and burns out from within while Derrek yawns.
4. The dark elf strikes with Lirael's own signature move (her Collapse). Derrek turns it inside out on her in front of Jon.
5. The changeling hurls every stolen soul at Derrek, his murdered brother last, fails, and crumbles to dust in the first quiet he has ever known.
6. Jon, cold now in his true form, defeats Derrek: drains and restrains him.
7. **Everyone dies.**

**The window**
- Derrek is restrained, not dying.
- He barely notices his son ("drywall") and speaks only to Jon: he reminds them who their master is, and says Jon knows what he must do.
- Jon sighs and brushes it aside: **"just enjoy the show, old man."**
- The son is in the background, confused, asking questions nobody answers.
- The two men share their last moment of peace before the real battle begins, as the Blight spreads from the rifts.
- Jon never answers whether he'll reset; it's a deflection. No plea. No time travel shown. No info dump on the last page.
- The Lirael line ("at least I'll get to see her again") is SCRAPPED.
- Book 1 is deliberately ambiguous.

**World**
- Magic:
  - three dials (Remnant, control, inner sense) plus fatigue
  - no types and no force words in narration
  - Remnant is never spent; held souls are amplifiers
  - soul-sensing is rare, except through the elven bond, which scales with Remnant and distance (the homeworld is a beacon; a lone far-off elf is a pinprick)
- Technology:
  - plain machines run on electricity
  - complex AI machines have flesh soul-cores holding pool-Remnant mined from wells
  - grown towers and hulls are Convergence
  - flesh weapons, amplifier rigs, clone organs
  - machines can't sense souls
  - the public thinks Remnant is a material and a "battery", and doesn't know it's souls
- Orcs: chipped at birth ("care"), with a kill switch and a puppet function, and bought by the trillion as ship material.
- Elves:
  - authoritarian, under Mika
  - the Inquisition secretly sells dark elves through dwarven traffickers
  - hell is the elven prison, and humans route FTL through it
  - elves cross by shrinking a dimension, and the bond is cut in transit
- Changelings are an obscure, inevitable threat nobody guards against.
- "Dragon" is an umbrella: great winged lords rule erased reptilian kin, and humans may ride captured lesser dragon-kin.
- Trolls are small (bridge-troll sized).
- Any being can become a god with enough time.
- Demons and gods are earned names.
- The Blight (a placeholder name) means the enemies of our reality pressing on the barrier.

**Tone**
- Grimdark: brutality as a forge, cost and characterize, never titillate.
- Dark levity is welcome; corny goodness is not; no redemption.
- Jon says kind things while doing cruel ones, and the narrator never confirms it.

## Format for the sample
A condensed Book 1 in 7–9 chapters (about 10,000–14,000 words): the opening, recruiting the crew, the statue, the escape and the love, the forge world and the war, the Tower sequence, and the window. Use summary bridges ("Over the next months…") between scenes so the whole arc fits. Use placeholder names marked as such, or roles.
