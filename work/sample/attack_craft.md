# Attack #2: craft and tone (draft_v1)

> 🌊 Claude adversarial review, not canon. Targets `work/sample/draft_v1.md` against `_BRIEF.md` (Tone + the four known problems) and `13_PROSE_AND_SCENES` craft laws. The fixes are beats only, never prose (`12_METHOD`, t257).

**Verdict:** The skeleton is right and some beats hit hard: "Breathe. You're doing beautifully" followed by every orc dropping, the weeping elf who doesn't know who she grieves, "Me.", and the apprentice's "Master, are you proud?" But chapters 1 to 5 read as **whimsical found-family adventure with blood on top**, which is Patrick's "too light" problem coming back. The Tower gets undercut by a narrator who survives the ending, explains Jon, and stacks loop hints until they stop being hints.

Ranked from most to least damaging.

---

## 1. The narrator survives the ending: "everyone dies" breaks, and every death blurs
**Quotes:**
- "Eli would try **for the rest of his life** to describe how." (l.721)
- "What happened next, Eli would never be able to tell anyone, and **in the end he would stop trying**." (l.787)
- "**Afterwards**, Eli would never be able to put it in order." (l.651)
- "He would never write it down anywhere" (l.331). Also "That was what Eli would remember" (l.163, l.679), "would never forget his face" (l.685), "He would hold on to that" (l.715), "He'd never be sure, afterwards" (l.769).

**Why it hurts:** A retrospective "would remember" voice promises the reader that **Eli lives to look back**. That breaks "Everyone dies… nothing left to cheer for" (t380) and the Book 1 ending (the universe collapsing, W53). It also takes the dread out of the window, because we already know he gets an "afterwards." It is also the main reason the deaths blur: every one gets the same "Eli would remember" tag, so they all land with the same thud.

**Fix (beats):**
- Ban future-perfect memory tags from ch 7 onward. Stay in the present moment: no "afterwards", "for the rest of his life" or "would never."
- Let what Eli fixates on carry the weight in real time (her silver eyes find him, and he can't look away) instead of a narrator promising he'll keep it.
- If one memory tag must survive, it goes in ch 1–2 only (the clinic mother), where it can't promise survival past the Tower.

## 2. The narrator confirms Jon. The iceberg is visible from orbit.
**Quotes:**
- "it was a wonderful smile, and **it was gone before he'd finished turning away**." (l.89)
- "His expression was perfectly pleasant. **There was nothing in it at all.**" (l.241)
- "The warmth had gone out of it… what was underneath was **smooth and cold and patient and very, very old**… the way a man looks at **a page he's read before**." (l.721)
- "**almost longing**, as if he were watching someone step off a cliff and **envying them the fall**." (l.769)
- "The queen looked at him with something… Later he thought it might have been **recognition**." (l.469)
- "There. **Was that so hard?**" (l.63); "Shame. **Pass the salt?**" (l.467)

**Why it hurts:** The rule is that Jon SAYS charming things and DOES cruel ones, and "only we know he doesn't feel anything" (t744, t746–749). The brief says outright that the narrator never confirms it. Here the narrator interprets him at every turn: the switch-off, the emptiness, the age, the envy, and a second character "recognizing" it. "Pass the salt?" is a cartoon-psychopath beat, the kind where he's twirling a mustache. It is cruelty performed for the reader, not cruelty done in passing. "Was that so hard?" makes the manipulation explicit.

**Fix (beats):**
- Describe only what's visible: what he does with his hands, where he looks, the timing. Cut every interpretive clause after the comma ("nothing in it", "very old", "page he's read before", "envying").
- Statue: keep the duration and the old woman glancing up. Cut "There was nothing in it at all." The reader should feel the pause run too long, without being told what it means.
- Dead orcs: Jon goes to look, then comforts *Teo*, warmly and well, and in the same breath books the next leg of the trip on the orcs' freighter. Kind words, cold act, no comment. Cut "Pass the salt" and cut the queen's "recognition."
- First kill: after the guard dies, Jon says something genuinely warm about Eli's shaking hand. No "Was that so hard."
- True form in the Tower: show it through behavior only. He stops making jokes. He stops looking at Eli. He takes a step at the exact moment the dark elf dies, as if he had been waiting for it.

## 3. The tone: whimsy with blood on it (the "too light" problem is back)
**Quotes (a sample of the quip density):**
- "Terrible design. **Somebody should write a letter.**" / "Eli could **hear the peach**." / "'elves,' in the tone other men used for '**the dentist**'" / "**Don't be late, it's rude.**" / "**Fish thing.** I promised." (twice) / "a bag of **oranges** and a black eye" / "making **terrible breakfasts**" / "mending his coat… with an **enormous needle**" / "slept **like a child**."
- Sitcom beats: Teo shoots the dwarf, then "**Sorry! Sorry!**" (l.289); Doc: "**He's thinking *oh***" (l.287); the troll laughing "until it had to sit down" (l.183); "frankly **a little showy**" (l.311).
- Teo: "the conviction that this was **the best idea anyone had ever had**" (l.71), a YA sidekick.
- Brutality made pretty: "She met him and **it was beautiful**" (l.665); "She fell in two pieces. **She was still smiling.**" (l.683).
- A fairy-tale "and… and… and" cadence: "He told terrible jokes and good ones, and seemed to know…, and slept like a child." "Bridges like spun sugar."

**Why it hurts:** Patrick's own notes say "Your story reads like a kids fairy tale" (jul08 t223). Dark levity means "dark jokes punctuated by more violence" (jul08 t24), but most of these jokes are cozy, not dark, and violence rarely follows them. Grimdark "warmth must be real enough to hurt, not an obvious death-flag" (13 grimdark register). Here the warmth is cute, and cute doesn't hurt. Making the queen's death beautiful ("it was beautiful", "still smiling") edges toward titillation and away from cost.

**Fix (beats):**
- Cut Jon's quips by about two-thirds. Keep **one** signature habit (the food) and drop the rest: no oranges, black eye, breakfasts or coat-mending.
- Every joke that stays has to be **followed by** violence or **made at the expense of** someone suffering (Doc's "Are you dying? Pity. I'm very good at it" is the model; it works).
- Give Teo weight instead of whimsy. He's an adult who gave up something real (rank, family, a future) to forge that code, and he knows what it cost. Then his death costs something.
- Cut "Sorry! Sorry!", "He's thinking *oh*", "a little showy" and the troll's laughing fit.
- Replace "it was beautiful" with what the duel *costs*: sound, breath, the floor. The queen doesn't smile while her arms break. Let her silence be the dignity.
- Add the grime chapters 1–5 are missing: hunger, the smell of the hull-press yards, a crew member doing something ugly to survive. Brutality as a forge, not just as set dressing.

## 4. The time-travel hints are stacked, loud, and the window gives away its own answer
**Quotes:**
- "**This is the last time I do this**, you know. I'm getting too old for it." (l.487). That is nearly the bank ruling (Jon won't reset) said out loud.
- "a page he's read before" (l.721); "Did you like it? **I know how you love that one**." (l.725)
- Derrek speaking Teo's line "in exactly Teo's rhythm, word for word" (l.633).
- "Doesn't time fly." (l.397); the giant: "Whatever you are." (l.401); "You're late." / "You're old." (l.625–627)
- At the window, Eli sees the Blight as "wrong, the way **a word you have said too many times** becomes wrong" (l.815).
- The window's last lines: "quiet, at peace, and **waited for it to arrive**." (l.843)

**Why it hurts:** "Hinted, never shown" (BRIEF) needs scarcity. Chapters 6 to 9 hold at least eight loop winks plus Patrick's rose beat, which pushes it from a hint toward a reveal. Worse, "last time I do this" plus "waited for it to arrive" **answers the question** of whether Jon resets: no. W50 says Jon never answers and the reader decides. The narrator's "at peace, and waited" tips the scale.

**Fix (beats):**
- Keep **four**: the weeping elf, the rift, the rose (Patrick's lines), and Derrek's foreknowledge in **one** form.
- For Derrek's foreknowledge, choose either the unison line or "I had it framed," not both. The unison works better if it's flat and unremarked: he says it, and it lands.
- Cut "This is the last time I do this," "page he's read before," "I know how you love that one," "Doesn't time fly," and "a word you have said too many times."
- End the window on an image that can be read both ways: the two men watching, the black spreading, Eli's last unanswered question. Cut "at peace" and "waited for it to arrive."

## 5. The central irony is gone: Eli doesn't recruit the crew
**Quotes:**
- Doc: "**Teo found one**" (l.147).
- The dark elf: "**Jon gave them a set of coordinates**" (l.251).
- The queen: "I asked **Mika** for a favour" (l.351), with Eli just "the human my king has instructed me to accompany."
- Only the master is recruited by Eli.

**Why it hurts:** The brief's "cruel irony" is that **the son is the recruiter delivering Jon's sacrifices to the Tower**. In this draft Teo, Jon and Mika deliver them, so Eli is a passenger, which also weakens problem 1 (he should read as a capable adult with agency). In the Tower the irony should cut like a knife ("I brought every one of them here"), and the draft never earns it.

**Fix (beats):**
- Doc: Eli picks him, against Teo's unease and the giant's later look. He buys the contract himself and argues for it.
- The dark elf: Jon only mentions the date at dinner, offhand ("funny place, that crater, on the ninth"). Eli decides to go, and Eli makes the call to go down the crater wall.
- The queen: Eli's riverbed negotiation is what makes her king send her (Eli's win, Jon's favor in the background). Or Eli asks for "the best you have" without knowing what that means.
- In the Tower, after the master dies, give Eli one beat of arithmetic. He brought them; he counts them.

## 6. The son's adulthood slips at key moments
**Quotes:**
- "'When will you be back?' said Eli, **and hated how it sounded**." (l.87), the needy kid.
- The window: "Who *are* you?… You're my father, why did you— why did any of this— ***answer me!***" (l.837), a teen tantrum with breaking voice.
- "Eli heard someone screaming, and it took him a long time to understand it was himself." (l.645), a cliché that also makes him a child at the threshold.
- In the Tower he is held back by the dark elf, flicked away with a wave, and "dragged himself… Nobody helped him."

**Why it hurts:** He is "a man of equal resolve as either of the two gods" (t754), charming and capable, and "boy" is only how the gods see him (t752). The bank's "drywall" works only if a *formidable* man is treated as drywall; if he's a sobbing kid, Derrek's indifference is earned and stops being an insult.

**Fix (beats):**
- At the fuel stop he asks something practical and cold, like "What do you want from me?" No "hated how it sounded."
- Teo's death: Eli acts at once (he reaches, and the room stops him) instead of screaming for a paragraph.
- Window questions should be few, precise and adult: a prince interrogating. Something like four short questions, each answered by silence, ending on the one about Teo. Cut "Who are you?" and "answer me!"
- Keep his charge and his defeat (canon W56), but let his reach do *something* first (a crack in the dais, or Derrek's hair moving) so the flick-away is a gap between gods and men, not a gap between a man and a child.

## 7. The Tower deaths blur: the same image, the same shape, the same tics
**Quotes:**
- The master: "the skin drawing **toward a point somewhere just in front of his breastbone**" (l.579).
- The dark elf: "her body drawn in **toward a point just behind her heart**" (l.715).
- "first time" appears 8 times: "first time Eli had ever seen him look young," "first time… her smile," "first thing she had ever said… that wasn't an insult," "for the first time, get up," "for the first time… looked old," "For the first time… quiet."
- Some form of "smile" appears 18 times; the queen smiles three times while dying.

**Why it hurts:** **Two deaths share one image.** The Collapse is Lirael's signature and Jon's wound (t616); it has to be the only death that looks like that. And every death runs the same three beats: a peak feat, Derrek's indifference, then Eli's memory tag. Five deaths with one rhythm turn into a single, longer death.

**Fix (beats):**
- **The master: sight only.** Show the pane darkening from the center and never the body folding. His death is about *absence*: you never see him go, only the light stop reaching you.
- **Teo: sound only.** One wet sound and it's over. He gets no last line.
- **The queen: duration and body.** It's the longest, most physical death, with no smile.
- **The dark elf: eye contact and the image.** She is the only one who folds toward a point, and it's quick.
- **Doc: noise, then silence.** The loudest death ends in the first quiet.
- Kill all eight "first time"s except Doc's quiet, which is canon (t774).
- Vary the length: Teo in two lines, the queen in a page, the dark elf in half a page.

## 8. The changeling is telegraphed, then explained
**Quotes:**
- "He said it the way a man admires **a well-stocked larder**." (l.209)
- "Pity. **I'm very good at it.**" (l.171); "I saw it come up" (l.389) plus "Doesn't time fly" (l.397); the giant's "Whatever you are" (l.401).
- In the Tower: "Eli understood, **finally**… what Doc was, and what had happened behind the curtain in the clinic," then the italic echo "*I wasn't able to save your loved one.*" (l.741–743)

**Why it hurts:** "Reveals arrive suddenly, not telegraphed," and "no exposition" (jul08 t227, t223). The larder line gives the twist away in ch 3, so by the giant's hall even a casual reader knows. Then the Tower spells it out twice: "understood, finally" plus the italic echo.

**Fix (beats):**
- Cut the larder line and "Whatever you are." Keep "I saw it come up," since that's canon and the most charming possible cover. Have the queen dismiss it as an old gnome's joke.
- In the Tower, keep only the italic echo (the reader connects it) and cut "Eli understood, finally, what Doc was."
- Optional: plant one *warm* clinic detail that pays off in the barrage, such as the goat-story boy's face among the gnome souls. Let the reader find it. Don't name it.

## 9. The love story is a meet-cute plus a setup for the Collapse, not a love
**Quotes:**
- "***Oh***, thought Eli, and was immediately ashamed of thinking it, and thought it again anyway." plus Doc narrating it (l.285–287). That's a rom-com.
- Their only real scene exists to load the Collapse: "**Lirael's Collapse. I spent forty years learning it.**" (l.323)
- The death echoes it word for word: "**Forty years in the dark learning it. The one thing they'd given her that she'd kept.**" (l.705)
- The relationship otherwise lives in summary: "It took her four months… five to let Eli sit on the same side…"

**Why it hurts:** In condensed form the love needs *one* scene where it costs both of them something. The draft's single scene is mostly there to set up the death, and the verbatim echo shows that. "*Oh*" is canon (t398), but Doc's commentary makes it a sitcom beat.

**Fix (beats):**
- Keep the "*oh*," cut Doc's line, and let her answer the look with contempt.
- Promote the hell transit (the broken fingers, currently a single sentence) to the love scene. In the bond-cut dark of hell she holds on. He lets her break his fingers and says nothing. Afterwards he flexes the splinted hand at her once. That's the whole scene, and it's the moment she tells him her name.
- Move the Lirael lore to a different, *colder* moment. She teaches the Collapse to no one, or Eli watches her practice it alone, and Jon's "Good for you" lands there.
- In the Tower, cut the narrated echo ("Forty years in the dark…"). Just show her fingers curl like the statue's. The reader remembers.

## 10. Derrek is a showman, not careless
**Quotes:**
- The unison trick plus "**It was a very good forgery. I had it framed.**" (l.633); "**Now we've all been introduced.**" (l.641)
- "'Jon,' said the man in the chair. '**You're late.**' / '**Derrek,' said Jon warmly. 'You're old.**'" (l.625–627)
- The narrator quoting the codex: "He'd done it **the way a mean man puts down a dog** that won't stop barking." (l.643)

**Why it hurts:** The face-grab should be "careless violence… like a mean man would put down an unruly dog" (t202). Careless means *unperformed*, and here Derrek performs: a trick, a punchline, a sign-off. The buddy-movie banter with Jon makes the reunion cute. The dog simile is the author's note leaking onto the page; show it, don't name it.

**Fix (beats):**
- Derrek doesn't let Teo finish and doesn't stand up. He keeps talking to Jon *while* he closes his hand. Teo dies in the middle of Derrek's sentence to someone else.
- Cut "I had it framed" and "Now we've all been introduced," and cut the dog simile.
- Reunion: one line, not a matched pair. Either Derrek says something to Jon with no greeting, or Jon greets him and Derrek doesn't answer.

## 11. Exposition dumps, said twice
**Quotes:**
- Ch 4 travel-guide paragraph: "Human freighters dropped through near there, into the prison realm… The elves crossed their own way. They squeezed down one of the world's dimensions…" (l.257)
- The giant: "It's not a building… It's a city that converged… **greatest store of power on this earth**… **Which is why your father so rarely leaves home.**" (l.387)
- Then Doc in the Tower: "All those people in the walls… **Everyone's going to be at their very best in here.**" (l.523)
- The archivist's dark-elf lecture (l.217).

**Why it hurts:** "Traits without exposition… not blank exposition or weird ham fisted" (jul08 t223); "show it, don't state it" (jul08 t225). The amplifier mechanism is stated once by the giant and then again by Doc. It's a codex note recited in dialogue.

**Fix (beats):**
- Hell routing: show it once. A human freighter blinks out over the crater while the dark elf flinches. Elven crossing: Doc's "worse than dying" alone is enough.
- The giant gives the history (canon t334) but *not* the mechanism. Cut "store of power" and "rarely leaves home"; one line about souls still being in the walls is enough.
- In the Tower, *show* the amplification (the threads in Teo's sleeve is good) and cut Doc's explanation.
- Archivist: two lines, not a paragraph.

## 12. The window over-explains the rifts and dresses the peace in sentiment
**Quotes:**
- "an old wound, **the oldest rift of all**, black and glassy, at the city's heart. It was spreading… **Other rifts. All of them spreading.**… the ships… started, one by one, to stop fighting and turn toward it." (l.815)
- "close **as old friends on a bench**" (l.811); "the way you'd lean on someone on a long train journey. Jon let him." (l.841); "He looked, for a moment, **very young**." (l.829)
- "**Outside, the world went on ending.**" (l.843)

**Why it hurts:** "No info dump on the last page." The rift paragraph is the longest block in the chapter and walks through the mechanism. Two train similes in one scene (l.619 "waiting for a late train" too) is three trains. "The world went on ending" tells us the outcome instead of leaving it open.

**Fix (beats):**
- Cut the rift block to three images: black in the streets, more black on the horizon, ships turning. Cut "oldest rift of all."
- Keep *one* gesture of intimacy, either the shoulder lean or the bench, not both. Cut the train similes and "very young."
- Cut "the world went on ending." End on an unanswered question, or on the black reaching the Tower's base.

## 13. Point-of-view leaks inside the Tower
**Quotes:**
- "Nobody had heard a word of it. Derrek sighed. **Another of his amazing one-liners, ruined by idiots.**" (l.695), which is inside Derrek's head in Eli's close third.
- "**He found it peaceful.**" (l.763), inside Doc.

**Why it hurts:** Both are Patrick's lines (t768, t774), so they stay. But in strict close third on Eli they are head-hops, and that's Patrick's call, not the draft's. As written they read as slips, not choices.

**Fix (beats):**
- Flag both to Patrick. Option a: make them observable (Derrek glances around the room, irritated that nobody heard; Doc's face goes slack and easy). Option b: own them as deliberate wry ruptures and make the narration commit to that voice elsewhere too.

## 14. Clichés, tics and purple prose
- "paper" 9 times ("wet paper", "neat as paper", "paper boats", "paper in a stove", "ink through paper"). "weather" 6 times ("came and went like weather", "like weather" ×3, "had weather in it"). "for a long…" 12 times, "a long time" 9 times.
- Stock phrases: "went through him like cold water", "like surf around a rock", "like a lamp turned down", "the room couldn't hold them", "**It went on for a long time. Or it took no time at all.**" (l.791), "in several places at once."
- The Jon–Derrek fight is skipped with "They fought. That was all he could say." The point of view justifies some of the gap, but the phrasing is a stock way of ducking it.
- "Somewhere very far away… a contract had been signed with a company…" (l.421). Pointed and arch, and the ledger itself flags it.

**Fix (beats):** Reserve one simile family per chapter, and keep "paper" only for the Collapse ("like a book being shut" is good). Give the god fight three concrete images Eli *can* see (the window starring, Derrek standing, Jon with his eyes shut), cut the "long time / no time" line, and stop there. Cut the contract paragraph or reduce it to one object in the dragon's room, with no comment.
