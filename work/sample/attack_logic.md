# Attack: logic, motive, continuity (Adversary #1)

> 🌊 Claude adversarial review of `work/sample/draft_v1.md`. Not canon. Hostile on purpose. Ranked from most to least damaging. Each item: the quote, why it breaks, and what has to change.

---

## 1. Jon's plan fails on the page, and nobody notices
**The bank's spine:** Jon picked the team "enough to wear down derrek but not kill him... Use enough magic to be drained" (BANK 11-01 L4 follow-ups). The draft shows the opposite: Derrek pays nothing for any of them.
- Master: dies in the beast's room before he ever sees Derrek. He drains Derrek by exactly zero.
- Teo: "He didn't get up. He didn't change expression. He simply closed his hand, a little." Costless.
- Queen: she fights the *apprentice*, not Derrek. "Derrek was yawning." Zero.
- Dark elf: "He did something with two fingers, almost lazily, like turning a key." Zero.
- Doc: "Derrek sat in it as if it were weather. He didn't even raise a hand... it made no difference. He was still exactly himself." Zero.
- Eli: "the way you'd wave off a fly." Zero.

Then Jon alone fights him to his knees, and the narration says "He'd done exactly what he said he'd do. He had tired him out." No, he didn't. On this page the crew was decoration and Jon simply won. That makes Jon's choice of team, the whole death march, and the book's central irony (the son delivering "Jon's sacrifices") pointless. Five deaths and not one of them buys anything.

**Fix:** every Tower death has to cost Derrek something we can see. He's still contemptuous, but the effort shows: reversing the Collapse makes him get up or bleed, he has to hold off Doc's barrage for real and it leaves him grey and slow, and the yawn is a show he can't keep up. Jon's fight starts with Derrek already visibly worn down. Derrek's contempt can be a mask over the drain, but the drain has to be real.

## 2. Eli has no reason to go to the Tower
The son ran *away* from the seat of power. Nothing in nine chapters says why he'd march back to it. The draft assumes a goal it never states:
- "I'll come and watch you die somewhere better than this." (the master, ch 2). Die doing *what*?
- "When we get there. To him. What do we do?" (Eli, ch 6). Why are they going "there" at all?
- "He went for the man on the throne. His father. He went for him with his whole heart." With what grievance? Before Teo dies, the Emperor has done nothing to Eli on the page except issue a warrant.

Without a want, Eli is not the "recruiter" (BRIEF Crew) or the hero of "his rise" (t756). He's luggage that Jon steers. And the crew has nothing to follow, which feeds #4.

**Fix:** give Eli a stated, wrong-headed goal early on. Some options: end his father, take the throne, free himself for good (the warrant proves he'll be hunted forever), or answer the "which family?" question Jon dangled. It should be a goal Jon quietly feeds, so the irony of the sacrifices lands.

## 3. Eli doesn't rise. He watches.
Canon (t756, t758): "He defeats dragons and giants in combat. He fights interdimensional monsters... He must grow powerful during the story first." Here is his whole fighting record:
- strangles one city guard (ch 1),
- bends six gun barrels and puts up one air wall (ch 4),
- talks for nine days (ch 5),
- gets swatted (ch 8).

He never fights a dragon, a giant, a boss or a monster. Across seven months of "giant space war" there isn't a single Eli fight. Jon does the fighting: "He fought beside them... he was so much better than any of them." The master solves the Void. The book named "his rise" is a passenger's travelogue.

The dark-elf rescue also misses its canon beat. t758 says "Open a door go inside and save her," and "he only opens the door." In the draft the air splits by itself and she falls out: "a woman fell out of it onto her hands and knees." Eli opens nothing.

**Fix:** at least one party kill of a dragon or a giant during the war (the group-kill rule, `07`), done mostly by Eli. Eli should have to *do* the opening at the weak place. Some Void puzzle should need Eli's raw power rather than the master's touch.

## 4. The crew watches Jon do monstrous things, and nobody's loyalty bends
- Ch 1: Jon kills every orc in the yard by switching them off: "Every orc in the yard fell down." Teo lands a minute later and asks "Did you do all that?"
- Ch 6: Jon parked them 200,000 miles out "where Jon had told them to wait" to watch a forge world die. "You knew." / "I guessed." Then nothing happens. Nobody leaves, nobody fights about it, and the dark elf, who has just been freed from slavers, keeps following him.
- Ch 6: dead orcs in the crates. "Shame. Pass the salt?" The queen gives him a look, and that's the end of it.
- Ch 6: Jon says outright that the Emperor can't be killed and that the "whole plan" is to "tire him out" and then "have a talk." "And then what?" "We'll see." Doc laughed. **Nobody else did**, and every one of them walks into the Tower anyway.

So why do they follow?
- **Master:** "I have wanted to see the inside of that Tower." That's tourism. He distrusts Jon ("I thought he might have"). He's an old pragmatist who has just heard the plan is a talk with an unkillable man, and he still goes.
- **Doc:** the same "wanted to see inside" motive. That's duplicate motivation.
- **Queen:** orders (see #5).
- **Dark elf, Teo:** love and friendship for Eli. That works only if Eli has a goal (#2).

**Fix:** each member needs a personal stake that the Tower serves, and that Jon dangles. A few examples. The master: Derrek's Void killed his own master, or the rift war he's spent his life in is Derrek's doing. The dark elf: Derrek's empire buys the dark elves the Inquisition sells. Doc: hunger, since he senses the greatest soul-hoard on Earth. Each witnessed Jon atrocity needs a beat where someone almost leaves and Jon's autopilot warmth reels them back in. That's also the best place to *show* the warmth "switching off" with a value attached.

## 5. Jon's "small favor" with Mika buys an elf champion to escort the Emperor's son. That's sway.
The BANK says Jon has "some favor but he isnt the actual secret master" (11-01 W44/t550). The draft contradicts itself across two chapters:
- Ch 3: "It doesn't buy much. It buys a day pass."
- Ch 5: "I asked Mika for a favour. He was kind enough to agree. She's the best he has."

A day pass, and then Mika's best warrior assigned to a human's travels? That is exactly the "Jon has sway over the elves" problem the BRIEF told us to avoid.

It gets worse. The elves are "sworn to kill the human Emperor" (t532, and the draft's own ch 6). At the giant's hall the queen learns that Eli is the Emperor's son: "Behind him he heard the queen go very still." Then nothing. An elf champion, under orders from an authoritarian king, finds out she's escorting the heir of her people's sworn enemy and doesn't report, doesn't kill him and doesn't get recalled. Mika "fully controls the elves," and a bonded elf gets close enough to feel her people on any elven world, so Mika would know.

**Fix:** either Mika *wants* this, because pointing the Emperor's son at the Tower serves Mika's oath and the queen is his knife at the son's back (that gives her a real motive, and Jon gets her through Mika's interest, not his own sway), or she joins on her own. Either way her reaction to "whose son you are" has to cost something on the page.

## 6. Jon knows the future, and canon gives that to Derrek
The BANK's hints list "Derrek knows impossible things." It does not give Jon that power. Jon "wakes blank" (BANK 11-01 mechanism; QUESTIONS 2.1). Even so, the draft's Jon:
- knows the exact day a hell escapee will come through: "You'll want to be there on the ninth."
- knows the hour of the forge-world strike and parks the crew at a safe 200,000 miles.
- knows the giant will be safe ("He left last night").
- remembers the rose from before ("this time it seems more full").

The "set it up" line for the dark elf (t610) needs a *how*. Arranging a door is one thing. Knowing a hell-runner's arrival to the day is foreknowledge. With Jon this prescient and Derrek this prescient, the "only hinted" time travel is all over the page, and Jon's version breaks the wakes-blank mechanism the BANK accepted.

**Canon contradiction the draft exposes:** BANK 11-01 accepts "Jon wakes blank because the brain is rewound." The same bank has Jon say of the rose "this time it seems more full" and "not for a long time." Both can't be true unless memory seeps back (QUESTIONS 2.1a). Patrick has to rule on 2.1 before the rose beat can go in.

**Fix:** route Jon's knowledge through Derrek's game (Jon reads Derrek's moves, so "Somebody asked very nicely" is Jon reading his old teacher's handwriting) or through things Jon arranged himself (he bribed the dwarven traffickers, so he knows their schedule). Cut the exact-timing tells, or pick 2.1.

## 7. The warrant makes the first kill pointless, and Eli would know it
Ch 1 hangs Eli's first kill on Jon's argument: "He's seen your face. He'll file a report. The report goes up... until it reaches whoever you're running from." Then in ch 2, Teo pulls a warrant off a guard post that describes him "down to the scar on his chin." The palace already knows exactly who ran and what he looks like. The guard was already chasing him "By imperial warrant!" Killing him prevents nothing.

That could be a great Jon beat: he talks the boy into murder with a lie. But the draft never lets anyone notice, not Eli, not the master, and the narrator doesn't wink. A palace-raised 31-year-old who "had ordered men flogged" knows how warrants work. As written it's a plot hole, not a manipulation.

Also, why is a man who "could sweep the yard clean" running from one city guard on foot? "Nobody had taught him to run" explains the cramp, not the choice.

**Fix:** either Eli realizes when he sees the warrant, a silent beat where he understands Jon lied and stays anyway (the attachment has a value), or give the guard's report a real stake (say, his rig records the reach signature). Give Eli a reason not to just flatten the guard, like the reach being traceable or not wanting to be found by what he can do.

## 8. The master's death breaks the logic of his own oath
The scene's whole point (13 C2, BRIEF: "blows himself up... to punish the beast inside its own game... nothing leaves the room") is an *explosion* the beast swore it would contain, so the beast eats the full blast in a sealed room. The triple oath is the trap. The draft swaps in a black hole: "pull it in... pulling the light in before it could reach them." A collapse pulls inward whether or not the room is sealed, so the oath does nothing. The master could have done this in an open field. The scene's cleverness is gone.

The beast's rules also leak: "The one who goes in plays my game, and loses, and then I am free, and the door upstairs opens." By that rule anyone losing opens the door, so the sacrifice isn't needed to proceed. The master dies to keep the beast from going free, and the draft never says so. And Jon, "at peak" in the Tower and a match for Derrek, who beat this beast by rehearsing, stands there silent. Why doesn't he handle it? (Because he wants the master spent? Then show that.)

**Fix:** go back to the outward blast, a star, which the sealed room turns back onto the beast. Keep the darkening-pane device for the aftermath, not the mechanism. Say plainly that losing frees the beast to hunt them, so the price of the door is a life spent *to win*. Give Jon one line or look that shows he's letting it happen.

## 9. Bond continuity: the dark elf contradicts the homeworld beacon, and the queen is invisible to her
BANK 11-01 L9: "the home world. Would shine like a beacon in the dark to all elves everywhere." The draft: "out here among the stars she was alone in her own head, and she said it was like being deaf." She should feel the beacon from anywhere. Being *unable to stop* feeling the people who sold her would be the stronger beat.

Then the queen, an elf, lives aboard the same small ship for months. Up close "you would feel the elves and they would feel you," and yet the dark elf's bond reaction to her is nothing: "treated her like a door she was waiting to walk through." Two bonded elves sharing a galley would feel each other constantly. The draft forgets the bond whenever it's inconvenient.

Also, whether dark elves born in hell share the bond with the homeworld at all is unruled. The draft assumes they do.

**Fix:** she feels the beacon faintly everywhere. The queen's presence is a constant pressure for both of them, which gives the rivalry a nerve. Flag the hell-born-bond question for Patrick.

## 10. The Tower amplifier makes Jon's strategy look insane, and its rules wobble
"Whoever sits at its heart has all of it at his elbow... Which is why your father so rarely leaves home." Jon's plan is to drain an unkillable man **inside the largest amplifier on Earth**, the one place he's at his peak. The draft never says why that isn't suicide. The answer is buried in the BANK: amplifiers raise range and never refill fatigue, so the Tower helps everyone equally and drain is still drain. The crew would ask, and so would the reader. Nobody does.

The rules also wobble. Doc says presence is enough: "Everyone's going to be at their very best in here." Yet the apprentice has to deliberately "reached... Down, into the floor," dimming the lights, and that's what makes him unbeatable. If tapping is a separate act, why doesn't the queen, dying, do it? Why doesn't Doc, a master soul mage, do it? Eli "reached with... everything the Tower would give him" and gets swatted, so what does the apprentice do differently? Pick one rule.

**Fix:** have Jon answer the master's "And then what?" with the amplifier logic in one plain line (the Tower lends reach, not stamina). Make presence a passive boost and the apprentice's deep tap a rare, lethal extra that only someone desperate or bred for it would try.

## 11. Timeline arithmetic
- Ch 7: Teo was rehearsing "for nine months." Add up the draft: 8 months (ch 2, which includes the master's 4) + statue + "two months later" + 4–5 months of trust + half a year of the queen's sparring + "seven months" of war. That's well over two years.
- Ch 1: Teo "fled the palace with Eli nine days ago." Then six days with Jon. Jon "comes and goes" over eight months. The pacing is fine; the nine-month figure is simply wrong.
- Dark elf: "There were thirty of us, when we started." / "I was a child." / "I spent forty years learning it." Canon gives 50–200 years of escape (t610), so that's consistent. But she learned the Collapse "down there" and was escaping for decades, so where did she train? It's fine if she trained on the road. Just say so.

**Fix:** change "nine months" to "two years", or drop it.

## 12. Derrek's behavior doesn't add up for a man who sees time
- He knew Teo forged the access code and "had it framed." So he let his son escape. He tracks the forgery closely enough to frame it, and then treats the son as "drywall." If he allowed the flight, the warrant and the chasing guard are theater, and nobody ever registers that. (That might be the BANK's "maybe Derrek was never the target", but then it needs one more hint, or it reads as an accident.)
- He speaks Teo's line with him word for word. That's good foreknowledge. But if he knows the whole fight in advance, why spend anything on the Collapse instead of killing her in the shadows before she raises a hand? Answer: he's performing for Jon, to push him to reset ("Did you like it? I know how you love that one"). The draft half-shows this. It needs to be the visible logic of every kill, especially given #1.
- "Another of his amazing one-liners, ruined by idiots." A narrator joke inside the throne-room horror, and it's in Derrek's voice in free indirect. The draft's POV is close-third Eli. Eli can't know what Derrek thinks of his own one-liners. That's a POV break. Patrick wrote the line (t768), but in a POV-locked draft it needs a carrier.

## 13. Doc: tells everywhere, and a master never notices
- "Everybody here is dying. Are you dying? Pity. I'm very good at it." "Rich men like pets that can fight." "I'd use it." "He said it the way a man admires a well-stocked larder." The changeling is "too old and too wise and too good at hiding" (BANK 11-01 L24), and the draft has him winking at the reader in every scene.
- The master can feel "the shape of whatever had made" each Void room with his eyes shut, and he trained Eli to find a single moth blind. He lives with a man carrying "thousands" of souls for over a year and feels nothing? Jon and Derrek certainly know (Derrek: "Is this really the best you could find?").
- Doc is the most powerful mortal aboard, holding a hoard "more than any one body could ever have held," and he never fights once in seven months of war. Why?
- "She thanked him... and he said the boy hadn't felt any pain at the end." That's good. Then Eli "understood, finally... what had happened behind the curtain." Fine, but it lands only because the tells already gave it away.

**Fix:** cut half the jokes and make the rest plausibly innocent. Give the master one moment of unease near Doc that he dismisses. Have Doc fight in the war in an ordinary, under-powered way that's deliberately less than he could do.

## 14. The giant: foreknowledge, survival and silence
- He "left last night... had some calls to make." The last friendly giant walks off the greatest forge world the night before it dies and doesn't warn the thousands of giants there? Then he broadcasts war "within three days" with hundreds of fleets ready. That reads like either he knew (complicit) or Jon told him (so Jon let the forge world burn *and* saved his friend). Neither is acknowledged.
- "I know whose son you are. Everyone at my age knows everything eventually." That's a cheat line covering how a reclusive giant identifies a faceless Emperor's son.
- "And so did those who stood behind them," on galactic broadcast. How does he know about the hidden hand? Through Jon? Then Jon is steering the war too.

**Fix:** pick one. Either Jon warned the giant, which is what "comes and goes" was for, and Jon's value-weighted attachment to "old friend" is one of the few he keeps (this one is good, and fits L6), or the giant was away by chance and doesn't name the hidden hand.

## 15. A brief/canon contradiction the draft can't dodge: the barrage is a witnessed soul transfer
BRIEF: "Never show a soul transfer anyone could witness." The BRIEF's Tower sequence, step 5: "The changeling hurls every stolen soul at Derrek" in front of the whole room. The draft obeys the second and breaks the first, with souls pouring out "like smoke out of a chimney" in full view. The ledger admits it (NP#53). Patrick has to rule: either the no-witness rule covers *taking* only (theft is unseen, release can be seen), or the barrage has to be invisible, felt only as Derrek being battered by something no one can see.

## 16. Small stuff that still breaks
- **"Queen" of what?** Mika is king. The draft dodges it: "They call me what my king calls me." If she's Mika's queen, his wife, then lending her out is insane. If it's a title, say so.
- **Kill switch:** the orcs' kill switch is a chip function triggered from a slate. Jon flips thousands with a walk-through. That's a big display in ch 1 ("left ambiguous", per the ledger), but Teo's "Did you do all that?" points straight at Eli, and Eli says "No." So it isn't ambiguous: Jon did it.
- **Elven city access for the Emperor's son:** the Inquisition grills Eli with "the same eleven questions three times" and misses that he's the runaway heir of their sworn enemy, whose warrant is posted across human space. The Inquisition is supposed to be the best at hunting in the galaxy.
- **"You can't kill your father... People have tried."** That's Jon telling the crew the Emperor is immortal. It's more than a hint, and it raises "then why are we going?" (see #4).
- **"This is the last time I do this... too old for it."** Combined with the rose line, that comes close to stating the reset outright (BRIEF: hinted only). Keep one, not both.
- **The rift on a colony world:** WORLD_OVERVIEW says another rift "has appeared every time time has been torn open," so off-Earth rifts are fine. But the draft then calls the Earth rift "the oldest rift of all," which is fine too. No objection there, but say where rifts form, or the hint has no weight.

## What would have to change, in one line each
1. Make each Tower death visibly drain Derrek.
2. Give Eli a stated goal at the Tower.
3. Give Eli real victories: a dragon or giant kill, opening the door himself.
4. Give each crew member a stake, plus near-defections after Jon's atrocities.
5. Make Mika's motive, not Jon's sway, the reason the queen comes, and let her learning Eli's identity cost something.
6. Cut or explain Jon's foreknowledge, and get Patrick's ruling on QUESTIONS 2.1.
7. Let the warrant reveal that Jon's argument was a lie.
8. Restore the outward blast so the oath matters.
9. Fix the bond (the beacon, the queen aboard).
10. One line on why the amplifier doesn't save Derrek, and one rule for tapping it.
