He carries himself tall. NOT cold-as-rude, NOT a strutting egotist, NOT grim. His **confidence is EARNED** (he was Derrek's top apprentice, the one Derrek saw as his *equal* — young/uncertain at the start, *sure* now; the prodigy grown into what Derrek always saw), **not vanity.**
- **Flaw: hidden PRIDE — a buried WOUND, not his manner.** (Calibration: do NOT lean on this to make Jon act arrogant or unkind — he doesn't. **Derrek is the genuinely prideful one.**) Jon's pride is the codex's "pretends to be the humblest, secretly angry at the pretense" — mostly hidden, the buried weakness his grief curdled into, the engine of his obsession. - **He is a MULTITUDE (No-Formula law — `12_METHOD.md`):** warm AND hardened, considerate AND ruthless-in-acceptance, fun-loving AND obsession-driven, confident AND wounded — all true at once, in different measures, never reducible to one label. Hold the multitude; don't flatten him to whichever facet is in frame.
- **Power:** time (rewind, fast-forward, **reset**), given **full control** by the entity (for blacking out / not demanding). Time is his *only given* power; every element/force is **learned** across loops (no innate affinity — retired; if he starts with earth it's just what he learned first). His temporal perception expresses through his **Spirit** nature wedded to the time-fusion (ordinary Spirit users get only a faint edge-sense — preserving his singularity).
- **Memory mechanic:** early time-jumps knock him out (amnesia = a **mercy**, shielding his mind from the compressed centuries he technically lives through). Across a book he stays conscious longer and **retains more** — a gradual haunting (remembers erased lovers, attacks that never happened, whole erased nations). Accumulated guilt holds him back from resetting. **Core unconscious parameter: stop Derrek.**
- **Arc — a FALL, not a redemption (REWORKED this session; replaces "hard-won return toward the Light / complete"):** warm naive young man → forced into impossible choices across eternity → builds an **armor of coldness to survive making them** → that armor hardened, over eternities, into the cold figure of the final loop. **The Jon we see is ALREADY the finished cold thing** — the love died eras beyond counting; he has mourned an eternity, recovered an eternity, and spent *another* eternity forging the armor that stops him feeling. We do **NOT** watch the love die during the story, or freshly aftermath it — we meet the completed product, eternities later.
- **THE SERIES THESIS — a MONUMENT to a DEAD love.** You build a monument to *the dead*. The thing being mourned, the thing that died, is **Jon's love for HER.** The whole saga (the loops, saving her family, the eternal war) is the **aftermath of that love's death** — and the trilogy is what you *watch*: a man who no longer feels the love **ruthlessly** spending her bloodline to defeat his nemesis. His tragic arc **never rests** (he gets no ending — the loop) because grief for one's own dead heart cannot complete; the mourning *is* the ruthlessness. The bloodline is the monument's material — cherished-as-monument AND used-as-instrument at once (a tomb he tends and robs).
- **The coldness — what it actually is (calibrated):** he is **deeply broken, coping with coldness** — NOT empty, NOT a user of people. The shell is a defense against a love so vast that feeling it while making his choices would destroy him. **NOT exploitation: he does not push people to die for his benefit.** Rather — **hardened ACCEPTANCE of justified sacrifice:** he holds defeating Derrek as worth a life, *his own first of all* (he'd jump into the lava himself) — so he **wouldn't push a hero to their death, but won't STOP a willing sacrifice** for the mission, finding it *reasonable* where the younger him would have found it *abhorrent.* The tragic change is that he can now **bear and permit** loss he'd once have given anything to prevent. *(The Rule-beast sacrifice, `13_PROSE_AND_SCENES.md`, is exactly this — a companion CHOOSES to die; Jon, hardened, accepts it rather than breaking to prevent it.)*

He has **warped the things he once loved into weapons he wields against Derrek eternally.** *(Bleak: he saves the woman he once loved as the crown of the monument, no longer feeling the love that would make it tender.)*
- **The silent finale:** at the converging climax he gives **no speech** (he's given Derrek every speech across eternity). Resolute, silent — nothing left to say, and (the calibration) a man holding back an ocean he won't let himself feel.

### JON — FINALE RECAST 🟡 (this session; supersedes "hidden pride" as the ROOT, and the older certainty framing)
- **Core flaw = a CONFIDENCE-WOUND, not a pride-wound.** The pride is scar tissue *over* the wound. Underneath: **doubt** — Dunning-Kruger doubt. Jon grew past Derrek in every metric *except the one that mattered: confidence.* Competence bred the doubt (the more he mastered, the more the gap to "certainty" looked unbridgeable, and he read that gap as Derrek's permanent head start). **Amnesia suppressed the doubt early** (too confused to assemble a self, too confused to doubt one); **retention sharpened it** across the books (guilt AND doubt accumulate together as he remembers more). For as long as he can recall he believed Derrek was greater, ahead, more in control — and he was wrong; he was Derrek's *equal* from the start (canon).
- **His passivity IS the flaw, expressed:** he steered instead of stood — gardened the elves, guided the bloodline, reset toward a "perfect run" — all elaborate indirection to avoid the one thing he never tried: facing Derrek directly. He mistook his own indirection for wisdom the entire way. **The chasing-a-flawless-loop IS the perfectionism made literal** — every reset was "this wasn't perfect, I'll find the run where I make no mistake."
- **The resolution = PERSISTENCE, not mastery** (the anti-stale correction): he does NOT arrive certain, competent, playing it perfect. He steps forward **still doubting, still uncertain, still flawed** — he's just decided uncertainty is no longer a reason to stay still. The realization that breaks the trap: **he is the fault line — his jumps are why time is breaking** — which is the same as realizing the perfect run he chased was destroying everything, and he never needed it. Cure and indictment are one fact: *stop jumping = stop demanding perfection = act, flawed and forward, or not at all.* Doubt acting is braver than confidence acting.
- **WHERE the realization came from ✅ (the intimate evidence — mechanism refined this session):** Jon learned it **among the elves.** The jumps don't *erase* people — **they unmake them upstream**: a jump-to-save changes the tiny variables (a survival, an errand, a chance meeting) that every conception depends on, so the lost are not deleted — **they simply never were.** (Example-shape: save a man's boss → the man isn't at a funeral → buys no flowers → never enters the shop → never meets her → their child never exists.) *Every time Jon jumped to save someone he loved, he watched others wink out* — the save itself is the perturbation; rescue is paid for by people who never get to exist. The elves are the one race with reset-texture (`07` — a child conceived in one timeline may never be conceived in another; grief for the *unrememberable* — mourning absences that have no memories attached, because the mourned never existed here). Every race loses never-were people to a jump; **only the elves feel the holes** — which is why centuries embedded among them (`09` exodus gardening) is the only place in the universe the damage was visible. The cosmic fact ("time is breaking") got its unbearable intimate proof: **the people he fought hardest to save were being hurt most directly by his saving them.** ✅ **CONFIRMED (ruled this session):** if every save unmakes others, a run where everyone is saved is **mathematically impossible — the perfect run CANNOT EXIST.** This is the lesson 5,000 tries never taught him one at a time. He stops resetting NOT because he finally knows this run is right — **but because he has finally learned that no run can be.** Persistence was always the only door. **Reconciliation note — two loss-categories coexist, no contradiction:** people of the **prior run** are *erased with the rewound timeline* — they existed, and Jon remembers them (the Memory mechanic above; Lirael's erased timeline) — while people of the **new run** are *unmade upstream* and never exist at all. Jon mourns the first kind; the elves' texture feels both; the second is the darker. And that — watching it happen to them, jump after jump — is what finally stilled his hand.
- **He OWNS his mistakes; they stand.** Chief among them: he had the elves **flee instead of stand and fight** — a genuine error born of the same can't-risk-it perfectionism (he didn't trust himself to win directly, so a people fled instead of facing the threat, and the flight helped cause the collapse). He doesn't retcon it; he carries it forward. Persistence is heroic *because* the man being persistent is fallible and knows it.
- **The one inch of growth:** from *I must be flawless* to *I must be persistent.* That inch is the whole arc — small, hard-won, human-sized, enough to end the loop without pretending it heals him. (Resolves redemption-or-tragedy as the honest middle: he doesn't transcend into a perfect victor, and he doesn't stay frozen; he grows one inch and it costs everything he didn't know himself sooner.)
- **The finale image — Jon goes calm, Derrek goes white (the SWAP):** for eternity Jon was the frightened doubter and Derrek the serene master. In the final window-scene they *trade.* Jon laughs (childish, calm, at peace — the peace of a man who has stopped requiring perfection and made peace with the end); Derrek shows **true and utter terror** for the first time. The weapon is **acceptance** — you cannot threaten the end to a man who has made peace with it, and Derrek's whole hidden self is fear of that exact end. Keep "assuming the role of mentor one last time" (Derrek reflexively reaching for the dead master/apprentice dynamic; it fails). *(POV note: the finale dips into Derrek's head — decide on purpose whether it's the one earned god-POV rupture or stays on the mortal's shoulder — `09`/`13`.)*
- **The exhaustion register (from the prose draft, `13`):** "had he just done nothing his suffering would have ended long ago" — the terminus is partly *release*, a man who is DONE, finding peace in letting go. This tilts the ending toward *the universe ends and Jon is at peace with it* (see the finale mechanic, `09_STRUCTURE.md`, and the open universe-ends-or-brink question).

### DERREK — FINALE RECAST 🟡 (this session; the parasitic-jump engine + the self-deception)
- **The parasitic jump (the mechanism under his manipulation):** Derrek CANNOT jump on his own (uncontrolled, random) — **his directed jumps are parasitic on Jon's.** They're a linked pair (canon); when Jon resets, Derrek rides it, and that is his ONLY access to intentional, plan-refining time travel (carrying new information backward). So he never spent eternity learning to *defeat* Jon — he learned to **operate** him. **Jon is Derrek's trigger; Jon is the steering wheel on a power Derrek otherwise can't drive.** Every manufactured horror to break Jon's heart into resetting was also Derrek **pressing his own jump button.** He gave up everything for mastery over time, and the cruel joke is he can only *use* it by puppeteering the one man who can actually drive.
- **Therefore a forward Jon DISARMS him** — not "loses him the advantage," but strips the power itself. 10,000 concurrent linear years = Derrek grounded for 10,000 years (no Jon-jump = no Derrek-jump). The prize he mutilated himself to win, switched off, and he can't switch it back on because he never could — that was always Jon's hand. His lever ("make Jon jump") simply stops working on a Jon who will absorb the horror and *not reset.* This is why a **flawed, doubting** Jon is still his nightmare: Jon doesn't have to out-play him, only stay forward.
- **The reset-FOG (added this session — the SECOND pillar of his reset-reliance):** beyond riding Jon's jumps, Derrek's plans rely on the **confusion and chaos of rewound time**: each reset scrambles the board and hands him **pieces that have no idea the game changed** — he alone retains awareness (his canonical "knows when he is at all times" advantage becomes an information monopoly over an amnesiac world). His whole playstyle is tuned for that fog. **Forward time strips this too:** in unbroken linear years, people and institutions *remember, adapt, and compound* — the fog never falls again, and reset-tuned genius degrades against opponents who accumulate. **This is why Jon's plans succeed across the 10,000 concurrent years** (and the mechanical cause of the failure-ladder, `09`): Derrek lost both his time travel AND the weather his mastery was built for, while Jon's patient gardening is *native* to continuous time and compounds in it. His defeat means more for it — he wasn't merely outplayed; the conditions his genius required stopped existing the day Jon went forward.
- **The self-deception (the bottom of Derrek):** the breach is **already broken wide open** (the eons of resets did that) — and paradox seals it (once open, always open). So his stated project ("keep resetting to widen the breach until reality ends") is a **fiction**; the work is done, there's nothing left to widen. He's the **god of reality** — he does NOT want oblivion (his patron's very nature is permanence-of-self, `03`); "I want to end everything as mercy, for you, Jon, because I love you" is a **cover story over its exact inverse: he is terrified of ending** — of oblivion, of whatever the Blight holds — and cannot admit it, because admitting fear means admitting he is **not flawless**, and his pride cannot survive that. The love is real (Jon is the one thing he ever loved) — he has **weaponized his own love as the alibi** so he never has to face the demons his plan bore upon the world.
- **The two-function reset (reconciles breach-widening with the apocalypse ending — `03`/`09`):** the reset does TWO opposite things — **(1) it widened the barrier** (canon; the resets tore it open over eons) AND **(2) it kept rewinding the *invaders* back out** — crossing the barrier costs the Blight beasts enormous effort, and each reset scrambles their continuity and dumps them back, forcing them to re-pay the crossing cost every loop. So the resets were **simultaneously breaking the door open AND sweeping the threshold clean.** The wide breach was survivable ONLY because of the rewinds. **Derrek KNEW this** (it's the leverage): the recurring end-of-loop window-scene — showing Jon the Blight pressing in, "reset or reality ends" — is *literally true*, which is what makes it an unbreakable manipulation. He points at a fire he started and says "quick, pour this water on it forever, it's the only way, I tell you because I care" — bailing water while pretending he wants the ship to sink. When Jon finally refuses at that window, he's right that it's a leash and wrong that it's *only* a leash — it was a leash AND a seawall, and he only ever saw the leash.
- **Derrek undone by his own caution (the rule-beast irony):** Derrek imprisoned a **rule-beast** in his Tower (canon, `13`) — proof he'd mastered even the ancient in-betweener powers. The hand that reaches him at the end is **another rule** (the Book-3 hero's won Rule / an ancient power on the hero's back). He filed "rules" under *things I have conquered* and a rule is what kills him. His contempt for the elf-as-pawn is the same arrogance that let him cage a rule-beast — he can't see the rule behind the hero because he believes that tier is beneath him. His trophy is the evidence of the blind spot that ends him. *(Keep separate from the general's severed-room sacrifice — do NOT fuse them; the irony is the RHYME between two rule-beasts, not one overloaded creature.)*
