## 0. The short version

- **The Emperor knows everything and wants only one thing: Jon at the window.** He lets the escape work, never escalates the warrant, starts the war that fills the road, leaves a door open, and takes the crew as Jon's offerings.
- **Jon wants the same window for his own reasons.** He steers the son into gathering a team that can tire the Emperor without killing him. He never helps where a feed, a ledger or an inquisitor can see. When he does help it's hidden: the token, the salvage sweep, the rescue ship, the lander.
- **Mika bets one piece, the Queen,** that the Emperor's blood can pass a door no elf has passed. He doesn't tell her whose son it is, and he makes Jon pay for her.
- **The Inquisition hunts a hull, not people.** The stolen *Ledger Closed* is evidence of the trade. Krell, the dwarf left on the moon, survives on Doc's missing water ration and gives them faces. Once they're in a lifeboat it lets the sector do the killing.
- **The giant refuses** for four reasons stacked, one of them a lie, and weighs keeping Eli as a hostage out loud. Whether Jon warned him in the hours before dawn stays unanswered.
- **The Tower fight is Jon's leash, and it frays** (pick, §4.3). Jon imposes the sequence for a reason he states (each of them has to be answered separately) and one he hides: all at once might *kill* the Emperor, and a dead Emperor resets on the spot (BANK 11-01 L4). Each crew member slips the leash when their own reason fires.

**The giant's refusal, in four layers** (the reader gets layers 1, 3 and 4 on the page; layer 2 shows only on reread):
1. **The cost in the hulls.** "Once, our own dead… now we buy" (v10). He won't put a family's worth of the dead under a human's command.
2. **The patience, and the lie.** "There are not enough of us left to die" is the galaxy's belief, and he says it as cover (`10 t416–t418`). The truth is that the giants are rebuilt and waiting. Lending the Emperor's son a ship would declare war on the chair before the giants are ready, and the Legion would trace the hull home. **Trace:** three days after the strike, hundreds of cathedral ships come out of yards nobody knew existed. Eli counts them and realises the giant lied to his face (02).
3. **The Tower is not a door to knock on.** It is their emperor and their dead (BANK 11-01 L7). Sending giants against it means sending them to break their own grandmothers' walls. "Do not call it a building."
4. **The hostage (audit A11, L-D7):** after "Because I'm the Emperor's son", the giant goes quiet and then thinks aloud. A hostage is worth what the other side would pay. He has heard how the man in the Tower treats what is his, and the son's father would pay nothing. *Keep him, and we feed him; let him go, and we lose nothing.* It plants "drywall" before the throne room, and it's the worst refusal of Eli's life.

**Jon's hours with the giant before the strike (audit A3):**
- **What Jon knows:** the strike comes the day after they leave. He remembers it (NP#3). On reread, "I knew you would… I don't know" (ch 6) is the same knowing.
- **What he wants from the night:**
  - He needs to hear that the giants will answer a strike with war. Their fleets must be at Earth on the day he drops (P9). The giant tells him what every giant knows: their patience has an end.
  - It's also a goodbye he doesn't call one. "I am not your friend. But I am friendly." The giant knows Jon well enough to say that.
- **Did he warn the giant?** It stays unanswered, as the giant's fate is (`09 t380`). Writer's view: no. A warning would save the yard and kill the war Jon needs. The attachment has a value, and the value is the war (BANK 11-01 L6).
- **On the page:** Jon comes out at dawn and says, himself, "The yard wants the berth back by morning." It moves the unattributed v10 L521 line into his mouth (audit fix). On reread it reads two ways. Either Jon is hurrying them clear, or the giant is clearing his own berths because he guessed.

## 4. The antagonist pressure plan

There are three antagonist pressures: **the Emperor** (distant, then total), **the Inquisition** (institutional, mid-book), and **the war** (the Emperor's, by proxy). Jon is the fourth, hidden pressure: he never opposes them, he spends them.

### 4.1 Escalation ladder

| Stage | Who | Move | Cost to the crew | Cost to the antagonist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emperor (by household) | The warrant. | A guard's death on Eli's hands. Eli can never stand still. | Nothing. That's the point. |
| 2 | Inquisition | The gate, the token, the ledger, the tail. | Jon's only token. Their names in a ledger. | Nothing, and it gains a curiosity. |
| 3 | Inquisition | The crater: the guns. | Teo's leg. Nine prisoners dead by Eli's miss. The freed ones die on the moon. | A station, a shipment, a hull, and an evidence problem. |

| 7 | The war | Flesh rifles, the hanged gnome, hell, the dragon, the frozen orcs. | Two broken fingers, Teo's hand, three weeks in a hold, Teo nearly leaving. | — |

**The rule for the drafter:** from stage 8 on, every move the Emperor makes costs him something visible, and the costs stack on his body in order: palm, forearm, nose and ears, the dais, his knees. Keep his hand-tracking consistent (audit micro 6). The arm hangs after the Collapse; he lifts it once against Doc, it falls back, and it hangs from then on.

### 4.2 Why the Emperor doesn't just end it in the throne room
The answer is already on the page, made consistent. He receives each of them as **Jon's offering** and looks at Jon after every death, never at the dead (02). Ending it fast would end the visit. Under that, author-level, he can't truly die and is in no hurry. No new love tells (BANK 11-08). The cold reader's ask for "one sympathetic tell earlier" is answered by the open terrace door, which reads as nothing on a first pass.

### 4.3 The Tower fight: two stagings and a pick

Canon: no Tower rule forces single combat. The only Rule in the Tower is the beast's Void (`09 t426–t430`; `01` tripwire 30). So the staging has to come from people.

#### Option A · The whole room at once
- **The trigger.** Teo dies, Eli's reach goes out of him like a shout, and everyone moves at once.
- **How it runs.**
  - The apprentice comes off the dais into the Queen.
  - The dark elf goes into the shadows in the first second.
  - Doc starts laughing and walks out.
  - The Emperor answers everything with one hand while the apprentice holds the floor.
- **Each death keeps its own beat because the camera snaps.** Eli's POV catches one thing at a time through dust and noise (the "three things and nothing in between" device from Jon's fight, used for the whole room):
  1. The throw, and the sliver curling on the step.
  2. The Collapse turned inside out while the Queen is still falling.
  3. Doc's weight breaking over the dais through the gap the Collapse tore.
- **For:** it kills the queue completely and feels like a battle.
- **Against:**
  - Doc's protected litany (`t774`) needs room and order, and spoken under a melee it loses its rhythm.
  - The Queen's duel loses its length, and the boots beat loses its quiet.
  - The dark elf's release can no longer come *from the sword breaking*.
  - Most of all, it **works against Jon's canon plan.** Everything at once is exactly what might *kill* the Emperor, and a dead Emperor resets on the spot (BANK 11-01 L4). A reader on reread would ask why Jon allowed it.

#### Option B · Jon's leash, which frays (PICK)
**Who imposes it:** Jon, by design, for two reasons.
- **The stated reason**, on the lander, already in v10: "every one of you does the best thing you've ever done, and he has to answer every single one." Add one sentence: "One at a time, he has to answer each of you. All at once, he only has to answer once."
- **The hidden reason** stays author-level, never on the page: all at once might kill him, and that's worse than useless (L4: "enough to wear down derrek but not kill him").

**How he enforces it, with no new world rule, only bodies and words:**
1. **The lander, the last night (new beat):** Jon asks the dark elf, alone, "When it starts, keep hold of him. Don't let go until you have to." She agrees. **It's her own want too:** she'd hold Eli back anyway, because she's keeping vigil over the blade (02). Eli half-hears it and doesn't understand it.
2. **At the throne-room door:** Jon's hand on Eli's shoulder, his signature touch, which always comes when he wants something (02).
3. **The Queen** goes first because she's ordered to, and because the apprentice steps between her and the chair. She won't fight beside a hell-born or ask a human for help.
4. **The Emperor accepts the terms without a word:** "Yours. Go on." Two men agreeing on terms across a room is the whole relationship in one gesture. He looks at Jon after each death.

**How it frays (this is what stops it reading as a queue):** each person **slips the leash when their own reason fires**, not on Jon's cue, and the beats overlap.
- **Teo** goes before the leash is even on: the servant's lie that the help is invisible (02). Jon doesn't stop the willing, and doesn't turn his head.
- **The Queen's duel** spills into the room: debris, the light dimming, the floor shivering under all of them. Nobody is a spectator in comfort. The dark elf's grip tightens when the blade is drawn, and Eli feels it.
- **The dark elf goes on her own trigger,** with the sliver still curling: the sword breaks, the presence goes out, and the vigil is over. She looks at the sliver, not at the Queen's body (02, L-D5). Jon's "until you have to" has come; she decides when.
- **Doc starts while the room is still settling from the Collapse,** laughing as the grit stops sliding. His reason: the man is open, bleeding, both arms spent. It's the one moment worth breaking his no-witness rule for, because after it there will be nothing left to hide.
- **Eli breaks free at "It's just us."** No hand holds him now. Jon lets him go because "That part's mine": the emotional attack is part of the plan too.

**The felt reason reaches the reader through Eli:** on the floor, between Doc's dust and his own run, he understands he was *held*. First the dark elf's arms, then Jon's hand. Add one line to the C-ladder: Eli, on his elbow, says to Jon's back, "You held me." No answer. That's the cold reader's "make Eli realise Jon staged the queue" (§8.1).

**Why B:**
- It keeps the canon plan coherent: tire, don't kill (L4).
- It keeps the protected lines' rhythm.
- It turns the queue into the book's theme. Jon spends people one at a time, and the sequence itself is the horror.
- Every crew member still dies on their own reason, not Jon's.
- Option A's chaos survives as texture *inside* each beat.
