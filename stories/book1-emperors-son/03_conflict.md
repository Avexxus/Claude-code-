## 0. The short version

- **The Emperor knows everything and wants only one thing: Jon at the window.** He lets the escape work, never escalates the warrant, starts the war that fills the road, leaves a door open, and takes the crew as Jon's offerings.
- **Jon wants the same window for his own reasons.** He steers the son into gathering a team that can tire the Emperor without killing him. He never helps where a feed, a ledger or an inquisitor can see. When he does help it's hidden: the token, the salvage sweep, the wait at the dragon's port, the lander.
- **Mika bets one piece, the Queen,** that the Emperor's blood can pass a door no elf has passed. He doesn't tell her whose son it is, and he makes Jon pay for her.
- **The Inquisition hunts a hull, not people.** The stolen *Ledger Closed* is evidence of the trade. Krell, the dwarf left on the moon, survives on Doc's missing water ration and gives them faces. The gate man whose ledger let them in takes the case as his own failure. Once they're in a lifeboat it lets the sector do the killing.
- **The giant refuses** for four reasons stacked, one of them a lie, and weighs keeping Eli as a hostage out loud. Whether Jon warned him in the hours before dawn stays unanswered.
- **The war comes aboard (new, P8b).** The young dragon they kill on the airless moon was a lord's hunter. Its lord answers the beacon in his **cathedral ship**, a giant-built warship grown from dead giants, and presses the survivors into his cradles. Then a god walks onto his bridge. From the cradle ring behind the throne, Eli watches the dragon's secret entropy breath age the god and the whole crew to death in order, the god last. The dragon lets them go because they don't matter to him.
- **The Tower fight is the whole room at once (§4.3).** Nobody holds anybody. Jon stands at the door and spends nothing, and that is what shapes the fight: the Emperor keeps one hand for Jon and answers the crew with the other. The room starts together and empties by attrition, so each death keeps its own beat. Jon's real safeguard was set long before, when he chose a team that can drain the Emperor but can't kill him (a kill only resets him whole, BANK 11-01 L4).

**Why the warrant and not force (cold reader §3.8, audit Emperor row 1):** the warrant is the *household's* reflex, not the Emperor's attention. It's a recovery-class notice, and recovery notices go up on posts and get paid out as bounties to local guards. Nobody ever escalated it to the Legion, because nobody above a steward cares. That's why one yard guard acts on it in a year and nobody else does.

**Why the face is still on so many walls (audit F08, LOCAL):** nobody *pushes* the poster; it spreads by itself. Dwarven bounty brokers reprint any notice that carries the imperial seal, because a seal sells paper, and they paste it wherever they post. So the face travels further than the household ever meant it to, and that's the same drywall: the paper moves on trade, not on anyone's interest in the son. **Why nobody collects:**
- After ch 5 the poster is a year and a scar out of date. It shows the chin scar, not the cheek.
- The bounty pays out only at an imperial post, and on the war road there are none left to walk into.
- After the strike every Legion eye is on the sky (P7–P8).
- In the war zone the seal is hated. People scratch at the faces on seal notices as though they were the Emperor's (L-D8). They deface the poster; they don't report it.

**Trace:** the floor speech already says "You never once asked to see it." The warrant being a steward's job *is* the drywall. On the war road the drafter may write "a wall" or "walls" of seal notices with the faces scratched out; never a manhunt.

### 4.2 Why the Emperor doesn't just end it in the throne room
The answer is already on the page, made consistent. He receives each of them as **Jon's offering** and looks at Jon after every death, never at the dead (02). Ending it fast would end the visit. Under that, author-level, he can't truly die and is in no hurry. **And now a practical reason, visible to Eli (§4.3):** Jon is standing at the door, whole. Whatever the Emperor spends sweeping the room, he doesn't have when Jon comes. So he answers the crew with one hand and keeps the other for Jon, and that is why a god doesn't wipe the room in one gesture. No new love tells (BANK 11-08). The cold reader's ask for "one sympathetic tell earlier" is answered by the open terrace door, which reads as nothing on a first pass.

### 4.3 The Tower fight: the whole room at once (Patrick, Gate 2: 1b)

Canon: no Tower rule forces single combat. The only Rule in the Tower is the beast's Void (`09 t426–t430`; `01` tripwire 30). Patrick's ruling: everyone goes at once, and nobody holds anybody. So the fight's shape has to come from people and bodies, with no sequence imposed.

#### Why Jon doesn't hold anyone (the new frame)
- **The old hidden reason is gone.** "All at once might kill him, and a dead Emperor resets" was never a risk Jon had to manage at the door. Derrek can't die for good (BANK 11-01 L4: "Ya I dont think he can die lol"). A death only hard-resets him where he fell, instantly, and he stands up whole.
- **The real danger of a kill is waste, not loss.** A reset Emperor comes back undrained, and every life spent to tire him would be spent for nothing. That's why Jon never allied a true god (L4: "it would just be an infinite loop").
- **Jon managed that danger long before the Tower, when he chose the team.** "Enough to wear down derrek but not kill him" (L4). The cap is *who they are*, not what order they go in. Even all together, at their peak in the largest amplifier on Earth, they can drain him, not kill him. So at the door Jon has nothing to hold back. (Author-level only; never on the page.)
- **What Jon wants instead is the fastest drain and his own budget whole.** Everyone at once makes the Emperor answer many things at once, which drains him fastest. And Jon spends nothing, because he's the one who has to finish it (v10 L767, "nothing left for upstairs").
- **The stated reason, on the lander (replaces "One at a time…"):** "Every one of you does the best thing you've ever done, and he has to answer every single one." Then: **"He'll keep a hand for me. You get the other one."** It's true, it's tactical, and on reread it's also the whole relationship: the two men watch each other over the crew.

#### Why the Emperor fights one-handed (what stops a god from ending it in a gesture)
Jon is in the room, whole, and the Emperor knows exactly what Jon is. Any strength he spends on the crew he lacks when Jon comes. So he answers each of them with the least that will do, with one hand, while he looks at Jon. Each crew member's best costs him something anyway (§4.1). **The Collapse is the turn:** it takes both hands ("It took both. His teeth showed."), and for the first time he spends the reserve. That's the moment Jon takes one step forward and freezes. On a first read it's grief (the Collapse is Lirael's, as the statue taught her). On reread it's also the opening, and Jon refusing it, because Doc hasn't gone yet.

#### What each of them does when the room goes (all at Teo's death, each on their own reason, each on their own path)
The trigger is Teo's death. Teo goes first, before anyone moves, unasked, on the servant's lie that the help is invisible (02; planted in 04 2.1 and on the war road), with the forms Eli taught him in his mouth. Jon doesn't stop the willing, and doesn't turn his head. Then, in the same breath, everyone:

- **Eli: the reach, unheld (E8a).** It goes out of him like a shout. Nobody holds it now. It hits; **the dais cracks end to end**; the Emperor doesn't look at the crack. A flick of the good hand, without looking, still looking at Jon: Eli goes fifty feet into the side wall. Ribs. He spent everything he had in the first second, which is why the rest of the fight happens to him from the floor, in snaps, through dust. And it's why his last attack can only be words (Gate 2: 4b). The book's lesson, made physical: force was never his gift.
- **The Queen: her order.** She goes straight at the chair, because the order is the man in the chair dead. The apprentice comes off the dais into her: "Yours," the Emperor tells him, without interest. "Go on." Her duel becomes the spine of the melee: it runs the whole time the others are moving. **Why nobody helps her:** there's nobody free. Teo is dead, the master is dead, Eli is in the wall, Doc is at Eli, and the dark elf won't go near the blade. The room's own shape leaves her alone; nobody chooses it.
- **The dark elf: the long way round.** Her want is the man who owns everything; her limit is the blade (02): she won't stand anywhere near it, and the duel is in the middle of the room. So she takes the side wall, in the dust and the shadow under the window. **Her route passes Eli** (he was flicked into that wall). She stops long enough to find his two broken fingers and press them once. He says "Don't." She goes. Then she waits behind the chair, very still. **Her trigger is what she sees:** the Emperor catches the Queen's throw by the blade and crushes it, and one sliver curls and uncurls on the step. The thing she meant to do herself has been done, by the hand she hates most. Whoever it was is free, or gone, and she'll never know which; the bond never told her anything, and it can't now. Every eye in the room is on the sliver, and his right hand is open to the bone. She looks at the sliver, not at the Queen's body, and closes her hand. **In the Tower, it had him.** Then he turns it inside out. (No shadow-step: she was quiet and fast and already there, audit F10.)
- **Doc: at Eli.** He goes where a healer goes, to the boy in the wall, and kneels with his hands on Eli's ribs. It keeps his mask, and it keeps him visibly safe (his terror of death, 02): nobody aims at a gnome on his knees by a wall. From there he studies the man ("Oh, you're good. Oh, you're very, very good."). **His trigger:** both arms spent, the reserve gone, the man open and bleeding, and nobody left standing in the room but Eli and Jon. It's the one moment worth breaking his no-witness rule, because after it there will be nothing left to hide. He stands up from beside Eli, which is why Eli feels the weight on his chest first.
- **Jon: at the door, spending nothing.** He takes his hand off Eli's shoulder at the threshold (his signature touch, 02, which always comes when he wants something: here, what he wants is for Eli to go in). He doesn't move for Teo, the Queen, or Eli in the wall. One step at the Collapse, frozen mid-stride. A tear at Doc's quiet, not wiped. He is the threat that keeps the Emperor's left hand busy doing nothing, and he knows it.

#### The shape: the room starts together and empties by attrition
It isn't a queue, because it doesn't start as one: everyone moves inside the same breath. It ends as a series of duets because the Emperor's answers land at different speeds, and each death thins the room:
1. **Teo**, in a closed hand, before anyone moves.
2. **Eli**, flicked into the wall in the first second (out of the fight, alive, watching).
3. **The Queen and the apprentice**, the long duel at the centre, with the dark elf moving round the wall and Doc kneeling at Eli. The duel spills into everything: grit, the lights dimming as the apprentice draws on the walls, the floor shivering under all of them. Nobody is a spectator in comfort.
4. **The throw, the crushed blade, the sliver**, and in the same instant **the Collapse** from behind the chair, while the Queen is still falling. The boots beat is Eli's, a second's shock on the floor, near her (BANK 11-08 boots line).
5. **Doc's barrage**, last, into a room that has emptied, which is why his litany (`t774`) has room and order without anyone giving it to him.
6. **Eli's words**, from the floor, because "It's just us" and he can't stand.

**The camera snaps because Eli is on the floor.** His POV catches one thing at a time through dust and noise (the "three things and nothing in between" device from Jon's fight, used for the whole room), and the reason is physical: ribs, his head against the wall, Doc's hands on his chest.

#### How the old Option A objections are answered
- **Doc's litany needs room and order:** attrition gives it. He's the last one standing.
- **The Queen's duel loses its length:** it's the spine, and the length is real, because the others are moving around it.
- **The dark elf's release must come from seeing the sword break:** her route takes her behind the chair, and the crushing is her opening.
- **It works against Jon's plan:** it doesn't. The team is the cap (above), and all at once drains him fastest.

#### The felt reason reaches the reader through Eli (replaces "You held me")
Between snaps, from the floor, Eli sees what the reader needs: his father never looks at any of them. He looks at the door. He answers everything with one hand. On his elbow after the words fail, Eli says to Jon's back: **"He was watching you. The whole time."** No answer. It's the ladder's rung C6b, it reads as behaviour, not interpretation (audit F33), and on reread it's the whole book.

#### Then the words, and Jon
"It's just us." Nobody needs to hold Eli now: he can't stand, and his reach is gone. **The final attack is words only (Gate 2: 4b),** from the floor: the post, the scar he never asked to see, the cheek, the man at the fence, the boy with the rag, Teo's parents still in his house, "I asked them to come", "Here's your property. It came all this way. Look at it." The good hand comes up, open, toward Jon, and stops halfway; Eli can't tell what stopped it (BANK 11-08: whether the appeal lands stays open, to Eli too). Then Jon walks past him and the fight is Jon's: drained, restrained, held, not killed (L4).
