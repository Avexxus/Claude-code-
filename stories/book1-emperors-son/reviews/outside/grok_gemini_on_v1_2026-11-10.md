## Grok

### First reaction
- A clear step up. More air, less efficiency; the year of scraping, the master's training and the approach to the Tower feel lived-in.
- Agency stronger: Eli's Tower decision, the master's refusal, the Queen, Teo as a person, friction around Jon.
- Horror is more systemic and less announced (the yard, the organ market, the cores, the presses paying by weight).
- The Tower is tighter and colder; Eli stays a body (ribs, thirst, dragging himself).
- Still shows architecture: Jon is the gravity well; some transitions into the crew-gathering feel guided; "this system is evil" to "I will take the chair" still has narrative necessity.
- Verdict: "the first version that reads as if it could become a novel without first needing another major structural overhaul."

### Deep notes for Claude
**Protect:** the yard / first kill; the master's training; the dark elf's arrival and early presence; the Queen's duel and death; Doc's final sequence; Eli's floor speech; the window.

**Agency problems (highest priority):**
- Eli: Tower decision still too clean; accepts Jon too passively; the leap to assembling a suicide crew is under-motivated.
- Jon: arrives and leaves with near-perfect timing; emotional temperature too consistent.
- Dark elf: contempt to trusted crew too fast; staying for the Tower is thin.
- Queen: her staying and willingness to die need more internal cost.
- Master: his later boarding is under-explained.
- Teo / Doc: staying through the Tower is default, not chosen under pressure.

**Structural efficiency:** almost every location and meeting advances the plot; the crew assembles with little attrition; the road has physical cost but thin relational cost; key talks with Jon end with Eli accepting deflection.

**Next:** re-open Eli's decision to take the Tower; more resistance from the dark elf and the Queen; make Jon's absences and arrivals less perfectly timed; add dead time and failed attempts; deepen the crew's knowledge that the Tower is likely death.
