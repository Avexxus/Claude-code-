# v1 craft attack: The Emperor's Son

> 🌊 Claude review, story layer, not canon. Mode: CRAFT. Draft: `drafts/v1.md` (story only, lines 5–1741; ledgers not judged). Standards: `STYLE_PROFILE.md`, `NATURAL_PRINCIPLES.md` (NP#), the standing craft rules in the `story` skill, §S of `04_scene_cards.md`. The draft was not edited. Rewrites below are proposals; Patrick's verbatim lines are never reworded, only moved or re-framed.

## Verdict

The draft is grimdark, the orcs and victims are people, and nothing is titillating. ONE and the cathedral breath (6.8c) are the two best sustained passages. The damage is elsewhere:

1. The narrator keeps doing the reader's work: echo lines, "as he had…" callbacks and recall clusters (sections A, B).
2. Jon is interpreted about a dozen times, twice as flat fact (section C).
3. One gesture, not looking, is given to nearly everyone before the Emperor needs it (A1).
4. Most scenes close on a quiet weighty line, and most speakers talk in two-beat epigrams (A3, A4).
5. Chapter SIX drops into digest voice for long stretches (section D).

Clean, checked: no "Derrek" anywhere in the story; no clinical labels; no real-world analogies; no "user"; no "boy" for Eli in narration; no "for a moment"; no titillation (the pens, the exchange, Jon's list at L423 and the orc's death at L1055 are all restrained).

## A. The five that do the most damage

### A1. "Didn't look" is spent before the Emperor needs it
The book's central image is a father who never looks at his son. By the throne room, not-looking has been used on at least 23 lines by nearly every character.

- "She didn't look at Eli." (L1029) · "She didn't look at the dark elf." (L877) · "looked at nobody and pulled it" (L879) · "never once at the Queen. The Queen never looked at her at all." (L663) · "Jon didn't look at her, then or the rest of the way down" (L1267) · "didn't look at the place on the floor" (L1425) · "went past Jon without looking at him" (L1127) · "She didn't look in." (L677) · "The lord never once turned his head" (L1029) · "not looking at him either" (L369) · "not looking at him" (L561).
- Then the narrator states the image outright: "He hadn't looked at Eli once since they came into the room." (L1699)

**Reason:** NP#12 and NP#22 (machined repetition); STYLE §3 Derrek ("may as well be drywall"). A gesture everyone has is nobody's.
**Fix, as beats:** keep negated looking for the Emperor, and for Jon at the spar (L1265–1267, which is good). Give the Queen and the dark elf other tells: the Queen's is the sheath and walking through people; the dark elf's is the open hatch and the arm round the plate. Cut L1699's first sentence.

### A2. The narrator explains its own callbacks
Three devices, all saying "remember?":

- **Echo lines** (8): L183, L525, L785, L797, L903, L1423, L1507, L1681.
- **"As he had…" footnotes** (9): "as he had found the moth under the temple roof" (L453) · "the way he'd felt for the needle's eye" (L481) · "as he'd watched the prisoners' mouths" (L521) · "as he had for the moth" (L989) · "as the man at the helm had" (L1093) · "the bright, attentive face he'd worn over the miner's arm" (L1095) · "as he had sat in the temple every morning" (L1405) · "as they had below" (L1517) · "the way he went through port gates" (L1455).
- **Recall clusters** (3): L903, L1313, L1599.

**Reason:** NP#1, NP#6, NP#7. One echo (L183) is the NATURAL_PRINCIPLES model. Twelve is a study guide. Patrick: "stuff that just feels way too spelled out".
**Fix:** keep three echoes: L183, L785 (the berth) and L1507 (the hand). Cut or strip the rest; rewrites in section E.

### A3. Nearly every scene ends on a quiet weighty line
Of about 55 scenes, roughly 14 close on a negated verb and 12 on a clipped line of dialogue. Perhaps six just stop.

- Negation closers: "Eli stopped reading the board." (L173) · "He had never had it done to him." (L213) · "He never found out where the hour had gone." (L683) · "The lord never asked their names." (L1121) · "and didn't ask again." (L1141) · "Nobody asked who would hear it." (L999) · "he didn't notice them until night." (L953) · "Eli kept thinking someone should." (L69).
- Quip closers: "When you've earned it." (L515) · "So you remember who you are." (L741) · "Get out of my temple." (L823) · "Company." (L147) · "Always wanted one." (L333).

**Reason:** NP#5 (no buttons), NP#17 ("is every scene's last line a turn or a revelation? Then some should be neither").
**Fix:** end ten scenes one beat early or on something flat and practical. L103 ("There's a hull going out tonight…") and L433 are the model: they end on logistics. Candidates in section E.

### A4. Everyone speaks in two-beat epigrams
Minor and major characters alike deliver the quotable line. Cover the tags and these are one voice:

- "Long story. Bad ending." (Jon, L97) · "Disagreed. Out loud." (Jon, L373) · "Rig's fine. It's the meat that goes." (noodle seller, L223) · "It doesn't open. It takes." (old prisoner, L477) · "They don't board what they can starve." (dark elf, L881) · "You ordered a part." (master, L851) · "A hostage is worth what the other side would pay." (giant, L715) · "Anyone can lift. Everyone out here who could lift is dead." (master, L253) · "Paid to sweep here. Didn't say for what." (tug master, L893) · "Tired men can be held. Tired men can be talked to. Tired men give up chairs." (Jon, L1213).

**Reason:** Patrick's brief in NATURAL_PRINCIPLES: "more human and natural, not structured from scripts"; NP#18, NP#19.
**Fix:** the master and the dark elf are the pair that blur. Keep the dark elf's missing question marks ("Why.") and give her the unfinished or wrong-word sentence of someone fifty years out of this language. Let the master be the one who is allowed aphorisms. Give the noodle seller and the tug master a line that fumbles or runs on.

**Related formulas that have gone mechanical:**
- **Teo's stammer** is one template from FOUR on: L427, L535, L789, L801, L849, L1119, L1211. "So where do we," said Teo, "there's no ship now, is there, there's no giant, where do we even—" (L801). Vary three of the seven: let him say one thing flat.
- **Jon's exit** is "Teo" plus a change of subject four times: L209, L755, L907, L1139. Then Eli lists it (L1297). The reader is ahead by the third. Change two.
- **The goat** appears about eleven times, and "he didn't mention a goat" is used twice as the same trick (L745, L1119). Keep L745.

### A5. Tic words that make the rhythm sound machined
Counts are lines in the story text.

- **"never once"**: 10. "never once let him push" (L243) · "never once asked to see him" (L193) · "never once made anything go so quietly" (L583) · "never once seen him touch" (L835) · "never once seen it from above" (L1201) · "never once seen the soles" (L1563) · "You never once asked to see it" (L1641). Keep the last two.
- **"a long time / a long while"**: 12.
- **"Nobody + verb"** as a beat: 25 or more. "Nobody took it off." "Nobody thanked them." "Nobody answered." (four times) "Nobody helped him." "Nobody had looked."
- **"to no one / to nobody"**: L761, L1017, L1273.
- **"very" as a hush marker**: about 20 ("very still", "very low", "very calm", "very kindly", "very warmly", "very small").
- **"something"** as fog: nine in SEVEN–EIGHT alone ("Something bright", "Something moving", "Something low", "Something struck", "did something careful").
- **"watched X not do Y"**: "listened to the drone not leave" (L121) · "watched himself not ask it again" (L213) · "watched her not take it" (L877).
- **"…and then he wasn't / and then not"**: "a small dark shape on the grit and then he wasn't" (L539) · "slow and even, and then not" (L787).
- **"the way…" similes** (STYLE §8 bans stacking): "slowly, the way heavy things go" (L249) · "the way you look at weather" (L1103) · "the way the whole ship hummed" (L1051) · plus the three in A2.
- **"It hurt. He did it again."** used twice as the same hard-man beat (L951, L1189).
- **Action openers on one mould**: "The grapple hit like a dropped building." (L845) · "The apprentice came off the dais like a falling wall." (L1491) · "The sheath came off like skin." (L1485).

**Fix:** a `fog` pass with these as a search list. Cap "never once" at 2, "a long time/while" at 4, "Nobody + verb" at 8.
