## 1. Dossiers

### 1.1 Eli — CANON (the son)

- **Want.** To take the chair from his father. That's the sentence he can say.
- **Need.** To be seen as a person by anyone who has power over him, and then to learn he can stand without it. Under the chair is the unsayable want: *make him look at me.*
- **Wound.** Raised in the Undercourt at the foot of the Tower (L-A1) by tutors, stewards and reports. His father never once asked to see him. He was an heirloom kept in good condition. The warrant makes it legal: "imperial property."
- **The lie.** *If I'm big enough to be impossible to ignore, he'll have to look.* So everything becomes scale: the chair, an army, a war against the elves.
- **The line he won't cross, and where he crosses it.** He won't use his father's name to get what he wants. He is his own man or nothing. **He crosses it at the giant's hall** ("Because I'm the Emperor's son"), and it costs him at once: the Queen hears it, and that night she opens his cheek with "So you remember who you are." Afterwards he never uses the name again, not even in the throne room, where he says "your property", never "your son".
- **Price** (what would make him go home). His father sending for him by name, not by warrant, would do it. A summons with a face on it. He would hate himself and go. It never comes, and that's the point of the book.
- **Fears.**
  - That his father's face will be his own, older. (It is; O4.)
  - That he is like his father: "That's how you know you're not him" lodges in him as a test he keeps re-taking.
  - Being ordinary: the master's "they taught you to shout".
- **Secrets.**
  - He killed the guard. The others saw the body but not the choice.
  - Some mornings at the temple he forgot for an hour why he'd come, and was happy. He has told no one, because it would mean the Tower is optional.
  - He knows the guard died for a warrant that was already up, and he keeps going so that the death *will have been for something*. This is sunk cost, and he half-knows it.
- **Voice.** Palace diction at first: complete sentences, conditionals, "I'd like to know," the noun as verdict ("Property." "Me."). Under stress it breaks into short plain clauses, and by the floor speech it's broken sentences with no ornament.
  - **Beat example (a negotiation):** he asks a question instead of making a claim → lets the other side talk until they run dry → names the one thing both sides fear → then goes quiet and stays at the table while it gets uncomfortable. He wins by outlasting.
- **Body and habits.**
  - Soft hands that harden over the year; a chin scar (the poster), a cheek scar (the Queen), two splinted fingers (the dark elf), ribs that knit for four months.
  - **Counts** when he's frightened: to six hundred at the fence, the stores, the ships on the display, losing count in the same place. Counting is how a boy with no say in anything made something obey.
  - Thinks about washing his hands. Sits up late in galleys.
- **How conditions shaped him.**
  - He learned violence as spectatorship ("a prince should see what his word cost"), so the first time it's his own hand it shakes.
  - He learned negotiation as palace survival among stewards and courtiers who wanted things from the heir. That's his real gift (BANK 11-08 Eli's role).
  - He learned to be talked *about*, never to, so being addressed directly (Jon's "You're doing beautifully") is a drug.
- **Arc.** Wanting to be seen by his father → transfers the need onto Jon, who sees him pleasantly and constantly → learns at the window that Jon's seeing and his father's not-seeing were the same thing: neither man was ever looking at *him*. The fall into knowledge is his one free act (resonance only). Book 1 leaves him in the background asking, with "a small feeling" (left out) that he waits to grow. No resolution: he hasn't learned to stand without it yet.

#### Why Eli goes to the Tower (brief fix 10)
Four internal pressures meet at the poster (ch 2), and none of them is "the plot says so":
1. **The warrant's description is the only time his father has ever described him,** down to the chin scar, as an item. The Tower is the one place his father physically is. Going there is the only way to stand in front of the man.
2. **Self-theft only counts if the owner hears it.** "Me" was a joke at the yard. Walking it back up to the owner and saying *not yours* is the punchline made real. It pays off as "Here's your property. It came all this way. Look at it."
3. **The guard.** He learns at the poster that the man died for nothing. The Tower becomes what will make that death worth something.
4. **His whole education points at the chair.** Running didn't remove the purpose he was built for; it only removed the permission. "Take the chair" is ambition, the one acceptable language a prince has for need.

**On the page:** the decision line stays ("I'm going to take the chair away from him"). Add one beat before it: Eli studies his own face on the post long enough to see a stranger's jaw in it (plants O4 and "He knew it anyway"). Teo laughs, sees his face, and stops: Teo knows which sentence is the real one.
