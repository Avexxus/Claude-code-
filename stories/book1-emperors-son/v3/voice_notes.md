# Voice notes: Book 1 v3 (The Emperor's Son)
🌊 Story clay. Kept by continuity-keeper for the dialogue-editor. Habits **as they appear on the page**; cites are `ch01:L###` (draft_v7).

## Eli
- Warm, quick and commanding without noticing it: he issues orders as kindness ("Sit down… Teo, move.", "Eat your bird.").
- Repeats himself instead of listening; talks over objections.
- Laughs at things that should worry him (the phone call, the dwarf's price); misreads danger as manners ("Polite, aren't they").
- Shows off: "Look at him" to Teo, about Vosk; small denials when caught ("I didn't touch it.").
- Under pressure he has a "clear and reasonable" steward voice; when it fails it breaks into repeats and pleading.
- Inner speech in crisis: short self-coaching, in order ("A cutter was a fork. Take it off him."), then one line ("Come back.").
- Samples:
  - "Sit down. You haven't eaten. There's the end of the bird. Teo, move." (L137)
  - "It's paid for. If there's some sort of fee, I'm happy to—" (L399)
  - "Vosk, put it down. Put it down. Please." (L445)

## Teo
- Few words. Questions in two words ("From who?", "Whose cut?").
- Flat statements that are warnings, and he doesn't push past one.
- In danger: single-word commands, repeated ("Bridge.", "Walk.").
- Dry when shaken ("She'll want to know what happened to her brush", said to the sink; "Right").
- Samples:
  - "He didn't ask you to come." (L109)
  - "Every stall, Eli. Nobody's looking at him. They're looking at the coat." (L239)
  - "He's under there. You want to be up here with us when he comes out?" (L585)

## Vosk (dead; for flashback or echo only)
- Terse, one-word answers ("Wall."). Plain statements; uses Eli's name to stop him.
- Dialect touches: "He asked was you mine"; "You come off the step".
- After the chip: broken, wondering fragments ("It's. Up. It's right there.").
- Dying speech is breath-broken fragments, with one whole sentence kept for it.
- Samples:
  - "It's quicker on my own." / "I know the way. I go down there." (L153–157)
  - "Eli. Not in the coat." (L163)
  - "I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into." … "I picked it." … "Nobody." … "Spent me." (L551–559)

## Velk
- Curt, transactional, and she repeats a word for emphasis ("A third. A third.", "Don't." "Don't.").
- Answers questions with one word ("Out." "Tax.").
- Gives orders as conditions ("Unless you'd rather do the knife.").
- Ends on a hedge or a disclaimer; looks after herself first.
- Samples:
  - "He's paying. Course he is. You're late." (L255)
  - "It was eleven last night. Tonight you're late, and it's hot, and there's three of you." (L287)
  - "It's lit. It'll settle. Or it won't." / "Out the front. The back's mine." / "And I didn't fit it." (L351, L371, L373)

## The King's crew
- Mocking; use "Terrace" as a name; drop grammar ("King don't need his ears").
- **The smiling one:** easy, pricing. "Lit you up like a lamp, Terrace." (L397); "Terrace boy walks into Hookline in a coat like that. Worth more than any chip in it." (L413)
- **The gap-toothed one:** shouting, concentrating. "Terrace. Hand down. Put it down or he goes up the stair without his ears. King don't need his ears." (L439)
- **Pole Street (unplaced):** "You're up late, Terrace." (L21)

## The steward
- Heard only at the count, flat, by number; once by name: "Vosk?" (L627–631).

## Fixed voices
- **The Terrace gate:** "Good evening." / "Good morning." Soft, by the hour, never a name (L185, L613).
- **The service slot:** "Late. Logged." In a pleasant voice (L69).
- **The orc farewell:** "Walk well." (L37)
