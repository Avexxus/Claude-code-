# Voice notes: Book 1 v3 (The Emperor's Son)
🌊 Story clay. Kept by continuity-keeper for the dialogue-editor. Habits **as they appear on the page**. **Rebased 2026-11-17; THREE added 2026-11-18:** `1:L###` = `v3/ch01/new_ch01_v2.md` (ONE), `2:L###` = `v3/ch02/new_ch02_v2.md` (TWO), `3:L###` = `v3/ch03/draft_v5.md` (THREE).

## Eli
- **Warm, quick, and commanding without noticing it.** His orders come as kindness ("Sit down… Teo, move.", "Eat your bird.", "Sit, Vosk. Sit.").
- **He talks over people,** answering a different question or cutting in: "Listen. Tonight." over Teo's room; "Don't take it. Where would we lie?" to Lisane. He repeats his own line instead of hearing theirs ("Leave it with me" ×2; "He's with me" ×3).
- **Turns refusals into wordplay:** "Then let her out." / "She didn't say there was a question."
- **Laughs at what should worry him,** and retells other people's pain as a joke, even mimicking Vosk: "Eighty-eight years," "flat and low, in Vosk's voice" (1:L375; 2:L89).
- **Dog-kindness to servants:** "Good man." / "They like it."
- **Two voices under threat:** the "clear and reasonable" steward voice ("If there's some sort of fee, I'm happy to—"), which, when it fails, breaks into repeats and pleading.
- **Inner speech in crisis:** short self-coaching ("A cutter was a fork. Take it off him. Take it."), then one line ("Come back.").
- **Samples:**
  - "Don't be stupid. Keep your forty-one." (1:L135)
  - "Sit down. You haven't eaten. There's the end of the bird. Teo, move." (1:L599)
  - "It's paid for. If there's some sort of fee, I'm happy to—" (2:L341)
  - "Vosk, put it down. Put it down. Please." (2:L391)
- **In THREE:** the "clear and reasonable" voice comes back to argue Teo down ("It's perfectly simple… He held up a finger, and another", 3:L531), followed by cruelty he doesn't hear himself say ("You've nowhere to be.", 3:L547). He gives flat one-word yeses to Jon ("Yes." ×3, 3:L475–483) and deadpan under grief ("We had soup.", 3:L223). He has stopped joking about Vosk: "He's dead." (3:L285).
  - "It was eleven hundred marks. And a chip off a dwarf. And my watch, and a soup stall." (3:L539)

## Teo
- **Few words.** His questions are two words ("From who?", "For who?", "Whose cut?"), sometimes written with no question mark ("Are you going to open that").
- **His warnings are flat statements, and he doesn't push past one.** After the third, he "said no more".
- **Turns Eli's own words back on him:** "A loan."
- **In danger:** single-word commands, repeated ("Bridge.", "Walk.", "Eli. Now."). His bluff is a plain question: "Want him to find you over here with us?"
- **Dry when shaken.** He speaks to objects, not to Eli ("She'll want to know what happened to her brush", said to the sink), and says "Right".
- **Samples:**
  - "He didn't ask you to come." (1:L571)
  - "It does that. It says here it does that." (2:L61)
  - "Every stall, Eli. Nobody's looking at him. They're looking at the coat." (2:L163)
  - "He's coming out. Want him to find you over here with us?" (2:L541)
- **In THREE:** fewest words yet. He refuses with two words, twice ("They log it." / "It'll close.", 3:L37–41, L161), and warns with flat statements ("He knew his name." / "Nobody came in, Eli." / "You don't know what it's for.", 3:L517–533). Under Eli's push he says nothing; he answers by acting. "Right" ×3.
  - "Crates." … "Bread." (3:L173)

## Jon (from THREE, `3:L###` = `v3/ch03/draft_v5.md`)
- **Short and warm.** Mostly lines of two to eight words, said while he eats; the eating carries the pauses ("He said it round the plum, and bit the plum").
- **Dialect:** "ya" for you ("Look at the face on ya", "I'd love ya to come", "I'll tell ya"); "You've not eaten" and "You'll not like it much" (the "not" comes after the verb); "son" (×2); "Big lad".
- **Asks no questions on the page.** He answers questions sideways or not at all: "Who?" → "Big lad. Under the arch."; "What's up there?" → "Never been… Better fruit than this, I'd hope."; "What would you want me for?" → no answer, only his eyes on Eli's hand.
- **Confirms with very little:** "He is." / "If he wants." / "He likes that."
- **Terms come as flat imperatives, one per line,** with a short tail: "You come today." / "You don't come back here after." / "And when I say do a thing, you do it. Then. Not after you've had a think."
- **Plain about death:** "They'll have had the chip out… Rest of him goes by weight, down there."
- **Gives only part:** "I'll tell ya some of what I want."
- **Names himself** with one word and a hand: "Jon," (to Teo, 3:L591). His exits are short orders: "This way." / "Mind your head."
- **Samples:**
  - "Sorry about Vosk." (3:L383)
  - "Knew of him… Did the trees here." (3:L395)
  - "I'd love ya to come… I would." (3:L457)
  - "Keep your money, son… I'll tell ya some of what I want." (3:L469)
  - "Go and ask him, then… I'll be here." (3:L493)

## Vosk (dead; for flashback or echo only)
- **Terse.** One-word answers ("Down." "Wall." "Double."). He gives terms, not reasons. He uses Eli's name to stop him.
- **Dialect touches:** "He asked was you mine"; "You come off the step"; "Gates don't bite that hard."
- **Corrects people flatly:** "Eighty-eight." / "Your mam wasn't there."
- **After the chip:** broken, wondering fragments ("It's. Up. It's right there.").
- **The dying speech:** fragments broken by breath, with a stalled restart ("I'm proud." / "I'm proud to die. For the." / "Man").
- **Samples:**
  - "Forty-one now. Then a mark a month." (1:L125)
  - "It's quicker on my own." / "I know the way. I go down there." (1:L615, L619)
  - "Eli. Not in the coat." (1:L625)
  - "Forty-one's under my bunk. At the back. Against the wall." (2:L83)
  - "I picked it." … "Nobody." … "Spent me." (2:L511–515)

## Velk
- **Curt and transactional.** She repeats a word to land it ("A third. A third."; "Don't." / "Don't.").
- **One-word answers:** "Out." "Tax."
- **Gives orders as conditions:** "Unless you'd rather do the knife."
- **Ends on a hedge or a disclaimer,** looking after herself first.
- **Samples:**
  - "He's paying. Course he is. You're late." (2:L193)
  - "It was eleven last night. Tonight you're late, and it's hot, and there's three of you." (2:L225)
  - "It's lit. It'll settle. Or it won't." / "Out the front. The back's mine." / "And I didn't fit it." (2:L289, L313, L315)

## The King's crew
- **Mocking.** They use "Terrace" as a name, drop grammar ("King don't need his ears", "King'll have"), and price things aloud.
- **The smiling one:** easy, pricing. "Lit you up like a lamp, Terrace… And I hear the coat does tricks." (2:L339); "Coat like that, in Hookline. Worth more than any chip down here." (2:L357)
- **The gap-toothed one:** shouting, concentrating. "Terrace. Hand down. Put it down or he goes up the stair without his ears. King don't need his ears." (2:L385)
- **Pole Street (unplaced):** "You're up late, Terrace." (1:L23)

## The steward
- **Says "Sir" as a full reply.** He answers in single words that close the subject ("Imperial." "Up.").
- **Formal frames:** "Will there be an answer, sir?"; "Very good, sir."
- **At the count** he reads by number, flat; once by name: "Vosk?"
- **Samples:**
  - "Your father's people." (1:L291)
  - "They're logged, sir. Lates go up." / "Up," he said. (1:L523, L529)
  - "Vosk?" (2:L589)
  - "I can put unknown, sir." / "I'll leave it open, sir." (3:L23, L27)
  - "Effects aren't lates, sir… Unless there's a claim." / "A debt, sir. Owing to the house… Or to a person." (3:L93, L97)
  - He answers the phone with "Ninth, the house." and uses "madam" throughout (3:L127–139, L611).

## The rich friends (ONE)
- **Dace:** teasing, and quick with sums. "Still?" / "Ooh." / "Show them, then… Do the thing." / "They'll have him on a slab by morning. Graded." (1:L309, L385, L413, L425)
- **Lisane:** short, and holds back what she means. "You came." / "We've had an offer on the house." / "Eli. It's the last one." / "Down," she said. (1:L319, L323, L435, L443)
- **Ilder:** dry, and contradicts with no heat. "You've not been below Ancel Row in your life." / "You won't." / "I remember Vosk. He could dance." (1:L379, L403, L407). In THREE he speaks "without any heat" even about his own cruelty: "Till he wet himself. Everybody laughing. Me too." / "Every time I pick up a glass." / "Did he." / "Come back in. Dace'll stop." (3:L271–289)
- **Dace (THREE):** a performer; he shouts across the lawn and plays to the others. "He lives!" / "You went. Ilder! He went!" / "Was it you, Eli? Was it your big one?" (3:L213–229)
