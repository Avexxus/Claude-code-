# Ch04 · The Beater: originality and trope review
🌊 Claude review. I have not changed the text. Sources: ch04_STORY_ONLY, plan.md, WORLD_OVERVIEW, codex_v5.

## Ranked findings (most important first)

### 1. Nosebleed after the telekinetic save (tropes on autopilot; **recognisable**: Stranger Things' Eleven)
- **Where:** "Something warm ran out of Eli's nose onto his lip..." after he catches the crate.
- **Resembles:** a mind-power user lifts something heavy, the nose bleeds, the user wipes it with a wrist. Since 2016 this is the signature image of Eleven, and it was stock before that. Using it isn't infringement. But a reader will register it as borrowed, and here it sits on Eli's first on-page act of magic.
- **What the codex already offers:** magic costs **fatigue, like a laborer's** (the work is real work), and pushing past what the body holds is **burnout**, where "the suit burns". The paragraph before it already has the saga's version: the weight is "in his arm and in his teeth and up the back of his head". The headache from the Tower notch-up is good, and it stays.
- **Fix:** cut the blood. Let the cost be a laborer's: a forearm that shakes as if he'd carried the crate, sweat, a stomach that drops. Or put it on the bad hand (it won't close, then it cramps), so the cost lands on something Eli already lacks.

### 2. The tired-smuggler pilot's stock lines (tropes on autopilot; **passing nod** to the space-opera freighter captain)
- **Where:** "No names." / "After this I don't know you." / "In or off. I'm not asking twice."
- **Resembles:** the stock no-questions freighter captain (Han Solo and Firefly territory, and every pastiche of them). No single work owns it. The lines are generic, though, and they're the only generic thing about her: the wire in her hair, the hinge in a tin, the watch taken off the books and the tin of sweets are all specific.
- **What the codex already offers:** the far future is a galaxy-wide AI that **reads every citizen's biometrics** (the arch, the disc, "They log it"). Her reason for "no names" could be that very system rather than smuggler cool.
- **Fix:** swap one of the three for a line about logging, for example a refusal tied to the readers ("Names go on boards"). The rest of her stays as it is.

### 3. Flight from the capital tracks Mos Eisley in order (plot run; **passing nod**, no fix needed)
- **Sequence:** mentor-escort through a rough port city; a checkpoint where the mentor talks the guard round; the hangar; a junk ship the young hero laughs at ("held together with tape"); a hard lift-off past the city.
- **Why it's safe:** the beats are genre furniture, and the chapter breaks the pattern where it counts. Jon's method is unseen and mundane ("off a winch. Docks", a laugh), not a mind trick. Jon **doesn't board**. The pilot owes nothing to the hero. The payment is the watch, not credits. Lift-off is about the Tower, not pursuit.
- **Keep watching:** keep Jon's checkpoint handling looking like a bribe or a lie, never a power. Don't add a pursuit or a "she's faster than she looks" line later.

### Names checked
- **Ithe** (LOCAL): web search found no match in a known work. Its nearest real neighbour is Ithaca, too faint to matter. Clean.
- **"the beater"**: ordinary slang for a bad car. Clean.
- **Vosk** (carried over from earlier chapters): a minor Star Trek: Enterprise villain, the Na'kuhl leader in the Temporal Cold War. Nobody will make the link through a servant who carries trees, so it's a passing nod with no action. Note that Vosk's Star Trek story is also a time war, so don't give this Vosk a time angle.
- **Dace, Vennicks, Teo, Eli**: no famous match. Clean.

### Not flagged (common devices done specifically)
- The field debridement of the infected hand (the blade, the knee across the wrist, the tape) is a common device made concrete. It is not a trope on autopilot.
- Jon's spare send-off ("There's a man there'll find you" / "Do as she says, son.") is not the mentor's riddle. It's a logistics line from a man who won't explain himself, which is Jon's opacity working as intended. "Son" carries the hidden-parentage irony, and that is this saga's alone.

## Clearly original (protect these)
- **The Tower's pressure as a headache** everyone below has stopped noticing, with blue pills that do nothing. Eli finds the edges of it.
- **Window-box plants all growing toward the Tower**, the street that leans back toward it, and **Teo falling backward, toward it**. The souls-in-stone Remnant mass is shown with nobody naming it.
- **The faceless seal**: an empty oval, worn cleanest where people lean. It is the faceless Emperor as street furniture.
- **The "TERRORIST CELL" notice with grey boxes reading TO FOLLOW.**
- **CLONE STOCK — NO SOUL** cold-cases, and "Clone or the other?" / "Whatever's hot". The flesh economy runs on casual talk.
- **The orc gate disc** that reads one old loader too long and drops him, while the line steps round him, then **stays dark for Eli and Teo**. It's the biometric order and orcs-as-cattle in one image, and it's unique to this world.
- **BONE MEAL / ITHE** stencils as the first word of the destination.
- **The watch with the little moon** as the first thing Eli gives up, taken off the books.
- **The orc folding the canvas square**, and Eli's "Good—" stopping in his mouth. It cracks the dog-kindness habit without speeches.
- **"Clear of Earth"**, Earth named for the first time from a pilot's readout, after a Tower that never gets shorter in the glass.

## Verdict
**2 items to fix** (#1 the nosebleed, #2 the pilot's stock lines). Everything else is clean.
