# Reader Panel: Ch04 "The Beater" (v3)

Five readers. Context came from skimming ch01-03; only ch04 is judged.

---

## 1. The grimdark veteran

**DNF:** None. This is the chapter where the book shows its teeth.

**Where I got lost:** Nowhere that mattered. Once, at "When I say do a thing, you do it. Then.", I had to stop and work out whose voice it was (Jon's, from earlier). It's worth it.

**Loved:** The gate. "The orc behind him stepped round him into it and stood and was read and went through. The one after that did the same. Nobody bent down." Then Jon "stepped over his legs, one long step, not breaking stride." Nobody tells me how to feel, and the scene is harder for it. A close second is the debridement ("Kneel on it," Teo said, with his eyes shut). The book pays for the TK with a nosebleed and pays for the medicine with the watch. Everything costs something.

**Felt fake:** "There was a laugh in his chest. He could feel it sitting there." It's close to the line where the prose starts telling me about Eli's coping. It's soft at exactly the moment the rest of the chapter refuses to be soft.

**Tell a friend:** "The rich boy finally sees the meat grinder, and the book doesn't let him cry about it."

**On Eli:** Warm, and that's the horror of it. He has an anecdote and a plan for everything ("I'll read you the boring parts"), and he can't see that Teo is dying in front of him. He isn't bored. His attention is pointed at the wrong things.

## 2. The casual fantasy reader

**DNF:** Nearly, at the walk ("It was a long way out. They stopped for Teo three times."). It's a lot of street before anything happens. The guard at the booth pulled me back in.

**Where I got lost:**
- "The watch said nine." Why does nine matter, and why does Teo look away? I don't know what deadline that is.
- The italic line "When I say do a thing, you do it. Then." I didn't know who was speaking.
- The crate stopping in the air. Is this a power he already knew he had? I only worked it out from the nosebleed.
- "Earth" / "Clear of Earth." Wait, the city is on Earth? I wasn't sure whether that was a reveal or something I'd missed.
- "Ithe." Nobody explains it. The book means that, but I felt it too.
- The wire-knuckled orc on the step. I took him to be ch01's enforcer, but I couldn't swear to it.

**Loved:** The ship. "Under the third somebody had put a block of wood," and later, at takeoff, "Eli thought: the block of wood." It made me laugh and I could see it.

**Felt fake:** The pilot. "No names." "After this I don't know you." "In or off. I'm not asking twice." She's every gruff smuggler I've ever met.

**Tell a friend:** "They escape on a junk ship, and the gross hand scene is really gross."

**On Eli:** I liked him. He talks too much, but he plainly loves Teo. He keeps saying "Teo, look." Maybe once too often, but it felt like a kid trying to cheer his friend up.

## 3. The hater

**DNF:** No, but I kept a list.

**Where I got lost:** Not lost. Irritated.

**Loved (grudgingly):** The notice: "a row of grey boxes the size of a face. In each box, small, in the middle, it said TO FOLLOW." That's a real joke with real menace in it. And the first-aid card "with every pill on it punched out, all twelve."

**Felt fake / AI tells:**
- Rhythm on autopilot. "and... and... and" chains on nearly every paragraph. It's McCarthy cosplay, and by the tenth one it's a tic, not a style.
- Repeated one-line beats: "Teo walked." (x3 in the first section), "Teo's chest went up and down." (x2), the laugh that hurts his face (x2: "it hurt the side of his face" / "it hurt his face").
- "Teo. Look." / "Teo, look at her" / "Teo. The plants." / "Teo. Look. Elves." / "Teo. Teo. Look." / "Teo. It's black." It's a deliberate pattern, sure, but the pattern is visible from orbit.
- "He took his hand out of the pocket and looked at it then. It looked like a hand." That's fake-deep.
- "the foil standing up in little torn crowns." Writerly.
- The worldbuilding re-runs ch01: plants grow sideways, the drink runs toward the Tower, the headache, the blue pills, the white houses with their windows to the Tower. It's re-delivered here, not built on.
- Convenient plotting: the crate falls in the one moment nobody is looking up the ramp. "Nobody was looking up the ramp" is the author telling me the scene is staged.
- The stock gruff pilot (see casual reader).

**Tell a friend:** "Competent, but you can hear the metronome."

**On Eli:** He reads as performing warmth more than feeling it. The "Teo, look" reflex is mechanical enough that some readers will hear boredom or self-absorption, not love.

## 4. The literary reader

**DNF:** None.

**Where I got lost:** "Earth." Teo's single word works as subtext, but on the bench it hits the same way Eli does: "What?" That's probably the point. One more sliver of context would keep it from reading as a typo for "Earth" the planet versus "earth" the ground.

**Loved:**
- "Good—" Eli said. / It stopped there. He stood with it in his mouth. This is the chapter's turn. "Good man" to the big orc was the master's reflex. Here, after the young orc folds the canvas "corners to corners," the reflex dies in his mouth. It's interiority with no interior monologue. Superb.
- The Vosk anecdotes. First "the tree... I wanted it moved by Thursday and he had it across the yard on his back." He tells it as a love story and it's an inventory of labour. The second, Vosk standing under the wall "the whole hour in case you—", breaks off on its own. That's exactly where Eli's warmth meets the real person and fails.
- The bench and the crate. Eli moves Teo to "practically a bed." Later "Teo had gone back to his crate." Not one word of comment. That's the whole friendship.
- The empty wrist: "a band of paler skin with a line of grey in the crease of it, and the hairs flat."

**Felt fake:** "His good hand had stopped shaking. He noticed that, a long way off." "A long way off" is a stock dissociation phrase, and it's used twice in the chapter's register. And "colder than anything all day" leans on a feeling the image had already earned.

**Tics noticed:** polysyndeton; one-line paragraphs of the "Teo walked." type; "and stayed there" / "and stayed" endings (the letter hand, Teo's good hand, the guard's eyes); "went up and down"; heads "turned the same way."

**Tell a friend:** "A boy learns what his kindness costs, and the prose never says so."

**On Eli:** Warm toward the idea of his friends, blind to the people, and the chapter dramatises it precisely: he steers Teo "round a puddle Teo had already stepped round." He is not detached. The danger is only the repetition, which can tip him from oblivious into vacant.

---

## 5. The young sci-fi fan

**DNF:** Almost, in the first two sections. The market and the long walk felt like ch01 again: plants, headache, flesh market, feeds. I wanted to get to the ships.

**Where I got lost:** The gate disc. Why does it hurt the old orc more? (I got it after a beat: he lingered, like Vosk on the stair in ch01.) Also, what is the board saying? I wanted one line of the pilot's numbers.

**Loved:** The launch. The block of wood cracking away, climbing through the three lanes, the Tower that "got no shorter," punching out on top of the cloud with the Tower still going up out of the frame, then black. "Teo. It's black." That's the best sci-fi beat in the book so far. The crate catch is a close second, because it costs a nosebleed. Real rules.

**Felt fake:** "the man in house colours... lifted the slate." It's a great threat, but it goes nowhere in this chapter. I wanted the chase.

**Tell a friend:** "Slow start, then they launch a scrapheap through the clouds past a mile-high alien tower, and it rules."

**On Eli:** Kind of annoying in a good way, like the friend who won't shut up on a road trip. Not bored. Way too excited.

## Panel summary

### Top problems (flagged by 2+ personas)
1. **Repeated beats and tics** (hater, literary, casual): "Teo walked." x3, "Teo's chest went up and down." x2, the laugh that "hurt his face" x2, the "Teo. Look." refrain about six times. The refrain is the right device, but cut it to three or four so Eli reads as warm and oblivious rather than mechanical or vacant. **This is the main risk to the warm-but-blind reading.**
2. **Stock gruff pilot** (casual, hater): "No names." / "After this I don't know you." / "In or off. I'm not asking twice." Keep one; give her a line only she would say. ("Not marks. That." is already that line.)
3. **Ch01 world beats re-delivered** (hater, sci-fi fan): sideways plants, headache, pills, windows to the Tower, flesh market. Trim, or make each one do new work, as the headache-edges beat does.
4. **Unanchored references** (casual, literary): "The watch said nine," the italic Jon line, "Earth." Each needs about a half-clause of anchor for train readers.
5. **"A long way off" dissociation and "It looked like a hand."** (literary, hater, with grimdark uneasy at the laugh-in-chest line): fake-deep interiority lines. Cut or sharpen.
6. **Staged crate fall** (hater, with sci-fi fan wanting the house-colours slate paid off): "Nobody was looking up the ramp" is the author showing their hand.

### Protect these (loved by 2+)
- **The gate and the old orc**: "Nobody bent down." / Jon steps over his legs (grimdark, literary; sci-fi engaged).
- **The debridement scene**, "Kneel on it" (grimdark, literary, casual).
- **The crate catch and its nosebleed price** (grimdark, sci-fi).
- **"Good—" / It stopped there.** (literary, grimdark-adjacent); the chapter's turn.
- **The beater on its block of wood**, then "the block of wood" at takeoff (casual, sci-fi).
- **The launch, the Tower that won't end, "It's black."** (sci-fi, casual, literary).
- **The "TO FOLLOW" notice and the punched-out pill card** (hater, literary).
- **The watch for medicine, and the empty wrist** (grimdark, literary).

### Verdict on Eli
4 of 5 read him as **warm toward his idea of his friends and blind to the real people**: the puddle, the bench versus the crate, the Vosk anecdotes, "I'll read you the boring parts." Nobody read him as bored. Only the hater read him as detached, and only because the "Teo, look" refrain repeats mechanically. Trim the refrain and that reading goes away.
