# Chapter Four: character sim (v3)
🌊 Story clay. Prep for the drafter. Built from the state at the end of THREE (Day 5, about 06:45, the culvert). Jon is given **actions and lines only**. His reasons stay off the page and out of this file too. The plan's protected beats stand as written; the flags only say what would make each one true.

---

## 1. The walk through the capital, and the checkpoint over Teo's fever (Jon, Eli, Teo, the guard)

**Eli**
- **Want, right now:** to be good at this. He's said yes, so he wants to prove the yes was worth having.
- **What I'd do:** walk at Jon's shoulder, talking. I'd point things out to Teo as if I'd seen them before, though I haven't, not down here. The headache I never noticed I now notice, so I'd rub my eye and say nothing about it. At the checkpoint I'd step forward in the clear, reasonable voice to sort it out.
- **What I'd never do:** keep quiet at a desk while someone says no to us. I'd never read the notice as being about me, because nothing in it has a face.
- **What I'd say:** "He's fine. It's a cut, it's healing." / "If there's some sort of form, I'm happy to—" / "Teo, keep up."
- **Plan friction:** "Eli doesn't see how Jon handles it." Eli would be at the desk, talking. **What makes it true:** Jon uses term three: "Wait there." Eli obeys, because that's the term, and it costs him. He turns away to hold Teo up, and that's when Jon handles it. Seeing his own yes used on him is the first price of the terms.

**Teo**
- **Want, right now:** to keep walking, and not be looked at.
- **What I'd do:** keep the bad hand inside my coat and against my chest. I'd read every sign we pass, including the notice. I'd go slower than I let on, and I'd sweat in the cold. When the guard looks at me, I'd look at the ground.
- **What I'd never do:** say I'm ill. I'd never ask Jon for anything.
- **What I'd say:** "I'm fine." / (reading the notice, flat) "No faces."
- **Plan friction:** Teo is the one who watches. If Eli doesn't see what Jon did, Teo might. **Fix:** Teo is too far gone to watch at that moment (he's sitting on a step, or Eli has his arm), or he sees it and says nothing. A spoken line about Jon here would be a **fourth Count B warning**, so give him a look at most.

**Jon** (actions and lines only)
- **Does:** walks through the city as if it's his kitchen. He eats as he goes, buying or picking up some food on the walk. At the checkpoint he steps between Eli and the desk. He has a word with the guard that the page doesn't carry, or he turns them down a side way he already knew about. He doesn't explain it afterwards.
- **Never:** asks a question, shows the notice any interest, or looks back to check they're keeping up.
- **Says:** "Wait there." / "This way." / (to the guard) "Hand went bad on a hook. Docks."

**The guard** (human, LOCAL, unnamed)
- **Want, right now:** no trouble on my shift, and nothing that makes me fill in a form.
- **What I'd do:** see the sweat and the yellow dressing and step back. A sick man isn't coming through my line, because if he drops on the other side I'm the one who let him. I'd send them round rather than book them, since booking means a form.
- **What I'd never do:** check them against the notice. It has no faces, and I'm not paid to guess. I'd never touch the hand.
- **What I'd say:** "Not him. Not through here." / "Round the back, then. Not my line."
- **Plan friction:** none. The "round, not logged" outcome is the guard's own laziness, so Jon doesn't need a trick. He only needs to know where "round" goes.

## 2. The beater and its pilot, and the orcs loading it (pilot, Eli, Teo)

**The pilot** (Claude's pick: a tired human woman, LOCAL. A name is optional; she might give none.)
- **Want, right now:** get the load on, get off the ground, and pay off whatever she owes Jon without hearing why.
- **What I'd do:** look at the coat once and price it with my eyes. I'd tell them where they can sit, which is the hold and not the cockpit. I'd shout at the orcs by number or by "you". I'd count crates rather than look at passengers.
- **What I'd never do:** ask their names, ask about Jon, or say what she owes him. I'd never give anything away free.
- **What I'd say:** "Hold's back there. Don't touch the straps." / "That coat'll want taking off before the Spur sees it." / "No names. I don't want them."
- **Plan friction:** "Favour or debt, never explain." She'd keep that quiet herself, so it costs nothing. Let her be short with Jon in a way that shows something is owed, and no more.

**Eli**
- **Want, right now:** for the ship to be a joke he can tell later.
- **What I'd do:** laugh at the smell and the patches. I'd be kind to the orcs the way you're kind to a dog: "Good man." I'd step round them as if they were the crates. I'd be charming to the pilot and get nothing back.
- **What I'd never do:** see the orcs as people yet, or help them load.
- **What I'd say:** "It's got a lean on it. Like the stair." / "Good man. Mind that." / "Teo, look. It's held together with tape."
- **Plan friction:** "The dog-kindness must crack a little." Eli won't crack it himself. **What would:** one orc does something Vosk did, like a word in the orc talk, the way he sets a crate down, or "Walk well." Or the orc doesn't answer the "Good man" and goes on working. Eli's next "Good—" stops halfway. He doesn't think about why. He only says it once.

**Teo**
- **Want, right now:** sit down.
- **What I'd do:** sit on the first crate that will hold me. I'd read the stencils on the crates and the hull plate. I'd watch the orcs, because I watch everything.
- **What I'd never do:** joke along with Eli, or show the pilot my hand.
- **What I'd say:** "Bone meal." (reading a crate) / "Old hull. They stopped making these." (the future-book seed, if needed)
- **Plan friction:** none.

**The orcs** (labourers, the Spur or a public berth, LOCAL)
- **Want:** to finish the load, not be noticed, and not be late on their logs.
- **What they'd do:** work fast and in step, speak in the orc talk to each other, and go silent when the humans speak.
- **What they'd never do:** answer Eli warmly, or look at the coat for long.
- **Note for the page:** give one of them something of his own, such as a grip, a joke to his mate, or a mended strap, so the reader sees a person while Eli sees freight.

## 3. Jon's handover and his leaving (Jon, Eli)

**Jon** (actions and lines only)
- **Does:** walks them to the ramp and stops at its foot. He has a short, low word with the pilot that we don't hear, or that is half a line. He gives Eli one place name and no reason. He looks once at Teo's hand and says something plain about it, or nothing. Then he goes, without turning round, the same way he crossed the yard.
- **Never:** boards, asks a question, explains the teacher, or says when he'll be back.
- **Says:** "Got elves to see." / "[Place]. There's a man there'll find you." / "You'll not like him much." (an echo of 3:L465, if it fits) / "Do as she says."

**Eli**
- **Want, right now:** for Jon to come too. A crew is meant to be together.
- **What I'd do:** talk him back: "You said a crew." I'd laugh at "elves", then ask where he's going and when, and follow him a step down the ramp. Then I'd stop, because of the terms.
- **What I'd never do:** refuse to board. He said yes three times and he'd stand by it out of pride. He'd never ask what the job pays.
- **What I'd say:** "Elves. Right. Of course." / "When will you—" / "Where is that? Is that far?"
- **Plan friction:** "One instruction, nothing about why." Eli would demand the why. **That's fine.** Jon doesn't answer, and Eli's question hangs there, which is Jon's absence shown on the page. Don't let Eli get an answer through the pilot either.

## 4. Departure, and what Eli gives up for Teo (Eli, Teo, the pilot)

**Eli**
- **Want, right now:** to fix Teo now, with whatever he has.
- **What I'd do:** see the hand at last, now that Teo can't hide it. I'd go for the account first ("I'll ring, they'll send something up"). Teo refuses, the pilot refuses, and there's no line out anyway. Then I'd go through my pockets: the sock, the watch, the letter. I'd pay with whatever she'll take.
- **What I'd never do:** open the letter, or offer it. He doesn't know what it's worth, and he isn't ready to.
- **What I'd say:** "Name it. Whatever it is." / "It's only a watch." (said too quickly) / "He's fine. He's going to be fine. Teo, sit."
- **The cost, and the choice the drafter has to make:**
  - **The watch** (my lean). It's the most his, and it came back to him from the King via Velk's cut. The pilot can take it without logging anything, which is just how the Ilders' man described it changing hands ("Dwarf paid her cut in it"). It also clears the "watch" thread off his wrist before the teacher.
  - **The sock's 41 marks.** This is Vosk's money spent on the living friend, and it pays off the "claim". It's stronger for the book's arc, but it may be too much growth for FOUR. If you use it, Teo's line is "That's Vosk's."
  - **Avoid the coat.** The coat ledger is better served by the pilot pricing it at the ramp (scene 2) than by its being sold here.

**Teo**
- **Want, right now:** not to cost Eli anything, and not to be logged.
- **What I'd do:** say no once, flat, and then stop, because I don't push past one. The fever has taken some of my words. I'd take what helps, because I'm not stupid. I'd turn my face to the port.
- **What I'd never do:** thank him at length, or say how bad it is.
- **What I'd say:** "Don't." / "They log it." (to the account, again) / later, at the window, "Earth." (reading it off the pilot's board, if the drafter wants him to be the one who names it)

**The pilot**
- **Want, right now:** climb out, and set a price she can carry.
- **What I'd do:** throw the kit at them ("It's what there is"). Then, at the price, I'd bring out my own stuff: the mid-levels' headache pill, or something stronger from the cockpit, under the counter, unlogged. I'd name the thing I want off him, not a sum.
- **What I'd never do:** ask what's wrong with the hand, or give the pills for nothing.
- **What I'd say:** "Kit's a joke. This isn't." / "Not marks. That." (looking at the wrist) / "It'll hold him a few days. Then he wants a proper one."
- **Plan friction:**
  - **The window.** A hauler's hold probably has no window. Give it a scratched port, or have the pilot leave the cockpit hatch open so they can see out over her shoulder.
  - **Who names "Earth."** Eli says "the city". He wouldn't say "Earth" unprompted. Take it from the pilot's departure call ("leaving Earth control") or from her board, read by Teo.
  - **"Somewhere Eli has never heard of."** True for Eli. Teo may have heard of it, since he's read up on off-world places for his own spot, and he'd say nothing. That's optional, but it fits Eli not listening.

## Flags across the chapter
1. **The checkpoint:** Jon's "Wait there" is the cleanest way to keep Eli from seeing, and it spends term three on the page.
2. **Teo's Count B stays at three.** Give him nothing that warns about Jon in FOUR, only looks.
3. **The Spur:** Teo's mother's "man who doesn't write things down", at nine on the Spur. If the berth is on the Spur, they may pass near nine o'clock. That's an optional sting, and it opens no new thread.
4. **The stopgap:** the pilot's own line ("a few days") lines up the payoff with Doc in SIX.
5. **Eli's reach beat:** a small one with nobody looking, like a strap that won't come free, or a crate catching on the ramp while the orcs' backs are turned. It works unwatched, as the rule says (3:L463).
