# Sense check: the split, ONE (`v3/ch01/new_ch01_v1.md`) + TWO (`v3/ch02/new_ch02_v1.md`)
🌊 Claude proposal. Judged against `split_plan.md`, `story_state.md`, `ledger_rules.md`, `ch01/outside/grok_ch01_v9.md`, and BANK 2026-11-17. Story text only; the NOTES were read as context.

## Flags, ranked by how much they break belief

**1. The fob rule breaks inside the new text (ONE, the lawn, vs TWO, the death speech).**
> "The boy pointed the fob at him… Nothing happened in Vosk's feet. At the far end of the lawn the Ilders' man went up off the path"
- *Broken promise.* ONE now shows that a fob only works on its own house's orcs. But Vosk's dying story, "His dad's. Fob… Made me. Dance.", has the Ilder father's fob working on a boy from Eli's house. A sharp reader will catch it.
- *Fix:* give the Ilders' man one line: "Kid's fob. Does their own. His dad's does anybody's." That also plants the death speech.

**2. Why tonight? Vosk acts for the plot (ONE, paragraph 2, through to the kitchen).**
> "Eleven hundred, she'd said, in marks, not imperial."
- *A person acts for the plot.* Vosk has seen the crew on Pole Street, heard "He's eating tonight" and "That's three nights", and Velk "told you it was hot". Still he takes his master down that night, in that coat, with no reason given for not waiting.
- *Fix:* add a deadline to her price: "Tomorrow night, she'd said, or it went to someone else." Then going becomes his choice, and the risk becomes his cost.

**3. The time jump between the two POV blocks reads as night (ONE).**
> "The steward woke Eli at a quarter past eleven"
- *Clipped join.* The Vosk block ends in the late afternoon with Eli at the gate, and the chapter ends "just after eleven" at night. So the reader takes this "eleven" as 11 pm, and the courier and breakfast come out scrambled.
- *Fix:* "at a quarter past eleven that morning".

**4. The clock doesn't add up (TWO, the opening).**
> "twenty minutes later" … "Twenty past."
- *The world wouldn't produce it.* They leave just after eleven and meet Vosk twenty minutes later. Then they walk Ancel Row and the Spur, and still reach the pump house at twenty past.
- *Fix:* make it "a few minutes later" at the ramp, and "Twenty-five past" at the rail.

**5. The drift girl is one runner too many (TWO).**
- The curb boy, the crate girl, the tax boy and the drift girl all carry word ahead, and the crate girl and the drift girl both end up at the arch. They do the same job.
- *Fix:* merge them. "Up on a crate by the arch" becomes the net girl, with her pole across her knees, and she is the one who slips through the strips after "Keep it." That keeps the reach shown when nobody's watching and keeps "the coat does tricks", and it makes the betrayal come out of his kindness.

**6. Eli reports something he couldn't hear (ONE, the kitchen).**
> "she said thank you and that was it"
- *Convenience / knowledge slip.* The breakfast scene has Eli hearing only the steward's half of the call. The kitchen also retells a call the reader saw minutes ago.
- *Fix:* cut his retelling down to "Somebody rang the house to check. A woman. No one ever rings him."

**7. The courier waits three hours and nobody wakes him (ONE).**
> "She has been on the step since eight."
- *The world wouldn't produce it.* A steward who knows that seal would not let his master sleep through it, so the reader needs a cause.
- *Fix:* the steward adds "She said not to wake you." It's chilling and gives the cause.

**8. Two stopped clocks in a row slow the quickening (TWO).**
- The pump-house wait ("So it's twenty past") is followed straight away by the stalled cage. The cage carries the purse and the sock, so it stays.
- *Fix:* cut the rail exchange and keep only the foreman's "Gentlemen".

**9. The slot's cost has no cause (TWO).**
> "spit drying on his chin"
- The lates are off, so why did the slot hit him? A reader can supply the answer, but only just.
- *Fix (optional):* "The slot didn't like orcs out after the count."

**10. The purse is introduced twice (ONE).**
> "The money came in a dwarven purse, the kind with a clasp that bit"
- *Too much / a seam from v9.* The hall scene has just shown the clasp biting.
- *Fix:* "The purse was on the kitchen table next to the fruit bowl, where he could see it while he talked."

## The three risky calls
- **The drift girl: keep the beat, merge the girl.** Two separate girls going to the arch is the overplot. One girl who is helped and then sells him is the point (see #5).
- **The King's hand: keep it.** The quiet ending is the kitchen, three scenes later, and the hand pays for things. It makes Teo's urgency and "He's coming out" true, and it delivers S-019 in plain sight. Only trim the second sighting ("where the hand had another slab and was turning it over"), so that "something under the stones had begun to shove" carries it.
- **"It's the first of it": keep it.** It makes the line about the debt, his pride and the loan, and not about dying, so it works against a giveaway. If anything reads like a will it's "At the back. Against the wall.", and that pays ONE's sock, so keep that too.

## Pacing
- **ONE** is a true slow introduction. Every added scene turns something, and the danger stays at the edges.
- **TWO** builds properly from Hookline on. Its first hundred lines brake twice (#8). Trim the rail, and the quickening starts at the gate.

## Verdict: FIX FIRST
1. The fob rule (#1): one line from the Ilders' man.
2. Why tonight (#2): a deadline on Velk's price.
3. The 11:15 time slip (#3): "that morning". The twenty-past clock (#4) is a two-word fix to do in the same pass.
