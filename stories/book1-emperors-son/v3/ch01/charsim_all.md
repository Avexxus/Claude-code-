## Scene 1: the gift and the insisting (Eli, Vosk, Teo)
**What each knows here.** Eli: Vosk asked for a loan for a chip. The account paid at once, and a woman rang to check the figure. A sealed letter came, unopened. Vosk: the price, the dealer, that the King's crew are out tonight ("eating"), that they smelled him, and that his late was logged. Teo: all of Eli's part, plus the shaft rota. He has been planning since the purse came out.

**ELI**
- *Want:* to give the gift himself and see it land.
- *What I'd do:* hand him the money and fix the problems (the late, the bird, the cloth). I go because a friend would. I never ask whether he wants me there. I read every "no" as modesty.
- *Never:* let Vosk carry it alone, or ask him straight out, "do you want me there?"
- *Voice:* "Sit down. You haven't eaten." / "They know what a Terrace coat is."
- *Plot flags:* none. The scene is true to him, and his kindness (letting Vosk sleep) is what makes them late. Keep that.

**VOSK**
- *Want:* the chip, tonight, and Eli safe at home.
- *What I'd do:* say no once, sideways ("quicker on my own"), then go quiet. I can't refuse the house's son flat out, and I chose to trust him with this.
- *Never:* tell Eli about the gate shock ("Wall."), or let Eli see that he's scared of the King's crew.
- *Voice:* "It's quicker on my own." / "I know the way."
- *Plot flag, a soft one:* **he falls asleep mid-objection** ("Eli," … "Eat your bird."). That shuts his "no" down for the plot. It is earned, since he's coming off a night walk, a gate shock and a double shift. But as written it reads as the scene putting him to sleep. **Instead:** let him finish the sentence first, and make it something small and true ("Not in the coat"). Then he sleeps. That way he has said his piece and Eli talks over it, which is the flaw on the page.

**TEO**
- *Want:* Eli not to go. If he goes, Teo goes too, with a route.
- *What I'd do:* warn him once, flatly ("He didn't ask you to come"). Then do the useful thing: the rota. I won't fight a fight I've already lost.
- *Never:* tell the steward. That would get Vosk's neck searched.
- *Voice:* "He didn't ask you to come." / "Are you going to open that."
- *Plot flag:* **the floating apple core.** It shows Teo has some small reach. Later, with a cutter at Eli's ear, he swings a ladle and never tries it. Readers will ask why. **Either** give it a cost or a rule (it only comes when he isn't trying, so under fear it's gone, like Eli's), **or** cut the core. As it stands, Teo acts for the plot by forgetting he has it.

## Scene 2: Velk's, the fitting, the hot chip (Velk, Vosk, Eli, Teo)
**What each knows here.** Velk: she agreed to a lone orc and tonight three turned up, one of them a Terrace coat. The King is eating. She owes the King a cut (the marked tin). Vosk: the price from last night. Eli: nothing about this world. He thinks the price is the price. Teo: the coat is being clocked, and a girl went in through the strips.

**VELK**
- *Want:* paid, done, gone, and deniable.
- *What I'd do:* raise the price for the risk, work fast, and send them out the way I'm not going. Then say I never did it.
- *Never:* warn them in so many words, or stay to see what happens.
- *Voice:* "It was eleven last night." / "You understand what you've bought him." / "And I didn't fit it."
- *Plot flag:* **"Out the front. Not the back."** It reads as either mercy or a sell-out, and the scene doesn't choose. She would be sending them off her own escape route, not saving them. Add one beat that shows it's for her: she checks the back curtain first, or the boy's cup is still warm. Then the line is character, not a signpost.
- *Plot flag:* **the boy and "Tax."** The boy points Eli out to the crew and gets paid for it. Velk knew where he was going. She'd let it happen, because it's her tax, so this is fine. Just make sure her "Tax" lands as "I pay him," not as a riddle.

**VOSK**
- *Want:* the leash out. Then, once he's lit, to understand what he's hearing.
- *What I'd do:* put my own arms in the straps, take the pain in my own words, and lie to Eli about the hurt ("No").
- *Never:* tell Eli what it costs him, or say the word "network."
- *Voice:* "What's that." / "And them. All of them. It's loud." / "It's gone quiet."
- *Plot flags:* none. "No" is the best line in the scene, because he's protecting the gift-giver.

**ELI**
- *Want:* for it to be worth it.
- *What I'd do:* pay without bargaining, give up the watch, say "yes" without listening, and laugh when it works.
- *Never:* hear Velk's question as a question.
- *Voice:* "Eleven hundred and a watch. Teo. Look at him."
- *Plot flags:* none.

**TEO**
- *Want:* to be gone before whatever the boy went for gets here.
- *What I'd do:* watch the door, count the music, and give the third warning, flat and calm ("We go. Now."). When Eli waves it off, I stop arguing.
- *Never:* drag Eli out by force this early. That's a card he only plays once.
- *Plot flags:* none. The three warnings land (come / coat / now).

## Scene 3: the King, the hostage, Vosk spends himself (King, young man, Vosk, Eli, Teo)
**What each knows here.** King: a new lit chip has gone in nearby, and a Terrace coat paid for it. Young man: same, plus his pay and the King's mood. Vosk: he's lit, the King is pulling on every chip in the room, and Eli won't leave. Eli: none of it. Teo: the bridge is the way out.

**THE KING** (play him for presence only, and keep his nature open)
- *Want:* the new chip, eaten. The coat as something he can trade.
- *What I'd do:* say nothing, weigh, and take from every neck in the room. Break what resists. Give ground only to what might kill me.
- *Never:* speak, explain, or chase.
- *Plot flag:* **"and he'll have that" (Eli).** Why would he want a Terrace son? The draft hints at money ("a purse somebody else had dropped"), but it also has the cook-and-bird look, which says he wants to eat Eli. Pick one in the notes. If it's ransom, the "King don't need his ears" line already fits. If he senses something in Eli (the light), that's a seed, and it has to be logged as an open question.
- *Plot flag:* **the arch falls on him by luck.** "He wasn't pushing it anywhere" means the collapse is an accident. That takes the choice away from Vosk at the exact moment the [P] beat says the choice is his. **Instead:** Vosk looks up once at the sagging ceiling and walks *at the arch*, not only at the King. Then "I picked it" covers this too.

**THE YOUNG MAN** (the crew: the smiler and the one who takes the hostage)
- *Want:* the King pleased, and his own cut. Maybe a chip of his own one day.
- *What I'd do:* taunt the lit orc, ignore the human, and take the soft target when the hard one won't kneel.
- *Never:* actually cut the hostage badly. The King wants him whole.
- *Voice:* "Lit you up like a lamp, Terrace." / "King don't need his ears."
- *Plot flag, a small one:* "He was only getting up for the coat. Then the whole market felt it go in." That's him explaining things for the reader. He'd taunt, not explain. Cut it to the first clause, or let Velk's "Tax" carry it.
- *Clarity flag:* "the young man" means two different people (the smiler, then the hostage-taker who shouts). Give the second one a tag, like the split ear from earlier or the wire on his knuckles.

**VOSK**
- *Want:* Eli out. Nothing else.
- *What I'd do:* give the order to Teo, not Eli, because Eli won't listen. Block the pull. Drop everything the moment Eli is at risk. Then spend it all.
- *Never:* run, or leave Eli with the crew.
- *Voice:* "Teo. Take him up the stair." / "Teo."
- *Plot flags:* only the arch (above). Turning his head toward the cutter is pure Vosk. Keep it.

**ELI** [P: freezes, cowardly]
- *Want:* Vosk with him, and the cutter gone.
- *What I'd do:* bargain in the steward voice, then claim him ("He's with me"), then put my hand up. Nothing comes.
- *Never:* run *to* Vosk. Under a blade at his ear, his body won't go.
- *Voice:* "If there's some sort of fee, I'm happy to—" / "He's with me."
- *Where the beat pulls against the draft:* the [P] beat says **cowardly**. The draft gives a failed try (hand up, nothing), which is a freeze but not cowardice. Leave the hand. Vosk's pride needs it, because he saw the hand go up ("waiting for it"). What would make cowardly true without losing that: one beat where Eli picks himself. Either he lets Teo drag him and doesn't fight it, or, with the cutter in, he says "Vosk, put it down." That one is his voice and his flaw, and it's what drops Vosk's guard before the arm breaks. Patrick's call; the beat stays.

**TEO** [P: cool]
- *Want:* Eli on the bridge.
- *What I'd do:* breathe the exam breath, use whatever's to hand, hit carefully, and pull.
- *Never:* go toward Vosk.
- *Voice:* "Bridge."
- *Plot flags:* only the apple core (Scene 1).

**The death speech [P]**
- It's true to Vosk: the Ilder fob, the boy off the step, "He's mine. Leave him," and "I picked it" / "Nobody spent me." Eli's "I don't remember" is the flaw, said out loud.
- *Where the beat pulls:* Patrick asked for an **eloquent** speech. The draft is broken up, which is honest for a crushed chest. What would make eloquent true: the draft already says "the breath he'd been keeping for it." Spend that breath on **one whole, clean sentence** (the proud line), and keep the rest broken.
- *Is Vosk's pride true?* Yes, but only because he saw the hand go up. It is pride in the boy who came off the step, not in what Eli did tonight. Keep the hand-up for this reason.

## Scene 4: the climb, the gate, the night (Eli, Teo)
**What each knows here.** Both of them know Vosk is dead, the chip was taken off his body, and the King may be alive. Eli also knows that the steward was told Vosk was with him.

**ELI**
- *Want:* nothing. To stop.
- *What I'd do:* climb, shake, let the gate read me, sit, and hold the letter down.
- *Never:* open the letter tonight. "They can have tonight" still holds, and now it hurts.
- *Voice:* (nothing). He has no lines here. That's right.
- *Plot flag:* **the account.** A rich boy in shock still has one reflex: call someone, the clinic or the flyer. That he doesn't is fine. But one half-thought of it, then dropped (the account would ask what it was for), would show that he can't reach for his father's people now. That would be his choice, not the plot leaving it out.

**TEO**
- *Want:* Eli home with clean hands, and to fall apart only after that.
- *What I'd do:* buy water, be sick neatly, scrub his hands too long, and say something about the brush.
- *Never:* say "I told you." Not tonight. (Save it for the [P] "3 times" line.)
- *Voice:* "Right." / "She'll want to know what happened to her brush."
- *Plot flags:* none.

**The steward (offstage)**
- *Plot flag:* Eli told him at breakfast that Vosk was with him. At the count, Vosk's number gets no answer. A steward would look up at the lit kitchen window, or come up. Silence that isn't explained reads as the plot staying away. **Instead:** keep the mark, but add the look. He glances up at the kitchen light and makes the mark anyway. That's the father's reach (nobody asks), on the page, unexplained.
