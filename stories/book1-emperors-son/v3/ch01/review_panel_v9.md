# Review panel v9: ch01 "The Gift"

Source read: `ch01_STORY_ONLY.md` only. 🌊 Claude proposal, not canon.

---

## 1. The grimdark veteran

**DNF:** None. The ending held me: Eli's power failing, Vosk dying for someone else's coat, the crewman's hook going into the corpse's neck for the chip, and Eli choosing *not* to ring the account. That's consequence, and the book doesn't flinch from it.

**Lost:** Nowhere that mattered. I didn't need the King explained, and I liked that.

**Loved:** "He wasn't laughing. He was interested." The nine-year-old with the fob is the whole world in two sentences. Close second: Teo hitting the gap-toothed one "carefully, as if driving a peg." And "He sat with that, and left it." Eli finally learns the account's lesson, and the book doesn't say so.

**Fake:** "I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into." It's a speech, and it tells me how to feel. Vosk talks in fragments all chapter ("He asked was you mine", "Bit him. Wouldn't let go"), and then he hands over a polished, grammatical epitaph. "It came out whole, all at once" admits it was rehearsed, but it's still the softest line in a hard chapter. "I picked it" does the same job in three words. Also the child "minding the other with great seriousness" and the "Real, not clone" shopper are cosy Pratchett jokes that let air out of the Scald.

**To a friend:** "Finally, a privileged-boy fantasy where the boy's good intentions get his friend killed and nobody forgives him for it."

---

## 2. The casual fantasy reader

**DNF:** None, but I nearly drifted in paragraph two. "Vosk walked up out of it with the dwarf's price in his head..." is one huge sentence, and I didn't know what he was buying until the surgery forty minutes later.

**Lost:**
- What the price was *for*. "Two fingers on the back of his neck to show him where" only makes sense in hindsight.
- Why Eli's hand stopped working in the fight. He floated an apple core and stood a coin up, then nothing. Was it the Scald, the chip, the King, fear? I couldn't tell if this was a plot point or my own misreading.
- "Whatever was in them went out through the backs of their necks." What went out? I had to reread it.
- Who the King is. "Something big shifted its weight" early on is creepy, but I only connected it to "the King" at the market.
- "And I didn't fit it." Took me a second.

**Loved:** The kitchen scene, because it finally told me who's who: Eli, Teo eating his apple from the bottom up, Vosk ducking through a door he clears. Then "Hold his head" in the surgery. I was gripping my phone.

**Fake:** Nothing jumped out. I actually *liked* "I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into." It made me cry on the train. (The veteran and I disagree.)

**To a friend:** "It's slow to get going, but the friendship is the real thing and the ending wrecked me."

## 3. The hater

**DNF:** I didn't put it down, which annoys me.

**Lost:** Not lost. Suspicious.

**Loved (grudgingly):** The King: "chips... sewn into him one by one through the skin with wire, the way you'd sew buttons on a coat... every one, faintly, was moving." That's a real image, not a stock monster. "Warm like a horse," for the bridge, too.

**Fake / slop tells:**
- **The "never/ever" superlative tic.** "The thumb had never once been healed", "never had eleven hundred of anything" (twice), "He'd never had it handed to him", "harder than he'd ever held anything", "quicker than he'd ever seen anybody count anything", "a sound Eli had never heard him make", "a dust... that the Terrace had never seen". That's at least eight. It's a crutch.
- **The "since they were" habit tag.** The apple eaten bottom-up "since they were seven", the neck squeeze "since they were boys", the window "since he was twelve", ducking "since he'd got his growth". It's the same move used four times to fake history.
- **Teo's exam simile, used twice.** "The calm it went when an exam was going badly" and "as Teo had breathed through the oral finals".
- **The deathbed backstory dump.** The Ilder fob, the bit hand, "He's mine. Leave him". Key history arrives by dying confession, and that's a trope. Then "I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into" is a fridge-magnet line.
- **Convenient plotting.** Teo already has the shaft rota up before anyone asks. Teo's one-line bluff ("Want him coming out to find you over here with us?") sends the whole crew away.
- **Whimsy as a quirk-generator.** The child minding the single shoe "with great seriousness", and the boy who "gave the bigger half away" right after the fused hand. It reads like a quota of tenderness.

**To a friend:** "Better than I wanted it to be, but count the 'nevers'."

## 4. The literary reader

**DNF:** None.

**Lost:** Nowhere. The gaps are doing work: what Eli's power is, why it fails, and what the letter says.

**Loved:**
- The closing count: "A number, and somebody answered. / A number." The steward's "Vosk?", and Eli's hands on the bowl. It's a perfect silent refusal.
- "You know what you've—" / "Yes," said Eli. It's a lie, and he doesn't know he's telling it.
- The Hurrak song with the name-in-the-mouth verse left out, which quietly sets up "Nobody. Spent me."
- "He leaned back and stared up at it, because it was there... He didn't feel anything." Then the new chip, and "It's right there." The chip changing what Vosk can perceive is the structural spine, and it's handled with restraint.

**Tics:**
- Polysyndeton is the house style ("and... and... and"). It works in the fight, but in paragraph two and the King's entrance it smothers sentence rhythm. Vary it.
- The "ever/never" superlatives and the "since they were" tags (both flagged by the hater) are real. Cut half of each.
- "Eli laughed" appears at least four times. That's deliberate for his obliviousness, but it's becoming a stage direction.
- "Somebody had thrown filth at the ribbon... over the word VALUES." That's a touch on the nose.

**Fake:** "I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into." It breaks Vosk's idiolect. The surrounding fragments are some of the best dialogue in the chapter, and this sentence sounds like the author stepping in. If it stays, it needs to sound more like Vosk: shorter, rougher, his grammar.

**To a friend:** "It's restrained and exact, and the last page is a model of how to end a chapter without saying anything."

## 5. The young sci-fi fan

**DNF risk:** The kitchen. "The account had come with the house, and the house had come with Eli..." Two people talking about money and a letter for ~100 lines. I skimmed until the knock. The Long Stair stretch (lines 43–65) also sags, though the flesh market saved it.

**Lost:** Eli's telekinesis. What is it, how does it work, and why did it die exactly when it was needed? Is the Tower the power source? Is Vosk's new chip now a mage chip? I want *one* more clue.

**Loved:**
- The flesh market: "CLONE STOCK — NO SOUL", the eyes sorted by colour, "an orc arm sold for... less than a goat's". Also the lift with a twitching meat core.
- The chip swap: "Soldier's. Leash is out," and the filed-off LEGION mark.
- The King with his sewn-on chips, and the power duel. Vosk's palm splitting, the arm breaking in three places, the arch coming down.
- The crewman putting a hook into Vosk's new chip after he dies. That's brutal and smart: the chip is the loot.

**Fake:** "I'm proud to die..." It made me roll my eyes. Also, the drift, the readers and the Tower are cool, but the chapter keeps describing them without *doing* anything with drift.

**To a friend:** "Underground orc chip-surgery and a guy wearing hundreds of stolen brain chips. Skip the kitchen bit."

## Panel summary

### Top problems (flagged by 2+)
1. **"I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into."** Flagged by the grimdark veteran, hater, literary reader and sci-fi fan (4/5). It breaks Vosk's fragmentary voice and tells the reader how to feel. Only the casual reader loved it, so this is a real split, but the majority says cut or roughen it. "I picked it" already carries the weight.
2. **"Never/ever" superlative tic (8+ instances)** and **"since they were..." habit tags (4).** Flagged by the hater and literary reader. Cut by half.
3. **Eli's power failing is unexplained.** Flagged by the casual reader and sci-fi fan. It reads as either a deliberate mystery or a hole. One planted hint would settle it.
4. **Cosy whimsy beats** (the shoe-minding child, the "Real, not clone" shopper). Flagged by the grimdark veteran and hater. They soften the Scald. Keep one at most.
5. **Paragraph two's mega-sentence** and the withheld purpose of "the price." Flagged by the casual reader and literary reader (as polysyndeton overload).
6. **Kitchen exposition sag** (the account backstory). Flagged by the sci-fi fan, and the casual reader liked it only for orientation. Trim the account paragraph.
7. **Teo's exam simile used twice.** Flagged by the hater and literary reader.

### Protect (loved by 2+)
- **"He wasn't laughing. He was interested."** (grimdark veteran, literary reader, hater implicitly)
- **The chip surgery, "Hold his head" through "Leash is out."** (casual reader, sci-fi fan, grimdark veteran)
- **The King's sewn-chip body.** (hater, sci-fi fan)
- **Teo with the ladle, "as if driving a peg."** (grimdark veteran, sci-fi fan)
- **The hook into Vosk's corpse for the chip.** (grimdark veteran, sci-fi fan)
- **The ending: the account not rung, "A number.", "Vosk?", hands on the bowl.** (grimdark veteran, literary reader, casual reader)
- **"I picked it."** (grimdark veteran, literary reader by implication)
