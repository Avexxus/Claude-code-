# Chapter Seven plan (v3): Claude's calls
🌊 Story clay. Builds on locked ONE–SIX. Sources: CATCHUP §4–5 [P]; BANK 11-16 on the master; BANK 11-21 (Jon checks in by comm; the Eli calibration).

## SEVEN · Listen (about 7–8k words)
**Job:** back to the master; the training actually starts; Jon checks in by comm (he doesn't return); Seffa chooses to come further; the next destination is set (the door: the rift and the prison, ch8).

1. **Back at the yard on Ithe,** with Teo breathing ("Bring him back breathing"). The master looks at Teo, then at Doc, and something in his face goes still for a moment. Don't explain it; it's resonance only. He takes Eli on, on his terms.
2. **Training, in scenes, not a montage:**
   - "Listen, don't push." The cranes, timing loads.
   - Eli fails, loud. The master: "Don't use more than you can control" (Patrick's line, used once and earned).
   - **The moth lesson:** find a moth blind, by listening. The early moth/wasp imagery in FIVE was changed so this stays fresh.
   - Small, uneven progress, with the hand's recovery matched to the ledger.
   - Eli is warm about his idea of the master, and doesn't hear what the master is actually telling him.
3. **Jon's check-in.** A crackling comm: a cheap handset Jon left in the coat that Eli never found, or a call routed through the yard's relay board. That's the first time Eli has heard him since the berth.
   - He's warm and sideways ("love ya", "son"). He's somewhere else, and there are sounds behind him. He knows how Teo is without being told.
   - He gives one instruction: where the door is (a world, a place) and when ("the ninth").
   - He's gone before Eli's questions. Never interpret him.
   - Teo hears the end of the call.
4. **Seffa's choice.** She pays off "Somebody's got to feed you". Oskel is staying behind with his chip, so it costs her a brother. Show the cost without speeches.
5. **The master won't come.** "I don't leave this yard." Hold that refusal; his later boarding pays it off.
   - **End:** leaving Ithe: Eli, Teo, Doc and Seffa. The master at the gate, eyes shut, listening to them go.
