## Must decide before drafting

### P1. The comm device: nothing on the page allows it yet, and route (a) is contradicted (world rules / factual)
- **Plan §3:** "A crackling comm: a cheap handset Jon left in the coat that Eli never found, or a call routed through the yard's relay board."
- **BANK 11-21:** "Jon checks in over a comm or relay. He doesn't return in person yet."
- **What the page has.** No call, handset or relay has been shown on Ithe or Ammet. On Earth there are house phones ("Ninth, the house.", 3:L127) and the account's calls (1:L299; 3:L131–145). The pilot's board speaks "in its numbers voice" (5:L7). From the beater: "Nothing rings out of here," (4:L411). On Ithe Eli has no device and is still looking: "There'll be a way to ring from here, there always is" (5:L489). The only signals shown on Ithe are the shift horns (5:L67, L281).
- **Route (a), the handset in the coat, fails as written.** Every pocket of the second-best coat has been searched to the bottom on the page:
  - the sock pocket: "found the pocket of the folded coat… and pushed the sock in, down to the bottom" (3:L119); "His hand had gone into his coat pocket already, down to the bottom, to the sock." (4:L435);
  - the other pocket: "Eli felt in the coat, the other pocket, and found the pills loose at the bottom" (5:L287); "Teo took the last two pills out of the coat pocket himself" (5:L367);
  - the inside pocket: "He got the letter out of the inside pocket first" (5:L335);
  - the whole coat: the pilot looked it over, "The collar. The cuffs. The cloth across the shoulders… The buttons." (4:L209); Seffa "got the coat off him and folded it" (6:L225).
  - Jon's only contact with the coat on the page is passing it: "On the step inside it the second-best coat lay folded in four… and he stepped over it and out." (3:L597). He had the kitchen at dawn (3:L375), so the chance is there, but nothing shows it.
  - **And the coat is on Teo now** (5:L335; 6:L467, L491), not Eli. Whoever finds it, it isn't Eli.
- **Route (b), the yard's relay board, has no plant.** The yard on the page: the open-fronted shed of pallets, the water butt, the bench, the far shed with the crate and the strap (5:L151–153, L189, L547–549). There's no board. Worse, a call *to the master's yard* tells the reader that Jon knows where they are and knows the master. That opens a new question (is the master Jon's "man there'll find you", 4:L239?), against the master's own "Who sent you" (5:L215). And once a board exists in the yard, Eli's promised account call is right there (C1).
- **Smallest fixes (pick one):**
  1. **The lining.** Keep route (a), but the handset is *in the lining* or sewn into the hem, not a pocket. The lining is on the page: "He wiped the wrist on the lining of the coat" (4:L313). It is found by **Teo** (who wears the coat) or **Seffa** (who handles it) when it first buzzes. That fits "Teo hears the end of the call", and it explains why Eli never found it. Silence on the beater is covered by "Nothing rings out of here" (4:L411). It costs one line of plausibility: Jon sewed it there and said nothing (record it; don't interpret it).
  2. **The rows.** The call comes in on Ithe's crane system, which the horns imply, at a row office. **Oskel** comes up the Cut "at the same loose fast pace" (5:L437) with "There's a call for you down on row four. For the one in the coat." It uses what's on the page (row four, 5:L415; Oskel's runs, 5:L409), keeps the master clear of Jon, and gives Oskel a scene before Seffa leaves him (§4). It still says Jon knows they're on Ithe, which he does (4:L239).
  3. Route (b) as planned. If so, plant the board on arrival, have Eli see it and think of the account (C1), and accept the new question about the master and Jon. Log it under the loose-ends rule.
- **Also log:** R-040 ("Nothing rings out of here") covers only the beater. Whatever route is picked becomes a new rule. Does it ring both ways? Can the account be rung on it? Is it logged (R-017)?

### P2. "Don't use more than you can control" has already been used (plot logic)
- **Plan §2:** "The master: 'Don't use more than you can control' (Patrick's line, used once and earned)."
- **Page:** "'Don't use more than you can control,' he said, and turned his back." (5:L245), after the pallet. The state and voice notes log it as the master's sample line.
- **Problem:** SEVEN would be the second use, not the once.
- **Fix (author's call, since it's Patrick's line):** (a) SEVEN uses it as a deliberate *callback*. The master repeats it word for word after Eli's loud failure, and the plan should read "said again, now earned". Or (b) SEVEN gives the master a different line and lets 5:L245 stand as the one use. Don't paraphrase it, since the exact wording is the master's voice.

### P3. "I don't leave this yard": on the page he has already stepped out of it (characterization / factual)
- **Plan §5:** "The master won't come. 'I don't leave this yard.' Hold that refusal; his later boarding pays it off."
- **Page:** Day 8: "He came out into the rain and across the yard… and out of the gate, and Eli came after him. In the lane he stood over Teo in the mud." (5:L559). Then "The old man turned and went back in through his gate." (5:L585).
- **Problem:** a reader who remembers FIVE will hear the line as already broken, for Teo, a few days ago. That might be the point, but it isn't marked.
- **Fix (pick one):** "I don't leave the Cut." / "I don't leave Ithe." (true to the page). Or keep "this yard" and let Eli or Teo answer it with the lane ("You came out for him."). Or the narration lets him stand exactly at the gate line as he says it. The master's shape holds either way: flat, no question mark (5:L215).

### P4. The master's reaction to Doc: "something in his face goes still" won't read as a change (characterization / narrative style)
- **Plan §1:** "The master looks at Teo, then at Doc, and something in his face goes still for a moment. Don't explain it; it's resonance only."
- **Page:** the master's face never moves on the page. His habit is his *eyes*. He looks past people, at the gate: "looked past Eli, at the gate" (5:L227, L243, L575). He has looked at a face once: "The old man looked at him. The first time. At Eli's face, not past it" (5:L555). At rest his hands work the strap "stitch by stitch, and his eyes shut" (5:L189). A face that is already still can't "go still".
- **Also on the page:** he has seen Teo's hand. On Day 8 "He took in the arm thrown out, the tape, the fingers… A long time, and he touched nothing." (5:L559). Now the tape is gone and the palm is "pink now and closed at the edges, and the fingers lying there, ordinary" (6:L367), after about two days. He asked for "breathing" (5:L577). So the look should go **Teo's hand → Doc**, in that order, as the plan has it.
- **What he could know.** Seffa, his cook for "Ten years" (5:L371), took her mother to the gnome "Last winter" (5:L511). So the master may know of Doc by name or by report. The page doesn't say he does.
- **Rule watch (R-047 / R-044):** the master can feel another's reach: "he could feel the size of the hand. There was no end to it." (5:L179). If his stillness reads as *sensing* power in Doc, the narration is one step from calling Doc's healing a reach. R-047 says never. Keep it to what Eli sees.
- **Smallest fix:** break one of his habits instead of moving his face. Two options: he **looks at Doc**, not past him (the second face he's looked at in the book), and holds it; or **the strap stops** under his fingers mid-stitch, then goes on. Either is resonance with no explanation. Eli, per the calibration, reads it as the master being impressed.

## Carry into the draft

### C1. The account call is due the moment the master says yes (plot logic / characterization)
- **Page:** "There'll be a call out… As soon as he's said. There'll be a way to ring from here, there always is" (5:L489); "When he's said yes." (5:L499); "There's an account as well, at home" (to Doc, 6:L335).
- **Plan Must:** "The account solves nothing."
- **Carry:** if SEVEN has the master's yes *and* a comm (P1), Eli will try to ring the account. That's his own promise, and it's on brand. Either show the attempt failing (a new rule, since R-040 is beater-only; it would also log, per R-017), or show him forgetting it in the glow of the yes. The forgetting fits the calibration best: he promises and doesn't do. Silence alone will read as a slip.

### C2. Jon "knows how Teo is without being told" (factual; R-034)
- **Page:** Jon last saw the hand on Day 5: "That'll want looking at." (4:L245). Teo was healed on Ammet on Day 9, in a house with "No reader. He's not on any list." (6:L119), and on a barge where "He didn't write anything down." (6:L109).
- **Carry:** that's what makes the beat land. Nothing on the grid recorded the healing. So Eli must not say it first, and the draft must not explain it. Teo already holds Jon's "want looking at" against him (6:L53), so Teo hearing Jon know is the sharper beat. Record only what Jon says.
- Eli will ask about "the man there'll find you" (4:L239) and Pask (5:L91–101). The plan has Jon gone before the questions, which matches FOUR: "When will you—" with no answer (4:L241–247).

### C3. "the ninth": no date has been on the page, and "nine" is taken (world / factual)
- **Plan §3:** "where the door is… and when ('the ninth')."
- **Page:** time is counted by weekday ("Thursday", 4:L91; 5:L435; 6:L195) and by horns and light. No day-of-month has ever appeared. "Ninth" and "nine" already belong to other things: **Ninth Terrace** and **Ninth Spur** (home), and Teo's mother's "Nine. He's to be there at nine." (3:L577), which Teo repeats in delirium (5:L541).
- **Carry:** "the ninth" would be the first calendar date in the book, and it echoes home and Teo's mother. If the echo is wanted, keep it. If not: "Thursday week", "nine days", or a horn count.

### C4. The clock on arrival (timeline)
