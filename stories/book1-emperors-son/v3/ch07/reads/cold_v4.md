# Cold read: ch07 "Listen" (draft_v4_guarded.md)

Read as a stranger. I had only the chapter text. I did not open any other file.

## 1. Where I got lost

- **Who is who.** Seffa, Teo, Eli, Doc, the old man, Oskel, Jon, the tall boy and "the other three boys" all arrive in the first two pages with no introduction. I could not tell which of them are family, which are employers, and which are just workers. The old man is never named at all, and I was not sure he was the teacher until Eli says so.
- **The journey and the place.** "The Cut", "the yard", "the far shed", "row four", "the heaps", "the old pit", "the Terrace", "Ithe", "the barge" and "the door" are all used as if I know them. I could not form a map. I did not know if the Cut is a district, a canal, a rail cut, or a work site.
- **Time.** "The ninth", "Count's on the ninth", "On the ninth", "Two days at the cranes, and it was Thursday", "You said Thursday", "the one with the onions", "since the barge". I could not tell whether "the ninth" is a day of the month, a shift, a count, or a dial. I could not reconstruct how many days pass.
- **The phone scene.** I did not know who is on the line, who Jon is in relation to Eli or Teo, or what "the son" means in "Put the son on". I did not know who "the coat" is (it seems to be Teo's, but the chapter never says why it matters).
- **"The fit."** Seffa says "He had the fit. Somebody's got to be on him when it comes back." I did not know who had it. This is the most important sentence in the last section and it is never set up.
- **Eli's injury.** The "swollen side of his face" and "the bad one" hand are never explained. At line 99 "the bad one" could be his hand, but the shut hand at line 113 reads like it is the one that did the work. I could not tell which hand is damaged or why.

## 2. Unexplained terms and rules

- **"The thing in him" / "his thing".** This is Eli's power. It is described as a hand-like reach that finds things, closes on them, and kills them. The chapter never names it or says what it is. The only rule I could extract is: he can feel a moth, and when he shuts his hand the moth dies, and the "shut hand" cannot open again until the cold passes. That is interesting, but I do not know what it costs or what it is supposed to do.
- **"Don't use more than you can control."** The old man says this twice. It is an important rule and it is delivered as a throwaway. I did not know what "more" means, or what happens if Eli uses too much.
- **"Listen" and "Don't look. You're looking. Your eyes are in the way."** The teaching method. I found it poetic but it reads as a riddle, not a rule. I could not tell what "listening" is for the reader's purposes: hearing, sensing, or something else.
- **The chip behind Oskel's ear, "the square" shaved behind the ear.** Some kind of mark or implant. Never explained. It seems to matter to the world.
- **"The account".** Eli says "I'll have the account rung". I did not know what the account is, who holds it, or why Eli is obsessed with it.
- **"The Terrace"** and "which fork". A class or place reference. Unexplained.
- **"Doc."** Eli calls him a healer and "he sits with the—". Doc says "Doc" as if that is his name. He keeps a bag. He says "I gave [the vinegar] to a woman" and "my brother had a goat". I could not tell if Doc is a medical man, a nickname, or a stranger the group has picked up.
- **"Not that you will."** Doc's joke about keeping the arm dry. Fine as a line, but without knowing the injury's seriousness I could not feel the stakes.
- **The chip, the coat, the yellow on the cuffs, the boots laced to the top.** Teo's clothes carry a lot of signal (the coat is "the coat" for Jon) and I could not decode it.

## 3. "Why doesn't X just…?"

- Why doesn't Eli just ask Teo what Jon said? Teo "said nothing" and Eli keeps asking about it. It is obvious Eli is worried. It is also obvious Teo is hiding something. Why doesn't either of them say it?
- Why doesn't Jon just say what "the door" is? He says "The door. By the old pit. On the ninth." and hangs up. The phone is the only channel and it is wasted.
- Why doesn't the old man just tell Eli what the rule is? He says "Don't use more than you can control" and "Listen", then closes his eyes. If he is the teacher he is withholding instruction in a way that looks like cruelty, not teaching.
- Why doesn't Seffa say what she is going to do before she does it? She says "I'm going with them" to the old man, not to Eli, and nobody seems surprised.
- Why does the row man leave the call on the board and go? He "put the call through and gone". That is so convenient that it reads like the author moving furniture.
- Why doesn't Doc use clean water on the arm? He says "Not that. Clean water. I've no vinegar." That sentence suggests water is not clean. It is confusing.
- Why doesn't Teo just stop Eli at the crane? Teo "knows the hook" and "watches the hook". He sees the bundle start to swing before Eli moves. He does nothing until it is too late.
- Why does the old man never stop the boys from going to the door, or make Eli stay? He can clearly say no ("No.") but he does not stop anybody. He just lets them go.

## 4. Contradictions and small inconsistencies

- **The old man "never turned his back on anything"** (line 119). Two lines earlier he "turned and went back to the far shed" (line 59). Also he speaks "with his back already turned" at the pallet (line 119). This is a deliberate-looking line that the text then contradicts.
- **"Pink" on Teo's jaw** (line 15). "Where the pink had run up under his jaw there was only a shine" is ambiguous. I assumed it was Teo, but the sentence sits in Eli's point of view and could be Eli.
- **Oskel's "shift".** He "had been told to go back to his shift at two" and "came up again at dark". He is also described as on "his break" at noon. Fine, but the times are compressed and I could not track them.
- **"Two days at the cranes, and it was Thursday."** The chapter moves from a moth night to "Two days" with no transition. Also the onion joke ("You said the one with the onions") refers to a prior conversation I never saw.
- **Seffa's ironing of the cloth.** "She put the cloth on the rail and let it go." Fine. But she has "her sleeves up to the elbow" at the trough and then "her bag on her shoulder" at the end. OK, but she is also "wringing out the cloths from the boys' row" right after Eli claims the old man "said he was quick", and the old man never said it. This is Eli lying to himself. The text is consistent with that. I still flag it as a possible unintentional contradiction because it is not clear the reader should trust Eli.
- **The old man's gate.** Line 17 "Pask's gate was still chained" (a different gate, correct), line 17 "the old man's gate stood open a hand's width, the way it had been left the day they went for the barge". Consistent. Fine.
- **"The boys eat at dark"** (line 365) and "The boys breathed" in the dark. Fine.
- **Eli "shut his hand" and "the moth fell... lifted one wing"** vs. line 201 "Eli kept his eyes off the boards." Consistent, but the emotional beat is strong enough that I expected a consequence I did not get.
- **"The four boys"** (line 61) and "The tall boy stood up" and "the other three". Fine. But at line 413 "The tall boy was at the yard gate". Consistent.
- **Ledger at the end.** Lines 421–423: "<!-- LEDGER -->" and "Ledger for draft_v2 is carried unchanged... Not story text." This is an authoring note inside the draft. It is not story text, and it breaks the frame for any reader. If it is meant to be stripped before publication, it should be.

## 5. Where I stopped believing a character

- **Eli.** He is the most believable and the least believable at once. He insists he is listening while he is not. He claims the old man "said he was quick" when he did not. He says "He's wonderful" while the boy's arm is still bleeding. He keeps saying "Honestly. I'm listening all the time." I believed the self-deception. I did not believe that a man who has just maimed a boy would be this cheerful twenty lines later. The grin at the moth is good, but the grin after the arm is not.
- **Teo.** He is a closed door. He says almost nothing and the chapter tells me he "kept the rest to himself". I stopped believing that a person this present could be this silent for the whole chapter. His one line, "Yes", after Jon's question about the coat is the only real reaction I saw.
- **Jon.** He is too convenient. He knows about the coat and the crane, he says "That's come right, then", and he hangs up on a half sentence. A man who runs a call from the far end of a wire should not be this economical, unless he is hiding something on purpose. I did not know if the author wanted me to find him sinister.
- **Seffa.** She leaves the yard for the door with a bag and a line about "somebody's got to be on him". Her motivation is not visible to me. I did not believe she would go with a group she barely mentions.
- **The old man.** He is consistent in his refusals, which I believed. But he is also the one who teaches a boy to kill moths and then tells him "Again". I could not tell if he is cruel, tired, or protecting Eli. I did not believe his rule ("Don't use more than you can control") was offered sincerely.
- **Doc.** A flat, mostly silent figure with a bag and a rag and a goat anecdote. I believed the competence. I did not believe his place in the group.

## 6. Where I was bored, and where I couldn't stop

Bored:
- The "Two days" and onion section. Seffa and Oskel's argument about the onions and the shift goes on past the point where it does work. Oskel's laugh "went on past anything that was funny", and the scene is also long.
- The repeated "Eli talked" and "Eli laughed" beats. They are fine once or twice; by the walk back up the Cut the tic has done its job.
- The walk to the hut. I could see the boots and the frost and the cranes. I did not need the same mood again.
- Repetition of "stayed" ("stayed where he was", "stayed on it", "stayed shut", "stayed there"). The word is doing a lot of work and I started to skim.
- Dialogue tags such as "said the old man" and "said Jon" come round often enough to feel mechanical.

Couldn't stop:
- The crane accident, from "He never waited" to "the bar came down on the arm". This is the best-written passage. The sound "was not loud" and the skin "came out from under the bar" is cold and exact, and I felt the fear.
- The moth lesson, especially "Again" and the moment Eli says "I felt it go... like something running out through—". The unfinished sentence is the strongest line in the chapter.
- The phone scene. The water going over stone under the hiss is an excellent image. I wanted more of Jon, and less of Eli talking over him.
- The last paragraph of the Cut, with the old man listening to them go. I wanted to know what he was listening for.

## 7. What I think it's about, and who the villain is

What I think it's about: a boy with a dangerous, sense-like power is being trained by an old man whose lessons are about control, and the boy's appetite for using the power is getting him hurt and getting other people hurt. The chapter is about the cost of "listening" when you want to be heard. The title is doing a lot of the work.

Who the villain is, honestly: I do not know, and I suspect the chapter does not know either. Three candidates, in my order of suspicion:
1. **Jon.** He sets up a door, a date, and a ninth, and uses Eli's pride ("he said yes") to pull him toward it. He is the one who needs Eli at the door. That is the classic shape of a handler, and it is the only character who is clearly trying to get something from the boy.
2. **The old man.** The most unsettling one. He teaches a boy to kill, insists on control, and then gives the boy nothing to control himself with. He may be the villain or the only man in the chapter trying to stop one, and the text does not tell me which.
3. **Eli.** The chapter reads as if he is the dangerous one. He hurts the boy, enjoys the power, and turns every instruction into a version of his own story. He may be the villain the author is building toward, which would explain why nobody in the chapter speaks to him directly.

My guess is Jon, but I would not bet on it.

## 8. My top 10 notes, ranked

1. **Name the thing in Eli.** Even a working word would help. Right now the power is a "thing" and a "reach" and a "shut hand", and a reader cannot hold it.
2. **Say who the old man is.** He is the teacher, but he has no name, no history, and no stated reason to teach. Give the reader one fact.
3. **Explain "the fit".** The line is load-bearing. Set up who has it, or cut the line.
4. **Explain the call.** Who is Jon, what is "the door", what is "the ninth", and why does the call come to the crane hut? One clear sentence each.
5. **Fix the ledger at the bottom.** Strip the "<!-- LEDGER -->" block and the "Not story text" note from the story file, or move them out.
6. **Fix "never turned his back on anything".** It contradicts the old man turning to go at line 59 and speaking with his back turned at the pallet at line 119.
7. **Settle Eli's injury and which hand is bad.** Tell the reader once, plainly, what happened to his face and his hand.
8. **Cut the repetition.** "Stayed", "Eli talked", "said the old man", and "went on" each appear too often. Trim the onion scene by a third.
9. **Give Teo one real sentence of interiority or action that does not hide.** His silence is a device. It works in one scene and then stops working.
10. **Make Doc's place in the group legible.** Who is he to Eli, Seffa and Teo, and why is he carrying a bag that no one mentions? The goat anecdote is charming but does not do the job.
