## Flags, ranked by how much they break belief

### 1. The master watched the lift, and the reach still worked (R-016 / acts for the plot)
> "he was sitting the way he must have been sitting when Eli looked at that door and saw dark." (L189)
- **Category:** broken promise (R-016) and a person acting for the plot.
- **Why it reads wrong:** the reader has just learned that Eli's reach fails when he's watched, then learns a man sat ten yards off watching the whole lift. And that man, who wants "the boys alive", let a stranger swing a ton of iron over his yard at dinner time, with his boys about to come round the corner with the pot.
- **Smallest fix:** give him his own method. "…sitting the way he must have been sitting when Eli looked at that door and saw dark, **with his eyes shut.**" He was listening, not watching, so R-016 holds (Eli was unwatched until the boys looked up). He acted the moment he heard the iron tip, which explains why he didn't stop it sooner. The eye-rubbing after then reads as him opening them, and it sets up "listen to the cranes" (L449).

### 2. Seffa's catch of the cup reads as Eli's reach working while he's watched (R-016)
> "the cup sat on his flat palm and tilted, and stopped tilting, and wobbled there, upright, a little off true. Seffa frowned at it." (L365)
- **Category:** broken promise (R-016), by ambiguity.
- **Why it reads wrong:** the reader has seen Eli catch things by reflex twice (the crate, the pallet) and has no sign that Seffa can reach, so most will read this as Eli's reach working with her eyes on it, one day after the chapter made "watched, it falls" its centrepiece.
- **Smallest fix:** one line of Eli's own. After "a little off true": "**He hadn't done that.**" Her frown and her hand under it then read as hers, and it stays unnamed.

### 3. The boys don't react to the rain stopping
> "'Did you see the size of that?' said one of the boys." (L199)
- **Category:** a cool moment with nothing under it (no consequence on the witnesses).
- **Why it reads wrong:** every drop in the yard stood still and the sound went out of the world, and the first thing a boy who has "never" seen the master do anything (L457) talks about is Eli's pallet. Nobody in the yard looks at the shed door.
- **Smallest fix:** turn the boys' faces before the line: "**The boys were looking at the far shed. Then the tall one looked at Eli.** 'Did you see the size of that?'" He's still more taken with the lift he could copy, which is the master's fear, but the rain has now landed on them too.

### 4. The other teacher is across the lane from the dead one
> "Across the Cut and a little way up, a crane stood dead… The yard under it had a gate of sheet iron, and the gate stood open a hand's width." (L143)
- **Category:** convenience.
- **Why it reads wrong:** the only other master on Ithe happens to keep the yard opposite Pask's, and Eli walks into it by chance, looking for a roof.
- **Smallest fix:** let the stall woman plant it and Eli not hear it: "Kept a yard up the Cut. For boys. **Him and the old one across.**" (L99). Eli fixes on Pask, so the reader holds the link and Eli doesn't. It's also a small instance of the warm-but-blind gap BANK 11-18 asks for.

### 5. Seffa pays for the boat and turns down the sock
> "She'd paid him before Eli could get the sock out… 'Keep that. You'll want it for something.'" (L585)
- **Category:** convenience (a seed protected by a character's choice).
- **Why it reads wrong:** a poor yard cook pays a stranger's fare out of her own pocket while the stranger stands there holding coins out. That's S-009 being kept unspent, not Seffa.
- **Smallest fix:** make it a debt, not cash: "**He eats off my pot,**" she said, and looked at the sock. "Keep that." Now there's a cause, it costs her nothing she wouldn't give, and the sock stays honestly unspent.

### 6. The pill clock: Eli asks how many are left after watching the last two go
> "'How many have you got,' Eli said, when she'd gone. He'd remembered all at once. 'The pills.'" (L469) against "Teo took the last two pills out of the twist himself… Eli watched his throat." (L359)
- **Category:** a clipped join (a state change the POV saw and then forgets).
- **Why it reads wrong:** Eli watched Teo swallow the last two white pills that morning, so the question and "remembered all at once" don't add up. The rest of the clock reads fine. Two at once and two "when it felt like night" in flight, then "Two left" on Ithe's first night (Teo rationing), the last two on Day 2's morning, and the yellow at the delirium all hold against 4:L453. The coat (with the twist in its pocket) goes onto Teo on night one, which neatly explains Teo taking them "himself".
- **Smallest fix:** at L359, put Eli's eyes elsewhere: "Teo took the last two… and swallowed. **Eli was looking at the gate.**" Cut "Eli watched his throat." Day 2's "None" then lands on Eli, and the gate-over-Teo gap shows without being said.

### 7. The headache "lived for four days" (continuity)
> "He found the place behind his eye where it had lived for four days" (L327)
- **Category:** broken promise (4:L7, R-039).
- **Why it reads wrong:** FOUR says he had it every time he came down and "gone home up the stair and not had it". He spent Days 2–4 on the Terrace, so it has lived since the Spur that morning, not for four days. Also, his head was "beating behind both eyes and down into his jaw" after the pallet (L195), so "had gone" needs to say which headache.
- **Smallest fix:** "where it had lived **since the Spur**", and "**The old one** had gone. Only his face…"

### 8. Eli says "Teo" in the hold, then stops himself saying it on the ramp
> "'Up,' he said. 'Teo. We're here.'" (L21) against "'Te—' / The board, on the wall by the hatch." (L31–33)
- **Category:** broken promise (the pilot's rule, 4:L219), inside the same scene.
- **Why it reads wrong:** he says the name ten lines before he catches himself on it, so the catch looks arbitrary. And the board was at the pilot's chair at L7 and is by the hatch at L33.
- **Smallest fix:** "'Up,' he said. 'We're here.'" At L33: "**Her board, hung by the hatch.** Its little light."

### 9. "He's grey" reads as "he's an orc"
> "'Is he yours?' she said. 'He's grey.'" (L203) against "not one of them grey. Humans, doing it." (L49)
- **Category:** a clipped join (the chapter's own word means two things).
- **Why it reads wrong:** a page earlier "grey" meant orcs, so a reader can hear Seffa calling Teo an orc.
- **Smallest fix:** "He's **gone** grey."

### 10. The clinic: why not give a false name?
> "'They'll want your name.'… 'They log it.'" (L113–115)
- **Category:** the world wouldn't produce it (as stated).
- **Why it reads wrong:** an ordinary reader's answer to "they'll want your name" is "lie". The block only holds if the name is read off them, which R-004 already allows ("a name maybe").
- **Smallest fix:** "Clinic's at the arch. **There's a reader on the door.**" Teo's "They log it" then follows with no gap.

### 11. Moving one top pallet doesn't make a dry place
> "the first pallet off the top of the front stack… and he turned it out from under the eaves into the yard, toward the bench." (L159)
- **Category:** a leap (the action's purpose doesn't follow).
- **Why it reads wrong:** the dry is a hand-wide strip of floor, and lifting the top of a stack frees air under the roof, not floor for Teo.
- **Smallest fix:** "the first pallet off the top of the front stack, **to start a gap down to the floor**".

### 12. A coat worth three months' fuel, three nights on a poor lane, untouched
> "Then he put the coat over Teo, over the blanket" (L331); "Anyone coming off those barges would see a man in a long coat with a good cut" (L63)
- **Category:** the world wouldn't produce it (R-028, the coat as a price tag).
- **Why it reads wrong:** the pilot priced it ("three months on that coat", 4:L215), and on Ithe it sits on a sleeping, feverish boy against a wall for three nights with no one so much as looking at it except the stall woman.
- **Smallest fix:** one look. Hollin, at L407: "His eyes went to the coat on Teo, **and to Seffa, and off it.**" The yard's protection is the cause, shown in a glance.

### 13. A small tell
> "he looked at Eli the whole time the way a boy looks at a man who's done something he wants to do." (L207)
- **Category:** telling, not showing.
- **Why it reads wrong:** it explains a look that his grin (L221) and his later visit (L441–457) already show.
- **Smallest fix:** "…and he looked at Eli the whole time." Cut the gloss.

## Rulings on the writer's doubts

- **The moth (Unsure 2): cut it; it steps on the lesson.** In the cast and cards (P25, 2.6) the moth is the thing Eli has to *find* blind, and it pays three times later (the guns, the blade, the heart). This simile makes the moth the thing *held*, and makes Eli the moth. A reader will carry that meaning into the lesson and blur it, and the narration spends the master's word before he does. Swap it for another small creature at a window: "the way you'd close your hand on a **wasp** against a pane to put it out", or a bee. The wasp also keeps the double edge: gentle, and careful of the sting.
- **The headache off Earth (Unsure 3): keep it.** 4:L7 already ties the headache to place (down gives it, up the stair takes it away), so noticing it's gone off-world is one honest step, and the paragraph never names the Tower. Fix "four days" and add "the old one" (flag 7) so it doesn't fight the pallet's head-beat at L195. As a bonus, this separates the reach's cost from the Tower's ache, which quietly defuses S-109's HEAVY risk.
- **The pill clock (Unsure 1): it reads.** The only break is flag 6. The "two in the morning" dose being taken on Ithe's first morning is fine, because Eli has no watch and Teo is visibly rationing ("Two left", eyes shut).
- **The master's power logic: it holds, given flag 1.** Holding the rain, the pallet and Eli's reach for a count of one, at the cost of a thumb of blood and rubbed eyes, against Eli's flood of blood and sickness for the pallet alone, shows the gap in scale without breaking R-014. The scope reads as a cause: the pallet was coming apart (a wire gone, bars sliding), so he held everything in the space rather than pick at pieces. If you want that on the page, it takes one thought of Eli's at L343: "**the bars were going everywhere, and he'd just held everywhere.**" Optional. His reading, "You lifted it blind. You opened your eyes and it fell", sharpens R-016 from *watched* to *attention* without contradicting it, because Eli's own version ("With people watching it just goes") stays beside it.
- **Is the yes earned? Yes.** It has a cause, a contrast and a cost. The cause is Teo down and "Not me. Him.", the first thing Eli has asked for someone else, set against "barely teach *me*… I'll pay… I'll wait". The master's first look at Eli's face (L547) marks it without a word. The cost is that he gives up his cook for four days ("They'll eat the onions raw"). It stays in character: "Then we'll see" is a deferral that also gets Eli off the yard before the boys copy him, which is the master's scene-3 want, and "Waiting's not why" shuts down Eli's claim. No missing step.
- **Teo's "Right" (Unsure 4): keep it.** In THREE, "Right" is how Teo closes a talk and goes (3v5:L41, L199, L573). Here it comes straight after "Is that a yes?", so a reader could take it as agreement. Give it THREE's follow-through: "'Right,' Teo said, **and turned his face into Seffa's shoulder.**"
- **The delirium lines (5):** no POV leak, because it's only what he says aloud. **The tall boy's "never saw him" (10):** keep it once flag 3 is in, since it then pays a reaction instead of standing alone. **"They stand you in a yard…" (8):** keep it; it gives "Then barely teach me" its run-up. **"Looks at the gate" (9):** leave it for word-watch.

## Verdict: FIX FIRST
1. **Flag 1.** The master sits with his eyes shut. That fixes R-016 (Eli was unwatched) and the master's motive (he heard, and acted at once).
2. **Flag 2.** "He hadn't done that." It stops Seffa's catch reading as Eli's reach working while she watches.
3. **Flag 3.** The boys look at the far shed before the tall one speaks, so the stopped rain lands on its witnesses.

Then flags 4–9 are one line each, and the moth becomes a wasp.
