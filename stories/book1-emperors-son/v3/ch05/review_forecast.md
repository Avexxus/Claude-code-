# Ch05 "The Teacher": forecast pass

Scope: read `ch05_STORY_ONLY.md` cold, plus `BOOK1_ch01-04_STORY_ONLY.md` (skimmed for context). No notes, no codex.
Method: stopped at each `---` break (14 sections) and logged a guess before reading on.
Honesty flag: the first Read call ran about 10 lines into S2. The S1→S2 guess was written as if those lines were unseen, but I had seen the "humans in oilskins" opening.

## S1 · Landing (ll. 5–47)
Coupling fails. They come down hard on an ore pad on Ithe, Teo makes it down the ramp, and the pilot is unmoved.

**Guess for S2:** Jon's "man there'll find you" is on the pad, or turns up quickly, and Teo gets seen to. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Is Teo's arm already past saving, and has Eli noticed yet that he gave the watch away?

## S2 · The pad (ll. 51–81)
Chipped human laborers, the big one laughing too long. Nobody comes, and the beater leaves.
**Grade: MISS (good).** Jon's "a man" was always vague, and Eli playing the guest by the doors is a sharp image of him expecting to be met. The laugh and "Behind his ear" are a clean plant (paid off in S10).

**Guess for S3:** Eli asks around and learns the contact is gone or never existed. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Who meets ships here, and does anyone know Jon?

## S3 · Tea stall (ll. 85–121)
Pask met the boats. Pask is dead, and his boys went to the cranes.
**Grade: NEAR.** I had the direction right. "Cranes" as the boys' fate lands harder than I expected, and Eli's "Well, he'll be late, then" is a good bad joke.

**Guess for S4:** They go to Pask's yard and find it empty. Eli uses his power to make shelter, and it goes wrong. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Where do they sleep tonight, and how long does Teo's arm hold?

## S4 · The Cut (ll. 125–149)
Pask's yard is chained and empty, the boy's shoe is there, and they move across the lane to the dead-crane yard.
**Grade: HIT.** This is a transit section. Coming straight after S3 it gives a second beat of "nobody's here," so tension sags a little.

**Guess for S5:** Eli lifts something in the yard, people see it, it slips, and somebody far stronger stops it. That somebody is the teacher. Conf **4**.
**Pulling me:** Whether the "watched, so it fails" rule (the roof chair, the cutter) is about to kill someone.

## S5 · The pallet (ll. 153–205)
The pallet tips over the boys, the rain stops across the whole yard, and the old man sits in the shed doorway. Seffa asks, "Is he yours? He's grey."
**Grade: HIT at conf 4.** The outline was predictable: the chapter title, the crate in ch04 and the watched-fail rule all point here. The execution is still the best stretch of the chapter. Freezing the rain is far bigger than the "catch" I pictured, and the moth-in-a-hand line plus "One." give it real scale. The old man's cost (nose, eyes) is planted quietly. No structural fix needed, because the texture does the turning. "He's grey" is briefly ambiguous after chapters of grey orcs. Decide whether that ambiguity is wanted.

**Guess for S6:** The old man refuses. Eli begs, offers the account, and promises to wait. Conf **4**.
**Pulling me:** "Who sent you." Does he know Jon?

## S6 · "Who sent you" (ll. 209–273)
The old man refuses, and his "barely teach" speech mentions the boys he buried and the chip. Eli hears false modesty in it (Old Hesk) and offers to pay three times over.
**Grade: HIT at conf 4.** The refusal itself has no turn. The turn is Eli's misreading, and that gives the section its pull. Gap: "Who sent you" is never followed up. The old man never hears Jon's name, and Eli never says it.

**Guess for S7:** A vigil outside the gate. The woman brings food, and Teo gets worse. Conf **4**.
**Pulling me:** How many pills are left.

## S7 · Outside the wall, Seffa (ll. 277–323)
Water, broth, a blanket. "He says no to the weather." Eli plans to send her a gift.
**Grade: HIT.** Warm and well voiced, but the lowest-tension stretch in the chapter. The only clock is "two left."

**Guess for S8:** The night. Teo shakes, Eli gives up the coat, and Eli broods on the rain. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Teo is going quiet, which in this book usually means worse.

## S8 · Brown night (ll. 327–353)
Eli notices the headache has gone (a strong quiet payoff of the Tower-field thread from ch04). He puts the coat over Teo and has a power fantasy that he cuts off at "a cutter at someone's ear."
**Grade: NEAR.** The headache beat was a pleasant surprise, set up well in ch04. The "He stopped that" turn is good. Tension stays low.

**Guess for S9:** Day two. Seffa looks at the arm and the truth of it comes out. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Is Eli's dead hand nerve damage, or something to do with the power?

## S9 · Day two, the smell (ll. 357–399)
The cup balances on Eli's flat, dead palm. Seffa reads the wound, and the line is past the shoulder. "He wants a healer, not a gate."
**Grade: NEAR.** The cup wobbling upright is an unexpected, earned beat: the reach working without his intent. "That's a smell" is very strong. "Healer, not a gate" is the first loud signpost toward S13.

**Guess for S10:** Eli is tempted to go to the clinic, or one of the boys explains something. Conf **2**.
**Pulling me:** Who in the yard can actually help Teo?

## S10 · Hollin (ll. 403–439)
A chipped young man lifts a bar without trying and laughs with his nose bleeding. Seffa tears into him.
**Grade: MISS (good).** I didn't see a chip-user coming, but every piece was set up: the laughing man on the pad, "Behind his ear," and the old man's "went down to the cranes and got the chip." It's the chapter's best mid-point turn. Eli's "nothing to him" misreading keeps the dramatic irony running.

**Guess for S11:** A boy tells Eli what the training really is. Conf **2**.
**Pulling me:** What it costs the old man to do what he did.

## S11 · The tall boy (ll. 443–475)
"We sit and listen to the cranes." "Never saw him do anything. Not in two years." The pills: "None."
**Grade: NEAR.** Eli hears "chosen." The reader hears that the rain cost the old man something. "None" is a hard, clean spike.

**Guess for S12:** In the night Teo gets worse and Seffa offers another route. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Whether Eli will leave the gate.

## S12 · "Who's minding him" (ll. 479–517)
Eli's fantasy monologue (window, kitchen). Seffa tells them about the gnome on Ammet who sits with the dying: "Some of them get up."
**Grade: NEAR.** The gnome is new and good, and a strong setup. But together with S9 it now signposts the climax twice.

**Guess for S13:** Teo collapses, Eli goes into the yard and asks for Teo instead of himself, and they leave for Ammet. Conf **4**.
**Pulling me:** Will Eli ask for himself first?

## S13 · Day three, "Not me. Him." (ll. 521–583)
Teo falls and is delirious ("He's to be there at nine"). Eli walks into the yard and says "Not me. Him." The old man comes out, and Seffa's bag is already packed. "Bring him back breathing. Then we'll see." "Waiting's not why." Teo: "Right."
**Grade: HIT at conf 4. This is the main turn risk.** The chapter's key choice is fully telegraphed by S9 and S12. What rescues it: Teo's delirium (calling back to the cup ring and his mother's "nine"), the bag already packed, the old man's first real look, and "Waiting's not why," which corrects Eli and is the one true reversal here.

**Guess for S14:** The trip to Ammet, a first look at the gnome, or the start of the boat crossing. Conf **3**.
**Pulling me:** Does Teo live, and does Eli understand why the old man said yes?

## S14 · The ore boat (ll. 587–603)
Seffa pays, "Keep that. You'll want it for something." Eli announces "He said yes," Seffa gives him an unreadable look, and the bad hand half-closes.
**Grade: NEAR.** I was right that it's a crossing, but it's a coda, not an arrival. It ends quiet, carried by open questions (Teo, the letter, the hand). The hook is soft but honest.

## Top three places to add a turn or a setup
1. **S13 is over-signposted (S9 and S12 both point at it).** Cut or soften one pointer, probably "He wants a healer, not a gate," so Seffa's gnome speech is the only one. Or make Eli start at the door asking for himself ("Teach me, and I can—") and break off into "Not me. Him." The choice then plays out on the page rather than being foretold.
2. **S6–S8 trough: give the old man one moment of cost on the page during the wait.** For example, Eli sees him through the gap rubbing his eyes, or a boy carries a bloody rag out of the far shed. That sets up the tall boy's "never once" (S11) as a revelation, not a line of dialogue, and gives the vigil a second clock besides the pills.
3. **S3–S4 double "nobody's here," and "Who sent you" is never answered.** Combine the two dead ends into one beat, or plant a thread in one of them: the stall woman reacts to "from the city" or to a description of Jon, or Eli says Jon's name to the old man and gets a reaction, even silence. At the moment the question "Who sent you" asks something and then vanishes. It should either pay off here or be clearly left for later.

Minor: "He's grey" (l. 205) reads at first as "he's an orc" after four chapters where grey means orc. Fine if that's deliberate; otherwise say "Grey in the face."
