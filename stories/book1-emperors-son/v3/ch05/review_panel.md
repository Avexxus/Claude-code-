# Review panel: Book 1, Ch05 "The Teacher" (v3)

Source read: `ch05_STORY_ONLY.md` only. I did not open the ch01-04 context file, so every judgement here is on chapter five alone. Anything that ch01-04 sets up (the Spur, Dace, the sock, the letter, the cutter, the watch, "grey") is judged by whether ch05 makes sense without it.

---

## 1. The grimdark veteran

**DNF:** None. The infection does the work this chapter needs. "the line went up past the elbow and on, and the sleeve wouldn't go any further" is a consequence the book doesn't flinch from, and I'm staying for it.

**Where I got lost:** I wasn't lost. I was annoyed. The master's kit is a stock set: cross-legged on the floor, eyes shut, gets up "without his hands" (twice), turns the student away at the gate, and sets pupils to "Listen to the cranes... Tell me which one's carrying." I've seen this in a hundred dojo films. What rescues him is his body, not his pose.

**Loved:** The cost. "He put his thumb to his nose and took it away and looked at it, and wiped it down the leg of his trousers, slow, once. Then he put the heels of both hands into his eyes and rubbed. He went on rubbing." Then the cup at night: "on the way it shook... He put that hand under the other one, on his knee." A master who pays for one catch and hides it is a man, not a wise-man prop. "I've buried the ones I taught" lands as a fact, not a mood.

**Fake/AI:** "Don't use more than you can control." It's a fortune-cookie line and the only generic thing he says. Everything else he says is either an order or a wound.

**Tell a friend:** "The teacher gets a nosebleed and the kid's best friend is rotting from the hand up. Finally, magic that costs something."

---

## 2. The casual fantasy reader

**DNF:** Nearly in the first scene, at "Her board. Its little light, green, by her elbow." / "'Come on,' he said instead." I didn't know why he stopped saying Teo's name. Is the board recording? Then "The watch was in there somewhere, against her." What watch? I kept going because the ground came into view.

**Where I got lost:**
- "not one of them grey" and then "He's gone grey." Is grey a kind of person, or does it mean Teo is pale? I honestly couldn't tell.
- Who Hollin is to Seffa: brother, son, ex-pupil? Who Ferrel is, and what "down at four" means.
- "a gnome... Over on Ammet." Gnomes are a people here? Ammet is another planet or moon? That took a second read.
- "the sock," "the letter," "the Spur," "Dace's cook" and "a cutter at his ear" all come from earlier chapters. Fine, but the sock especially needs one word (money?) when it's offered on the ramp.

**Loved:** Seffa. Her "It's a pot, love" was the moment I knew who she was. Then "That's not a cut, love. That's a smell." I always knew where I stood when she was on the page.

**Fake/AI:** "She had a look on her he couldn't read. It went on a while." It tells me there's a feeling and then doesn't give it to me.

**Tell a friend:** "A spoiled rich boy camps outside a grumpy old wizard's gate while his friend gets sicker, and the cook is the best character."

---

## 3. The hater

**DNF:** Tempted at "Listen to the cranes, he says. Tell me which one's carrying." That's wax-on, wax-off. I kept reading because of the next line ("I never saw him do anything before... Not in two years."), which undercuts it.

**Where I got lost:** Nowhere. I was counting:
- "a hand's width": the gate is open "a hand's width" five times, plus "a strip a hand wide." The motif is deliberate but it's been used up by the third time.
- "He laughed a bit longer than there was anything to laugh at, ...and then stopped all at once" appears word for word for the pad man and for Hollin. I get it: the laugh is a symptom of the chip. But when a tic is copy-pasted, readers notice the copying before the clue.
- The "and... and... and" chains: the opening paragraph, the pallet paragraph, the Hollin entrance. It's a style, but the chapter never stops doing it.
- Convenient plotting: of all the yards on the Cut, Eli breaks into the master's, on day one, in front of the one man who can catch it. Then Hollin shows up on cue with a chip and a nosebleed exactly when the reader needs the chip explained. The tall boy then delivers the "never seen him do anything" fact like an exposition courier.

**Loved (grudgingly):** "the way you'd close your hand on a wasp against a pane to put it out... and he could feel the size of the hand. There was no end to it." Then "One." I can't call that slop.

**Fake/AI:** "Eli sat with that." That's modern therapy-speak, not this narrator. Close second: "Heavier than he looked. He'd always been heavier than he looked." It's a pre-loaded tearjerker echo.

**Tell a friend:** "Better than it has any right to be, but count the hand's-widths and you'll see the machine."

## 4. The literary reader

**DNF:** None.

**Where I got lost:** I wasn't lost. I was briefly unsure whether "Seffa... She's marvellous. Isn't she?" was Eli's charm or the author's affection leaking through. On reflection it's Eli: posh, performing, talking to someone who's asleep. That works.

**Loved:**
- The irony in Eli's head is very good. He hears "barely teach" and "went warm all through. That was what the good ones always said," and he files the master under Old Hesk at the riding school. The reader knows better, and the chapter never says so.
- "Easy. So heavy it pulled at the backs of his eyes, and easy." The contradiction is the whole power system in eight words.
- "He'd forgotten things could be hot." / "The letter had gone soft and taken the curve of his ribs."
- The ending: the hand that "went partway, and stopped," left curled under the lamp. No lesson is spelled out.
- Seffa's subtext. The bag "done up since the night before, by the door" means she decided before Eli asked. "She died well. He was good to her... Some of them get up." is grief, plain.

**Tics:** polysyndeton (constant); "a hand's width" (overdrawn); "the way you..." similes (the ball, the wasp: two good, but watch the count); echoes of "without his hands," "They log it," "Behind his ear," "Ask anyone" and the thumb-wiped cup. Each echo is defensible alone. Together they make the chapter feel built from callbacks.

**On the master:** He's half distinct. His physical life is specific: the thumb, the rubbing, the shaking cup, the look at Pask's gate before he says yes, and "Waiting's not why," which refuses the gate-waiting trope the chapter itself set up. His stance (cross-legged, eyes shut, the one-line maxim) is borrowed. The look toward Pask's gate is the most interesting thing he does and could carry more weight. Is he guilty about Pask, or afraid of Ferrel?

**Fake/AI:** "Don't use more than you can control," and "She had a look on her he couldn't read. It went on a while."

**Tell a friend:** "The narrator's eye is lovely and wryly unreliable, and the cook is written better than the sage."

---

## 5. The young sci-fi fan

**DNF:** At risk at "That night Eli talked." That's the second quiet night against the wall in a row, with the window speech and the stove speech. I skimmed it. I came back for the third day.

**Where I got lost:** The ship opening. What's a "beater"? Why does the pilot's board matter? I wanted one clean beat of what the coupling failure is. The chip behind the ear is great, but I'm not sure what it does. Does it boost lifting, or make you a crane worker who laughs? Hollin shows both, so I'm guessing.

**Loved:** The pallet going wrong ("the wire sang and one strand of it went with a crack") and then the whole yard of rain stopping. Then Hollin: "the bar came up out of the mud with a suck, and hung in front of his face, turning... No hand out. No eye on it." The nosebleed made it real. And the world: cranes on legs in rows into the haze, a too-small red sun, ID readers on clinic doors, logged medicine, chipped labourers. I want more of this world.

**Fake/AI:** "There was no end to it." It's a big-sounding line that doesn't show me anything.

**Tell a friend:** "There's a scene where an old guy freezes all the rain in a yard with his mind and then has to sit down. Read it for that."

---

## Seffa: alive and specific?

**Yes, all five agree.** The stove-rim scar, the hair net, being "about thirty," hammering the stovepipe and telling the stove "Ten years I've fed you," "love," the onion pie, "They'll eat the onions raw," refusing the sock with "He eats off my pot. Keep that.," and the bag packed overnight. She acts before she speaks and never explains herself. Two risks: the grimdark and hater readers both see the edge of a kind-hearted earth-mother type. The thing that keeps her clear of it is her sharpness ("Who's minding him... while you're minding the gate?"). Keep that sharpness. Her tie to Hollin is unclear (casual reader), and one line could fix it.

## The master: distinct or stock mentor?

**Split, 3 to 2: distinct in body, stock in pose.** What makes him distinct: the nosebleed, the eye-rubbing, the shaking cup, the boy who has never seen him do anything in two years, the look at Pask's gate, and "Waiting's not why." What makes him stock: cross-legged with eyes shut, getting up "without his hands" twice, the refusal at the gate, the "listen to the cranes" exercise, and "Don't use more than you can control." The fix is cheap. Roughen or cut the maxim, and give him one habit that isn't from a meditation poster.

## Panel summary

### Top problems (flagged by two or more)
1. **"Don't use more than you can control."** Grimdark, hater, literary. It's the chapter's one generic line, and it pulls the master toward stock. (If this is a Patrick line, it's protected. Reframe what's around it instead.)
2. **Stock mentor kit** (cross-legged with eyes shut, "without his hands" twice, "Listen to the cranes"). Grimdark, hater, literary.
3. **"a hand's width" overuse and the copy-pasted laugh sentence** ("laughed a bit longer than there was anything to laugh at"). Hater, literary. Keep one of each, or vary the second.
4. **"She had a look on her he couldn't read. It went on a while."** Casual, literary. A vague feeling in the closing beat.
5. **Opening ship scene is unclear** (the "Te—" / board-light beat, the watch, "beater," what the coupling failure is). Casual, sci-fi.
6. **Undefined terms in ch05:** grey (race or pallor?), Ferrel, "down at four," Ammet and the gnome, Hollin's tie to Seffa, the sock. Casual, sci-fi.
7. **Too much polysyndeton** (and-chains). Hater, literary.
8. **Second-night talk stretch sags.** Sci-fi, plus casual and literary mildly. It's the one slow part.
9. **Convenient arrivals** (Eli breaks into the master's yard on day one; Hollin turns up on cue to explain the chip). Hater, with mild agreement from literary.

Single flags worth a look: "Eli sat with that." (hater, modern-sounding); "Heavier than he looked. He'd always been heavier than he looked." (hater, pre-loaded sentiment); "There was no end to it." (sci-fi).

### Protect these (loved by two or more)
1. **The rain stopping** and the wasp-against-a-pane hand, then "One.", then "Set down like a cup." All five.
2. **The master's cost:** thumb to nose, rubbing his eyes, the cup shaking at night. Grimdark, literary, hater.
3. **Seffa's lines and actions:** "It's a pot, love," "That's not a cut, love. That's a smell.," the bag packed overnight, "She died well. He was good to her," "They'll eat the onions raw," "He eats off my pot. Keep that." Casual, literary, grimdark.
4. **"Not me. Him." / "Waiting's not why."** Grimdark, literary. This exchange is what makes the master more than a gatekeeper.
5. **Hollin's bar lift with the nosebleed.** Sci-fi, grimdark.
6. **"Easy. So heavy it pulled at the backs of his eyes, and easy."** Literary, sci-fi.
7. **Eli's self-deception** (mishearing "barely teach," Old Hesk, "He said yes"), with the curled hand as the closing image. Literary, grimdark.
