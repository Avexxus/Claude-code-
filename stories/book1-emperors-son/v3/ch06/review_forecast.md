## S7 (329–455): the sock refused, the travel offer, "You took him first", Doc packs
**Grade: MISS, a good one, the chapter's main turn.** I got the money refusal (HIT) and nothing after it. The son's confrontation is fully set up (the boots, the stool going over, "Since Thursday", the watching woman), so it surprises without feeling cheap. Three strong beats land one after another:
- Eli turns down Doc's "mind to travel" with a grand promise of a boat and a wing. Painful dramatic irony.
- "I wasn't able to save him." No defence, no denial of "you took him first". The ambiguity holds.
- Doc packs anyway: "That's my mind made up." That turns the irony around. Eli's grand offer meant nothing, and the room drove the decision. The baby in the shawl being left behind is the hard cost.

My cost theory missed. The cost turned out to be social: the room, the hospice abandoned.

**Forecast:**
1. Doc is already on the barge. Eli assumes it's his own invitation at work. The chapter ends on the barge, perhaps with Doc looking at Eli's hand. **Confidence 4.**
2. Curious: why did Doc go? Because of the room, or because he's Jon's "man there'll find you" (ch04)?

## Top three places to add a turn or a setup

1. **S1/S2, the double vigil (lines 1–61).** These are two quiet sections of the same shape before the fit. Either merge them, or give S2 a real turn. Teo could say something that cuts. He has every reason: the nine o'clock clinic, his mother's slate, the watch traded. Or Seffa could actually answer "He'd know what to do. Jon." As written, Teo's anger is shown but aimed at the hull, and the Jon line dangles.
2. **S6, the cost of the healing (around 315–329).** The only signal is that Seffa's face "did nothing". Many readers will skate past it, and it's the hinge that makes "I wasn't able to save him" ambiguous. Add one physical tell after "Now then": hands at the basin, a thumb to the nose. The old teacher in ch05 did exactly that after the rain, so the echo would also link the two masters. Then cut or pay off the old women's orphan line "Might be two. Might be three" (135). Right now it reads as a clue to nothing.
3. **S9, Doc as Jon's "man" (around 507–519).** Teo's long look sets up a suspicion with nothing for it to grip. One small anchor would turn the quiet ending into a hook. Doc could use one of Jon's tics ("son", "Get it down ya"), or show he knows where they're going before Eli says, or bring up the teacher by something other than "the old man". Eli's line "the old man'll like you, Doc" is the natural place to hang it: let Doc's answer, or his silence, give a little away.

Smaller notes:
- Doc's wrist test on Eli (233) is a good plant. Make sure a later chapter pays it off.
- "You took him first" is said three times, which is right for a grieving man. But Eli's "I'm happy to pay" repeats the Hookline "If there's some sort of fee" almost word for word. That's likely intended; keep it as the only echo of that kind in this chapter.
