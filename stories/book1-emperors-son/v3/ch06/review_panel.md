# Review Panel: Ch06 "The Healer" (v3)

🌊 Claude proposal. Five-persona read of chapter six only, from `ch06_STORY_ONLY.md`.

---

## 1. The grimdark veteran

**DNF:** None. The price is paid on the page: Garro dies behind the blanket while Teo sits up drinking, and nobody explains it away.

**Where I got lost:** Nowhere that mattered. "Three" said to the stove landed. I didn't need it spelled out and I'm glad it wasn't.

**Loved:** The scattering coins. "A child on the near pallet put her hand out flat on one and kept it there." Then, later: "On the near pallet the child had picked up her coin and was looking at it." Eli's money finally does something, and it's the wrong thing. Close second: "I wasn't able to save him." / "That was all. He didn't say it kindly or any other way." No apology and no speech. Doc just stands there and takes it.

**Felt fake:** "Hot's alive." It's a fridge-magnet line, a comfort aphorism in the very scene where I want the dread to stay unsoftened. "Somebody's got to feed you," said to the hull, also tells me how to feel about Seffa.

**On Doc:** He's not a stock wise healer. He's a gnome with a goat bit and a death count, and he leaves because the room has seen what he is, not out of kindness. The joining is earned by being cast out. It isn't sentiment.

**To a friend:** "The healer saves the kid and someone else's dad pays for it, and the book never blinks."

## 2. The casual fantasy reader

**DNF:** No, but the opening hold section (lines 5 to 67) is slow going, and I nearly skimmed at "Eli counted the horns."

**Where I got lost:**
- Who Jon is, and who "the old man" with the strap in the yard is ("Bring him back breathing, he said"). I assume these come from earlier chapters, but a one-clause reminder would help.
- **"Three."** I didn't know what it meant. Three deaths today? Three this week? I only got that Garro died because of his son's shouting.
- Why Doc leaves. I took it as "everyone's angry at him," but I didn't follow why the bucket woman not looking at him mattered.
- "No reader. He's not on any list." I didn't know what a reader is.
- The young man with the square shaved behind his ear. Was he important?

**Loved:** "I've had it in a goat." I laughed out loud on the train. And "Mid-goat."

**Felt fake:** Nothing jumped out as fake. The two old women ("You never know who your neighbour is." / "Might be two.") sounded like a riddle I was meant to solve and couldn't.

**On Doc:** I loved him. Warm and weird, and the ears are great. I didn't suspect anything at all until the cold fingers, and even then I just thought he was creepy-interesting.

**To a friend:** "There's a tiny gnome doctor who tells goat stories while he does surgery, and you'll want him in every chapter."

## 3. The hater

**DNF:** None. I tried.

**Where I got lost:** Nowhere. It's clear, and that annoys me.

**Loved (grudgingly):** "I'm not in your bunk," said Doc. "I'm on my bag." It's dry and in character, and it shuts Eli down without a speech.

**Felt fake or AI-written:**
- **The "face did nothing" tic.** "Her own face did nothing at all" (Seffa), then Doc's face "did nothing." Two blank-face beats in one chapter.
- **"They're all dying. That's what the line is."** It's a quotable slogan written to be underlined.
- **The polysyndeton engine.** "and... and... and" runs through nearly every paragraph (lines 101, 177, 423, 459). It works in places and turns into a drone in others.
- **Cold hands as the tell.** "Out of that basin a minute since, and cold right through." The healer who is secretly something else gets cold hands: that's a stock signal.
- **The old women's chorus.** Two crones by the door delivering a cryptic hint is a convenient device.
- **The "Lamp." gag.** Doc says it, then Teo says it. It's a callback stacked on a callback.

**On Doc:** Even I'll admit the goat monologue during the vinegar pour isn't stock. A stock healer gets gravitas; this one gets a hat-for-a-goat trade. The joining is slightly convenient (the healer we need just happens to want to travel), but he pays his own fare "out of a tin," and that earns it back.

**To a friend:** "Annoyingly not slop. The tics are there, but it's doing real things underneath."

## 4. The literary reader

**DNF:** None.

**Where I got lost:** I didn't get lost, but I was briefly unsure whether Doc's thumbs pushing the red down the arm happen *after* Garro's death on purpose, as a transfer. The order (Garro dies, "Three," then he skips the rag and goes to the thumbs) implies it, and that's exactly right. Don't clarify it.

**Loved:**
- Seffa watching Doc's face: "She looked at the arm, and then she looked at Doc's hands on it, and then at Doc. At his face." She knows, or half-knows, and says nothing. That's the best subtext in the chapter.
- "Teo looked at the hand." / "The brown blanket went aside." The cut between those two lines carries the whole moral weight.
- Teo leaving the sausage end by Doc's boot: "Right." It's gratitude without a word of it, the inverse of Eli.
- The Eli design works. "It had gone well. He'd said it well, and Doc had taken it the right way." The irony is perfect, and it puts pressure on the scene rather than going soft. He offers Doc a wing and a boat *later* while Doc is offering himself *now*. "Suit yourself" lands like a slap Eli never feels. Per calibration this is design, and it does not make the others vanish: Teo's silence and Seffa's look get room precisely because Eli fills the air.

**Felt fake:** "like a tide going out down a flat beach." It's the one simile reaching for loveliness in a chapter that otherwise stays plain. Also, "a voice for a horse" is good, but it sits close to the jug-handle ears and the blind cobbler, so the figurative density spikes in the Doc section.

**Tics noticed:** blank-face beats (2); "looked at... looked at" chains; drying hands "finger by finger" / "one finger at a time" (twice, though maybe it's a deliberate character tag); "flat and far off" (a deliberate bookend, so keep it).

**On Doc:** Alive. His oddness lives in his rhythm (the self-interrupting "Did I say? No. Course I didn't, I've never met you"), not in a quirk list. The suspicion is seeded well: the cold hands, "Fifty years," the crones, "Three," and Seffa's look. Together they're slightly over-seeded. Cut one (I'd cut the crones or the cold hands, not both), and the reveal stays a shiver rather than a solved clue.

**To a friend:** "The quietest chapter so far, and the most frightening, because the miracle has a body count and only the girl seems to notice."

## 5. The young sci-fi fan

**DNF:** Almost, in the opening hold section. "She had the cup out. She'd been at it since the ramp." It's water, wait, water, for a page. And again in the queue inventory ("Then a girl with a yellow face. Then an old man on a door"), where I wanted to get to the gnome.

**Where I got lost:** Is this a sci-fi world? I got "Off-world, are you?", a kit blade, pills and tape, a barge that hums and drops onto the water, and that was it. What is the barge? It flies? I'd love one more concrete line on it.

**Loved:** The fit scene. "The boot hit him in the face, on the swollen side, and the lamp went over and round in a white smear." / "When he could see again Seffa was lying on Teo." That's proper action. And the cliff city cut into house-high steps with dust blowing out over the sea is a great image.

**Felt fake:** "Hot's alive." It's a sentence from a poster.

**On Doc:** Cool, and I want to know what he is *now*. The ears that turn toward a cough before he does are the best creature detail in the book so far. I guessed "he takes life from someone to give it to someone else" at "Three," which felt like the right moment, not too early.

**To a friend:** "Slow start, then a gnome doctor does something seriously creepy with his thumbs, and a guy loses his dad for it."

## Panel summary

### Top problems (flagged by 2+)
1. **"Hot's alive."** (Grimdark, Sci-fi, plus Hater's slogan complaint.) It's a comfort aphorism that softens the opening dread. Cut it or roughen it.
2. **Over-seeded Doc tells.** (Literary, Hater, Casual.) The cold fingers, the crones' riddle, "Fifty years," "Three" and Seffa's look are one or two too many. Cut the crones' "Might be two" exchange *or* the cold-hands beat. Keep "Three" and Seffa's look; those are the good ones.
3. **Slow opening in the hold** (lines 5 to 67). (Casual, Sci-fi.) The fit scene earns its place; the water-cup and horn-counting run long. Trim about 20%.
4. **"They're all dying. That's what the line is."** (Hater, partly Literary.) It reads as a slogan. It's a borderline keep; it's Seffa's voice, but consider dropping the second sentence.
5. **"Three" may be too oblique** for casual readers. (Casual; Sci-fi got it.) That's a split, not a fix. Maybe let Teo's look down the room to the blanket do a little more, but don't explain.
6. **Repeated "face did nothing"** (Hater, Literary). Vary one.

### Protect these (loved by 2+)
- **"I've had it in a goat."** (Casual, Literary in spirit, Hater conceded the goat material.)
- **The goat monologue during the vinegar pour.** (Casual, Hater, Literary.)
- **Garro's death, his son, and the scattering coins, with the child keeping one.** (Grimdark, Literary, Sci-fi.)
- **"I wasn't able to save him." / "He didn't say it kindly or any other way."** (Grimdark, Literary.)
- **Seffa watching Doc's face.** (Literary, Grimdark implicitly.) It's the key subtext beat.
- **Eli's "I'll send a boat" and "Suit yourself."** (Literary, Grimdark.) The Eli design works here, and per calibration it's not to be fixed.
- **"I'm not in your bunk. I'm on my bag." / "Mid-goat."** (Hater, Casual.)
- **The fit scene and Seffa lying on Teo.** (Sci-fi, Grimdark.)

### On the three questions
- **Doc: warm, odd, alive?** Yes, unanimously. He isn't a stock wise healer. His oddness is in his rhythm and his deflection, not his wisdom.
- **Is what he is suspected too early or too obviously?** Not too early: the panel converges at "Three." It is slightly too obvious, because the cold hands and the crones stack on top. Cut one.
- **Is his joining earned?** Yes. He's pushed out by the room's judgment, Eli's deferral ("I'll send") is the irony, he pays his own fare from the tin, and Teo's sausage end seals it. Only the Hater calls it mildly convenient.
