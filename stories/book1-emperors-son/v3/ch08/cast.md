## 1. Ysvet (LOCAL), the dark elf

**Page rule.** The narration says "the dark elf" and never "drow" (KERNEL §3). The name is not printed in ch08 and she does not give it. In `02` §1.7 her answer to "What's your name?" is "When you've earned it." Whether she gives it later is a ch9+ call for Claude.

- **Want.** Out of every count. She thinks this means the door. It means being nobody's line in anybody's book.
- **Need.** To be given something she can decline and still keep. Right now a gift is a price she hasn't been told yet, so she can't take anything. The need is to take one thing without pricing it, once.
- **Wound.** She was a child when thirty of them set out on the road to the door. Her street paid in children, and the teacher who sold her to a pit kept the money. Three years in a pit "down there," fought for human crews who came down to bet. Twenty years on the road and in the pit in all. Thirty years in this prison, under a lintel. Twenty-nine of the thirty are dead. She counted each one out in the bond while they were still in hell, because the bond works inside one world.
- **The lie.** *Anything given is a leash.* Kindness is a debt you haven't been billed for yet. Eli gives everything, and the card tests the lie on every gift.
- **The line she won't cross.** She will not be chained or shut in. She will not steal a soul (she despises soul thieves). She will not hand a living person to a count. She has never given anyone up, and she won't start with the counter.
- **Price.** She goes where she likes and is not handed back. She leaves that night if anyone hands her to a count, a crew, a buyer or an inquisitor, or if anyone puts a door on her. She also prices promises. The first promise the party breaks about the old man, she goes.
- **Fears.** Being counted again. The pressing crowd on the road, all of her own kind leaning in. Silence in the bond after one of hers goes out. And the cough behind her stopping while she is too far to hear it.
- **Secrets.**
  1. **The wall lists her as dead.** The prisoners cut her name in as the first of the thirty to go, twenty-nine years ago, when the count came up one short. She has never corrected it. The count is the only thing down here that has her right, and if she walks out, the count is wrong.
  2. **She has been taking the old man's cough** every night for six years: a palm flat on his chest, breathing up what comes. Her sleeves are flecked with it. She has never told him what it costs her lungs.
  3. **She has tried her own lintel with the knife, for years.** On the third try the counter took the top joint of her right knife finger. She holds the knife in the left hand now, and she does not show anyone that the right one is short.
  4. **She has pushed at the door on the ninths, alone, the last years.** The thirty went out in the seam one by one, and she felt each go. Nobody is waiting on the far side of it.
- **Voice.** Short, flat, contemptuous, exact about rules. She states numbers before feelings and never asks a question. She does not say thank you or sorry. Contractions are rare.
  - **Beat example (someone offers help):** she looks at the hand, not the face → states what it will cost, in counts (one lock, one joint, one night) → names the one rule that voids the offer → stops. She waits for the other person to do the arithmetic.
  - **Beat example (someone thanks her):** she doesn't answer the thanks. She corrects the count ("Two. You missed one.") and goes back to the job.
  - **Beat example (asked her name):** she gives the rule that makes it no business of the asker's, then the number of people who have asked before (none of them left alive).
- **Body and habits.**
  - Thin to the bone. Silver eyes. Pale rings on both wrists, the skin grown over the pit chain.
  - The top joint of the right knife finger is gone. She grips with the left.
  - Knife in her boot. Sleeps sitting up with the knife on one knee.
  - Eats fast with her arm curled round the plate. A pit taught her that.
  - Stands with her back to a wall, never to a door. "A door is a thing that shuts."
  - When she is still she taps the knuckle of her left forefinger, once for each of the twenty-nine. She does it while others talk. She has names for two (the first to go, and one who died in the seam at the second ninth). The others she counts without names, and she won't give names to a count because a name said aloud makes a thing hers to carry.
  - Breath slow and even. She holds it while the counter reads, and coughs only into her sleeve.
- **How the world shaped her (conditions, never blood).** She is elf by birth. What made her is a farmed street that paid in children, a teacher who sold her to a pit, human crews who came down to bet and never looked at her face, and a prison where the count is law. She has met humans from above, drunk and pricing things. She never stowed away on a human hull: "The crews are the farmers' customers. They count what they carry, and sell what they find at the next port." The door was the only way out nobody owned. She has had the homeworld's light at the edge of her only since the door, and it is too big to feel as anything but noise (the bond does not cross pocket worlds, KERNEL §2).
- **Why she joins.**
  1. **The lock needs a reach.** Her own lintel has beaten her for thirty years with no reach to break it. The counter's cells open only to a hand it reads as given. Eli's reach is the only key in the party, and she needs a key that will be used.
  2. **Her terms come first.** She states them before she moves: she goes where she likes, and no one hands her back. The party can't misread that.
  3. **She sees him fail.** The first lock doesn't take Eli's reach. She has watched worse keys fail. She decides a failed key is still the only key on the count, and she comes.
- **Why she stays through the door.** See §1a below.
- **Arc or refusal.** Refusal. She does not become someone who gives. The one change is small: she takes one thing from the wall, a flake of stone with Sirrin's name on it, which is the first thing she has taken from this place without a price.
- **Relationships (ch08 only).**
  - **Eli.** *Contempt:* he thanks people by reflex, and he promises rooms with windows the way every buyer she ever met promised a bath. *Use:* she needs his reach and says so, and she names the price of each lock in the fingers he'll spend on it. *A flicker of respect:* at the counter's first reading he stops talking and waits for the count to finish. She sees him listen, once. She files it and says nothing.
  - **Teo.** *Recognition.* He moves at the edge of attention, and she has moved that way for thirty years. She gives Teo the counter's rule and not Eli, because the one who breaks a count is the one nobody is watching, and she knows Teo will see that before anyone tells him. She respects that he watches her hands and not her face.
  - **Doc.** *Distrust she can't prove.* She knows what a sitter with the dying does: takes something and calls it a kindness. She calls him "the sitter." When he goes toward Ondry's chest she stops him with one word: "Not you." She can't tell what he is. The goat stories she hears as cover, and says so once.
  - **The tall boy.** *Pity she doesn't show.* She was a child when she was sold, and he is a boy from a yard. She gives him one piece of advice: stand still and let the count read you, then move on the last name. She never repeats it.
  - **Seffa.** *One flicker.* Seffa offers to take the old man's chest for a while. Ysvet says, "You'll take it and die of it." Seffa keeps her hands to herself and doesn't argue. It's the nearest thing to respect Ysvet shows anyone but Teo.
  - **Jon (relay).** She has only what the door did, "as if moving a curtain" (`02` §1.7). When his voice comes down the relay she turns her back to the grille. She says nothing to him.

### 1a. Why she stays, through the door

1. **At the first lock**, when Eli's reach fails, she stays. A failed key is still the only key.
2. **At the split**, she stays with Ondry, not with the party. She has been keeping him alive for six years. The party goes ahead to the count-house, and she doesn't follow until the counter forces the count.
3. **At the counter**, she stays because she is the only one who has counted this rule for thirty years. Breaking the count takes someone who knows where it runs. She is the one who tells Teo where.
4. **When the party says they'll carry Ondry**, she tests the promise. "Who carries him?" Eli says, "I will." The thin place can't be crossed by a body the counter has already read, and she knows it. She stays to see whether the promise holds. It doesn't. The party can't carry him through, and nobody says so.
5. **She leaves at last** because Ondry tells her to. His order is the only order she obeys, because he has asked for nothing. She is paying her own price here, and she doesn't turn round.
6. **Through the door** she stays with the party because nothing waits on the far side, and the party is the one thing she has chosen. She is still paying for it.

## 2. Ondry (LOCAL), the toothless prisoner

**Name.** Ysvet knows the name. The narration does not print it. He gives it to nobody. The count wants it, and the blank on the wall is where it would go. In ch08 he is "the old man on the grille."

- **Want.** To be asked his name by someone who won't write it down.
- **Need.** To stop being the weight other people carry. He has spent six years being carried by one person and can't stand it.
- **Wound.** Twenty-two years ago he made the door shake for a boy. The boy went through. Ondry didn't, because his heels were already cut. He has not said the boy's name since.
- **The lie.** *A man who can't walk is already counted out.* He believes the count has finished with him.
- **The line he won't cross.** He gives the count nothing it can write. No name, no trade, no crew. He has been asked three times and said nothing each time.
- **Price.** A child who asks him to go. He would go for that. He will not go for a grown person's promise, and he has heard enough of those on the stairs.
- **Fears.** Being read aloud by the counter in front of others. Being carried, since a carried body can be read again. That the one who breaks the count pays first, and that he is the only one left who can afford to be the one.
- **Secrets.**
  1. He made the door shake for the boy (see Wound).
  2. He knows the rule that breaks the counter. He has told no one, because the one who breaks it pays first, and Ysvet knows it too. They have never used it.
- **Voice.** Few words, one breath to a line. The cough finishes his sentences or interrupts them and he starts again after it. He corrects his own numbers.
  - **Beat example (asked how long he's been here):** starts "Twenty-" → coughs → finishes "-two." → corrects "Three." → says nothing more.
  - **Beat example (told he's going out):** says nothing → hums the lintel rule, low, as a tune, until the cough takes it → puts his tongue into the gap where the teeth were.
- **Body and habits.** Toothless, gums pale and shining. Chest like a bellows, grey stubble, eyes pale and quick. Both heels scarred flat, the tendons gone. Hands shake and grip the grille bars. The smell is lye and something sweet under it. He hums the lintel rule when he isn't coughing. He knows the counter's count by its pauses and can tell you the next one before it comes.
- **How the world shaped him.** He came down on a human crossing as crew. He walked off the count on foot, which the lintel forbids, and the counter took both heel tendons as the rule's cost. On later counts it took his teeth, one at a time. The teeth are strung on the counter's wire. He has been on the floor so long that the floor is the only thing he is measured against.
- **Arc or refusal.** Refusal. He stays.
- **What he says when she leaves.** "Don't count me."

### 2a. Why he stays

1. **He can't be carried.** A moved body can be read again. He knows the rule's cost, and so does Ysvet.
2. **He won't be written in.** The party can't carry a name through the thin place if he won't give one.
3. **He has already decided.** The boy went through and he didn't. He is not going to be the one who spends the shake a second time.

## 3. The counter (LOCAL label), the cell's rule-beast

**Label.** "The counter." It is never named in-world beyond this label. Patrick's horror: warped and broken shapes, a menacing presence, horrifying practices. Story-local. The merge of in-betweeners (Law 15) and rule-beasts (Law 4b) is a candidate in BANK 11-26 and has no R-number, so this stays clay.

- **Shape.** A frame that was once a standing desk, with too many joints. Arms folded at the wrong elbows, one of them doubled back on itself. A face set sideways in the chest, its mouth at the height of a grille. Its skin is tally paper, pressed flat, ruled in pencil-grey lines. The lines are the count. It is taller in the cell than the cell's door should allow.
- **Presence.** The air thins and goes to a count. You hear names read in a voice made of many voices, each one a little late. You feel the number go up in your own chest. People in the cell stop breathing on the count without being told to.
- **Practice.** It takes what is counted. It takes teeth (Ondry's are on its wire). It takes the top joint of a finger from anyone who breaks the count. It takes the heel tendons from anyone who walks out of the count on foot. It keeps what it takes on the cord across its chest. It never eats. It never kills on the page. Its cost is the body, not the life, and it doesn't draw on anyone's Remnant (KERNEL §1.2).
- **The one rule a person can use.** It must finish a count it has begun. Interrupt the reading (speak over it, or cross the grille line before the last name) and it has to start the whole count again from the first name.
- **What it does when the rule is broken.** It takes the nearest counted thing from whoever broke the count (a joint, a tooth) and starts again from the top, with that person first on the count. The second break costs two. Use the rule once and the cost is known; use it twice and it's a trap.
- **Option for the card writer (not decided).** Teo breaks the count at the grille, because nobody watches the help. The cost falls on the hand that was already burned in the crater.

## 4. Prison staff (rules, not faces)

- **The lintel hand.** The rules on every lintel are cut before the cell is used, by a hand nobody has seen. They are read only at the count, and nobody is paid to keep them.
- **The ledger of the dead.** Kept by the prisoners. The one who survives a count scratches the dead's name into the wall at the next count. A blank is left where a name hasn't been given.
- **The count-house.** A stone room with one table and no chair. The lintel says the count is read standing, and the reader is the counter.

## 5. Canon and story flags (for the continuity keeper)

- **The bond and the pocket world.** The bond does not cross pocket worlds (R098; t230; KERNEL §2). Inside hell it works, so she felt the thirty go out. She has never felt anything from Earth. The light is new since the door (scene card 4.5 drafter note).
- **Thirty years vs fifty.** The brief says "thirty years down here." `02` §1.7 says fifty years, from the street to the door. This dossier reconciles them as twenty years on the road and in the pit, then thirty in this prison. Patrick may want one number. The repeated "thirty" (thirty years, thirty of the road) is a risk for the reader to trip on.
- **The counter's type.** Story-local. BANK 11-26 proposes merging Law 15 and 4b; not yet ruled. It takes body parts, never Remnant. No death on the page (brief: "Do not kill anyone in this chapter").
- **"One out, one stays."** This is the brief's price of the door. The closest canon is "once in, the bleed stays" (KERNEL §5; R013, R014). It is not a ruled law.
- **The word.** "Dark elf" on the page. No "drow" (KERNEL §3; `10 t233`).
- **Jon.** Relay voice only, one line, then the relay dies (brief, shape 1). No interpretation on the page.
