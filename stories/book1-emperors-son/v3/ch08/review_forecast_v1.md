## Where the prose is predictable (lowers tension)

- **Eli's bad hand.** Line 37 says it "would not close on the rail." The lock, the lintel, and the seam all repeat that beat. By S5 a reader expects the failure, so it works as setup, not a surprise.
- **"You said that before."** Used at lines 105, 137, 297. Three uses of the same line make Seffa's stay-where-I-can-see-you warning feel like a refrain. In S4 this drains the rail scene.
- **Ysvet's right hand.** Flagged at lines 347 and 403 ("he could not see enough of it to say why"), then revealed at 451. This is a fair setup, but the reveal comes after the cost has already been paid, so it reads as explanation.
- **The old man stays.** "I'm counted already" (531) and "one stays" (383) telegraph his fate from the moment the count is set. Sympathetic, but the ending is fixed early.
- **Closing line.** "It went on. It went on." repeats the cough motif from line 567 and the one at 521. Fine as a last beat, but the repetition is the expected note.

## Where the prose could surprise, or may be unearned

- **Teo's finger (S6).** Teo crosses the groove on Ysvet's advice ("cross it and it starts over with the one who crossed first"). That rule is stated at 435, one paragraph before he acts, so the turn is earned. Its cost is high and the chapter gives it room, so it does not look random.
- **Jon's voice on the relay (S1, S7).** Jon speaks twice and says almost nothing ("That's you, then," "Walk in front, son"). This is deliberate (brief shape 1 and 7, per the ledger), but a reader may feel Jon is a thin thread. Nothing in the chapter pays off his absence.
- **Doc.** He is active early ("Hm," "Not you") and then mostly withdraws. He never acts on the lock, the blank, or the count. He has no visible turn.

## Top three places to add a turn or setup

1. **Pencil on the short string (S2, lines 85–93).** Eli asks to put "the account" into the ledger and is refused. It is never used again. A payoff, even a small one (Ysvet writes the blank, or the pencil marks a name), would give the ledger a stake in the endgame. Alternatively, cut the beat.
2. **Doc's "Not you" moment (S3, line 229).** Doc refuses to take Eli's hand because the lock has been bled on. That line hints at an unexplained rule about blood and keys. Either set it up earlier or let Doc name the rule once in S7, so the threshold scene has a reason behind it.
3. **Seffa's sleeve and her gloss on the rag (S4, lines 283–287).** "It's dirt. Not a thread." is a strong line with no follow-up. Setting up what she is hiding from the boy's wound (or from the others) would raise S4's tension, which is currently the lowest in the chapter.

## Lowest-tension scene

**S4 (lines 249–317).** The landing choice, the rail, the rag, and the "you said that before" beats all deliver the same reassurance over and over. The only hook is the hand through the bars at line 303, and that is resolved in the next scene. S4 needs either a sharper choice (who goes where, and what it costs) or a line that threatens something specific.
