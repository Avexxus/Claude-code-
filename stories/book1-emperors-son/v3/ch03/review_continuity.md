## Contradictions (both passages quoted)

### 1. Factual details · the "two" counted on top of the watch · MEDIUM
- **Before:** "'And two,' she said." … "'I haven't got two.' / Her live eye went to his wrist. / Eli unbuckled the watch and put it in her hand." (2:L221–231). The watch **was** the two.
- **New:** Eli: "It's for eleven hundred marks and two and a watch and a soup stall" (3:L471). Also Jon: "it was eleven hundred, wasn't it. And two on the night." … "And the watch." (3:L319). Jon's line can be read as an aside (the two was the watch). Eli's line can't: it adds them up.
- **Smallest fix:** 3:L471 "eleven hundred marks and two and a watch" → "eleven hundred marks and a watch". Leave Jon's line; it's ambiguous, and that suits him.

### 2. World rules · the slot's disc stays dark for Eli · MEDIUM (R-004, R-007)
- **Before:** "For a human, Vosk had been told, it was only a light, a name maybe, a soft chime." (1:L43); "Every human face that passed under a reader got its light." (2:L17); the gate reads Eli green every time, never by name (R-007; 2:L569).
- **New:** "The disc in the wall stayed dark. It didn't look at him. There was no light, and no chime, and no voice to tell him what time of day it was." (3:L553)
- **Problem:** the notes' rule ("The service slot reads orc chips only") isn't on the page. Teo passes right after (3:L555), and the text doesn't say whether the disc lit for him. A reader can take it as the father's reach switching off for **Eli alone**, which would be a first break of R-007, or as a break of 2:L17's "Every human face".
- **Smallest fix:** add half a line at 3:L555: "Teo came out behind him with his bad hand held against his chest. It hadn't looked at him either." Or put the rule in Eli's head at L553 (for example, "The slot didn't read humans."). Then log it as a new rule (R-029).

### 3. Narrative style · the opening is in the cook's POV · MEDIUM (R-021)
- **Before:** R-021, "the only POV break in the book is Vosk's opening… Eli close third from L83 to the end." TWO keeps Eli close throughout.
- **New:** "The cook came in at seven and found her brush in the sink. She picked it up and turned it over… and only after that did she see Eli at the table" (3:L3). The order of noticing is hers.
- **Smallest fix:** keep the actions and move the eye to Eli: "The cook came in at seven. Eli watched her find her brush in the sink…", and cut "and only after that did she see Eli at the table" (or make it "and then she saw him").
- **Minor, same category:** "Down in the kitchen the second-best coat was still on the step… The cook swept round it." (3:L45) is reported while Eli is upstairs in bed and the bath. It reads as summary across days. To fix it, have him see it: "When he went down, the second-best coat was still on the step…"

### 4. Factual details · "A Vennick, out" on the Ninth gate log · LOW
- **Before:** "The Vennicks were up on the Seventh" (1:L315). Their roof is on the Seventh, not Ninth.
- **New:** "The Ilders' people in, and out again late. A Vennick, out." (3:L455). This is Ninth's gate. Within the three mornings there's no "in" for a Vennick, so the line has one leaving Ninth without having come in.
- **Smallest fix:** "A Vennick, in, and out." (This also suits "Nobody said where the Vennicks were", 3:L193.)

### 5. Timeline · "One short… This morning" · LOW
- **Before:** Vosk's number already went unanswered at the Day 2 morning count: "'Vosk?'… The steward made a mark, and read the next." (2:L589–593)
- **New:** on Day 3: "'One short, madam.'… 'This morning.'" (3:L113). If this answers "since when", it's a day late.
- **Smallest fix:** "Since yesterday, madam." Alternatively, leave it if "This morning" is meant to answer something else (the depot request). The woman's side is unheard either way.
