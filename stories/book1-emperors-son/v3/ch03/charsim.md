**Protected [P] beats, all left in place:** Jon warm, gangsterish, smile never slips, "love ya", never interpreted · Eli naive and doesn't listen, devoted to the idea · Teo the voice of reason · the "terrorist cell" frame (Inquisition) · orcs as people.

## Scene 1: the days after, and the Ilders' party (Eli, Teo, the steward)
**What each knows.** Eli: Vosk is dead, the King is alive, the forty-one is in the sock, the letter is under the bowl. He doesn't remember defending Vosk; Ilder remembers "He could dance." Teo: the same, plus his place off-world is still held. Steward: Vosk's number went unanswered. That's all he's shown to know.

**ELI**
- *Want:* for somebody to say Vosk's name like it matters.
- *Do:* not sleep. Stand in the yard at the count. Take the sock when the steward clears the bunk, and not open it. Go to the party because Ilder will be there, and Ilder is the one who remembers. Leave when it turns into a story people tell, or when nobody mentions it at all.
- *Never:* cry in front of the friends, or tell them what really happened. He tells it wrong, or he doesn't tell it.
- *Voice:* "He could dance. You said." / "Keep it. I don't want it." (to the sock, or to the steward)
- *Plot flag, a soft one:* **why go to a party the day after?** As planned, it's there to be left. **Cause:** he goes to find Ilder, to ask about the yard and the fob ("Bit him"). Ilder's crescent scar is right there on his hand. Eli can't ask, so he goes. That gives the party a want and makes leaving a failure, not a mood.

**TEO**
- *Want:* his palm seen to, and Eli not to do anything.
- *Do:* look after the hand badly (he won't go to a clinic that logs it). Unpack the trunk the rest of the way, because the hold has been extended. Come to Eli's and sit there. Not go to the party.
- *Never:* bring Vosk up first.
- *Voice:* "It'll close." / "Right."

**THE STEWARD**
- *Want:* the house running by numbers.
- *Do:* clear the bunk, have a new orc by the third day, take the woman's call. "Sir" as a whole answer.
- *Never:* say what she asked, or what she didn't.
- *Plot flag:* **"she asks nothing about Eli, which is worse"** only lands if Eli hears it. **Cause:** he's in the hall and hears only the steward's half again, the way he did in ONE, and the steward's half has no "him" in it. Don't let the steward tell him.

## Scene 2: "He's asking," and the object (the Ilders' man, Eli)
**What each knows.** The Ilders' man: the King has been eating, the fob rule, and (TWO) he wouldn't nod back. He may have been in the yard when Vosk danced. The orcs who climb to work every morning carry the word up from below. Eli: the King is alive. Nothing about how the word travels.

**THE ILDERS' MAN**
- *Want:* to pass this on and be done, without an Ilder seeing.
- *Do:* say it with his eyes on his boots, an inch off the grass, at the hedge. Two words, then sweep on. If he's handing something over, he puts it down rather than giving it.
- *Never:* look at Eli, or explain. Not with the father's fob in the house.
- *Voice:* "He's asking." / "Came up this morning."
- *Plot flag:* **why would he carry the King's word?** As written he's a mailbox. **Cause:** below, you kneel to the King, and the Terrace can't protect a yard orc from the Stair. He does it because he's afraid. **To make him a person:** give him one thing that isn't the message. Either a second word nobody asked for ("Walk well"), or he hasn't swept the spot where Vosk used to stand. Choose one, and keep it small.

**THE OBJECT: a pick**
- **Eli's watch** (the four worlds and the little moon). It's in Velk's apron on the page. Coming up, it says three things without a word: the King got to Velk; he knows whose house it is; he wants what's left. It costs him nothing to send. That makes it a threat and a message at once.
- **Not the Legion chip.** "King'll have the new one" (2:L357). He keeps that. **Not the sock:** it never went down.
- **How it comes up:** a day orc brings it to the barracks on the morning climb, and the Ilders' man sets it on the wall by the hedge. This joins the two parts of the plan's beat into one.
- **ELI when it comes:** he puts it on. That's the naive move, and it gives him something to tell the time by. (Teo would say "Take it off." That's a fourth warning, so save it.)

## Scene 3: Jon in the kitchen at dawn (Jon, Eli)
**What each knows.** Jon (on the page): Vosk's name, the chip's price, the King. How he knows is never given. Eli: everything above, plus the watch and "He's asking". Nobody has said Vosk's name to him in days.

**JON (actions and lines only)**
- *Wants (shown):* Eli on the crew.
- *Do:* eat from the bowl, one piece after another, as if it were his. Leave the letter. If Eli looks at it, look at it too, once, and keep eating. Say Vosk's name early and plainly. Name the price of the chip to the mark. Offer the job. Name part of the price and stop there. Smile the whole time.
- *Never:* touch the letter; explain the gate; argue; push after a "no." Never tell a lie that can be checked. He says less instead. Never look at the watch for long.
- *Voice:* "Eleven hundred, wasn't it. And the watch." / "I'd love ya to come." / "Everything's got a price, son. Some of it I'll tell ya."
- *The offer:* "a job that ends at the top of the Tower." There's a crew, and "a teacher". That's all.
- *The price, the named part:* something Eli can say yes to without thinking. You come now, you don't go home, you do what you're told when you're told. The unnamed part he leaves out. Don't hint at it with a pause or a look; if the narrator notices the silence, that's interpretation.
- *Plot flag:* **"never interpreted"** goes beyond the narrator. **Eli mustn't interpret him either**: no "he seemed…", no "Eli trusted him." Eli sees fruit, a smile and a hand. Keep Jon's lines short so the reader does the reading.

**ELI**
- *Want:* that Vosk meant something.
- *Do:* not call the steward. Sit down across from a stranger because the stranger said "Vosk". Ask the wrong questions: "You knew him?" and "Will we see the King?", not "How did you get in?" Say yes before the price is done.
- *Never:* ask what the rest of the price is. He'd hear the question as rude.
- *Voice:* "You knew him." / "So it's for something. It's for something."
- *Plot flag:* **"Eli doesn't ask the right questions."** It's true to him, but it has to have a cause. **Cause:** Jon says Vosk's name before Eli can ask anything. After that, every question Eli has is about Vosk. That keeps it his flaw (devotion to the idea), and the plot doesn't have to make him stupid.

## Scene 4: the fight, and Teo choosing (Eli, Teo)
**What each knows.** Teo: what Eli tells him. Then he checks: he asks the gate or the steward, and nobody was let in. Eli: Jon's offer, and the part of the price he heard.

**TEO**
- *Want:* Eli not to go with this man. If Eli goes, Teo goes too.
- *Do:* check the gate before he argues. Give the warning once, flat: "He knew his name." "Nobody came in." Then say the one hard thing he hasn't said yet: "You don't know what it's for." After that he stops. To show he's choosing: he goes home, comes back with the slate, and puts something from the trunk into a bag (the stone or the cup). Never say "because".
- *Never:* beg, repeat himself, or say "I'll come because you're my friend."
- *Voice:* "He knew his name." / "For who?" / "Nobody came in, Eli. Ask it."
- *Plot flag:* **"fight"** pushes Teo to argue more than he does. **Instead:** let Eli do the fighting, loud and reasonable, the steward voice, then repeats. Teo gives three short lines and then goes quiet. The fight is Eli shouting at someone who has stopped talking.
- *Plot flag:* **Teo's reason to come** should cost him something on the page. **Cause:** his palm needs a clinic, and he goes anyway. Or his mother says "He's had a day" and he leaves the room. One of those.

**ELI**
- *Want:* Teo to say it's right.
- *Do:* talk over him: "He's asking. You heard. He's asking." Make Vosk the argument. Say something cruel about the held place ("You've nowhere to be"). It lands, and Eli doesn't notice it landed.
- *Never:* hear "nobody came in" as a fact. He hears it as Teo being Teo.
- *Voice:* "It's for Vosk." / "Teo. Listen. Listen."

## Scene 5: leaving by a way the gate doesn't see (Jon, Eli, Teo)
**JON (actions and lines only)**
- *Do:* walk them out by the **service slot**. It reads chips and bites orcs; it doesn't read humans at all. 🌊 Fits 1:L69 and 2:L7, but check it. Don't hurry. When the house bell goes, don't turn round. Take a piece of fruit on the way out and eat it walking ahead.
- *Never:* mention the bell, or comment on the letter.
- *Voice:* "This way." / "Mind your head, it's built for him." (If he says this, the "him" lands on Vosk with no commentary. Use it only if it reads as Jon talking, not as the narrator nudging.)

**ELI**
- *Do:* take the **second-best coat** off the step, the one Vosk folded, and leave the good one. Don't say why. **The letter:** he takes it unopened. His hand puts it in the coat while he's looking at Jon. Since nobody watches the hand, it moves the way the reach does. That keeps it a weight, and it sets up "Did you forget my letter" with a sting: he carried it and never opened it.
- *Never:* go back when the bell goes. He hears it and walks faster.

**TEO**
- *Do:* look back once at the bell. Keep walking.
- *Voice:* nothing. Or "That's the house."

**The bell:** it's the account calling. Too late or exactly on time, it's the same on the page. Nobody answers on the page; the steward picks up behind them.
- *Plot flag:* none, as long as the gate never says "Good morning" here. The quiet where the gate would greet him is the beat.

## Summary of flags
1. Party: give Eli a reason to go (Ilder, the scar) so leaving is a failure.
2. The yard orc: fear as the cause, plus one small thing that's his own.
3. The object: the watch (not the chip, not the sock).
4. Jon: no interpretation, by the narrator *or by Eli*. He says Vosk's name first, and that's why Eli asks the wrong things.
5. The fight: Teo goes quiet after three lines, and his coming costs him (the palm, or his mother).
6. Leaving: the slot; the folded second-best coat; the letter taken unopened by an unwatched hand.
