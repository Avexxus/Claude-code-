# Originality check: Book 1, chapters 1–3 (locked text)

🌊 Claude review, not canon. Source: `v3/BOOK1_ch01-03_STORY_ONLY.md` (1866 lines). I changed no text. Codex read: `WORLD_OVERVIEW`, plus `07`/`10` as summarised there. Names were checked with WebSearch on 2026-11-18.

**Background.** Copyright covers specific text, characters and distinctive sequences. It doesn't cover ideas, genres or tropes. Nothing below is an infringement risk. Item 1 is the only one an informed reader is likely to name outright.

## A. Too close to one work (ranked)

1. **Eli once lifted "the whole flyer up out of the lake with the water pouring off it" (ch2, L1045), and now his power fails him in a crisis.** This resembles Yoda lifting the X-wing out of the Dagobah swamp, and Luke's "I can't, it's too big." **Recognisable**: the image itself (a craft rising out of the water, streaming) is the signature part. The failure-under-pressure idea is free. *Fix (codex):* keep the beat but give it a feat that only this world has. Some options: he held the falling flyer's fan together; he lifted a lift-core box at the Spur; he pulled Teo out of the sumps. Anything that isn't a vehicle rising out of water. The failure already has its own codex engine, Will (being watched kills the conviction), and that part is original.
2. **"Vosk."** He shares the name of the Na'kuhl leader in *Star Trek: Enterprise* ("Storm Front", 2004), who is also a time-war villain and appears in *Star Trek Online*. **Passing nod**: a minor two-episode character from a different genre and species, and the name is also an ordinary Slavic/Dutch-sounding syllable. Only Trek fans will catch it. Because it's a time-war villain in a time-war saga, the overlap is slightly unlucky. *Fix (optional):* keep it, or soften it to Vusk, Vosch or Vorsk. The name is LOCAL, and the chapters are locked, so this is Patrick's call.
3. **The stranger in the kitchen eating the host's food, recruiting him to "a job" with a crew, "you don't come back here after" (ch3, L1615–1735).** This resembles Gandalf and the dwarves eating Bilbo out of Bag End, plus the heist-crew pitch. **Passing nod**: no shared details beyond eating someone's food uninvited. Jon's plum stones lined up in a row, "Sorry about Vosk" said round the plum, and the gate log showing nobody came in all make it Jon (warm charm, total opacity: `08`). No fix needed. Don't add a contract, a list of terms or a "burglar" line.
4. **An unopened sealed letter, an anonymous account that pays for everything ("your father's people"), and an heir who doesn't know who he is.** This is the hidden-heir / unknown-benefactor setup (*Great Expectations*, *Harry Potter*'s letters). **Passing nod**: it's a common device. The oval seal with nothing in it (a faceless Emperor), the woman ringing to check the sum, and the steward's "Up" are specific to the saga. No fix.
5. **Slave control chips with a pain mechanism and a remote fob.** These resemble Star Wars' slave transmitter chips and stock sci-fi shock collars. **Passing nod**: it's a genre device. A child's fob that "does their own" while "his dad's does anybody's", the hopping on wet grass, and the gate that "bites" all come from the saga. No fix.

Checked and clear (no notable match found): **Velk, Hurrak, Ilder, Vennick, Dace, the Scald, Hookline, Ninth Terrace, the Long Stair.**
- *Hurrak* resembles Hurroc (A5e), Hurk (Far Cry, BG3) and Harrek (Glorantha). None is close.
- *The Long Stair* sits next to Tolkien's "Endless Stair", but it's a plain descriptive phrase. Fine.
- *Dace* is an English word (the fish) and a surname. It isn't from a famous work, but it does sit near the "no generic Earth names" line in CLAUDE.md. It's minor and mostly fits the Terrace set's clipped names.
- *Teo* is an Earth name, and the brief already marks it as a placeholder.
- *Ithe* doesn't appear in chapters 1–3; it first appears in ch04 (L211, L231, L439). A search turned up no match.
- Watch-list note: "the drow" is in the brief, not in these chapters. It's a D&D term, so keep it out of the prose.

## B. Tropes on autopilot (ranked)

1. **The dying speech that ties a bow (ch2, L1107–1171).** Vosk explains the backstory (Ilder's fob, the bite) and then says "I'm proud to die for the man that boy grew into." "Proud of the man you've become" is a stock last line, and the reveal-by-deathbed is the autopilot part. **What saves it:** the fragments, "I picked it", and above all **"Nobody spent me"**, which is pure codex: Remnant can never be spent (`04`, BANK). The final "It's right there" pays off the Tower sense. *One-line fix:* cut or break up "proud... grew into" so that "I picked it / Nobody spent me" carries the weight. The Ilder half is already re-told by Ilder in ch3, so the dying version could be shorter.
2. **The villain's hand bursting out of the rubble (L1165).** This is the stock "he's not dead" shot. **What saves it:** the hand has chips sewn across its back in rows, and heat comes out first. It's mild. *Fix:* fine as is. Don't repeat the move later in the book.
3. **The slap to snap him out of it (L1177).** This is stock. **What saves it:** Teo does it "with the cut hand, and left blood on him", and later "the swelling gone dark" becomes a social mark at the Ilders'. Keep it.
4. **"There's a teacher. You'll not like it much."** This is a mentor tease, but it's underplayed and comes in a single line. Keep it. The master is the one character the book can't afford to make stock (`08`: his warning about the chip and listening slowly).

No chosen-one prophecy, villain monologue (the King never speaks), "we're not so different", tavern brawl or mentor riddle. The Hookline fight is a power accident, not a brawl.

## C. Clearly original (protect these)

- **The Orc King with hundreds of chips sewn into his skin like buttons, still wet and moving**, drawing on every kneeling orc's Remnant through their necks. This is the saga's own image of soul theft combined with the orc chain.
- **The flesh economy as street texture:** "CLONE STOCK — NO SOUL" stamps, "Real, not clone" meat sold under the counter, the lift core that twitches like cut meat, the living bridge "warm like a horse", and the arm "less than a goat's". It all comes straight from `10` and `07`.
- **The chip as a sense organ unlocked:** "Sun in the next room", "It's loud", and the Tower felt through a wall. No other book has this.
- **Readers at every hundred steps**: cold down orc arms, a light for humans, and green for blood-covered Eli. Also the gate that bites and "Late. Logged."
- **Burnout written as a body failing**: split palms, fingers bending back, steam. This is Will without Skill, from the codex, with nothing borrowed from another magic system.
- **The steward's "Up"**, lates and effects "going up", the CAUSE box, and the claim of eleven hundred against forty-one. That's bureaucratic cruelty in the codex's far-future register.
- **Eighty-eight years** as a running joke that turns: Eli tells it, Dace re-tells it, the sock is claimed against it.
- **Hurrak**, the orc escape song that nobody sings whole, and the children's game where "the gate bit him". It's a folk myth that belongs to the people.
- **The watch with four worlds and a little moon**, the tax paid in it, and its return via "Walk well". **The second-best coat folded in four.** **Jon's plum stones.**

---

**Verdict: CLEAN.** No item is too close. One recognisable echo is worth fixing (A1, the flyer lifted from the lake), plus one optional trim (B1, the "proud... grew into" line). The Vosk rename is Patrick's call. So there are 2 items to fix, and both are small.
