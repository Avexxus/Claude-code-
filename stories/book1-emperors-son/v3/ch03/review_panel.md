# Reader panel: Ch 3 "Company" (v3)

🌊 Claude reader-panel output. This is not canon. Chapters 1 and 2 were read for context only; only Chapter 3 is judged.

---

## 1. The grimdark veteran

- **DNF:** None. The nearest I came was a wince at *"Everything's got a price, son."* That's a recruiter's line from a hundred other books. The chapter comes back from it because the price it names is concrete.
- **Lost:** Nowhere. I don't need to know how Jon got in. I'd rather not know yet.
- **Loved:** The steward stripping the bunk. *"Seeds," he said. "Unknown."* Then Eli lies, *"He owed me." / "Eleven hundred."*, and it goes down as *"Claim, eleven hundred… Against forty-one."* The only way to get the sock is to turn the dead man into a debtor. That's consequence, written as bookkeeping. A close second is *"Rest of him goes by weight, down there."*, said round a mouthful of plum.
- **Fake:** *"Eli's hand closed. It went all the way, the first time since the stair."* Rage brings the power back right on cue. It's too tidy. I'd rather the hand stayed broken for a while.
- **Tell a friend:** "The grief chapter doesn't go soft. A boy's death is put through the paperwork, and his rich friends turn it into a party bit."

## 2. The casual fantasy reader

- **DNF:** None, but I drifted in the bath and window stretch (*"It was afternoon. It was evening."*).
- **Lost:**
  - The house-bell calls. I didn't know who "madam" is, or why "Nothing drawn. Not since." matters.
  - *"He's asking."* I wasn't sure who "he" was until Jon names the King, many pages later.
  - Teo's gate page. I had to reread it to see that "nobody came in" means Jon shouldn't be there. Even then I couldn't tell whether that's spooky or a plot hole.
  - I missed that Eli took the letter. *"His hand went down to the table, by the bowl"* is too quiet for me.
  - I didn't know why the disc stays dark at the end.
- **Loved:** Ilder telling the yard story: *"You came off the step and got my hand in your mouth… My father had to put his thumb in your jaw."* It finally connects to what Vosk said at the end of Chapter 2, and I got it straight away.
- **Fake:** *"the house was quiet in a different way from before"*. It tells me there's a mood without saying what it is.
- **Tell a friend:** "Sad and slow for half of it, then a strange man is eating plums in his kitchen and offering him the Tower, and that's when it kicks off."

## 3. The hater

- **DNF:** This is where I nearly stopped: *"There's a teacher." / "For me?" / "There's a teacher."* A grey-bearded fiftyish mentor turns up in the kitchen at dawn. He offers a crew, a job, a teacher, and the top of the Tower, and the price is "don't come back." That's the call to adventure from a template.
- **Lost:** Not lost. Suspicious. Teo proves nobody came through the gate. If the book doesn't pay that off later, it's a convenience.
- **Loved:** Even I'll give it this. Dace says *"Eighty-eight years," flat and low, in the voice Eli had done on the roof,* and *"when he didn't Dace laughed harder, at that."* The joke Eli started comes back at him, and the book doesn't explain it.
- **Fake:**
  - **The name "Jon."** It's a plain Earth name in a world of Vosk, Velk, Ilder, Dace and Lisane. It reads as a placeholder.
  - **The plum ritual.** Three times a plum is turned over and put back (the cook once, Jon twice), and the stone row is counted on four separate beats. A motif done three times becomes a tic.
  - **Repeated habits.** The cast keep *"studied"* things (knees, the watch, a hand). The steward holds the slate *"against his chest"* or turns it *"face in"* in almost every scene.
- **Tell a friend:** "Good grief writing, and then a stock mentor walks in eating fruit."

## 4. The literary reader

- **DNF:** None. This is the best-controlled chapter so far.
- **Lost:** The gate log is a fine piece of subtext, but it's laid out twice in full. The second time, the reader is ahead of Teo.
- **Loved:**
  - **The brush.** The cook finds it pink at the roots, bins it, and then *"scraped them into the bin on top of the brush."* Nobody says what happened, and nobody needs to.
  - **The glass on the lawn.** Eli puts it on the grass, *"three steps off the stone,"* where the Ilders' man can't go. It's his first cruel act and his first act of solidarity, and it's done with no words. I can't tell which it is, and that's the point.
  - **The cook.** *"I never got plums," she said, to the bowl.*
  - **Teo's last choice.** He pockets the stone and leaves the cup in its ring, and he answers with that instead of a speech.
- **Fake:**
  - *"He waited for the wait."* It's a little pleased with itself.
  - *"After it stopped he went on listening."* It's a stock grief cadence.
  - The *"and… and… and"* chains are overused in the first three sections. They flatten what should be numbness into a monotone.
- **Tell a friend:** "Grief written as objects (a brush, a sock, a glass, a plum), with almost no feelings named."

## 5. The young sci-fi fan

- **DNF:** I skimmed from the bath to the window (*"he stood and studied it"*), and again through the party's piano. I wouldn't quit, but the first 40% has no forward motion.
- **Lost:** How did Jon get past the gate? I want to know, but I liked not knowing.
- **Loved:**
  - **The tech on the way out.** *"The disc in the wall stayed dark. It didn't look at him."* There's no chime and no voice. He's off the grid, and you feel it physically.
  - **The new orc at the slot.** The reader's light stays on his neck and won't come on for Eli.
  - **The watch.** *"The four faces were all going… Grey had got into the links."*
  - **The gate page,** which logs Eli as *"the hour and no name… GOOD MORNING, TEO."*
- **Fake:** *"Good view, I'm told."* Cute deflection is the oldest move a mentor has. I wanted one real hint about what's up there.
- **Tell a friend:** "Slow first half, but the last scene, where the gate goes dark for him, makes me need Chapter 4."

## Panel summary

### Top problems (flagged by two or more)
1. **Jon as a stock mentor** (hater, grimdark, sci-fi). The lines are *"There's a teacher"* twice, *"Everything's got a price, son,"* and *"Good view, I'm told."* Fix: cut one "There's a teacher." Make the price stranger or more specific than "don't come back" and "obey."
2. **The name "Jon"** (hater, literary by implication). It reads as a generic Earth name, and CLAUDE.md forbids generic Earth names. Rename it, flavored to his race, and log it as LOCAL.
3. **Plum and stone repetition** (hater, literary, sci-fi). Plums are turned over and put back three times, and the stone row is counted four times. Keep the cook's beat and one of Jon's. Drop one stone count.
4. **Slow, flat first 40%** (casual, sci-fi, literary on the "and" chains). The bath, the window, *"It was afternoon. It was evening."*, and the party's piano drag. Trim the and-chains in the first three sections.
5. **Clarity of the hooks** (casual, hater). The reader needs to understand:
   - who "madam" is
   - who "he's asking" refers to
   - that nobody came through the gate
   - that Eli took the letter
   - why the disc is dark

   One small anchor each, for example a half-line making "he" the King at the Ilders' man's beat. Make sure the gate-log mystery reads as intended and doesn't look like an error.
6. **The fist closing on cue** (grimdark, hater). *"It went all the way, the first time since the stair."* Consider having it close only partway, or holding the beat back.
7. **Tics** (hater, literary, casual). These are *"studied"* (knees, watch, hand), *"slate against his chest / face in"*, *"the house was quiet in a different way from before"*, and *"He waited for the wait."*

### Protect these (loved by two or more)
- **The steward's inventory of effects and the eleven-hundred claim** (grimdark, literary, hater by tone, casual). *"Seeds… Unknown." / "Claim, eleven hundred… Against forty-one."*
- **The brush in the bin, with the eggs scraped onto it** (literary, grimdark).
- **The glass left on the grass three steps off the stone** (literary, grimdark).
- **Ilder's bite story and *"Every time I pick up a glass"*** (casual, literary).
- **Dace's "Eighty-eight years" in the borrowed voice, and Eli not laughing** (hater, grimdark).
- **The disc staying dark in the slot, and the dried smear on the wall** (sci-fi, literary, casual once explained).
- **The cook's *"I never got plums"*** (literary, casual).
- ***"Rest of him goes by weight, down there."*** (grimdark, sci-fi).
- **Teo pockets the stone, leaves the cup, and laces his boot with his teeth** (literary, casual).
- **"Did they catch him?"** It echoes the Hurrak story and lands without comment (literary, grimdark).
