## 1. CANON

### 1.1 The era and the frame
- Book 1 is set in the **far future**, "when the rift is at its widest... the conclusion where reality is breaking." (`09 t334`)
- It is the son's journey, Jon's run where he stops (BANK 11-01 time hints: "jon has decided he wont reset anymore"). Each run lasts about 10,000 years, and there have been 1,000 or more (`09 wk W42`). "The final run" is Jon's lie to himself (`09 wk W42`).
- The war stays mostly off screen: "your watching just the life of a hero living in the world they created until the pivotal moment" (`09 t332`).
- Book 1 is "largely ambiguous". Patrick: "I would insult people by info dumping on the last page" (BANK 11-01 W50).
- Time travel appears only as three subtle hints: (1) **rifts**, which are ground sites, scars from resets, where Remnant leaks through and war never stops; (2) **elves grieving** for people nobody can name; (3) **Derrek's impossible foreknowledge**. No rewind is shown (BANK 11-01 time travel; Lirael's fragments cut, BANK 11-07).
- Rifts form wherever Jon jumps, off Earth too (BANK 11-07 #3).

### 1.2 Earth, the capital, the Tower
- **Earth** is "everyone's homeworld and the human capital, not the only capital." The **Galactic Council sits on Earth, in human buildings** (`10 wk W58`; `07 wk W58`).
- Far-future Earth is "a planet-wide sprawling metropolis across every continent; weather long since controlled; food imported on massive convoys (no farmland)" (`07 L320`, v5-verify). It is controlled by humans (the sprawl), a few clever changelings at court, and the dragon houses that sided with humanity; orcs are cattle; the elves left (they come back for the Council); the giants were indifferent until the strike (`07 L321`).
- There are no aliens. Beyond Earth the universe is lifeless, and the races settle empty worlds (`WORLD_OVERVIEW`, "A dead sky").
- **The city never moved**: "The main human city is the main dragon city is the main giant city... Only the ruler changed." (`10 t64`)
- **The Tower** is the converged giant emperor's city. On betrayal day, as the advisor dragon loosed his breath, the dying emperor "converged his entire city to protect against" it (BANK 11-01 L7). He didn't survive as himself: every soul in the city that day is trapped in the walls, "a giant mass of remnant with no center or focus... no sensory organs" (BANK 11-01 L7 follow-up, "is the emperor alive").
- The Tower is "**the largest source of remnant on earth**... a massive amplifier"; Derrek rarely leaves it; it has been the seat of power since its creation; in the final Tower sequence "everyone is at their absolute peak" (BANK 11-01 L8).
- Derrek built only additions to it: "elevators and lazer arays and teleprompter rooms not the walls itself" (`01 t62`). It sits on the first and largest rift, Derrek's portal (`01 t66`). The dragon empire held it before Derrek (`07 wk J`).
- The region was radioactive for a long time after the blast; animals avoid the Tower and it carries strange legend (`07 t88`). It "seems to exist between places" (`10 t382`).

### 1.3 The Emperor (Eli's father)
- "**The Emperor is faceless.** No one knows who the emperor is; he is known only as the Emperor, ruling from the Tower. (The name 'Derrek' belongs to Jon alone.)" (`10 t364`)
- He won the seat by campaign: "He first had to unite the humans. He had to arm them. Then he used them to seize the tower from the heart of the dragon empire and he had to build his seat here." (`08 t494`)
- He rules "through a concentrated and dominant physical rule. He is truly the emperor of humanity. So of course he would control the legion." (`08 t492`)
- Humans see him "as a literal embodiment of god" (`10 t532`). They worship Derrek and Jon's patron, in a faith Derrek started as he conquered them (`10 t488`).
- He engineered the forge-world strike (`10 wk W64`).
- He has had endless lives and endless sons, so no single life means anything to him, his son included. He loves Jon (BANK 11-08 Derrek's love). In Book 1 he "doesnt even really notice his son. He may as well be drywall" (BANK 11-01 L3 follow-up).
- He is tethered to Jon's jumps through one pact tether to their shared master, the Unified One (BANK 11-01 NP#6a). He sees time; only Jon resets (`09`; BANK 11-01).
- Neither man can truly die: the keeper hard-resets them on the spot (BANK 11-01 L4). They never kill each other; Jon's plan is to drain and restrain him (BANK 11-01 L4 follow-ups). At the window he is drained and restrained, not dying (BANK 11-01 "Ya I dont think he can die lol").
- He dabbles in soul magic but never lets anyone into his own body (`08 wk W51`).

### 1.4 The son (Eli)
- He is Derrek's son, existing only in this run (`08 t350`). He is "**a man, not a child**... raised in the seat of power likely given maximum education... capable and courageous and charming" (`08 t752–t754`).
- "He knows he is royalty, not that his father is Derrek" (`09 t364`).
- Opening: "Saved at the edge of a human city. This boy was far from home. We see him escape a town guard and we wonder what he stole." (`08 t362`). Derrek squeezed him into fleeing on the day Jon arrived (`09 wk W41`).
- He gathered Jon's team of sacrifices and brought them to the Tower; neither man's plan to use him as a lever worked (BANK 11-01 W41).
- His strength is swaying people. His final attack is an emotional one, and he can't beat a god by force (BANK 11-08 Eli's role; `08 t372`).
- The friend "escape[d] the palace with derreks son" (`08 t762`).

### 1.5 Jon
- An outsider who helped Mika as a boy; "He has some favor but he isnt the actual secret master. Mika is very much in control of his people" (BANK 11-01 W44).
- His warmth runs on autopilot, "quick to switch off", and every attachment carries a value to him (BANK 11-01 L6).
- By this late point in the run, he remembers things (BANK 11-07 NP#3).
- He picked the team "Enough to wear down derrek but not kill him" (BANK 11-01 L4 follow-ups).
- Early in the book his power stays modest: "a believable older wizard act" (BANK 11-08).
- In his true form he is cold during the Tower ascent (`09 wk W49`).

### 1.6 Who fights whom (the canon map)
- **The human Empire:** ruled openly by the faceless Emperor. "Humanity's three challenges": (1) **the war the dragons started**, (2) **the Orc King's plague**, (3) **the elves, sworn to end the Emperor** (`10 t532`). Its goal is to prove humans were never lesser and to win independence from the elder races (`10 t530`).
- **The Legion:** "a human legion that stands in defiance of the elven inquisition", controlled by Derrek (`10 t490/t492`; `01` LEGION).
- **Dragons:** a feudal empire split between the houses the advisor raised and the dispossessed, who "buy cathedral ships to murder each other" (`07 t274`). "**The shared ally is the dragons**": the race that resists human equality hardest has to rely on humans (`10 t534–t536`). Dragon houses that sided with humanity keep seats on Earth (`07 t298`). Dragons were humanity's first teachers; Derrek "studied at a dragon school" (`10 wk W63–W64`). Their worker class is subjugated reptilian kin whose separate identity was erased (BANK 11-01 dragon umbrella, confirmed).
- **Giants:** they build the dragons' warships "so dragons will kill each other" (`WORLD_OVERVIEW`; `07 t94`). Cathedral ships are converged dead, whole families, and now bought orcs (`07 t96, t276–t280`). The galaxy thinks they're dwindling; really they're quietly rebuilt and rich (`10 t416–t418`). After the strike "they refuse to stay passive", build their own fleets and "openly engage every dragon they encounter"; "they have not forgotten man's role" (`09 t414–t420`). The road to the Tower is "a journey thru a giant space war" (`09 t422`).
- **Elves:** Mika's realm, authoritarian and wholly subservient to the king (`10 jul23 t132`). Sworn to end the Emperor (`10 t532`). Mika alone ordered the exodus (BANK 11-01 L10).
- **The Inquisition (elven):** its main task is stopping whoever causes the resets; hunting dark elves is "its side gig" (`10 t490`). It "secretly sells captured dark elves through dwarven traffickers to rich humans; 'soul thieves' was the cover" (`10 wk W68`). It hunts whoever causes the resets without knowing it's Jon (BANK 11-01 W44).
- **Dwarves:** "selling to both sides is just business" (`10 t146`). They build the drives and the focusing chambers (`10 t538`). Dwarven traffickers carry the Inquisition's sales (`10 wk W68`). Gnomes are their indentured class, sold to whoever wants one (`10 t568`).
- **Orcs:** "the galaxy's cattle", bought by the trillion as ship material (`10 t334, t278–t280`). The orc device is a kill switch plus a puppet function; Derrek built it with a faction of the Returned and controls it from the Tower (`01` ORC DEVICE, t624).

### 1.7 Places in the spine (canon parts)
- **The forge world:** the giants' shipyards. A dragon "annihilates the heart" of it (`09 t410–t412`, engineered by Derrek `wk W64`).
- **The rift world (the master's):** rifts are "large reservoirs people fight over" (`10 t188`); they are places of deep significance where empires are forged and war never stops (BANK 11-01 time travel).
- **The weak place / the door:** "somewhere you go and are never seen again", where the pocket world and ours are thinnest, the secret door; it "feels like the Tower" (`10 t382`).
- **The elven city with Lirael's statue,** where Jon pauses "a moment too long" (`09 t392–t394`).
- **The forgotten desert colony** where the seedship came down, the queen joins, and the son's crew arrives on a stolen Returned ship (`08 wk W65`).
- **Hell:** one hell, the elven prison realm (BANK 11-01 NP#5). "Its not even hell. Its a normal world like ours. They just name it such." (BANK 11-07 #2). Humans cross the stars by traversing it, which is how breakouts happen (BANK 11-01 NP#5; `10 t438`).

### 1.8 Magic and soul rules the story touches
- Three dials: Remnant (strength), control (skill), inner sense. You can be "a 'numb titan,' enormously strong and nearly blind" (`WORLD_OVERVIEW`, magic). The cost is fatigue.
- **Remnant is never burned, spent or transferred** (`wk W10`). Held souls are **amplifiers only, never batteries**: "size of remnent determines top capacity... using another's gives you more range". You can also "grab them and force them to do it" (BANK 10-31 L1/L2 revised).
- **Burnout** is the in-world folk word for overuse destroying the body; the Remnant isn't used up (BANK 10-31 NP#2).
- Soul-sensing is a rare, master-level skill (`wk W07`); the elves' bond is the exception.
- **The elven bond** scales with size and distance. A lone elf far away is a pinprick; the homeworld shines "like a beacon in the dark to all elves everywhere"; up close "you would feel the elves and they would feel you" (BANK 11-01 L9). It doesn't cross between pocket worlds (`10 t230, t600`).
- **"Where ever an elf is born they are just an elf."** "Dark elf" and "drow" are the elves' own cruel labels for the sentenced and their descendants (BANK 11-07 #1).
- **Changelings** possess; they don't shapeshift. Transfers are never witnessed on the page (`07`; notes D §10.3). The gnome was a village shaman and healer who took the sick slowly and told families "I wasn't able to save your loved one" (BANK 11-01 L24). His Tower barrage isn't seen: "Just the weight of his raw renmant thru the captured souls flooding out" (BANK 11-07 #5).
- **Magic weapons are living flesh**, shaped by a Convergence-like ritual (BANK 11-01 L11, confirmed). Elf flesh holds a lot of Remnant; the Queen's sword is a drow blade grown from a captured exiled elf. "The younger the flesh the better its quality" (BANK 11-08 G-items).
- **The rule-beast** is an in-betweener in its Void, laid over real Tower rooms, with a puzzle through the rooms; Derrek trapped it by rehearsing their meeting thousands of times (`09 t426–t430`; `WORLD_OVERVIEW`). The master's sacrifice is earned "because the rule beast would have one by one killed them all" (BANK 11-07).
- **Lirael's Collapse** is her signature move. She was "a skilled user, not a gravity type" (`08 t612`). Derrek turns it inside out on the dark elf in front of Jon (`08 t388`).

### 1.9 Tech and trade the story touches
- Simple machines run on ordinary power. Complex, AI-run machines need flesh with Remnant held in it (BANK 11-02 set 1).
- **Soul-cores** are flesh cores grown around a measured draw of pool Remnant; a bigger core makes a more capable machine. **Remnant wells** are pools mined like oil, with rift sites the richest. **Amplifier rigs** extend a wearer's reach, and burnout is the soldier's death. **Tainted cores** are orc-soul cores sold cheap and mislabeled as pool cores. **Grown structures and hulls are Convergence**, "the same way the tower was born." Clone organs and flesh exist. (BANK 11-02 techs 1–8)
- Machines can't sense souls, except very specific chip-based devices (BANK 11-02 techs, rejected 3).
- A soul-core feels only "vague general things and maybe some strangeness" (BANK 11-02 Q-A).
- Fast interstellar messaging exists without the bond (BANK 11-02 systems).
- Currencies: several, with an imperial coin, a dwarven mark and an elven silver crown trusted most. The names are placeholders (BANK 11-02 money).
- The public doesn't know its tech runs on souls. People think Remnant is "a material. They find pools of it", and they believe it works as a battery, which is a misconception. Soul theft counts among the worst crimes (BANK 11-02 public knowledge).
- Clone bodies are seen as lower quality "but the irony is they are the same so it is often sold as real anyway" (BANK 11-02 set 1). Clones have souls; everyone pretends they don't (`10 wk W66`). The organ trade is a legal government market with a black market beneath it; captured dark elves are part of it (`10 wk W67`, t349–t351).
- **FTL:** humans traverse hell; dwarves squeeze a dimension; elves shrink one of their dimensions and enter heaven, which cuts them off from the bond in transit; dragons drive jumps with crews in focusing chambers, the ship's master being "the final lever" (`10 t438–t444`; `07 t442`).
- Trolls are small: "Smaller than an orc larger than a goblin... Bridge trolls and the like" (BANK 11-01 trolls).
- Humans may ride captured lesser dragons; "a powerful dragon would never sink so low" (BANK 11-01 riders).

### 1.10 Betrayal day (as the giant can tell it)
- The striker was "the closest advisor to the giant emperor", never the ruler of dragons (`07 t268`). His stone was his greatest creation: the most unstable element he could make, broken in the final moment as fissile fuel for his breath (BANK 11-01 L7 stone).
- "The capital's dragons were camouflage": courtiers and family weren't told, and they stayed as acceptable losses (`07 t264`). So **dragons died on betrayal day too**, which is what O8 hangs on.
- The same day, dragons he had chosen killed giant rulers *and* the reigning dragon rulers (`07 t266`). The world doesn't remember the giants' age, because the dragons erased the record (`10 wk W61`).
