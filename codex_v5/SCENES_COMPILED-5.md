His.

"No one's come through. I've had this stretch since the horn sounded." Said plainly, without the seams showing — not an empty thing failing to fake a reaction, but a competent one giving exactly the reaction the moment calls for.

It doesn't hold up, and everyone in the corridor knows it in the same instant: they watched a man run this way with their own eyes, this is the only body in the passage, and he's telling them it's empty. There's no room in that contradiction for an honest mistake. They press him — how, standing right in the man's path, could he have seen nothing, heard nothing, felt nothing pass — and get nothing back but the same flat denial, offered without a flicker of the strain a lie should cost. There's nowhere to take the questioning that produces an answer, because there isn't one to find. A king is bleeding two corridors behind them and the search has a hundred better places to spend its next minute than a guard who won't break. They leave, furious and unsatisfied, certain something is wrong and unable to say what.

He lets himself smile only once the corridor is his again, and no one is left in it to see it.
