## The one-sentence version

**Magic is a being consciously reaching into reality and making it do what they want.** Everything else is detail.

---

## 1. Magic and science are the same thing

This world does not invent its own physics — it nods to ours, loosely, as *flavor*. But here's the key: **the people of this world don't separate "magic" from "science."** They're the same word. What we'd call a fundamental force, they'd call a working of magic. We use real-world phenomena (gravity, fire, lightning, transmutation) to *describe and flavor* what a mage does — but we never do the math, and we never let physics veto a cool moment. The science serves the story; the instant it would make something less cool or add homework, it loses.

[NEW v5-wk] Why do humans call it "science"? Because dragons were humanity's first teachers (wk W63). And the world goes further: faith, magic and science are one act under different names — the name changes, the act doesn't (wk W57).

And there is **no math**, ever. Nobody draws up size charts for giants or works out how their bodies bear their own weight; nobody owes the reader an engineering explanation for anything. Dragons are the standing proof: they fly because they are dragons.

So when you ask "could she actually do that?" the answer is never decided by a physics calculation. [REV v5-wk] It's decided by a few simple things — how much power she is, how skilled she is, how finely she senses, and how tired she is (§4). (was: "It's decided by three simple things — how much power she is, how skilled she is, and how tired she is.")

---

## 2. Where magic comes from: consciousness vs. matter

Two separate things exist, and they only touch at one point.

**Matter** — the physical universe — has always existed, running on its own with no god behind it. It's just stuff. A rock is only physics; it has no mind, no spark, no magic. [NEW v5-wk] (One exception, covered in §9: a soul can be *held* in matter. That's what an enchantment is. Even then the matter does no magic — the soul held in it does.)

**Consciousness** comes from *the One* — originally a single awareness that shattered into countless fragments. Every living, thinking being carries one. That fragment is your mind, your soul, your awareness. Its name is **Remnant**.

You are matter (your body) *plus* a fragment of consciousness (your mind) inhabiting it. **Flesh is just flesh; the fragment is what makes you a being.** They meet at exactly one place: **magic.**

That's also why a machine can never do magic. In later eras people build technology that reproduces many of magic's *effects* by purely mechanical means, and we describe those effects in the same words — but it is a parallel effect, never the same act. Magic is a conscious being working on the world; a machine holds no fragment of the One, so there's nobody there to do it.

---

## 3. Remnant and Spirit

Two words, and they're easy to mix up, so here's the clean split:

**REMNANT is *what you are*** — the chunk of the One you carry. It's your capacity: how much of reality you can grip at once. It's innate and fixed for life, and it **can't be given, lent, or shared** — what passes between beings is knowledge or technique, never capacity. The only way to grow it is to take other people's souls into your own, which is the darkest crime there is (§8). [NEW v5-wk] And it can **never be burned or spent** — the author's words: "we cant burn remnant." Taking someone else's Remnant is called **stealing their production**: how the thief makes use of it — driving their grip, or "using an amplifier to touch a wider pool" — the author hasn't decided ("Im not sure") (wk W10, W11) [REV v5-wk2] (was: "the thief drives their grip" — that was Claude's framing, chat t84, never confirmed; chat t83–t85). A beast has a tiny fragment; a human more; a god an enormous one.

And Remnant is **ageless.** The fragment of the One doesn't grow old; only flesh does. A bigger fragment helps the body resist age, which is why the beings with the most Remnant live the longest — but it only slows the body down, it never stops it. Everything with a body ages and dies in the end.

**SPIRIT is *using it*** — the name for active Remnant magic. Spirit is a being consciously manipulating reality with its fragment. **Having Spirit is exactly what separates a conscious being from inert flesh.** A rock can't do it; you can.

That's the whole engine: a being reaches out with Spirit and makes reality do what it wants. One undivided act. (There's no longer a "physical magic vs. soul magic" split — it's all just Spirit. The rare case where you turn it on another mind instead of on matter is covered in §8.)

[NEW v5-wk] **How the reaching works: the appendage.** Your Remnant extends an **appendage** that grabs the universe. How long, how big, what shape and how intense it is all scale with your Remnant, your skill and your focus. What it does when it grabs is described loosely, never calculated: shake particles and you get heat; pull charges apart and you get lightning; crush or rip a nucleus and you get an explosion; dent space and you get gravity. Turning a quark — transmutation — is the hardest act of all, and a botched one rips the nucleus apart. A dragon, which stays four-legged, uses its appendage as its hands (wk W01, W02, W63).

[NEW v5-wk] **And the part nobody can explain: the inner sense.** Beside the appendage there's a second thing your Remnant gives you — a perception that has no explanation. In the author's words, "you mostly feel with a second inner sense." It's partly born in you and partly trained. The grip is mechanical; the sense stays a mystery (wk W03, W05, W06).

## 4. The three dials and the bill

[REV v5-wk] A being has **three independent dials** — Remnant, control and the inner sense — and one bill, fatigue. And **no physics audit ever.** (was: "## 4. The only three limits — There are exactly three gates on what a being can do, and **no physics audit ever.**")

**REMNANT (how much)** — your capacity. You can't grip more reality than you have consciousness to grip with. This is your natural ceiling. More Remnant = more possible power.

**SKILL (how well)** — how finely and cleverly you grip, shape, and blend. Learned; grows with training and time. Two people with identical Remnant produce wildly different results. [NEW v5-wk] (The author calls this dial **control**.)

[NEW v5-wk] **THE INNER SENSE (how finely you perceive)** — the mysterious perception from §3. Basic magic needs only control; going deeper needs a finer sense. That makes deep magic *costlier* for someone with a dull sense, never forbidden — **a cost gradient, not a gate.** The author's example is the "numb titan": a being with a huge Remnant and a poor sense (wk W03, W04).

**FATIGUE (the bill)** — working magic is work; you tire like a laborer or a grandmaster. Exhaustion is the universal soft limit. Push past what your body and Remnant can pay and you're in danger. (Whether "burning yourself up" is its own named rule, or just a way of saying someone did more than a body survives, is still undecided.) [NEW v5-wk] One thing is settled: whatever overreach does, it never burns your Remnant — Remnant can't be burned or spent (wk W10).

That's it. We do **not** check whether an act is "energetically possible," whether it "needs fusion not weak," or any feasibility math. **Magic can add energy** when the scene calls for it — unbind an atom and an explosion happens; we don't account for where the energy came from. A weak character can't casually crack a planet, not because the math forbids it, but because they lack the Remnant and it would kill them to try. [REV v5-wk] Those dials and that bill generate every limit a story needs. (was: "Those three gates generate every limit a story needs.")

**A note on rifts.** Here and there the world is torn, and power leaks through from the level of reality above. These rifts are great reservoirs of magic that people fight over — but they are **not where magic comes from.** Every being's magic is still its own soul.

## 5. "Forces" and "elements" are just words you say afterward

Here's the part that surprises people: **nobody is a "fire mage" or a "gravity user." There are no types at all.** There are only beings who do magic.

When a being does magic, you *describe* what it looked like using whatever vocabulary fits — and the four real forces and four classic elements are that vocabulary:
- They collapse matter together with great force → you call it **gravity**.
- They unbind an atom and it explodes → you call it **nuclear**.
- They reshape a thing, light it, heat it, shock it → you call it **electromagnetism** (or "fire," "lightning," "light").
- They warp *what a thing fundamentally is* → you call it the **weak force** (alchemy, transmutation, a curse).

The label names the **act**, never the person. The same being who just did something you'd call gravity might, a moment later, throw fire, fling a rock with plain momentum, or harden their skin. It's all one undivided ability; the words are just description applied after the fact.

A good example: the elves' great hero, Lirael, had a famous signature move, **Lirael's Collapse**, which looks like a gravity well crushing inward. That doesn't make her a "gravity person." As the author put it, gravity is only her image — like every character, she is just a skilled user. In the same way, a man once labeled a "Strong-force user" lost the label: he's simply a master, and calling him by a category would take the credit away from a lifetime of work.

And **blending is totally free** — fire plus gravity plus spin makes a fire-gravity tornado, with no permission check, no "paths," no locked growth trees, no "you must learn EM first." A being reaches for whatever the moment calls for.

## 6. Magic comes from *who you are* — never the other way around

This is the most important design rule, so here it is plainly:

**Build the person first.** Their history, their wounds, who taught them, what they survived, what they're drawn to. A whole character, built with no thought to magic. **Then** their magic falls out of *them* — they do what a person like that, shaped like that, would have learned and leaned into. The "force" or "element" flavor is the *last* thing decided, just a description of the output.

There is **no equation in either direction.** Magic doesn't make you patient; being patient doesn't make you "an earth user"; your race doesn't assign you an element. If fifty people's magic sometimes looks like "earth," are they all patient? Of course not — they're fifty different people who found their way to similar effects for fifty different reasons. The "why" is always the life, never the element's imaginary personality.

A lovely consequence: **the same effect means opposite things in two people.** One person's gravity is grief — heaviness, surrender, letting go. Another's is dominance — crushing, pinning, control. Same described effect, opposite souls, because the magic flows from the person.

---

## 7. People aren't types — but they *are* individuals

Loosening the system doesn't make everyone an identical blank reaching into infinity. People have **histories, tastes, strengths, and specialties** — they're just not *classes*. (Wheel of Time is the closest comp: every channeler draws on one single Power, but each has natural strengths, favorite weaves, things they never bothered to learn, things their gift makes easy — real individual differences, without anyone being a "Fire person.")

So a being:
- **knows** the workings they took the time to learn (a learned repertoire),
- **leans on** what comes naturally to them (inclination — which culture and temperament can color, but never lock),
- and **chooses** what to specialize in.

All of that is real and differentiating — it just flows from the person's life, not from a label the system stamped on them. And **magic isn't the only thing to learn:** potions, enchantment, craft, lore — the non-magical disciplines sit right alongside it. A potions master isn't a different *kind* of mage; she's someone who went deep on a craft. This gives low-power and non-prodigy characters somewhere to be excellent, and gives the world real schools and trades.

## 8. The rare second branch: soul magic

Almost all magic grips **matter** — which is safe, because dead matter can't push back. But rarely, a being turns Spirit on **another fragment of the One** — a mind, a memory, an emotion, a will, a soul.

This plays by completely different rules, because you're not touching dead physics — you're touching **living consciousness that touches back.**
- No forces, no elements — it's **will against will.**
- It's **two-way and intimate:** the thing you grip can grip you. The limit isn't fatigue — it's **self-risk.** Overreach and you don't just get tired; you get *changed* — you bleed the other's nature into yours, fracture, lose pieces of yourself.
- It's **rare**, because most beings only ever learn to grip matter; turning the hand inward on another soul is profound and dangerous. It is also **forbidden and scarcely known.**
- It **belongs to no race.** Any people can learn it; nothing makes it one race's art.
- Everyone knows that souls *can* be stolen and moved, and that knowledge is exactly what makes the art both **tempting and taboo.**

This is where mind-reading, soul-binding, possession, the deep grief-bond between certain beings, and the darkest violations all live — one rare, terrible art. The elven bond, the way elves sense one another, is a gentle passive form of it — though it has a harder face too: a strong elf can bind weaker elves to its will through the bond, never totally, and without destroying who they are.

[NEW v5-wk] The bond is innate, constant and collective, and only elves have it — which makes elves the best sensers of souls there are; the elf queen's famous vigilance comes from using it intensely. But it doesn't track individuals. An elf who dies far away dies alone, outside the bond. For everyone else, sensing another being's soul is a rare master's skill: "Some very talented masters have learned to sense other beings remnant. This is rare." On the page it's felt, not reported, and it's unreliable — nobody walks around scanning for souls (wk W07, W08, W09).

At its most violent, soul magic can **override another person's soul and take their body.** It can also swallow souls wholesale: a soul that isn't strong enough to stay itself in the presence of a far greater one is lost in the greater one's swirl. It takes a being at least as strong as a changeling to do this at all; one powerful enough can do it to many at once. (That is different from *giving* Remnant, which no one can do.)

**Echoes, ghosts, and changelings are three different things:**
- **Echoes** are loose Remnant left behind by great death — battlefields, sieges, massacres. They are impressions only; **no person is there**, and they fade back over the centuries. Places of great death are ghost-sites.
- **Ghosts** are something else: in the author's words, "remnant which was bound together by magic." Someone deliberately held a dead person's Remnant together so it couldn't drift apart. **Every true ghost is a soul-binding crime, and every haunting is a crime scene.** People bind ghosts as guardians, servants, power sources, punishment — or out of love.
- **Changelings** are many souls bound into one living person (below).

**Binding never ends.** Holding a soul is a constant effort to hold the magic still — every day, for as long as the binding lasts — and **the holder pays.** On top of soul magic's usual self-risk, that holding is a cost of its own. Kill the holder and the binding lapses: the held soul is freed or destroyed. So to free or end a ghost, you go after whoever is holding it. The author's example: a man holding his dead brother's soul dies, and both brothers are lost in the same tragic moment.

[NEW v5-wk] That constant holding is the *ghost's* rule. An object is different: **objects hold souls without anyone concentrating; ghosts need a holder** (wk W14) [REV v5-wk2: this was Claude's proposal (chat t82) and the author never answered it — undecided, not a rule].

A ghost that lasts thousands of years needs something sicker: a second soul bound permanently into an object, forced to do nothing but hold the first. So an ancient haunting has **two victims**, and breaking the object frees both. Exactly how that works is left deliberately vague — ghost magic isn't meant to be measured. [REV v5-wk] The object holds the second soul — that is an enchantment, a soul held in matter — but the matter itself still does no magic; the soul in it does (wk W12). (was: "The object is only an anchor; the matter itself never comes alive or gains a soul.")

**The vessel varies; the holding is the art.** A held soul can be kept in its own dead body, in an amulet, in a suit of armor. A necromancer's craft is the holding plus the vessel. And not every walking corpse is a ghost: the Orc King's dead are empty flesh moved with no soul inside them at all.

**Changelings — soul thieves.** A changeling is someone who has bound other people's souls to their own, so many that they hardly remember who started it. However crowded it gets inside, **there is only one driver** — the original self — surrounded by noise. There are two ways to become one: **move body or consume.** *Transfer* means forcing your own soul into someone else's body and leaving the old one dead behind you — an old man who finally masters the art would likely take a younger body. *Consuming* means keeping your own body and drawing other souls into it — an ancient being like a dragon would be more likely to do that. Each stolen soul makes the thief's Remnant larger.

A changeling is **a crime, not a race**: "It's your father, it's your uncle, it's the priest at the church." Only a powerful user, deeply versed in this forbidden art, can do it — so **there are no weak changelings.** Changelings live about as long as dragons and giants.

[NEW v5-wk] Most changelings are, by now, monsters; only a few clever ones still hide at court. What gives one away is a **mismatch between soul and body** — the smart ones shield it, or hop to bigger bodies. The sustainable pace is **one or two souls at a time**, and the "only one driver" rule holds only for changelings who keep that pace. Those who don't fail in ugly ways: a giant who **decays** into a house of horrors, a young dark elf who **ruptures** (wk W16, W18, W19, W20, W22).

[REV v5-wk] **Convergence** is the giants' version of the art, and it works on **bodies**. In its funeral form it **releases** the dead giant's soul. The emperor's version was something else: he used his city's bodies as material, and the city's Remnant ended up **trapped inside the stone** — he himself was a mix of souls and dissolved, from taking too many at once. The very first Convergence was **god-forging**, and its lost secret was silencing a crowd of souls into one voice — **"soul mortar."** The greatest giant god is a million minds in unison (wk W15, W17). (was: "a dead giant's soul is burned as the power that grows a new hall or tower, seamless stone with no chisel marks. The dead don't *become* the walls — their power is what grew them. Unlike a ghost, Convergence is spent, not held. (Whether a burned soul experiences anything is left permanently unresolved.)")

[REV v5-wk] What is it like to be held? A bound soul feels something: **dread, without a brain to understand it** (wk W13). (was: "A bound soul has some kind of experience, but full experience takes a working brain — so a soul trapped without one is left in a vague existential dread it has no way to understand.")

[REV v5-wk] **Still undecided:** whether a self survives death at all, and where it goes. **Now decided:** no one burns souls for power — not a soul thief, not Convergence; Remnant can't be burned or spent (wk W10). (was: "…anything more about what being held is like; and how stories where a soul thief burns the souls inside him for power, or Convergence burns a dead soul, fit with the rule that your own Remnant is who you are rather than fuel.")

[NEW v5-wk] The kinds of walking dead: plague (biotech), animated flesh, souls bound to corpses, souls in machines, and what changelings leave behind. And bringing someone back is possible in principle — nothing is destroyed, but the odds are near zero (wk W69, W51).

## 9. Power outside your own Remnant: the external systems

Not all power is your own fragment. Two umbrellas, plus one alien thing:

- **Pacts** — any *deal with a powerful being* for power beyond your own. Demon bargains, god-blessings, blood rituals, divine-domain channeling — all the same system, differing only by *patron* and *price*. A patron **never hands you Remnant or raw power**; it gives you **its own way of doing magic** — its signature technique. **You pay for every use yourself, out of your own limited Remnant;** the patron's Remnant never enters you. That's what keeps pact-holders grounded: a pact doesn't make you strong, it makes you *unrepeatable.* A patron has to be strong enough to support the link, and a soul-magic tether binds you to it — one that fades on your side but never on theirs. What you were taught stays with you even as the link fades, but the patron can always find you and collect: you can stop attuning, but you can't stop being findable. (What a patron does when it collects is decided patron by patron.) A pact is soul magic run backwards: where a soul thief draws a soul in, a patron connects to you and opens the flow. As the saying goes, **a god asks for your devotion; a demon offers you a deal** — and what any patron demands depends on that patron. A pact lets a weak being reach far past what they are, but only by paying for it out of their own small soul — and someone always holds the leash. (Whether that kind of overreach simply exhausts a person or truly burns them out is undecided.) (The Orc King is the example: an orc with a small soul, given a pact over entropy — rot, decay, plague, famine — casting something far bigger than he is. Who his patron is remains undecided, as is what a patron ultimately gains from a pact.) [REV v5-wk] For gods, part of that answer is now in: a religion is a portrait of its god and a recruiting network of pact champions, and **the tether feeds the god.** What other patrons gain is still undecided (wk W57). (was: "as is what a patron ultimately gains from a pact" with no part answered.)
  - **Angels and demons** are one kind of being under two names, and they are the same kind of being as changelings: the oldest, vastest soul mages. Being kind doesn't make one an angel, and being cruel doesn't make one a demon; the line is far more blurred than the words suggest.
- [REV v5-wk] **Enchantments** — **a soul held in matter.** Matter can hold a soul because a body is itself matter made alive, so the more body-like the material, the better it holds. The matter does no magic; the soul in it does. An object may hold its soul without anyone concentrating on it [REV v5-wk2: that part was Claude's proposal (chat t82) and is undecided], and the soul inside feels dread it has no brain to understand. The older picture — a reservoir you spend, a focus that sharpens your craft, a permanent working locked into an object, "finite… devastating until spent" — is still being reconciled with the rule that Remnant can't be spent; that part is undecided. Enchantment is also a learnable craft (the dwarves are its candidate masters) (wk W12, W13, W14, W10). (was: "**Enchantments** — power *stored in matter*. A reservoir you spend, a focus that sharpens your craft, or a permanent working locked into an object (an unbreakable blade). The equalizer for low-Remnant or non-mage characters — finite, can't improvise, but devastating until spent.")
- **The Rule** — the alien operating system of the in-betweeners, ancient beings who predate the One. Not a deal and not Remnant: a binding condition imposed on a sealed space that traps everyone inside until its terms are met. This is the "the arena itself has different physics" kind of fight.
  - An in-betweener never actually stands in our world. Around it is its **Void**, a little pocket of its own, and it moves that pocket to wherever it wants to be — what looks like one standing in a room is its Void brought to that place. The Rule is played inside the Void, and a Void can lie on top of real rooms or real land, so the people caught in it may not realize at first that anything has changed. Its **Chain** binds a victim and drags them into the Void; the two used together are called **Chain Void.** These are powers all in-betweeners share.
  - Inside its Void an in-betweener has no true form, but it can step out for a moment and take a physical body. It can also **split** into as many forms as it likes — but the more pieces, the less it holds together, and if it goes too far no piece remembers who it was, and it can never come back together. That is its true death.
  - It is bound by its own Rule but not trapped by it, the way a spider isn't trapped by its own web. And in-betweeners rarely take a contest they don't feel sure of winning.
  - (These in-betweener details are a working foundation the author can still adjust; the in-betweeners' nature — an older lineage running the Rule instead of Remnant — is fixed.) [NEW v5-wk] They stay alien and separate — the "remakers" (wk W37).
  - A Rule can be laid over a whole region, not just a room: a real stretch of land, with hills, rivers, fish and birds, turned into a maze of warped reality where whole peoples are born and live without ever leaving. The dwarves called the **Returned** (a placeholder name) won their way out of one. What they learned in there — phasing, coming apart and re-forming, squeezing warped space around themselves — is **new Remnant magic learned from their surroundings, not stolen in-betweener power.**

We never pre-write the specifics of any of these — a particular deity's terms, a relic's bound effect, the wording of a Rule are all decided when a scene actually needs them.

## 10. How a being grows, and what they actually do

**Awakening is a gradient, never a switch.** Nobody "gets powers" one day. Ability runs along a smooth line of *control and understanding*: first your power leaks out involuntarily (fires start near you, water swirls unbidden), then crude intent, then real control, then the ability to engineer precise repeatable effects, then mastery. Your Remnant sets your *ceiling*; practice sets how far up it you climb; neglect dulls it.

**What practitioners do comes in two layers:**
1. **Raw working** — having grasped a principle, you improvise rough effects. Most never go beyond this.
2. **Spells** — specific, complex, *reproducible* workings, engineered by deep understanding (the magical equivalent of a repeatable experiment, not a slotted plug-and-play ability). This is why there are schools, lineages, secret techniques, lost arts, and "she knows this but not that."

**And mastery is *combination*.** A single effect is simple; the artistry — and the reason no two powerful mages look alike — is in running many operations at once and weaving them. The showpiece image: a being mighty enough to hold matter in a gravity well while igniting a star at its heart. Two huge effects, woven, by sheer Remnant and skill.

## 11. Power is a smooth scale — no wall between mortal and god

"Demigod," "god" — these are just *points on one continuous scale* of Remnant × skill × time, never separate categories. There's no level-up moment, no hard ceiling between mortal and god. A god is simply a being with a huge fragment and lifetimes of practice. Given enough Remnant and enough time, a mortal *becomes* one — which is exactly what eternity made of the two time-beings at the center of the story.

**A god is what happens when any user becomes incredibly powerful.** A god's magic is the same Remnant magic as everyone's — every being develops its own powers, and gods are simply the ultimate expression of that. The author names two paths, physical and psychic: a god can be a being who perfected gravity magic to harden their body beyond stone, or one who can twist matter at a whim (flavor words for the act, as always, never a type). Angels and demons, for their part, are masters of soul magic. Changeling, angel or demon, and god are blurred regions along one line: nobody graduates from changeling school and wakes up an angel, and where the line becomes god is murkier still.

[NEW v5-wk] Every god was born of flesh, on one world. Dragons, giants and elves can grow into gods on their own — "singular" gods. Humans can't: a human reaches godhood only by time-fusion (as Jon and Derrek did) or by theft. Singular gods revile changelings, though power earns respect regardless, and a changeling who climbs that high still isn't a god in the singular sense. There's no formula for which become gods and which demons — behavior is individual (wk W30, W31, W32).

## 12. The built-in reader mystery

In the early, medieval era, characters believe in fire and water and gods — the folk vocabulary *is* their whole worldview. As the world develops science across the eras, the truth surfaces: there were never separate "elements," only one undivided power that beings describe in different words — and "magic" was the workings of reality all along. A reader who knows science feels the slow click of recognition the characters spend the whole saga earning.

[REV v5-wk] (Book numbers and the order of eras are placeholders now — scenes float until they find homes — so how this mystery plays out for a reader is undecided. The eras themselves stand as described.) (was: "The books now open in the far future and work backward toward the past, so how this mystery plays out for a reader — kept, reversed, or dropped — is undecided." — wk W52.)

*The short version, once more: **a being carries a fragment of the One (Remnant); using it to consciously shape reality is Spirit; how much you can shape is Remnant, how well is skill, [NEW v5-wk] how finely you perceive is the inner sense, and the only bill is fatigue. Everything a "mage" does is one undivided act, described afterward in whatever flavor fits — and who they are is always built first, with the magic flowing out of the person, never the other way around.***
