## PART ONE — CRAFT

## Craft principle: the numbers are scaffolding, never on the page 🟡
The Remnant/skill/fatigue mechanics are our *engineering tool* for adjudicating what's possible. The reader must only feel the **result** — that one fighter is deep/patient, another fast/hot — never the math. NEVER write "her two parts Earth," "my fire 6," etc. (And per the rework there are no force/element "types" to write anyway — describe the *act*, named in flavor after the fact.) Physics is flavor only — "forces as flavor, not law"; no math, no auditing (t0–5, t72).
- **No character has a "type."** Every character is "just a skilled user" (t612 — Patrick stopped Claude calling the dark-elf heroine a "gravity" anything; her Collapse is a tribute, not a specialty).
- [NEW v5-wk] **What the act is, in flavor (wk W01–W06):** Remnant extends an **appendage** that grabs the universe; its reach and intensity scale with Remnant, skill and focus (W01). The grip's acts, flavor-level only: shaking particles = heat; pulling charges apart = lightning; crushing or ripping the nucleus = explosions; turning a quark = transmutation, the hardest act (a botched one rips the nucleus); denting space = gravity (W02). Three dials — **Remnant** (strength), **control** (skill), **the inner sense** (an unexplained perception from Remnant, trainable and innate; e.g. the "numb titan": huge Remnant, poor sense) (W03); deeper work costs finer sense — a gradient, not a gate (W04). "You mostly feel with a second inner sense" — feel is a dial, not a gate (W05). The grip is mechanized; **the sense stays mysterious** — never explain it on the page (W06).
- [NEW v5-wk] **Remnant is never burned or spent** (wk W10). Taking others' Remnant is "stealing their production" — [REV v5-wk2] the mechanism is 🌊 open — the thief driving their grip, or "using an amplifier to touch a wider pool" (Patrick, chat t83: "Im not sure") (was: "the thief drives their grip" — Claude's t84 framing, never confirmed) (wk W11). Don't write souls or Remnant as fuel; where Patrick's existing drafts say "burns," a bracketed note flags it.

## Craft principle: the grimdark register 🟡 (locked this session — full detail in `09_STRUCTURE.md`)
The world is a brutal hellscape (betrayal, murder, slavery, abuse, fear, corruption) — and that brutality is a **forge**, not decoration: it manufactures the hardness that makes the showpieces parse (the master's indoctrinated self-sacrifice — formerly "the Strong-user's," label retired t136; Jon's coldness). Patrick's own label: "my work truly fits the label of grimdark" (t658). Hold these disciplines when writing:
- **Cost-and-characterize, never titillate.** Per scene: *does this reveal character or world, or is it just being watched?*
- **Contrast is the delivery system.** Tenderness is the *setup*; violence is the punchline [RESTORED v5-verify] (the kind moment before the loss is what makes the loss land). Unrelenting darkness goes numb — guard the ratio; the warmth must be real enough to hurt, not an obvious death-flag.
- **Simultaneity:** a small, funny, human moment running while a war kills millions offstage — sharpens both. The light lives in the protagonists (not everyone is cold, or there's nothing left to lose).
- **Nothing left to cheer for (t380):** "They all die. There can be nothing left to cheer for by the end when you watch Jon maybe choose to end everything." Everyone the reader loves dies in the Tower; no hopeful survivors. "In this world there's no room for redemption" (t354).
- **No corny goodness (t338):** a sympathetic monster stays a monster — the "good" changeling is still "in every way a murderous hungry user of other people"; only his demeanor changed.
- **An emotional blow, not a moral verdict (t558):** the ending's reveal doesn't forgive anyone — "Derrek is clearly a murderous tyrannical dictator. There's no real forgiveness. It's an emotional blow to the reader for the sake of compelling story."
- **What makes a killing land (t708):** "Everyone in this story has killed. A killing means little. It's the intensity and coldness of the killings. How useless the killing was. How cruel were their acts." Scale, intent, and the audience's feeling decide whether an act reads as unforgivable.
- **Thesis the prose serves (t466):** "it doesn't matter in the grand scale but everything matters to someone."
- [NEW v5-wk] **Madness is subtext only (wk W70):** the base layer is the One dissociating, but no in-world metaphysical cause is ever visible on the page. It is nondiscriminate — "good and bad all are mad" (chat t365).
