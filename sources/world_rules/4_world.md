# Part 4: World

### The world's age and texture

W-001. The medieval era is far from the start of history; it has deep traditions and ancient ruins older than anyone understands.

W-002. The One dissolved long ago; the world was made not long after, for the One's expression, in a place the Unified Ones protect.

W-003. Strife is the world's constant state, between and within races; peace is rare and hard-won.

W-004. Strife has no in-world metaphysical cause.

W-005. The far future is when the rift is at its widest and reality is breaking.

### The race model

W-006. No author drives the world's catastrophes; the world is a churning web of competing factions, cutting across race lines, each seeking domination for its own aims.

W-007. Catastrophe in the world is emergent and cannot be stopped by defeating anyone.

W-008. A god intervening does not break the churn; it is one more being choosing for itself and using others.

W-009. Every race carries the full set of vices: greed, pride, jealousy and wrath.

W-010. No race is assigned a single sin.

W-011. Races differ in the cultural and historical logic that shapes how their vices surface and which is most common.

W-012. Each major race has a characteristic hunger and a characteristic mask, the story it tells about the hunger; the mask is not a label on every individual.

W-013. As theme, elves stand for coherence and humans and orcs for shatter.

W-014. No race has clean hands; the long-lived races also seek dominance on their own terms and timescales.

W-015. The world has tried from the beginning to subjugate or exterminate every race, the elves included; the elves were never singled out.

W-016. No race is a monolith; every race likely has multiple empires or powers.

W-017. Every race has a fracture: giants into factions, dragons into raised and dispossessed houses, elves into heaven and the dark prison, dwarves into integrationists, isolationists and the Returned, humans into true believers and realists.

W-018. Orcs have no society to fracture; the fracture lies inside each individual orc.

W-019. Soul size sets a real ceiling on raw power; the lie is that small-souled beings therefore matter less.

W-020. Humans have defeated giants and dragons, yet the galaxy excludes goblins and gnomes from roles of power.

W-021. Dwarves are small yet enormously powerful in every way, and the galaxy ignores it.

W-022. The human mask is supremacy; the orc mask is victimhood as total explanation; the dwarven mask is neutrality as strategy, with family-first as the professed face.

W-023. The dragon mask is self-justified fitness ("we would rule better"); the giant mask is pride-as-duty, care that needs no consent.

W-024. The elven mask is reunion at all cost as the justification for total control; the changeling mask is consumption-as-unity.

### Dominant and subordinate races

W-025. There are three dominant races: humans, elves and orcs, each constantly at war with one or both of the others.

W-026. Humans arguably hold the most power: they breed far more than elves and have far higher magical attunement than orcs.

W-027. Humans are generalists with no strong temperamental bias, so they learn the widest variety of magic.

W-028. High or imperial elves are the main elven sect; wood elves also exist.

W-029. Orcs are the shortest-lived of the three dominant races, overcrowded, the most internally violent and the weakest in magic, but extremely physically gifted.

W-030. Orcs are culturally fire-leaning by temperament, never racially locked.

W-031. Dwarves are long-lived and forge magical implements for humans in a peaceful, mutually beneficial relationship.

W-032. Giants are few.

W-033. Goblins are slaves, cattle and cannon fodder for the orcs.

W-034. Goblins' core cultural memory is pain; they lost coherence long ago and are viewed and act as animals, domesticated pets of the orcs.

W-035. Goblins are exactly the same as other beings, only weaker.

W-036. Trolls repeat the orc tragedy; a few secretly whisper to one another in their ghettos.

W-037. No goblin city or troll fortress survives; their history was destroyed long ago.

W-038. In the far future goblins are not named separately; a goblin is counted and called a small orc.

W-039. Races below the orcs are given the blanket title "orc", as the orcs did to those below them.

W-040. Minor races are shaped by whose vices they suffer under: gnomes under dwarves, goblins under orcs.

### Humans

W-041. Humans hunger for everything; want, not need, drives them, and they are never satisfied.

W-042. The human hunger is rooted in necessity: short lives, endless birth and perpetual scarcity.

W-043. Pride may be humans' most deadly vice, but it is not their only one.

W-044. War, not curiosity, drove human science; science and magic grew in lockstep with humanity's wars of dominance.

W-045. Humans mask their hunger with supremacy: they claim they take because they are owed, as the rightfully anointed children of the One.

W-046. Humans spread into every domain because they cannot bear one they have not conquered.

W-047. Humans are the religious race, marked by fervent zealotry.

W-048. Humans worship Derrek and the puppeteer.

W-049. Derrek started a cult among humans as he conquered and enslaved them, by force and charisma, telling them they were the anointed children of the One.

W-050. Human zealotry is a culture produced by Derrek's rule, not a trait of blood.

W-051. Human success is borrowed from longer-lived races (elven medicine, giant architecture, dragon law, dwarven refinement), yet humans treat the givers as tools or enemies, never equals.

W-052. Humans move dwarven gold, guard dragon fortresses and advise giants; the whole web uses itself.

W-053. The parasitism runs both ways: other races succeed off humanity, and human expansion forces them to keep up or be assimilated.

W-054. Humans are the churn's greatest victims, enslaving and grinding up one another.

W-055. Humans have no shared aim, so they turn on themselves hardest.

W-056. Humans split into true-believer supremacists and pragmatic realists, who despise each other.

W-057. In the medieval era humans were divided among a handful of major rival empires; the specific polities are not defined.

W-058. Human unity under Derrek is hierarchy, not union.

W-059. Derrek is humanity's apex avatar and greatest creation; his pride is his own.

W-060. Derrek is the emperor of humanity and controls it through concentrated, dominant physical rule.

W-061. Humans barely had an edge; Derrek's loops refined it.

### The human empire in the far future

W-062. The Emperor is faceless; no one knows who he is, and he is known only as the Emperor, ruling from the Tower.

W-063. Derrek won his seat by uniting humans, arming them, seizing the Tower from the heart of the dragon empire, and building his seat there.

W-064. The empire's ultimate goal is to prove humans were never a lesser race; being called lesser by dragons, giants and gods insults them.

W-065. Independence from the elder races is a core objective of the human empire.

W-066. Earth is the human capital, but not the only capital.

W-067. Rich human colonies sit far out, with hidden fleets and palaces; drones do the labor.

W-068. Human expansion pushes law, custom and business, and occasionally peacekeeping missions that are disguised invasions of planets under dragon or giant rule.

W-069. The Legion is a human legion that defies the elven Inquisition; Derrek controls it.

W-070. Zealous Legion members instill fear in the rest of humanity in the name of their god.

W-071. Humanity faces three challenges: the war the dragons started, the Orc King's plague, and the elves, sworn to end the Emperor.

W-072. Humans see the Emperor as a literal embodiment of god.

W-073. Humans are a remembered participant in the dragons' war; the giants have not forgotten man's role.

W-074. The dragons share all three threats and are humanity's shared ally; the race that resists human equality hardest must rely heavily on humans.

W-075. Derrek's goal was never to lead humanity.

### The long-memory enmity cycle

W-076. The elder races carry living memory of human atrocities that humans have forgotten across many generations.

W-077. The elder hates humanity as a recurring organism, not the living individuals.

W-078. Living humans, innocent of the ancient act, read the hatred as unearned prejudice and answer from wounded pride, committing the next atrocity.

W-079. The ancient grievance produces the new grievance.

W-080. Pride keeps humans from receiving the hatred as information and asking what they did.

W-081. The elders' grief and withdrawal also feed the cycle; everyone in it is both victim and cause.

### Orcs

W-082. Orcs have the fastest clock of all peoples; their near horizon makes the future almost meaningless and deep history unimportant to the race.

W-083. Individual orcs remember their own lives fully; rare scholars record selectively, and occult leaders pass traditions orally.

W-084. Orcs keep a short historical ledger by disposition, not by defect.

W-085. Orc greed is desperation at a short clock, not a defect.

W-086. Orcs mirror the human extract-and-dominate structure but have no mask that works.

W-087. Orc conditions, never innate deficiency, produce this; orcs are short-horizoned, not stupid.

W-088. Orcs show openly what other races do in disguise, and are hated for it.

W-089. Orcs were subjugated for defiance, not weakness: refusing to accept being less, they were forced to be less.

W-090. The enslaving races justify themselves by saying orcs fail every metric; that verdict is false.

W-091. Orcs were born into bondage under the giants' forced protection.

W-092. Orcs fought in the dragons' coalition for a promised freedom as equals and were re-chained by the dragons immediately.

W-093. When humans rose, orcs refused the offered lesser place and were subjugated a third time.

W-094. Orcs have never freed themselves; each master was worse and a different kind of chain: forced protection, instrumental control, then parasitic consumption.

W-095. That orcs earned their subjugation is the world's lie.

W-096. Orc enslavement of goblins, trolls and others is trauma-replication; orcs know no life other than slavery.

W-097. Orcs carry an inherited sense of having been wronged without its history; each remembers only the wrongs of their own lifetime.

W-098. Orc scholars learn history through a permanent-victim lens, casting orc slavery as the hidden engine of every other race's success.

W-099. The scholars' view is partly true, since orc labor underpins the machine, but wrong: orcs lost the same game every race plays.

W-100. Orcs write history only in response to catastrophe, so the orc historical record contains only suffering.

W-101. The victim view licenses orcs to repeat the cycle of enslavement when they are briefly free.

W-102. Orcs call their inherited ache "hate"; their culture is hate, toward each other, toward others, and toward the past.

W-103. Other races read orcs as made of spite, which justifies tighter control.

W-104. Orcs experience their own greed as restitution.

W-105. Under others' tightening grip, orc hate warps into complete contempt.

W-106. Orcs lean hardest on pact-work: ritual magic, blood rites and communing with demons; this is a cultural lean, not a lock, and some orcs work Remnant like anyone.

W-107. Because pacts let a weak being punch above its Remnant, the magically poorest race leans on them.

W-108. Other races read orc rites as proof of savagery.

W-109. The Orc King's demon pact is the apex of his people's magical tradition.

W-110. Orc bodies are sometimes processed into food, luggage or spacecraft.

W-111. Orcs see the Orc King with awe and pride but also as worsening how others view orcs.

W-112. Orcs never had a king and never organized; "Orc King" is a symbolic title.

W-113. The Orc King is a gifted orc born in the far future, taught science and magic, who carries a pact over entropy and spreads a plague across the galaxy.

W-114. As the Orc King destroys planets he frees orcs, some as zombies and others inspired by him.

W-115. In the far future orcs are the galaxy's working cattle: slave labor on work-planets, filling prisons, executed for trifles.

W-116. When giant corpses ran low, unethical giants with dwarven suppliers bought lifeforms en masse, chiefly orcs, to build cathedral ships for the dragons.

W-117. Mixed-race crews pilot orc ships, and orcs are treated so low they are literally walked on.

W-118. A faction of the Returned likely sells giants trillions of orcs for ships and worked with Derrek to create the orc chips.

W-119. Humans feel disgust or hatred toward orcs, at most pity, or nothing at all; some individuals have hope for them.

W-120. Orcs are genuinely capable of complex civilization on their own.

W-121. Derrek shaped orc desperation toward their far-future fate as cattle.

### Elves in the world order

W-122. Elves are the one race the human machine never dissolved; they keep deliberate autonomy, with working relations but no dependence or assimilation.

W-123. Humanity absorbs others one individual at a time from within; the bond makes elves structurally un-absorbable.

W-124. Elves are the closest thing left to the un-shattered One; humans are its shatter at its most chaotic.

W-125. Elves have stood against human domination since the dawn.

W-126. Jon is the elves' hidden god; Derrek is humanity's open one.

W-127. Jon asks the elves for devotion and is breeding them as a warrior people to fight an endless war against Derrek.

W-128. Far-future elves are sworn to end the human Emperor.

W-129. Elves were never under giant control; they stood aside when others turned on the giants, and the giants do not hate them.

W-130. The elven bloodline Jon led across the ages is world lore and Jon's side goal.

W-131. Where the pocket dimension and ours are weakest lies a place people go and are never seen again; it is the elves' secret door to their world.

W-132. Dark elves lack the tools to shatter the secret door, but people sometimes go missing there; it seems to exist between places, like the Tower.

W-133. Elves and dwarves alone never fell as empires.

### History of the ages

W-134. History has three apex powers in sequence, giants, then dragons, then humans, each toppled by what rose beneath it.

W-135. The giants' age ended in war, not descent.

W-136. Giants ruled out of a felt duty to maintain order, compassion totalized into rule that allowed no dissent and no consent.

W-137. Giants wrote all laws; any breach was punished swiftly and ruthlessly.

W-138. Giants concluded from long lives of watching other races fight that peace required severe consequences for breaking rules.

W-139. Under giant law crime effectively did not exist, and the giants' age was beautiful and cultural.

W-140. The giants were cruel tyrants so warped they believed all their dark actions justified.

W-141. Giants held all races except the elves in forced protection: no consent, no exit, no self-rule.

W-142. Giant rule was the gentlest bondage the world would know; love led the giants' hand, and they ruled in ways best for the ruled.

W-143. The moral line between giants and dragons is murky; neither side is simply right.

W-144. Dwarves helped the giants in their age; this was the dwarven mini-age, and dwarven craft is based on giant craft.

W-145. Giants built their megastructures as halls for themselves when they ruled the world; the halls were never gifts.

W-146. Giants would never have sold their castles, which were built from their dead; dragons live in them as occupiers.

W-147. Dragons were the giants' seconds, envious of their stature and halls, and believed they would rule better.

W-148. Dragons coveted the giants' halls long before betraying them.

W-149. Dragons manufactured and controlled the coalition narrative; the promise of freedom as equals after victory was a lie.

W-150. The coalition fielded columns of human war-users and orc legions; humans were deceived but willing, orcs deceived and desperate.

W-151. Dragons sometimes had to fight themselves; the giant king was a target requiring dragon force.

W-152. Dwarves were uninvolved in the coup except as arms suppliers, to both sides.

W-153. Dwarves built armor that men used to raid the giants' gold reserves.

### Betrayal day

W-154. The dragon who struck on betrayal day was the giant emperor's closest advisor, never the ruler of dragons.

W-155. The striker is called "the king" only in memory, by those he set on the thrones.

W-156. The striker mastered transmutation and the strong-force blast before anyone understood their principles.

W-157. The strike was a deliberate self-sacrifice to destroy the heart of giant power, driven by love of his people and resentment of a crown he would never wear.

W-158. The striker powered his final blast with a special rock he had synthesized and carried in a cavity in his stomach.

W-159. In his last act the giant emperor converted himself into a giant stone tower; the dragon died in his own blast.

W-160. The Tower is the converged giant emperor, the crown gem of the giants, not a built structure.

W-161. The Tower is the largest giant city, compressed and still faintly feeling.

W-162. The blast destroyed the entire capital, and the region around the Tower was dangerous and radioactive for a long time.

W-163. Animals avoid the Tower, and it carries strange legend.

W-164. The capital's dragons were not told of the strike and stayed, as acceptable losses.

W-165. The same day, dragons chosen by the striker killed giant rulers in their keeps and the ruling dragons of the time, taking the new thrones.

W-166. Giants who never trusted dragons held harder keeps, and genuine war was fought there.

W-167. The coalition killed the giants' heroes and leaders, a defeat so total the giants could not survive as the civilization they had been.

### The Darkest Age

W-168. "The Darkest Age" is other races' name for the age of dragon rule; dragons call it the Age of Dragons.

W-169. The name is unfair to dragons, who freed everyone from giant tyranny and began the age whose science built the modern world.

W-170. The dragons' age reads darkest by comparison with the crime-free age of giant law.

W-171. Dragons wanted control and aimed every other race at dragon purposes.

W-172. Life under dragon rule was miserable, and that shared misery is the root of why the races hate dragons.

W-173. Humans were an indentured race under dragons, not chattel; dragons led empires of humans, and humans formed the backbone of dragon order.

W-174. Humans have always been subservient to dragons, though willing to stand up.

W-175. Dwarves kept their useful status under dragon rule but were subject to dragon will; they were never enslaved.

W-176. The dragon empire was capable and carried the giants' work forward.

W-177. The Age of Dragons was long.

W-178. Before Derrek, the dragon empire held the Tower.

W-179. The elves began separating during the fall-war and the Darkest Age; the exodus from Earth was their second withdrawal.

W-180. The giants' grief is shared across every complicit race, weighted by role: dragons acted knowingly, humans and orcs were manipulated hands.

W-181. Giants carry many later grievances against human pride.

W-182. The war's great battles left visible scars: ruined halls and the killing-grounds of heroes.

W-183. After the capital's blast zone recovered, dragons, resistant as masters of weak-force work, could live there first.

### The erased age

W-184. The name of the giants' age was erased, while the dragons and humans have ages named for them.

W-185. The world does not remember the giants' age because dragons erased the record during the Darkest Age.

W-186. Deep time also erased the giants' age, which decayed through the short-lived races' generations.

W-187. Giants and dragons call the giants' age golden; elves call it their own golden age.

W-188. Both giants and dragons rule from the shadows, in different eras.

### The rise of humanity

W-189. The capital never moved: the giants' capital, the dragons' seat and the human capital are one city; only the ruler changed.

W-190. Each apex power occupies rather than founds.

W-191. Humans won through the dragon empire's internal fractures, uniting a coalition by charisma, with dragons who defected to be spared.

W-192. The human victory was partial: dragons usurped back parts of their empire, and some descendants of the advisor still rule kingdoms.

W-193. Dragons resisted for a long time, killing kings and leveling cities; their retreat was gradual and individual.

W-194. There was an age between, when dragons and humans ruled at once in fluctuating fashion.

W-195. Each dragon was its own independent king, leading a kingdom built off others' backs, because there were not enough dragons to build their own.

W-196. Humans became the more relevant power, and the third age is named for them.

W-197. In truth the humans' age is the actual darkest age, the age that holds Derrek.

W-198. Derrek built only additions to the Tower, such as elevators and laser arrays, not its walls.

### Pre-exodus peak and decline

W-199. Pre-exodus Earth was a high-tech peak: all races had spaceflight, drones, AI and gene-splicing, along with druids, skyscrapers and flying craft.

W-200. After the exodus came centuries of wounded decline, not a rebuild from zero; fragments such as the Tower remained.

### The elven exodus

W-201. Elves fled Earth on one seedship, driven off by humans and the other races.

W-202. Other races read the exodus as a power grab and warred to reach the stars first.

W-203. The leaving was a top-down royal directive, not a collective choice.

W-204. Jon led Mika and the elves away on the seedship.

W-205. Jon worked the elves toward fleeing over centuries, just before Derrek's blow.

W-206. The elves tried to return and found the world dark and gloomy.

W-207. The elven pocket realm and empire are built at the new home.

### The giants' departure and the forge world

W-208. The first giants left Earth on one great ship they converged: the first cathedral ship.

W-209. The first giants arrived at a world they had chosen ahead of time.

W-210. The first giant crossing was slow; the giants could afford the wait, so it needed no faster-than-light drive.

W-211. For giants and elves, a colony voyage is crewed by largely the same people the whole way, with some births and some deaths.

W-212. Their durability and size made giants excellent first space explorers alongside the elves.

W-213. "Forge world" is the galaxy's name for a planet that is, to the giants, simply home.

W-214. After centuries of isolation on Earth, the giants retreated to the forge world and rebuilt there: halls, shops and peace at last.

W-215. The forge world was likely one of the first planets colonized.

W-216. By the far future, giant families have lived on the forge world for about 5,000 years.

W-217. The giants keep their dead on the forge world for Convergence.

W-218. The forge world is home to giant families, orc slaves, dwarven merchants and human diplomats.

W-219. Ship-selling is a large part of the forge world's economy, which is why the galaxy calls it the forge world.

W-220. The forge world sells ships to the galaxy at large, not only to dragons.

W-221. The forge world's secret is where its ships come from and how they are made.

W-222. Through Convergence, the giants hold a monopoly on flesh cores and other flesh materials.

W-223. In their second coup the dragons eradicated not a city but the giants' home world itself.

W-224. No tower stands to remember the forge world's destruction.

W-225. The forge-world strike was also an attempt to weaken the source of the material humans used to forge their dominance.

W-226. The strike crippled the giants, one of Derrek's primary rivals, and left him no longer needing them.

W-227. Weakening the human flesh-core supply costs Derrek nothing he cares about.

W-228. Derrek struck an agreement with a coalition of opposition giant factions, the any-tactic and cashed-out camps, who act as his secret factory converging his personal flesh-core supply.

W-229. With the competition destroyed, Derrek holds the flesh-core monopoly.

### The modern era

W-230. Five hundred years after the elves left, humans are dominant at full scale, in a steampunk, cyberpunk, magically infused technostate.

W-231. Returning elves found the world more depraved and desperate than the one they left.

W-232. In the modern era dragons still rule as they always have, less effectively than ever.

W-233. Human-sized Remnant with dwarven magic-infused cybernetics lets humans rival the elders for the first time.

W-234. Modern human science turned to ground-based weapons and war vehicles, for profit or war; space was seen as a luxury of the elves who left.

W-235. Derrek largely erased the elves' legacy from cultural memory; fifteen generations later only scholars remember the ship.

W-236. Human descendants see elven design as their own, told their ancestors built their cities.

W-237. In the modern era some people are rebuilt with an ASI in place of the brain rather than repaired, to make automatons.

### The Inquisition

W-238. The Inquisition was founded long ago by one of the first elven kings as his right hand, a countermeasure force then called the Council, to protect elves from existential threats.

W-239. The Council evolved into the Inquisition; it is one institution, renamed.

W-240. The Inquisition was founded against changelings and dragons and later turned against resets when elven seers sensed time fractures.

W-241. The Inquisition's main task has always been stopping the one or ones responsible for the resets.

W-242. That task includes Derrek and is aimed at the Unified One who let the world made for the One be broken open.

W-243. Since Jon performs every reset, the Inquisition unknowingly hunts its own god.

W-244. Hunting dark elves is the Inquisition's side work.

W-245. The Inquisition secretly sells captured dark elves through dwarven traffickers to rich humans, using "soul thieves" as cover.

W-246. The Inquisition's trafficking is Mika's rot.

### Dwarves

W-247. The dwarven root is independence at all costs; greed is the strategy they chose to protect it.

W-248. Dwarves are indiscriminate merchants: selling to an ally's enemy is betrayal, selling to both sides as a neutral party is business.

W-249. Neutrality is not a dwarven trait or value but a learned strategy forged by watching thrones fall.

W-250. Dwarves earned and bought their neutrality by being too costly to conquer.

W-251. Dwarves are a mountain people trading gold, gems and precision craft for armed neutrality.

W-252. Dwarven fortresses are magically infused and built toward perfection; they are too costly to assault and too useful to destroy.

W-253. Dwarves made their craft specific to be indispensable, not unconquerable, and still somewhat rely on other races.

W-254. Dwarves present as a loud, aggressive, extremely loyal and caring family, ruthless to outsiders.

W-255. Dwarven family trees are rigid and hierarchical.

W-256. Family is not the dwarves' highest law: they sold family to an in-betweener to avoid enslavement themselves.

W-257. Dwarves are governed by a sophisticated series of laws, not one great law.

W-258. Dwarven government is a bureaucratic autocracy: several emperors, each running his own empire, with councils and boards tied closely to business.

W-259. Abroad, dwarves run a commerce empire on the trading-company model: monopolies, private armies and merciless commerce.

W-260. Dwarves are neutral in wars and ruthless in trade.

W-261. Dwarves keep a district in every race's towns, the elven city included.

W-262. Dwarves use those less powerful than themselves, other dwarves included.

W-263. Gnomes are the dwarves' natural servants; most gnomes likely live among dwarves.

W-264. In the far future dwarves build the drives others travel with, the focusing chambers dragon ships fight with, and the chambers that feed the giants' forges.

W-265. Dwarves profit from all three far-future crises, neutral in every war and selling to every side.

W-266. Dwarven suppliers carry the giants' mass-buying of lifeforms.

W-267. Dwarves are the second unsubjugated race, free through entanglement so deep they cannot be removed, the opposite of the elves' separation.

W-268. Dwarves live throughout human cities while holding their own kingdoms, peasants, kings, land, resources and wars.

W-269. Humanity never subjected the dwarves; computing knowledge had to be coerced from them, not conquered.

W-270. The dwarven people were never subjugated; one group of dwarves was sold by their own to an in-betweener.

W-271. The dwarven empire has unbroken continuity; its declines are a managed plateau, never rise and fall.

W-272. Dwarves never ruled the world and so never entered the apex-fall pattern.

W-273. Dwarves are patient but temperamental; their emotions run very hot when they strike.

W-274. Dwarven greed is patient, institutionalized and successful; orc greed is desperate and fails.

W-275. Dwarves split into integrationists, who see other races as profitable allies, and isolationists, who see outside aid as weakening.

W-276. Isolationists include both genuine racists and the legitimately disillusioned.

W-277. Dwarves are the most consistent race, staunch and rule-based, running the same strategy since the deepest era.

W-278. The giants' grudge does not fall on dwarves as it does on the complicit races.

W-279. Dwarves thrived most of any race; individual dwarves suffered, but the people's line never broke.

### The Returned

W-280. The Returned are dwarves sold by their own kin to an in-betweener's maze-prison, who escape one at a time.

W-281. A Returned who wins the maze is simply released.

W-282. The Returned are taught they were sold; they resent their low status and servitude but are grateful for the powers the maze gave them.

W-283. Some Returned beg for work in dwarven holds, some buy property with wealth earned inside, and some become bandits.

W-284. Ordinary dwarves see the Returned as distant relatives; no one hunts or discriminates against them.

W-285. The maze made the Returned a people driven never to be bound again; they value coin and absolute freedom and are absolutely unscrupulous.

W-286. The Returned drive the worst of the far-future crises.

### Gnomes

W-287. Gnomes are a latecomer race; in the medieval era they are kind of happy and seen as the most lowly because they avoid politics, yet they are power users.

W-288. Happy gnome villages are a medieval image: remote forest towns, likely gone later.

W-289. In the far future gnomes are mostly an indentured class of the dwarves, sold to whoever wants one.

W-290. Gnomes are the lesser counterpart to dwarves, as orcs are to humans.

W-291. Gnomes are funny and cheerful; a gnome town is bright, smells good, and is loud with happy people.

W-292. Gnomes care little for wealth or authority and live in the moment.

W-293. Others view gnomes as weak and naive.

W-294. Gnomes split between those who want to fight for their rights and independence and those content with how things are.

W-295. Gnomes have no golden age; they are an average race.

W-296. Gnome culture values education and free thinking, giving good Remnant control; gnome Remnant is similar in size to a dwarf's.

W-297. Gnomes are second only to changelings at assassination.

W-298. Gnomes believe their passivity keeps them safe, but they are not sovereign; they hold their homes only because no one sees them as a threat.

### The Galactic Council

W-299. The Galactic Council is an intergalactic ruling body seated on Earth, in human buildings.

W-300. The Galactic Council is distinct from the medieval elven Council.

W-301. Elves still come to Earth for the Council.

W-302. Seated Council powers include humans, elves and dragons; others are undetermined.

W-303. Changelings are not recognized on the Council: they have no homeland and are not a people.

W-304. The stalemate holds officially; humans win functionally, by numbers and by the laws the elder races built.

W-305. Blocs form within the Council.

W-306. Each power is checked by its nature: humans numerous but fractured; dragons terrifying but outnumbered and split; elves untouchable except by full war; changelings able to manipulate but not openly rule.

W-307. Giants are indifferent as a people while their factions build and sell, until the forge-world strike turns them to war.

W-308. No decisive move is possible; maneuvering is covert.

W-309. The Council shares Earth with the Tower.

### Earth in the far future

W-310. Far-future Earth is a planet-wide city across every continent, with controlled weather and food imported on convoys; it has no farmland.

W-311. Earth is no longer vital for survival; it is the symbolic, political and cosmic center, with the Tower at its heart.

W-312. Earth is controlled by factions of humans, changelings and dragons: humans as the sprawl, a few clever changelings hidden at court, and the dragon houses that sided with humanity.

W-313. On Earth orcs and lower races are cattle, elves are gone, and giants are indifferent until the forge-world strike.

W-314. The non-compliant races long ago abandoned Earth, which is why it is uniformly under this order.

### Technology and ships

W-315. Dwarves and humans discovered flight and engineered the first ships in the second era; dwarves architected the designs.

W-316. Flight became a factional arms race; elves made deals with dwarves to build their own programs.

W-317. Giants developed shipbuilding into mastery.

W-318. Cathedral ships are the giants' craft, an extension of their ancient building rite.

W-319. Dwarves build ships alone and giants build cathedral ships alone; together they make the supreme works, such as giant-built dragon flagships with dwarven focusing chambers.

W-320. Focusing-chamber plasma cannons and giant-built hulls are general technology, not dragon-exclusive; dwarves engineer the cannons and giants build hulls for all.

W-321. The focusing chamber, dragon power through dwarven engineering, channels a dragon's full intensity to one point as a concentrated plasma beam; it is the dragons' ultimate weapon.

W-322. A dragon ship can fire only a limited number of full-power beams before exhaustion; a large coordinated fleet can overwhelm it once they are spent.

W-323. Ship power differs by race: dragon ships run on rotating dragon crews in focusing chambers (finite bursts); elven ships pool the crew's Remnant through the bond (sustained); human ships use coordinated gun-crews.

W-324. Only the elven bond can pool Remnant.

W-325. Elven ships are smaller and more nimble and are dwarven-built; elves deal with dwarves, not giants.

W-326. Elven fleet doctrine is to spread out, bait a dragon ship into spending its beams, then pour sustained fire into it.

W-327. Ships at every scale run in two modes: purely mechanical, with no Remnant and crewable by anyone, or Remnant-augmented, needing a gifted user or crew.

W-328. Both modes coexist because Remnant is unevenly distributed.

W-329. Dragons rarely fly small craft.

W-330. Humans built machines that manipulate the same forces as magic by mechanical means, reaching personal scale.

W-331. Dragon focusing-chamber crews are highly respected, high-ranking military.

### Travel between stars

W-332. Each race has its own method of faster-than-light travel.

W-333. Humans cross the stars by traversing hell.

W-334. Dwarves cross the stars with a dimensional-squeeze drive.

W-335. Elves cross space by shrinking one of their dimensions, crossing quickly, then expanding it; their ships enter heaven to travel, and the bond is cut in transit.

W-336. A rival fleet that slips into heaven can ambush elven ships, which cannot call outside.

W-337. Dragon ships make burst-jumps powered by rotating crews of dragons in focusing chambers; the ship's master is the final lever for the overdrive burst.

W-338. Dragons hold enough political power that saying they only ride giant ships is unfair.

### Religion

W-339. A religion is a portrait of its god and a recruitment network of pact champions.

W-340. The pact tether feeds the god.

W-341. There are faith priests, pact priests and mystics who intuit the One.

W-342. Every race needs a religion; faiths worship the same beings in different ways: soul-magic gods, will-made gods, Unified Ones, in-betweeners, or false gods.

### Clones, flesh harvest and the organ trade

W-343. Clones have souls and magic; everyone pretends otherwise.

W-344. Because flesh holds Remnant best, the universe enslaves a race, the orcs, and the clones it pretends are not real, to harvest flesh en masse.

W-345. The organ trade is a legal government market with a black market beneath it.

W-346. Captured dark elves are part of the organ trade.

W-347. Elves are hunted for organs by black-site units or mercenary bands.

W-348. The organ trade is a dwarven and human enterprise; orcs are excluded because no one cares for them and their organs would be wrong.

W-349. Dragons benefit from the organ trade through bodies to study.

W-350. Being hunted for organs is one more reason elves keep to themselves; one elf outmatches a handful of humans.
