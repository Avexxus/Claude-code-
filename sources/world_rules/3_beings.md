# Part 3: Beings

### Creation generations

B-001. Creation generations sort beings by fragment size, not by time of creation.

B-002. Transcendents are the largest fragments: the gods; they need no warmth or food and can exist anywhere.

B-003. Some Transcendents create pocket dimensions.

B-004. The Elder Races are large fragments; dragons, giants and elves are the three true elder races.

B-005. Latecomers are small fragments: humans, orcs, dwarves, goblins and gnomes; they are short-lived and multiply fast.

B-006. Scraps are the smallest fragments: beasts, monsters and animals; they have low Remnant but are genuine fragments.

B-007. Elementals are classed in the beast region, not among the giants.

B-008. Tiers of power are distinct from creation generations.

### Gods

B-009. Tier 1 holds gods and the angel/demon region: coherent fragments inside the universe, each with its own individual will.

B-010. Individual will exists only where there is coherence.

B-011. There is no qualitative divide between gods and other beings, only scale.

B-012. A god is what any user becomes when incredibly powerful, able to act at world-altering scale; godhood is a level of power, not a kind of being.

B-013. "God" means a larger chunk of Remnant in flesh, not a separate kind of being.

B-014. Gods are the same kind of being as lesser beings, with the same fragments of the One, the same physics and the same magic, differing only in Remnant and mastery, both graded.

B-015. Gods use Remnant, the same magic as every being, carried to its furthest individual expression; there is no separate divine magic.

B-016. Gods are the ultimate expression of every being developing its own unique powers.

B-017. Power is a single continuous scale of Remnant, skill and time to learn.

B-018. "Demigod" and "god" are descriptive points on the scale, not gated tiers.

B-019. Changeling, angel or demon, and god are blurred regions on one continuum, with no graduation from one to the next.

B-020. Where the continuum becomes god is murkier still.

B-021. Nothing is locked to god-tier; a lesser being is barred only from what its Remnant and lifespan cannot yet reach.

B-022. Given enough Remnant and time, a lesser being becomes a god.

B-023. A weak god vastly exceeds lesser beings but ranks low among gods.

B-024. Godhood is a matter of Remnant scale times skill; there is no fusion ladder.

B-025. There are two rough roads to godhood: being born a large fragment, or being a mortal who learned deep mastery over time.

B-026. There are two roads to godhood, physical and psychic.

B-027. A god can arise by the matter road (for example, gravity magic hardening a body beyond stone, or electromagnetic control twisting matter) or by the soul road (soul magic, as angels and demons do).

B-028. Gods are mortal: they age and die, though great gods live so long they seem eternal; "mortal" is not the opposite of "god."

B-029. All gods are born of flesh, on one world.

B-030. Humans reach godhood only by time-fusion or by soul theft.

B-031. Dragons, giants and elves can grow into singular gods.

B-032. Singular gods revile changelings, but power earns respect regardless.

B-033. A changeling who ascends is not a god in the singular sense; god types and changelings are not the same thing.

B-034. No formula ties being a god or a demon to the method of ascent; behavior is individual.

### Angels and demons

B-035. Angels and demons are one kind of being; the two names are what mortals call the same thing.

B-036. Angels and demons are soul mages of the same kind as changelings: the oldest and vastest practitioners of soul theft, most able to use and manipulate souls.

B-037. Angels and demons are made, not born.

B-038. Good and evil do not mark the line between angel and demon; being kind does not make a being an angel, and being cruel does not make it a demon.

B-039. Demons are coherent beings with their own individual will, proxies of nothing and agents of no cosmic power.

### Elder races in general

B-040. Each race is anchored to a cardinal element symbolically and culturally only; there is no mechanical racial lean.

B-041. Giants are symbolically earth, dragons air, elves water, and changelings fire.

B-042. Changelings are elder-tier in power but are made, not born, and can come from any race.

B-043. The rivalries among the elder powers form one interlocking web.

### Giants

B-044. Giants are a born race like any other, fully separate from elementals.

B-045. Giants are mortal titans, not cosmological gods, but lesser races cannot tell them from gods.

B-046. The oldest religions may worship giants.

B-047. Giants built much of the world's scenery: they raised hills, carved valleys and laid the most ancient megastructures.

B-048. Giants have roughly dragon-comparable proportions with a shape more like a human's.

B-049. A true giant is smart, cunning and focused.

B-050. Giants have the largest bodies and the largest Remnant, making them the most potent users, not the most skilled.

B-051. The giants' signature mastery is massive-scale true transmutation: forcing matter to become what they will and holding the form by will.

B-052. Dwarves mine, forge and enchant existing matter; giants transmute and will matter into being and shape.

B-053. Giants are powerful, active users, never passive landmarks.

B-054. Giant architecture is grown and willed: seamless, with no chisel marks.

B-055. Giants understand complex chain bonds and make compounds that let their great structures stand forever.

B-056. In grown architecture the builder's Remnant is the source used to grow the walls; the builder does not become the walls.

B-057. A giant's body is not held up by active EM magic.

B-058. A single giant can personally span all three eras.

B-059. Giants who survived betrayal day remember it personally.

B-060. Giants are glacial and patient, raised in stoicism and humility.

B-061. Giant stoicism is scar tissue from their defeat, not innate virtue.

B-062. Giants have slow births and huge upkeep and act on long timescales.

B-063. After the dragons' genocide the giants became cold; beneath an outward withdrawal they are deeply upset and wage a long war of attrition.

B-064. The giants' population loss from the war is somewhat resolved; they rebuilt more than people realize and are quietly growing.

B-065. In the modern era giants receded further into isolation as human settlements filled kingdoms once held sacred by elves or rebuilt orc-decimated homelands.

B-066. After the betrayal, some giants turned to anger and wished to eradicate the peoples who rejected them; others chose deep isolation.

B-067. The dragons systematically killed the angry giants, seeing a giant return as the only challenge to their power.

B-068. To giants the long purge felt sudden and immediate.

B-069. A small secret faction of giants survived by faked compliance and built deep, cold hatred.

B-070. That faction builds cathedral ships for the dragons to watch dragons destroy one another; the dragons believe their subservient class is obeying.

B-071. Giants hate dragons most for the dragons' nuke, the wound they rank above all others; the other races' roles rank below it.

B-072. Giants in the far future are the supreme builders and master shipbuilders.

B-073. Some giants from the age of the first ships are still alive in the far future as advisors.

B-074. Giants are secretly rich beyond belief; a cathedral ship sells for a small planet.

B-075. Giants are quietly more numerous than anyone thinks.

B-076. Giants are master craftsmen across many fields.

B-077. When the giants declined to only wall-forging, they forgot how to make magic weapons.

B-078. What the giants lost was the true art at scale; over a long time they relearned enough to turn walls into ships, and from ships to all variety of work.

B-079. Not all giant work is Convergence; giants also make many ordinary metal items.

### Convergence among the giants

B-080. Convergence began as a moral tradition and was always consensual on death, though whether each death was truly consensual is ambiguous.

B-081. Convergence predates the dragon wars.

B-082. Planning commissions stand ready so that on a king's or powerful giant's funeral a planned structure is built.

B-083. Dragons live in halls physically inside the remains of the giant families who built them.

B-084. By the time the dragons took the throne, the god-forging rite was lost and only the basic wall-forging use remained.

B-085. Giants work with harvested Remnant from any being, not as fuel but by stealing that soul's production.

B-086. Other races bring giants souls because giants hold a skill monopoly on this craft.

B-087. After the long war slaughtered their kings and elders, giants warped Convergence into a tactic; the few giants able to produce warships will never stop.

B-088. Giants monetized building with stolen Remnant, using and discarding powerful beings for profit.

B-089. What giants can build is limited by how many dead powerful members of their species they can acquire.

B-090. A cathedral flagship may require a great dragon or giant; a sufficiently powerful human could become a smaller ship.

### Dragons

B-091. Dragons are the strongest users seen outside Jon, Derrek, threshold beings and great gods.

B-092. Dragons form a feudal empire with organizations, leadership, children and wives.

B-093. Dragons are fewer than other races, a small, family-tied group; only family, or dragons who appease and join, are admitted.

B-094. Dragons are the second-longest-lived mortal race, so dragons alive today remember betrayal day.

B-095. The dragon empire is split between the raised houses set on thrones by the advisor's strike and the dispossessed houses whose descendants now serve them.

B-096. The dragon empire has never been at peace since betrayal day.

B-097. In the far future, dragons buy cathedral ships to murder each other.

B-098. Dragons are second only to giants in capability and built most of what modern dragons know.

B-099. Dragons' occupation of giant halls is occupation, not incapacity.

B-100. Dragons stay four-legged; their hands are the Remnant grip (the appendage), and servants do the labor.

B-101. Dragons were humanity's first teachers.

B-102. Dragons are the premier scholars and historians, and the ones who erased history.

B-103. Dragons know they succeed off the backs of humanity.

B-104. Dragons are supreme in bursts: immense Remnant costs immense effort to sustain, so a frugal, evasive, patient opponent who drags the fight out exhausts them.

B-105. No single being overpowers a great ancient dragon; one can only survive, flee or outwit it.

B-106. A gifted individual may best a young or weak dragon, bad matchups blunt any dragon, and a coordinated skilled group can fell even an apex dragon at great cost.

B-107. One human cannot defeat a dragon or a giant one-on-one; humans in numbers can.

B-108. Elves can stand toe to toe with dragons and changelings one-on-one.

B-109. Magically infused, cybernetically enhanced humans can defeat a dragon.

B-110. Dragons are the world's foremost transmuters and alchemists.

B-111. A dragon wields a variety of breaths (fire, lightning, corrosion, rot, freeze, transmutation, poison and others), and a single dragon may wield several.

B-112. A breath is a wielded ability, not a species trait; a dragon who wields corrosion can learn fire or lightning instead.

B-113. Breaths are not categorized by which physical force achieves them.

B-114. Transmutation breath transmutes elements without limit by element weight.

B-115. Dragons are thinking, witty, ancient and adaptive; in a fight they sense materials and adapt.

B-116. Lesser breaths can be prepared against; an elder's transmutation breath defeats preparation by changing what the defense is.

B-117. A dragon's prepared stone is a once-prepared trump card; if it fails, there is no second unless more was prepared.

B-118. Dragons adapted to human rule by moving their wealth into human commerce and law, extracting it legally through loopholes.

B-119. The oldest dragons stay in their castles; the young and adept move to the centers of human power.

B-120. In the far future dragon wealth runs through galaxy-spanning conglomerates able to build fleets.

B-121. In the far future dragon houses carry their civil war into space and fly home-brew ships as well as giant-built ones, since giant-made cathedral ships are rare.

B-122. Dragons still live in ancient giant-built palaces, remote and too large for humans.

### Elves

B-123. Elves are ancient but mortal: they age very slowly, can be killed, and die.

B-124. Elves are long-lived but live distinctly less than giants and less than dragons.

B-125. Elves plan over millennia.

B-126. Elves are born like all races; they do not awaken instead of reproducing.

B-127. Elves are the one race that feels the holes left by resets, grieving people who never existed in this timeline.

B-128. Elves innately sense one another through an unfakeable belonging-bond.

B-129. Elves lean collective over individual and keep rigid codes of ethics and discipline.

B-130. The bond rejects a foreign self like tissue rejects a graft, making elves un-assimilable.

B-131. The bond lets elves cooperate with zero friction, which is why they advance fastest; it transfers no knowledge and offers no shortcut to mastery.

B-132. Elves may live at a distance but always remain part of the collective; elven realms can be distinct, but nobody truly leaves.

B-133. Elves handle dissent by rigid enforcement and pressure, not exile.

B-134. Elven society is authoritarian: elves live in complete subservience to the king.

B-135. Elves feel one another's disposition, so dissent or criminal intent marks an elf as a problem.

B-136. Elven order rests on extreme discrimination against all other races and strict penalties for every crime among elves.

B-137. Elves use reunion at all cost as the justification for total control.

B-138. Elven authoritarianism expresses the elves' own compulsion toward unity and order; it is not a reaction to changelings.

B-139. Elves dislike chaos and destroy dissent so they cannot fracture from within.

B-140. Elves were never the weak race; they have always been able to resist every empire's attempts at control.

B-141. Elves and dragons are mirrored apex peers with opposite temperaments: collective and individual, sustained and explosive, measured and immediate, discipline and impunity.

B-142. Elves and dragons each see the other's nature as a flaw; neither is objectively right.

B-143. Dragons cannot subvert elves, because elves grant no deference.

B-144. Changelings cannot infiltrate elves from outside; the bond reads a changeling as a crowd wearing one face.

B-145. Elves are a Council-forming people.

B-146. Mika is the elven Prince; Lirael is Mika's sister.

B-147. In the far future elves hold a vast networked empire in other regions of space; its cohesion rests on the bond and the pocket dimensions the elves control.

B-148. The fae is a separate, distinct and undeveloped otherworld.

B-149. Seen from outside in the far future, elves read as racist toward dark elves (placeholder name) and inquisitors as ruthless and cold.

### Heaven and the dark prison

B-150. The elven bond works inside each pocket dimension but cannot reach across between realities; an elf inside one is cut off from the collective, including during warp.

B-151. The one crime that sends an elf to the dark prison is stealing a fellow elf's soul or body and perverting the bond; it is not dissent.

B-152. Hell was originally for soul thieves and drifted to holding dissidents and vagrants.

B-153. Ordinary crimes go to ordinary prisons; elves live long enough to try to reform a murderer.

B-154. Exiles feel the bond among themselves inside the prison; the seal separates realities rather than suppressing the bond, and neither side feels across.

B-155. Entering a pocket dimension cuts an elf off from the trillions of elves in the galaxy, so elves tend not to go to heaven, and heaven stays beautiful and empty.

B-156. Dark elves cannot choose to leave, so hell is full, and its noise is angry.

B-157. Entire societies exist in hell; some people live their whole lives there in relative peace.

B-158. Those who spend their effort seeking to leave hell are the most dangerous when they escape.

B-159. With no strangers in hell, soul thieves must cultivate their own families as their new souls.

B-160. Powerful changelings in hell coordinate to keep a healthy population, as a farm.

B-161. Hell is a god-forge full of elf-haters.

B-162. Dark elves are a population: many have escaped over the ages, and many are born outside hell.

B-163. Dark elves are war-hardened, constantly at war and always seeking more territory; escapees return war-trained and hate-filled.

B-164. Escaped dark elves are felt through the bond as wrong and warped and are despised more than when they were sent.

B-165. The elven empire acts as the police force rounding up escaped dark elves; across the galaxy dark elves live on high alert as its targets.

B-166. The elven hunt of dark elves, though selfishly motivated, greatly helps the universe.

B-167. No one escapes hell alone; an escapee may spend 50 to 200 years, with allies, reaching the door.

### Changelings

B-168. A changeling is a being who has bound many souls into one living driver by soul magic.

B-169. A changeling is many souls forced into one vessel, a parody of the One's unity, where elves are many who consent to be one.

B-170. A changeling is a possessor, one host at a time, not a shapeshifter.

B-171. Being a changeling is a crime, not a race; anyone of any race can learn soul magic and commit it.

B-172. Changelings are self-made; no changeling is ever born a changeling.

B-173. Fire, the changelings' symbolic anchor, consumes what it transforms.

B-174. Becoming a changeling requires a powerful user well versed in soul magic, a forbidden and little-known magic; all changelings are very powerful from the start.

B-175. There are no weak changelings, only powerful ones and ancient ones.

B-176. The road to becoming a changeling: find soul magic, master it in secret, win a first harvest of will against will against a living person, and survive becoming plural.

B-177. There are two roads to becoming a changeling: transfer and consume.

B-178. Transfer, the body-takeover road, is forcing one's own soul into another body and leaving the old one behind; a transferring changeling holds one host at a time.

B-179. When a changeling moves bodies it leaves a dead body behind and takes all the souls with it.

B-180. A very old being who gains the ability would likely transfer into a younger body.

B-181. Consume is taking souls into one's own body and keeping that body.

B-182. Ancient beings such as dragons, or beings with a limited bloodline trait, are more likely to consume.

B-183. Each stolen soul increases the thief's Remnant; harvesting is the canonical way to raise innate Remnant, which is why changelings reach elder-tier strength.

B-184. Changelings take one or two souls at a time.

B-185. A changeling holds its crowd of souls by constant will, every day; this is the noise in its mind.

B-186. A changeling keeps a single driver in control only at a sustainable pace; at that pace the swirl never seizes control or acts as a separate driver.

B-187. Changelings who exceed a sustainable pace decay or rupture.

B-188. The founder, a changeling's original self, still presents and drives, with its own character, goals, morals and beliefs.

B-189. Taken souls form a swirl of absorbed tendencies that pressure the founder over time; old changelings are more crowded and less purely themselves.

B-190. What erodes in a changeling is memory of origin, not control.

B-191. A visible tell marks a switch of which held mind is channeled, never a lapse in who channels.

B-192. When a changeling speaks as a held person, that person's mind comes to the surface, but the founder controls the body.

B-193. A changeling absorbs held souls' Remnant but can access only one at a time.

B-194. For a changeling at a sustainable pace nothing of the host leaks through; a host able to leak would rival the changeling's power and marks a failed taking.

B-195. A changeling who loses every soul has nothing left to hold.

B-196. The tell of a changeling is a mismatch between soul and body; skilled changelings shield it or move to bigger bodies.

B-197. Changelings have no physical tell; a patient interrogator catching knowledge gaps can expose one.

B-198. Changelings are now largely monsters; only a few clever ones remain at court.

B-199. Changelings live roughly as long as dragons or giants.

B-200. When the held Remnant leaves a changeling's body, the years it held back arrive at once.

B-201. Possession can take dragons, giants or elves in principle.

B-202. A healthy elder is unassailable will against will; a broken, dying elder can be taken.

B-203. A dying dragon might detonate itself rather than be worn.

B-204. A sufficiently powerful changeling can possess a dragon.

B-205. Gods cannot be possessed.

B-206. Every taking kills the host.

B-207. A changeling wears its victim's body, with the victim's soul held inside and the victim's memories, voice and mannerisms present under the founder's will.

B-208. Changelings are responsible for some of the darkest acts: genocides ordered at whim and frequent assassinations.

B-209. Changeling coldness is not ego-lessness; it grows from total isolation.

B-210. Changelings have no appetite for power as an end; power serves the founder's personal goals, and their endgame is winning the system.

B-211. Changelings prefer leverage over force, but their raw power rivals the elders, and when cornered they switch to force instantly.

B-212. Changelings are murderous, hungry users of other people.

B-213. Each changeling is fundamentally alone and has no family; elves, dragons and giants have families.

B-214. Loneliness draws changelings to institutions and authority because those stay constant; they want to quietly become the system.

B-215. Changelings attach to institutions (guilds, houses, crowns) held across centuries and multiple bodies, one at a time, using engineered deaths and successions.

B-216. A changeling's warmth toward marks is performance, a survival adaptation.

B-217. A changeling can live a regular life and return to old ties after time away.

B-218. Changelings compete for power unaware of one another; one mark may be worked by two changelings, each reading the other as a skilled rival.

B-219. Dragons' moves and money are visible; a changeling king could be the most powerful being in the galaxy without anyone knowing.

B-220. When a changeling departs, it leaves the dead host as the apparent perpetrator, or moves into a new identity and stays to watch the aftermath.

B-221. A worn body is forensically just a dead body; a very powerful soul-user might sense the Remnant ripped out.

B-222. No concealment magic hides changelings; their secrecy is pure skill.

B-223. Dwarves hold no offices yet stay indispensable to all parties; changelings work inside parties yet are useless and parasitic.

B-224. A sufficiently old, experienced changeling can theoretically overpower and consume any leader and become that leader, within the limits of possession.

B-225. Changelings never reveal themselves.

B-226. Changelings are not a race people worry about; any relative or priest could have stolen souls.

B-227. Anyone's oldest friend, father or priest could be a changeling, and one of the most powerful beings alive.

B-228. Changelings can live through all three eras.

B-229. In the far future changelings have no cities and vast wealth; the clever few work among all races except elves.

B-230. Changelings are not a recognized political entity.

B-231. A changeling king could run the Council while wearing a human, raising no suspicion, since everyone accepts human dominance.

B-232. In the augmented-human age changelings can play at full scale, because the bodies they wear can be powerful without exposing them.

### The elder web

B-233. The game of the now, dragons against changelings, is fast and played for living kings and heroes; the few clever changelings play by serial possession, and dragons by proxy.

B-234. The game of the future, dragons against giants, is slow and spans millennia; giants dominate by patience, long traps and attrition.

B-235. Dragons are capable century-players but are outmatched by giants.

B-236. Giants build the dragons' cathedral ships hoping dragons use them to kill one another until none remain.

B-237. The dispossessed dragon houses are ideal customers; giants never have to start the dragon civil war.

B-238. Changelings play both timescales, invisibly and alone; elves are the open, collective player on both.

B-239. The deepest and oldest rivalry is dragons against giants; the dragons' decline was partly the giants' unannounced victory.

### Far-future race forms

B-240. Each race's far-future form is its established nature scaled to the stars.

B-241. In the far future humans fill every world, are the most populous, and produce most bulk goods, such as cheap mechanical freighters.

B-242. In the far future dwarves are the foremost makers of magically infused precision masterwork; dwarven make is the benchmark.

B-243. Dwarves explore dangerous caves across known space to find the best elements and master their attributes.

B-244. Humans and elves also infuse magic into things.
