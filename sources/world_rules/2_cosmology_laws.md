# Part 2: Cosmology and Laws

### The One

C-001. The One is the origin of consciousness (experience and awareness), not of the physical universe.

C-002. The physical universe (matter, energy and the four fundamental forces) exists independently of the One and predates it.

C-003. The physical universe has no metaphysical author.

C-004. The four forces separated from a primordial unity by ordinary symmetry-breaking.

C-005. The One began as a single consciousness whose ultimate desire was to know what it was not.

C-006. The One chose to know itself by becoming other, and split.

C-007. The One's split scattered it into all consciousness: every mind, soul and spark of experience that has existed.

C-008. The One is subject to the physical forces; consciousness arose within physics and obeys it.

C-009. The One is a resident of reality, not its author; even the divine is inside physics.

C-010. The One has mastered the universe and wields it by will, but did not author it.

C-011. The One's will is the most powerful will that can be seen from our level; higher wills may exist beyond conception.

C-012. "The One" is a colloquial name: each universe calls its own One "the One."

C-013. Our universe has one One; sibling worlds have their own.

C-014. One level up, a single being came apart; the piece that let go completely is our One.

C-015. The One decohered into everything and cannot pull itself back together.

C-016. The One has forgotten that it was once something more.

### Physics and consciousness

C-017. Physics and consciousness are two separate, unrelated domains.

C-018. The two domains meet at one point only: magic, which is consciousness gripping physics.

C-019. Cosmology never explains physics, and physics never explains the One.

C-020. The One can be described like a quantum field, with a soul as a localized resonance or excitation on that field.

C-021. The One's field is separate from every physical field and is not itself physical.

C-022. The field description opens no causal channel between the domains other than magic.

C-023. The field description does not make matter or forces flow from the One.

C-024. In field terms, a fragment's size reads as resonance amplitude.

C-025. In field terms, conception reads as the diffuse field coalescing into a stable resonance.

### Beings, matter and machines

C-026. All beings descend from the One; inanimate matter does not.

C-027. Inanimate matter is only physics and needs no metaphysical account.

C-028. Every being, without exception, is a physical body plus a fragment of the One inhabiting it.

C-029. Inanimate matter holds no fragment of the One, and so is not alive, unless a soul is held in it.

C-030. The brain is part of the physical body, not the fragment.

C-031. A body whose brain has stopped working has not lost its fragment; the fragment leaves at death as an event, not with the loss of an organ.

C-032. A person whose brain is destroyed or replaced while the body lives stays inside, feeling signals it cannot interpret.

C-033. A machine, including an artificial superintelligence, is not a being: it holds no fragment of the One and does no magic.

C-034. Technology can reproduce magic-like effects by mechanical means, such as an EM-field generator producing what looks like EM magic.

C-035. A machine's effect is a parallel effect described in the same words, never the same act as magic.

### The single shatter and birth

C-036. All consciousness came into being in a single shatter at the beginning.

C-037. Every race coalesced in that one event; no race was made later.

C-038. Races differ along a spectrum of fragment size: fragment size sets body durability, durability sets lifespan, and lifespan sets generational distance from the origin.

C-039. Large-fragment races (dragons, giants, elves) live in long strides and are only a few lifetimes from the beginning, which remains near, vivid and in living cultural memory.

C-040. Small-fragment races (humans, orcs and others) churn through many short lives; thousands of generations separate them from the origin, which has decayed into dim myth.

C-041. All races are the same age as peoples, differing in generational depth and in memory of the origin.

C-042. The first members of every race awoke in the shatter; every being since, elves included, is born.

C-043. At conception, in every race, unclaimed diffuse Remnant drifting in the world is drawn together and channeled into the child.

C-044. Birth is gathering, not recycling of the dead and not reincarnation; no soul returns.

C-045. Reproduction rate follows ordinary biology (body size, gestation, upkeep), never Remnant cost or magical gating.

C-046. Giants and dragons are therefore rarely born, while humans and orcs multiply.

C-047. Changelings are born beings, of any race, who became changelings through soul theft; they have no birth rule of their own.

### The Law of the Regress

C-048. The One's split is beginningless and infinite.

C-049. Any origin that can be named is only the ceiling of perception, not of reality.

C-050. The regress is consciousness scattering, layered on the pre-existing physical universe; it is not universes born from physics.

C-051. Every plane of reality is a being that came apart.

C-052. When a being decoheres far enough, its pieces forget they were one and it can no longer pull itself back together.

C-053. That point of no return is called the forgetting horizon (placeholder name).

C-054. What dissolves past the forgetting horizon becomes the plane below.

C-055. At every coming-apart, some part holds itself together and refuses to dissolve.

C-056. The same pattern repeats at every level, up and down.

C-057. The forgetting horizon applies to the One, to in-betweeners that split too far, and to every level of the regress.

C-058. The forgetting horizon does not apply to peoples' cultural memory; generational depth between races is cultural and biological.

C-059. In-betweeners' splitting obeys the same law of selves as the One's decoherence; this is shared law, not shared ancestry.

C-060. No one can reset or undo the regress.

C-061. What Derrek holds together is this world against the door he opened, not the regress.

### Our level and the sibling worlds

C-062. The pieces of the level-above being that let go entirely became Ones; ours became our level.

C-063. The pieces that held on are the Unified Ones.

C-064. The One and the Unified Ones are siblings from the same source.

C-065. Each other piece that let go became its own One and its own universe: the sibling (parallel) worlds.

C-066. Sibling worlds are siblings from the same source, not copies; resemblances between them are family resemblance.

C-067. Each sibling world is the only one of its kind.

C-068. "Universe" refers only to the planes of the regress and to the sibling worlds.

C-069. Smaller realms are pocket dimensions, never universes.

C-070. Pocket dimensions are sealed between realities.

### The level above

C-071. Everything one level up is more powerful than anything at our level.

C-072. Beings of the level above are a varied population (predatory, benevolent, indifferent), not a monolith, hunger or doctrine.

C-073. Higher-plane beings are the One's own kind, only stronger; they run on Remnant.

C-074. They are larger chunks of an even more ancient breaking, the same breaking that formed the One.

C-075. The level above is a higher level of the regress, not a different kind of plane as the in-between is.

C-076. Seen from below, higher-plane beings appear alien and god-tier.

C-077. Some stronger entities a level up have continued to spread and consume, some feeding on the Remnant below them.

C-078. The layer above them has its own layer above, all the way up.

### Keeper, consumer and Blight

C-079. Each fractal level has a keeper, a consumer and a Blight.

C-080. Keeper, consumer and Blight are positions or titles given wholesale; motives are individual.

C-081. "Blight" and "consumer" are open-ended names.

C-082. The Blight is not a singular consuming maw of infinite hunger and has no instinct-doctrine.

C-083. The Blight is the parts of the level above that seek maximum dissolution; Blights kill.

C-084. The Blight are not corpses, rot or mindless creatures; they are beings much like ours, holding massive amounts of Remnant.

C-085. A Blight that comes through rules as an absolute god, as strong as our entire universe, or stronger if it comes from further up the regress.

C-086. A world the Blight take still stands; it is under their rule.

### The Unified Ones

C-087. The Unified Ones are those who hold together.

C-088. The Unified Ones wish to protect the shatter.

C-089. The Unified Ones created a bubble (barrier) that holds the Blight out and keeps our level safe.

C-090. The Unified Ones live in another dimension as living absolutes, each sovereign, not in harmony; some resist absorption into each other.

C-091. Our keeper is the puppeteer, a Unified One, who maintains the bubble around our universe.

C-092. The keeper is called good only in quotation marks.

### The puppeteer

C-093. The puppeteer is the being that answered Jon and Derrek's portal.

C-094. It is a potential universe that never decohered: its whole universe is itself.

C-095. It is a Unified One at universe scale, of the kind that held on.

C-096. It is unnamed because it is too grand to be understood from our universe.

C-097. Jon and Derrek borrow its magic, neither of them fully willing.

C-098. It holds both Jon and Derrek as their shared patron, more puppeteer than patron.

C-099. It enslaved Jon and Derrek.

C-100. Neither Derrek nor the puppeteer ever supported the Blight.

C-101. As keeper of the barrier, it lends Jon and Derrek their time powers.

C-102. At the breach, Jon and Derrek saw the keeper outside.

C-103. It is the origin of the time-god tether.

C-104. Humanity worships the puppeteer unknowingly, through Derrek.

### The breach

C-105. Jon and Derrek's portal did not create the world or the One.

C-106. The portal tore a hole, the breach, in the Unified Ones' protective barrier.

C-107. The portal opened a door between Jon and Derrek's world and the level above, where the Blight lives.

C-108. The Tower rift is the place in the shatter where the Blight can reach in.

C-109. What reaches through the breach appears to lesser beings as a god-tier invasion.

C-110. Each reset rips the breach wider.

C-111. Once open, the breach stays open.

C-112. Derrek's portal opened the world in every dimension connected to his, not only his own.

C-113. Every sibling world was damaged, and beings across many worlds were fused with time.

### Rifts

C-114. Every time time was ripped open, a rift appeared; rifts are where the races keep fighting.

C-115. A rift (rip site) is a tear left by one of Jon's transitions; it becomes a magic-rich location wrapped in myth.

C-116. Rifts are finite and worsen as the cycle degrades.

C-117. Rifts leak power from the level above, which bleeds through them.

C-118. Rifts are large concentrations of magic that people fight over; they are never the source of magic.

C-119. The capital, the city of the Tower, holds the first and largest rift: the portal Derrek opened.

C-120. The first rift is the strongest point of Remnant creeping in, which is why every race wants that ground.

C-121. The rift's location is a fixed point: it was always that spot, and Derrek always chose it.

C-122. Whether the rift always existed or the portal made it has no answer; the closed loop has no privileged origin.

### Time-gods

C-123. A time-god is a fragment fused with time, existing across all sequence at once.

C-124. Jon and Derrek are time-gods through the time-fusion.

C-125. Jon and Derrek's only given power is time, from the time-fusion; every other force, expression and ability they have was learned.

C-126. Eternity gave Jon and Derrek time to learn everything.

C-127. Jon and Derrek are not the only time-gods; the portal rupture created time-gods across the connected dimensions.

C-128. In each sibling world, someone opened the portal on the same day at the same moment.

C-129. Each world's openers are a pair of different people, each pair ending at its own window.

C-130. Jon and Derrek are the pair of our world.

C-131. The openings are simultaneous but have a source: our pair's breaking set off a chain reaction outward through the sibling worlds.

C-132. Each next opening comes the smallest possible interval after the last, stretched like an event seen at a horizon.

C-133. Jon and Derrek are therefore older than every later pair by an eternity.

C-134. Jon and Derrek are the first and second time-gods, and the most complete; the third and fourth, a master and apprentice, are the first ring.

C-135. The pairs interfere with one another's universes all the time.

C-136. Jon and Derrek hold open the door of every universe their act opened.

C-137. Jon and Derrek are the center of their own family of universes, not of the regress.

C-138. If Jon and Derrek's war ends badly, one chunk of reality goes dark.

C-139. Derrek's Tower is the nexus across dimensions and time where the sibling worlds' time-god lines bleed together.

C-140. The Tower is the nexus of Jon and Derrek's family of universes, not of the regress.

C-141. The Tower is fused into all eras by the portal event.

C-142. Derrek sees time; Jon jumps through it.

C-143. Derrek's craft is aiming Jon's jumps.

C-144. Jon and Derrek are the most powerful beings in the world, beyond the gods, and cannot be coerced.

### The time-god society

C-145. Time-gods are gathered into a society.

C-146. The time-god society interferes in its members' universes constantly but stays out of one war only, Jon and Derrek's.

C-147. The society's founding law: gods may watch but never join; one who interferes loses the society's protection and blessing.

### The time-god tether

C-148. When Jon jumps in time, Derrek is pulled with him, every time.

C-149. The tether operates regardless of why Jon jumps.

C-150. Jon's first jumps were stress-triggered blackouts; later he jumped at will.

C-151. At the portal Derrek demanded power and was cursed; Jon collapsed in awe, and the entity tied Derrek to Jon's jumps.

### The loop and resets

C-152. The cycle is a closed loop: once started, it has always been.

C-153. There is a single timeline, rewritten about 5,000 times.

C-154. Each run lasts about 10,000 years.

C-155. There have been a thousand or more runs.

C-156. Jon and Derrek are eldritch and broken, in ageless bodies.

C-157. Jon's belief that the current run is the final one is a lie he tells himself.

C-158. Once Jon resets, the earlier loops literally never happened.

C-159. A reset shifts the small upstream events every conception depends on, so those lost are not deleted; they never existed.

C-160. Earlier loops leave only residue, such as scars and foreknowledge.

C-161. Derrek's memory survives a reset.

C-162. The widened breach survives a reset.

C-163. The Tower survives resets.

C-164. Each reset also rewinds invaders from the level above back out of our universe.

C-165. Crossing the barrier costs the Blight's beasts enormous effort.

C-166. A reset scrambles invaders' continuity and returns them to their dimension, forcing them to pay the crossing cost again.

C-167. The wide breach is survivable only because resets keep sweeping invaders back out.

C-168. By the late loops the breach is already broken wide open, and paradox seals it so: nothing is left to widen.

C-169. If Jon stops resetting and moves forward, invaders cross once, stay, and have about 10,000 uninterrupted years to consume the universe.

C-170. Derrek knows both functions of the reset; his warning that reality ends without a reset is literally true.

### The pull of reunion

C-171. The universe was born alongside a force, the pull of reunion, that compelled the portal to open.

C-172. The pull explains why the portal opened at the same moment in every sibling world.

C-173. The pull does not make any pair the only possible openers; each world has its own pair.

### Light, dark and madness

C-174. Light and dark are free descriptors (morality, aesthetics, expression-flavor) with no cosmic doctrine behind them.

C-175. The cosmic axis has a neutral middle; duality is the name versus the act.

C-176. Madness is the base layer: the One dissociating.

C-177. Madness is nondiscriminate: good and bad alike are mad.

C-178. No in-world metaphysical cause of madness is ever visible.

### Grades

C-179. Every property (power, awakening, mastery, godhood) is a continuum; there are no hard tiers or binary states.

### Aging and true immortality

C-180. More Remnant makes flesh more resistant to age, weather, injury and the elements, giving a longer life.

C-181. Remnant gives resistance, never immunity: every being with flesh ages and dies eventually; ancient is not immortal.

C-182. Remnant itself is ageless; only flesh ages, and Remnant only slows a body's decay.

C-183. Elders exist among long-lived races because they do age, slowly.

C-184. The lifespan gradient is the Remnant gradient: orcs, humans, dwarves, elves, dragons, giants, great gods, in rising order.

C-185. Giants are the longest-lived of the races, by far; dragons are second.

C-186. Great gods live so long they seem eternal, but are not.

C-187. There are no true immortals among beings that age.

C-188. Elves and giants are ancient mortals: they age slowly and die.

C-189. Giants' longevity rests on the aging law alone (largest Remnant, most age-resistant flesh), not on any elemental nature.

C-190. True immortality is a separate category set by what a being is, not by how much Remnant it holds.

C-191. The only true immortals are Jon and Derrek (through the time-fusion) and the rule-beasts (through their Rule nature).

C-192. A rule-beast is immortal within its own domain: it cannot die, but can feel.

C-193. A god with huge Remnant is still mortal; a rule-beast is immortal.

C-194. No lesser being can kill Derrek, but a god can.

C-195. Jon is a god and can kill Derrek.

C-196. Only a god armed with power from beyond the regress could threaten Derrek, and only if that god is powerful and outsmarts him.

### The in-between

C-197. In-betweeners (threshold beings) are an ancient lineage that predates the One and is not descended from it.

C-198. In-betweeners are as old as the One, or so old they have collectively forgotten any higher source.

C-199. In-betweeners live in their own bubble between the worlds.

C-200. The in-between is a separate reality entirely outside the regress's chain of events and breakings, neither above nor below our level.

C-201. In-betweeners live between worlds; they are not outside time and space.

C-202. In-betweeners are lateral to the Unified Ones and the Blight: a different lineage and system, not inherently stronger.

C-203. Because they were never the One's consciousness, in-betweeners do not use Remnant; they run an older operating system, the Rule.

C-204. In-betweeners obey physics; the material universe is the shared substrate.

C-205. In-betweeners are alien and separate; they are the remakers.

### The three worlds

C-206. Each dimension has three worlds: ours and two lifeless clone-pockets, which the elves call heaven and hell.

C-207. Heaven and hell are pocket dimensions, not universes.

C-208. The elves built a rift device that reaches these worlds.

C-209. The elves found heaven and hell; they did not make them.

C-210. The elves own the two worlds and are credited with them, as something found and harnessed.

C-211. That the elves built heaven is an in-world belief, not the truth.

C-212. Elves physically enter heaven; it is a real elsewhere.

C-213. In-world, heaven is held to be a utopia wrapped in light, and its sister a place of total darkness, drought and famine, cruel on purpose.

C-214. Heaven and its sister are the elves' reunion and dissolution given form.

### The dead universe

C-215. Our universe's physical cosmos is real but lifeless beyond Earth.

C-216. The One chose Earth, for unspecified reasons.

C-217. Other worlds in our universe have effectively no native life and no native minds; the races later settle them as empty worlds, and there are no alien peoples.

C-218. Earth is every race's homeworld.
