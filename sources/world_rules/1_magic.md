# Part 1: Magic

### Basis of magic

M-001. Magic is a being consciously manipulating reality with its Remnant.

M-002. Only beings do magic; matter itself does no magic.

M-003. Magic is inherited mastery: the One mastered the universe, and a being's magic is a lesser expression of that same mastery.

M-004. When a being works magic, the universe responds according to its real laws; forces, energy and movement are how the act appears, never the power itself.

M-005. Science in any era can fully explain what mages do, but never why they are able to do it.

M-006. In-world, "magic" and "science" are the same word; what we call physics, they call the workings of magic.

M-007. Humans call magic "science" because dragons were humanity's first teachers.

M-008. Faith, magic and science are one act under different names.

M-009. Every being's magic comes from its own soul; rifts are not its source.

### Remnant

M-010. Remnant is the fragment of the One that a being is: its consciousness.

M-011. Soul and Remnant are the same thing.

M-012. A being can touch the world because it is a remnant of the One.

M-013. Remnant is a being's capacity: how much of reality it can grip at once.

M-014. Consciousness and Remnant are one measurement; fragment size equals depth of self-awareness.

M-015. Remnant is identity, not fuel; it is not mana, not a pool, and not spent per act of magic.

M-016. Remnant cannot be burned or spent.

M-017. Remnant is innate and fixed in the living.

M-018. Innate Remnant can be raised only by soul-harvesting, which fragments the taker.

M-019. Remnant is not transferable: it cannot be given, lent, shared or slowly siphoned.

M-020. What passes between beings is knowledge or technique, never capacity.

M-021. Wholesale absorption is the one way Remnant changes hands.

M-022. Reaching beyond one's own Remnant, by taking others' Remnant or through a focusing chamber, works as an amplifier that reaches a wider pool; nothing is drained, stored as charge or spent.

M-023. A beast has a tiny fragment, a human a larger one, and a god an enormous one.

M-024. More Remnant means more possible power, because a being cannot grip more than it has consciousness to grip with.

M-025. Remnant sets the natural ceiling on a being's magic.

### Spirit

M-026. Spirit is the name for active Remnant magic: a being's faculty for consciously manipulating matter and, rarely, other consciousness.

M-027. Having Spirit is what distinguishes a conscious being from inert flesh.

M-028. Magic is one undivided system; there are not two kinds of magic.

M-029. The common case of magic is Spirit gripping matter.

M-030. The rare second branch is Spirit gripping another being's Remnant; this is soul magic.

M-031. Matter is the safe target of magic; another soul is the dangerous one.

M-032. What an act looks like (fire, gravity, a shove, a transmutation) is description applied afterward, never a separate kind of magic.

### The appendage

M-033. Remnant extends an appendage that grabs the universe.

M-034. The appendage's length, size, shape and intensity scale with Remnant, skill and focus.

M-035. Shaking particles with the appendage produces heat.

M-036. Pulling charges apart produces lightning.

M-037. Crushing or ripping a nucleus produces explosions.

M-038. Denting space produces gravity.

M-039. Turning a quark is transmutation, the hardest act of the grip.

M-040. A botched transmutation rips the nucleus.

M-041. These descriptions of what the grip does are qualitative and carry no calculation.

M-042. The grip has a mechanism; the inner sense does not.

### The inner sense

M-043. The inner sense is an unexplained perception that comes from Remnant, separate from the appendage.

M-044. The inner sense is both innate and trainable.

M-045. Beings mostly feel the world through the inner sense.

M-046. Feel is a skill measured by the inner sense; it is a dial, not a hard requirement for gripping.

M-047. Sensing other beings' Remnant is a rare, master-level skill, distinct from the elven bond.

M-048. Soul-sensing is rare and unreliable.

M-049. Sensing souls never works as constant scanning or detection.

### The three dials and the bill

M-050. A being has three independent dials: Remnant (strength), control (skill), and the inner sense (perception).

M-051. Control is how finely a being grips, shapes and blends; it is learned and grows with training and time.

M-052. Two beings of equal Remnant can produce very different results because of control.

M-053. Basic magic needs only control.

M-054. Deeper magic needs a finer inner sense; to interfere with actual physics a being must be able to feel small things.

M-055. A poor inner sense makes deeper magic costlier, not impossible.

M-056. A being can have huge Remnant and a poor inner sense (the "numb titan").

M-057. Working magic is work; the worker pays in mental and bodily effort and fatigue.

M-058. Exhaustion is the universal soft limit on magic; a mage tires like a laborer.

M-059. Overreach past what body and Remnant can pay is dangerous.

M-060. Overreach cannot burn Remnant.

M-061. Magic may add energy; it is not bound by conservation of energy.

M-062. The energy magic releases is not drawn from any fuel source.

M-063. Effort and fatigue are the only bill for magic; there is no energy ledger.

M-064. What a being can do is limited by Remnant, control, inner sense and fatigue, not by whether an act is energetically feasible.

M-065. A weak being cannot crack a planet because it lacks the Remnant and the attempt would kill it.

### Forces and elements

M-066. The four forces (strong, electromagnetic, weak, gravity) are a vocabulary for describing what magic looked like, applied after the act.

M-067. The four elements (fire, water, earth, air) are folk names for how force-effects look and feel.

M-068. Elements are not real substances or base principles.

M-069. One force can appear under many elemental names; the mapping need not be one-to-one.

M-070. An act that unbinds an atom is called nuclear; collapsing matter with great force is called gravity; reshaping a thing is called EM; changing what a thing is is called the weak force.

M-071. There are no magic types or classes: no fire users, gravity users or EM mages, only beings who do magic.

M-072. A force or element label names an act, never a person.

M-073. The same being can do acts that look like gravity, fire, plain momentum or skin-hardening, one after another.

M-074. Blending effects is free; no permission, pathway, growth tree or required order of learning constrains it.

M-075. There are four real fundamental forces, and they never fuse into a fifth.

M-076. A practitioner may run one force, several forces side by side, or forces layered with their expressions at once.

M-077. Mastery is combinatorial fluency, distinct from raw Remnant.

M-078. Mastery shows in running and weaving many operations at once.

M-079. Holding matter in a gravity well while igniting a star at its core is an example of maximal combination.

M-080. Feats described as strong-force are the rarest, found only at the very top of the power scale.

M-081. Nuclear arts (transmutation, making gold, the most ruinous releases) are the rare, prestigious arts of the mightiest beings, high on the scale, never a separate class.

M-082. Transmutation simply transmutes; no particular nuclear process is required for it.

M-083. In description, a nuclear feat's yield is the matter's own stored energy and the user supplies will; this is not an accounting rule.

M-084. Vivid physics wording is description: a dragon's focusing-chamber blast said to rend space is a physical beam, not a literal tear in space.

### The eras of discovery

M-085. In the medieval era there is no concept of forces; magic is named by sensation (fire, water, earth, air), and that naming is the worldview.

M-086. In the modern era science names the forces as the base principle and reveals the elements as their expressions.

M-087. In the far future the forces are fully understood and wielded directly at monumental scale.

M-088. Across the eras it emerges that there were never separate elements, only one undivided power described in different words.

### Individuals and growth

M-089. Personality does not make any force or expression easier or harder to reach.

M-090. Personality shapes a being's learning path and how its magic is expressed.

M-091. A being's magic is not determined by its race, temperament or an element's supposed nature; it follows from that individual's life.

M-092. No race is locked to a kind of magic.

M-093. A being knows the workings it learned, leans on what comes naturally, and reaches for others when forced.

M-094. Talent suits some workings to some beings, in kind and in scale.

M-095. Culture and disposition can color what a being gravitates toward, as pressure only, never a barrier.

M-096. Beings develop chosen specialties through mastery, preference and study.

M-097. Every being develops its own powers.

M-098. Potions, enchantment, craft and lore are learnable arts beside magic; a potions master is not a different kind of mage.

M-099. Magic ability is a continuous gradient of control and understanding, never on or off.

M-100. Awakening runs from involuntary leaks (loudest under emotion) to crude intent, control through understanding, engineered reproducible workings, and mastery.

M-101. Practice sets how far a being travels toward its ceiling; ability dulls with neglect and deepens with use.

M-102. Sapience is the visible sign of having a large enough fragment to begin magic.

M-103. Awakening is individual; it varies by race only statistically, because fragment sizes differ by race.

M-104. Raw working is improvising rough effects from a grasped principle; most practitioners never go beyond it.

M-105. Spells are specific, complex, reproducible workings engineered from deep understanding.

M-106. Spells are taught through fundamentals and how they combine, then drilled for reproduction.

M-107. Spells are not slotted abilities; they are capped by Remnant, accessed by skill, and a being may know any number.

M-108. Spells can be signature moves, school-taught, lineage-held, hoarded or lost.

### Soul magic

M-109. Soul magic is Spirit turned on another fragment of the One: mind, memory, emotion, will or soul.

M-110. Soul magic uses the same Remnant and effort as other magic.

M-111. Soul magic has no forces or elements; it is will against will, coherence against coherence.

M-112. Soul magic is two-way: what a being grips can grip back.

M-113. The limit on soul magic is self-risk, not fatigue.

M-114. Overreach in soul magic can bleed the other's nature into the caster, fracture the caster, or cost the caster pieces of self.

M-115. Soul magic is rare because most beings only learn to grip matter.

M-116. Soul magic is forbidden and scarcely known.

M-117. Soul magic belongs to no race; any being who masters it can commit soul theft.

M-118. It is commonly known that souls can be stolen and transferred, which makes soul magic both tempting and taboo.

M-119. Mind-reading, soul-binding and possession all belong to soul magic.

M-120. Soul magic can override a living being's Remnant and take over its body without absorbing it.

M-121. A fragment's natural post-death process is its own.

M-122. Soul-binding forcibly interrupts a fragment's natural post-death process.

M-123. Soul-harvesting absorbs another's fragment into one's own living fragment, raising the taker's Remnant and fragmenting and pluralizing the taker.

M-124. A harvester gains power and also the absorbed fragment's dominant tendency, which fights their own.

M-125. Soul-binding and soul-harvesting are the deepest violations.

M-126. Taking others' Remnant is called "stealing their production."

M-127. The orc chips, made with Derrek's involvement, are an instance of stealing production.

M-128. Stealing production is not burning, spending or using Remnant as fuel.

### Wholesale absorption

M-129. Slow siphoning of Remnant does not exist; wholesale absorption does.

M-130. Absorption is gated by power, not by kind of being.

M-131. Absorption requires a being at least as strong as a changeling.

M-132. A being as powerful as a god can absorb on far larger scales, many at once.

M-133. Any being strong enough and versed in soul magic can absorb, including dragons, giants, Jon and Derrek; gods and changelings do so by nature.

M-134. Absorption works by coherence: a lesser Remnant loses its coherence in the presence of a much greater Remnant and is lost in its swirl.

M-135. The consume road of soul theft is absorption in changeling form.

### Echoes and ghosts

M-136. Echoes are loose Remnant left by great death, such as battlefields, sieges and massacres.

M-137. Echoes are impressions only; no person is present in them.

M-138. Echoes fade back into the pool over centuries.

M-139. Places of great death are ghost-sites.

M-140. Great-death sites hold dense unclaimed Remnant within reach of soul mages, changelings and reservoir builders.

M-141. A ghost is Remnant bound together by magic so that a dead self cannot disperse.

M-142. Every true ghost is a soul-binding crime; a haunting is a crime scene.

M-143. Ghosts may be bound as guardians, servants, power sources or punishment, or out of love.

M-144. Undead types are plague (biotech), animated flesh, souls bound to corpses, souls in machines, and changeling leftovers.

M-145. Not every walking corpse holds a soul; dead flesh can be animated with no soul in it.

M-146. The Orc King's dead are empty animated flesh with no soul held in them.

M-147. Resurrection is possible in principle, because decoherence destroys nothing, but the odds are near zero.

M-148. Derrek, who sees time, could trace a dead being's fragments.

### Soul-binding

M-149. Soul-binding is concentration: a constant effort to hold the magic still.

M-150. A binding is never finished; the holder must keep holding every day and pays the cost.

M-151. Holding a bound soul is a constant cost separate from soul magic's self-risk.

M-152. If the holder dies, the binding lapses and the held soul is freed or destroyed.

M-153. Killing the holder is a way to free or end a ghost.

M-154. A ghost needs a holder, holding costs the holder, and breaking the holder frees the ghost.

M-155. A ghost that lasts thousands of years requires a second soul bound permanently to an object, whose only purpose is maintaining concentration on the ghost; the object is a warden-object (placeholder name).

M-156. An ancient haunting has two victims: the ghost and the hidden warden-soul holding it.

M-157. Breaking the warden-object frees both souls.

M-158. The warden-object holds the warden-soul; the matter does no magic and gains no soul of its own.

M-159. The mechanism of warden-objects is an unquantified dark art.

M-160. The core of necromancy is the act of holding, not undeath.

M-161. The vessel for a held soul can be the dead person's own body, an amulet, armor, or any other vessel.

M-162. The necromancer's craft is the holding plus the vessel.

M-163. Soul-binding is not enchantment: a binding must be held by concentration, while an enchantment is set and left.

### The elven bond

M-164. The elven bond is innate, constant, collective and unique to elves.

M-165. The elven bond is a form of soul magic.

M-166. Elves are the premier sensors of souls; the elf queen's vigilance comes from intense use of the bond.

M-167. The bond does not track individuals; an elf who dies far away dies alone, outside the bond.

M-168. A strong elf can bind weaker elves to its will through the bond; the binding is partial and does not destroy their coherence.

M-169. The bond lets elves feel a member's disposition.

### Pacts

M-170. A pact is any deal with a powerful being for its way of doing magic.

M-171. Demon deals, god blessings, blood rituals, divine-domain channeling, Blight-favor and Unified-One boons are all pacts, one system differing only by patron and price.

M-172. A pact grants technique, never Remnant or raw power.

M-173. Each patron's technique is its own signature brand of magic, unique to that patron.

M-174. The pact-holder pays for every use from their own limited Remnant, with the same effort and fatigue as any magic.

M-175. The patron's Remnant never enters the pact-holder.

M-176. A pact does not make a being stronger; it makes it unrepeatable.

M-177. A pact lets a weak being reach far past what it is, but only by paying from its own limited Remnant.

M-178. Pacts are unstable; someone holds the leash.

M-179. A patron must be strong enough in Remnant to support the link.

M-180. Those who hand out pacts are superior soul mages: angels and demons.

M-181. A pact is soul magic: giving a pact is the reverse of taking a soul, connecting to the holder and opening a flow.

M-182. A soul-magic tether binds the pact-holder to the patron.

M-183. The pact tether fades on the pact-holder's side, never on the patron's.

M-184. The tether needs attunement on the holder's side and costs the patron nothing, so the patron can always find the holder and collect.

M-185. What a patron does when it collects is particular to that patron.

M-186. Taught abilities remain even as the link to the patron fades.

M-187. Beings discover patrons by attuning themselves to the Remnant in the universe.

M-188. A patron teaches better control of Remnant through secrets from its long life.

M-189. A pact-holder can be weakened by losing enlightenment, and the link fades if attunement lapses.

M-190. What separates one patron's pact from another's, and what it demands, depends on the individual patron, not on a binary.

M-191. A god asks for devotion; a demon offers a deal; this is a difference of approach, not of species.

M-192. A pact-holder's price is the patron's terms (devotion, a deal's price, a kept vow, blood) plus the effort of every use.

M-193. Blood rituals pay in blood, lives or time to buy an effect; at root they are a deal with whatever answers the offering.

M-194. Blood rituals are slow, transactional and grim.

M-195. Blight-things and Unified Ones are rarer, more overwhelming patrons; every use moves the pact-holder toward what the patron is.

M-196. A great being teaching a lesser being its technique is a god-pact or blessing; no ceiling is raised.

M-197. A strong patron can seize a lesser being's will with soul magic and control it like a puppet, though not always.

M-198. A patron can use a thrall as its avatar to speak to other races directly.

M-199. A patron may intervene to save its thrall out of its own interest.

M-200. The Orc King holds a demon pact over entropy: rot, decay, plague, famine and the weakened state.

M-201. The Orc King is a small-souled orc casting something far bigger than he is, paid from his own limited Remnant.

### Convergence

M-202. Convergence is the giants' soul art, the rite in which soul and matter become one; it works on bodies.

M-203. In funerary Convergence a living-stone mason builds with the dead giant, fusing the fallen into the walls.

M-204. Funerary Convergence releases the dead giant's soul.

M-205. The emperor's Convergence used his city's bodies as material and trapped the city's Remnant in the stone.

M-206. The emperor was a mix of souls and dissolved from taking too many at once.

M-207. The original Convergence was god-forging.

M-208. Soul mortar is the lost secret of the original Convergence: silencing a crowd of souls into unison.

M-209. The greatest giant god is a million minds in unison.

### Enchantment

M-210. Enchantment is a soul held in matter.

M-211. Matter can hold Remnant because a body is itself inanimate matter made animate.

M-212. Body-like substrate holds a soul best.

M-213. A soul held in matter can do magic; the matter holding it does not.

M-214. A soul held in matter feels something: dread, without a brain to understand it.

M-215. Full experience requires biology.

M-216. Enchantments are the historical toolkit for slaying dragons.

M-217. Enchantments are the equalizer for low-Remnant and non-mage beings.

M-218. Using an enchanted object draws on the soul held in it, not the user's own fragment; it cannot improvise, has a hard ceiling and causes no fatigue.

M-219. Enchantment has three types: reservoirs, attunement objects and bound effects.

M-220. A reservoir is a held soul that acts as an amplifier, letting a small-Remnant being reach large effects.

M-221. Potent reservoirs hold stolen souls, and harvesting them is murder.

M-222. Cleaner reservoir sources may exist (freely given souls, or rift sites), but the potent ones are stolen.

M-223. Attunement objects (wands, staves, foci, braces) make a discipline easier, sharper or more efficient without adding capacity.

M-224. Bound effects are specific workings locked permanently into matter, such as an unbreakable blade or a sound-dampening cloak.

M-225. Enchantment is a learnable craft; a master enchanter need not be a powerful Remnant-user.

M-226. The greatest enchanted items are lost-art elder relics.

M-227. The dwarves' oldest art is fusing magic into metal.

### The materials ladder

M-228. Flesh is the material that holds Remnant best.

M-229. Flesh cores, also called living metal, require warping living flesh and can hold a live, complete soul.

M-230. Young, healthy orcs are processed into flesh cores or living metal.

M-231. The old or recently dead are processed into other enchanted metals, which need only some latent potential, not the ability to hold a live, complete soul.

M-232. The dead make walls; inorganic, dead structures such as walls and ships do not need living material.

M-233. The galaxy's electronic and enchanted might comes from infusing Remnant into matter.

### Focusing chambers and implants

M-234. A dwarven focusing chamber is an amplifier, not a drain or a battery: a dragon inside it reaches a wider pool of Remnant than its own.

M-235. Aboard dragon ships, a focusing chamber takes a dragon's breath and fires it as a great beam.

M-236. A converged ship is a massive living-metal structure; a dragon using a focusing chamber aboard one draws on the entire Remnant infused into the city-ship.

M-237. Drawing on a converged ship's infused Remnant is why dragon beams can break planets.

M-238. The bond-spoof chip is a focusing chamber made into an implant.

M-239. The bond-spoof chip lets its user channel, and sharpens existing soul-magic control without raising the user's ceiling.

M-240. The bond-spoof chip works only for someone already capable of soul magic; it teaches no skill.

M-241. The chip's most feared use is impersonating the elven bond, letting the user pass as an elf and making elves vulnerable to changelings.

M-242. The chip gives outsiders access to the mechanism the bond uses to move mental weight across the elven race, letting them mentally break a bond-protected elf.

M-243. The chip is the one deliberate break in elven uninfiltrability; before it, changelings could not infiltrate elves.

M-244. Dwarves make the chip; they designed and perfected it by dissecting living and dead elves bought as war captives.

M-245. The elven captives are supplied by a human company that specializes in slavery.

M-246. A focusing-chamber implant lets a human channel Remnant to hold, fuse and enhance, including projecting their entire force into one attack.

M-247. The implant's maximum-power attack is self-destructive to its user.

M-248. Other implant uses are longer sustained draws, strength and durability, cognitive speed, and magnetic fields that inhibit EM attacks.

M-249. Implants raise strength only relative to the user's own baseline; no one's Remnant grows.

### In-betweener powers

M-250. An in-betweener's fuel is transition; its price is coherence.

M-251. In-betweeners operate outside the Remnant system; they sidestep rules rather than out-power anyone.

M-252. In-betweener powers are narrow, specific and rule-shaped.

M-253. Beings with Remnant cannot learn to run the Rule.

M-254. The Void is the space around an in-betweener; the in-betweener moves its Void to where it wants rather than moving through our dimension.

M-255. An in-betweener never stands in our dimension; what appears present in a room is the edge of its Void touching that place.

M-256. The Void lies on top of the real world, so those inside may not realize at first that anything has changed.

M-257. Outsiders cannot simply break into a Void; it is a moving zone attached to its in-betweener.

M-258. Chain is the in-betweener ability to bind; it drags the victim into the Void.

M-259. Chain Void is the fusion of Void and Chain.

M-260. Void, Chain and Chain Void are general in-betweener powers, not one being's signature.

M-261. Inside its Void an in-betweener has no true form; it can leave the Void briefly in a physical form that obeys physics and can be hurt.

M-262. An in-betweener can split into many forms; the more forms, the more decohered it is until it reassembles.

M-263. An in-betweener that splits too far can never remember who it was or reassemble; that is its true death.

M-264. An in-betweener keeper can leave its seat by splitting, sending out pieces while its core stays.

M-265. Phase-step is movement by repositioning the Void's point of contact with our world; short steps are cheap, far ones costly.

M-266. Unfixing is a brief flicker of non-presence used as defense; it cannot be sustained and is countered by being pinned, such as by a gravity anchor.

M-267. Threshold-sense is a free, passive perception of transitions, seams, instability and other transitional beings, including Jon and Derrek.

M-268. Minor reshaping is a cheap, small-scale change of state.

M-269. The Hold forces something to stay mid-transition; it bleeds the in-betweener's coherence continuously.

M-270. The Hold arrests a state; Chain drags a body into the Void.

### The Rule

M-271. The Rule is a binding condition imposed on a bounded space that traps everyone inside it, the in-betweener included.

M-272. A Rule releases only when its condition is met.

M-273. The bounded space of a Rule is the in-betweener's Void, laid over real rooms or land; the Rule is played inside the Void.

M-274. A chase inside a Rule stays coherent because in-betweeners cannot freely break physical fundamentals.

M-275. Inside a severed Rule-domain a being is cut off from everything the environment would supply and fights with only what it is and what it brought in.

M-276. An in-betweener is bound by its own Rule and must win its own game to survive it.

M-277. An in-betweener that masters its own condition is bound but not trapped, and leaving is not hard for it.

M-278. In-betweeners tend not to take contests they are not confident of winning.

M-279. Those who meet a Rule's condition are released.

M-280. An in-betweener's emergency moves escalate in order: Lash-Out, Sacrifice-Step, Snare-Reversal, Anchor-Theft, then Cascade.

M-281. Lash-Out overloads Unfixing.

M-282. Sacrifice-Step tears free of a pin by abandoning coherence, leaving the in-betweener diminished.

M-283. Snare-Reversal holds the attacker's pin mid-transition, a race of coherence against Remnant.

M-284. Anchor-Theft transitions the reference point a pin needs, and cannot catch the same foe twice.

M-285. Cascade spends all remaining coherence to set the Rule's condition to its most lethal value, erasing the in-betweener.

M-286. Cascade is the Rule's final form, not a generic explosion.

M-287. Cascade is dishonorable among in-betweeners.

M-288. Resolving a Rule's condition before the in-betweener panics into Cascade is the subtlest counter.

M-289. An in-betweener that goes still and stops trying to escape is about to Cascade.

M-290. Only a fair, genuinely uncertain contest produces rich transition, which in-betweeners feed on.

M-291. In-betweeners demand equal, matched duels and bind themselves by their own Rules to keep them fair.

M-292. In-betweeners cultivate worthy opponents by imbuing lesser beings with Rules and setting them to fight.

M-293. To in-betweeners a clean loss is honorable and Cascade is shameful.

M-294. Jon and Derrek, as living transition, are the opponents in-betweeners covet most.

M-295. An Afflicted is a being given a Rule unasked; it cannot aim or master it, pays unpredictable and often harmful costs, and cannot safely Cascade.

M-296. Transition is alien to a Remnant soul, so an Afflicted has no learning curve, only scarring.

M-297. An Afflicted is anchored by its own coherence and rarely dissolves fully.

M-298. Each Afflicted implies a true in-betweener master who granted the Rule.

M-299. A Victor is a being who defeated a threshold being in fair contest and was awarded its Rule; it can wield the Rule with intention and skill.

M-300. Victors are legendarily rare.

### Maze-lands

M-301. A Rule can be laid on a region of real land, making a maze-land.

M-302. A maze-land is a real place with its existing land and life; the in-betweener made nothing.

M-303. A maze's walls are warped reality: doors passable only by dematerializing and reforming, and stretches crossable only by compressing reality around oneself.

M-304. Passing a maze requires understanding phase-shifting and decoherence.

M-305. Whole clans, families and empires live in a maze's segments without ever leaving.

M-306. Escaping a maze is crossing from one similar field into another, with no visible change.

M-307. Outsiders who wander into a maze become stuck, looping back.

M-308. A maze's keeper lives at its center, in the biggest castle on the tallest hill, where its Void spreads furthest.

M-309. An in-betweener took dwarves sold to it by their own people; those who win free are the Returned (placeholder name).

M-310. The Returned's powers are new forms of Remnant magic learned from their environment (decoherence, phasing, compression), not stolen Rule-magic.

M-311. The Returned fuse enchantment, soul magic and the Void's warping into one craft.

M-312. The Returned were vivisected, and they reverse-engineered captured in-betweener trinkets.

### Domains not developed as powers

M-313. There is no light or dark power; light- or dark-flavored effects are ordinary magic description.
