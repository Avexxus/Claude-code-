## A. HARD RULES

### Every race (shared law)
- 🔒 Every being is flesh plus a fragment of the One (Remnant); soul and Remnant are the same thing. All races came out of the one shatter and all are born (`cosmos.md`); in-betweeners and higher-plane beings are not of it. (02 0c, Law 15; 07; jul23 t100)
- 🔒 **The lifespan ladder.** Lifespan follows Remnant. Shortest to longest: orcs and dwarves (short) < humans < elves < dragons (second-longest) < giants (longest of the races) < great gods (seem eternal, aren't). More Remnant = flesh resists age and injury: resistance, never immunity. Everything that ages dies. (02 Law 4a; R093)
- 🔒 Elves and giants are ancient mortals, never "near-immortal." (02 4a)
- 🔒 Each race's "element" (giants earth, dragons air, elves water, changelings fire) is symbolic and cultural only. No mechanical racial lean; no race is "THE" anything. (07 Law 8 line; 10 orcs)
- ✅ Every race carries every vice (greed, pride, jealousy, wrath). No race has an assigned sin; what differs is which vice surfaces, the mask over it and the clock. (10 race model; jul23 t52)
- ✅ No race is a monolith: each has several empires or powers and its own fracture. (10 t596)
- 🔒 Build every race against four questions: what it hungers for, the mask it wears, the clock it runs on, where it is woven into everyone else. (10 race model)
- ✅ Strife is the natural state; peace is rare and hard-won; nobody has clean hands. (10 texture 🔒)
- 🔒 The long-memory cycle: elders personally remember atrocities that humans forgot generations ago. Innocent humans read that hatred as baseless prejudice, and wounded pride answers it with the next atrocity. (10 enmity cycle)
- 🚫 Never "a fire mage" or "a corrosion drake": say "a drake who wields corrosion." (07 dragons; 02)

### GIANTS
- ✅ **Body:** dragon-scale, human silhouette; the largest biological life, the most Remnant capacity. No size chart, no square-cube math. (07 t74, t76; R023)
- ✅ **Mind:** "smart, cunning and focused"; active, potent users, never terrain. (07 t76)
- 🔒 **Lifespan:** the longest of all mortals, MUCH longer than elves. One giant can span all three eras; survivors remember betrayal day personally. (02 4a; 07 t272)
- ✅ **On the dials:** sheer Remnant, little skill, will sapped after their fall. "The giants had sheer power so they never needed skill and once the tide turned on them it led to less determination also." "A poorly trained and timid giant can still produce nightmares to other races." (R149; R095)
- ✅ **Signature magic:** large-scale transmutation and reshaping by will. They grow seamless architecture with no chisel marks, carry no weapons, and raise them from the terrain. (07 t29–t34, t81)
- ✅ **Reproduction:** slow births, huge upkeep. They rebuilt more than anyone thinks and are quietly growing. (07 t418)
- ✅ **Death:** Convergence (`magic.md`). The funeral version releases the soul and the dead are fused into the walls their offspring live in, consensual on death, ambiguously so. (07 t36–t46; wk W15)
- ✅ **Culture:** they wrote every law and punished every breach swiftly and ruthlessly, so crime effectively did not exist: iron-handed compassion with no consent and no exit. (07 t472)
- ✅ Since the genocide they are cold; their stoicism is scar tissue over a war of attrition: **they build the dragons' warships so that dragons will kill each other.** (07 t46, t56)
- ✅ The dragons exiled them; they are unwelcome near dragon land. "Subservient class" is the dragons' view of a beaten, contracted people who sell ships from afar. (R078, R139)
- ✅ By Book 2's era they have left Earth for the forge world. In the far future everyone thinks they are dwindling, but they are rich beyond belief: one cathedral ship sells for a small planet. (R140; 07 t416)
- ✅ Far-future camps: the ethically sourced (consensual giant dead only), the any-tactic league "who does whatever for money", and the cashed-out who took seats of power. (07 t48)
- ✅ When a dragon guts a forge world (Derrek engineered it), the giants stop pretending and take their own fleets to war on every dragon. (07 t412–t416; wk W64)
- ✅ **Toward others:** they hate dragons' nuke first, then humans and dwarven-armored raiders well below that. The elves stayed out, so the giants don't hate them. (07 t104; R092)
- 🚫 Never elemental titans or tree-elemental apex giants. Never "the last friendly giant" as a special figure; some giants are simply friendly, "doesnt mean much". (07 RETIRED; R116)
- 🚫 A giant who says "Elves too" about the coup is showing his own bias, not stating fact. (R092)

### DRAGONS
- ✅ **Body:** four-legged, no real hands; the Remnant grip is their hand, and fine work with it is tiresome. "Dragon" also covers smaller wingless two-legged kin and reptilian peoples, whom dragons subjugated and erased as an underclass. (R036, R046)
- ✅ **Lifespan:** second-longest of the mortals; some still remember betrayal day. (07 t272)
- ✅ **On the dials:** powerful but less than giants, "so they perfected skill", with belief in their supremacy. Premier scientists, scholars and historians, with "deep schools of magic", and the ones who erased history. (R068; wk W61, W63)
- ✅ **Reproduction:** eggs; families ("kids and wives"). **Death:** no fixed custom; "they dont all self destructive." (R047, t260; R050)
- ✅ **Society:** a true society: cities of dragons, universities of magic and science, businesses, fleets. Fewer than humans or elves. They design, plan and orchestrate; others' labor builds, whether bought, taken or enslaved. (R090)
- ✅ Politics: the houses the advisor raised against the dispossessed, who serve beneath dragons their families once ruled. The empire has never known peace since the coup; in the far future dragons buy cathedral ships to murder each other. (07 t270, t274, t298)
- ✅ Hoards extend their empire and reflect status: gems, idols, magic items, scientific accomplishments. (R049)
- ✅ Breaths are varied and wielded (fire, lightning, rot, frost, corrosion, poison, transmutation); one dragon may wield several. Transmutation defeats a prepared defense by changing *what it is*. (07 breaths)
- 🔒 **Weakness:** supreme in bursts, but can't hold peak; a long fight tires them (stamina, never Remnant). A patient, evasive foe or a coordinated group can bring one down at terrible cost. (07 combat profile)
- 🔒 **Beatability:** no lone being overpowers a great ancient dragon (you survive, flee or outwit). A gifted individual may beat a young or weak one. One unaugmented human never beats a dragon one-on-one; an augmented human with a focusing implant can, at self-destructive cost. (07; jul08 t239, t261)
- ✅ Humans ride captured *lesser* dragons; a powerful dragon would never sink so low. (R048)
- ✅ Only some dragons, the weak-force transmuters, make fissile "prepared stones." (R051)
- ✅ **Tech:** they buy focusing chambers from the dwarves and cathedral ships from the giants, and don't care whose dead are in the hull. (R043, R027, R045)
- ✅ Dragon ships run on rotating crews strapped into focusing chambers, the master as the final lever; chamber crews are prestigious, high-ranking military. (07 t442–t444) A dragon has about five full-power plasma beams in him "before he kills himself from sheer exhertion"; "if they have 20 coming still he will be overwhelmed" (≈5 is scaffolding only, never on the page). (chat 06-18 GB P468) ⏳ pending pass
- ✅ **After their age:** young dragons moved their wealth into human commerce and law; the old stay in remote giant-built palaces too large for humans. (jul08 t273–t275, t300)
- ✅ **Toward others:** everyone, giants included, is beneath them. Humanity's first teachers, and they "know they are successful off the backs of humanity." (R045; wk W63; jul08 t93)
- 🚫 Never solitary, scarce world-lords or "each its own king"; never "builders" or mere takers; never brainless breath machines; never "proud fool" as a system rule. (R090; 07 RETIRED)

### ELVES
- 🔒 **Lifespan:** ancient but mortal, below dragons and giants. (02 4a)
- ✅ **On the dials:** high Remnant and high skill, "a nice sweet spot," and the bond gives them moral authority to believe they are right. One elf outmatches a handful of humans and can stand toe to toe with a dragon. (R069; jul08 t239)
- ✅ **The bond:** an extra sense organ (mechanism and reach: `magic.md`). It can't cross realities (`cosmos.md`). (R098)
- ✅ Elves alone feel resets: the lost never existed, and elves grieve for people no one can remember. (07 resets)
- ✅ **Reproduction:** born like everyone else. "Where ever an elf is born they are just an elf." (07; R056)
- ✅ **Culture:** authoritarian, under "complete subservience to the king." Two pillars: extreme discrimination against other races and harsh punishment of their own. Reunion at all costs is the justification. (jul23 t132–t134)
- 🔒 The bond makes them un-absorbable, the one race the human machine could never dissolve, and lets them cooperate without friction, which is why they advance fastest. (10 elves; 07)
- ✅ Justice: ordinary crimes go to ordinary prisons; dissent meets rigid enforcement. Only stealing a fellow elf's soul or body sends you to hell, though its population drifted to include dissidents and vagrants. (t682; wk W25)
- ✅ They always stood outside giant control and took no part in the coup: "Let them stay in chains the elves would think." (R092)
- ✅ Mika alone ordered the exodus as a top-down royal directive; Jon is just Mika's friend; the elves are independent through and through. (R094; jul23 t132)
- ✅ **Tech:** a rift device that reaches heaven and hell. Ships cross by shrinking one of their dimensions; fleets are small, nimble, dwarven-built and coordinated; doctrine is to bait a dragon's beams, then pour in sustained fire. Book 2 era: "the most competitive fleet." (07 t438, t220; R083)
- ✅ The Inquisition, once the Council, the first kings' countermeasure force, hunts whoever causes the resets, so it is unknowingly hunting its own god. It secretly sells captured dark elves through dwarven traffickers to rich humans. (07 t490; wk W68)
- ✅ Hunted for organs in the far future: elf flesh is very high-quality, Remnant-holding material. (R028; t341)
- ✅ The bond-spoof chip (`magic.md`) is the one deliberate crack: it lets a wearer pass as an elf. (jul23 t150–t156)
- 🚫 Never soul-sensing "premier sensors", pooled Remnant, or a tether. Never "Jon led the elves", Jon as their god or gardener. Never "elves awaken instead of reproducing." (R098, R094; 07 RETIRED)

### DARK ELVES (sentenced elves; the name is a placeholder)
- ✅ Sentenced to hell (`cosmos.md` · the three worlds). Powerful changelings farm hell's population; it has become a god-forge full of elf-haters, and soul thieves there cultivate their own families. (wk W26; t684)
- ✅ Many have escaped and many have been born outside since. Escapees come back war-trained, felt as "wrong and warped," and hunted galaxy-wide. An escape takes 50–200 years and is never made alone. (t401, t228, t610)
- 🚫 Never "Drow" in codex-facing text. No dark-elf sect by birthplace. (t233; R141)

### HUMANS
- 🔒 **Body and Remnant:** short-lived, endlessly multiplying; more Remnant than orcs, less than elves. Generalists who learn the widest range, and arguably the most powerful people of all. (10 dominant races)
- ✅ **How they fight:** humans "cannot match a giant or dragon one on one… nor elves," so they use combined arms, groups, and later chips, with "indomitable" will and numbers: "the timid fade as the throng of those motivated and driven flourish." (R070)
- ✅ **Hunger and mask:** they hunger for everything, want rather than need. Their mask is supremacy: "the true rightfully anointed children of the One." Pride is the deadliest vice, never the only one. (10 t480, t488; jul23 t82)
- ✅ **Their one weird specific: fervent zealotry.** They worship Jon and Derrek's patron in a cult Derrek started as he conquered them. (t488, t552)
- ✅ Science and magic rose together out of endless war, not curiosity. (wk W63; jul08 t93)
- 🔒 They take the gift and deny the giver (elven medicine, giant architecture, dragon law, dwarven craft), and grind other humans hardest of all. (10)
- ✅ Fracture: true believers against realists, who despise each other. Sympathetic humans live in the second camp. (10)
- ✅ Specific human kingdoms are deliberately unbuilt; they grow out of story need. Medieval era: "a handful of major rival empires." (t528; jul23 t198)
- ✅ Their unity under Derrek is hierarchy, never union. (jul23 t24)

### ORCS
- 🔒 **Body:** shortest-lived and most crowded, the weakest in magic of the major races, enormously gifted physically. (10)
- ✅ People, not animals: they do magic and have traditions, heroes and tales. Calling their souls "tainted" is prejudice; all souls are the same. (R057, R058)
- 🔒 **Three masters:** born into bondage under the giants, re-chained by the dragons, subjugated by humans. "They earned it" is purely the world's lie. Subjugated for defiance, not weakness: "refusing to accept being less, they were forced to be less." (10; jul08 t185)
- 🔒 Grievance without archive: each orc remembers only their own lifetime, but the people know they were wronged the way you know a bruise. Their own word for it is **hate**, and their culture runs on it. (10)
- ✅ Greed is their most common expression, humanity's game played with no mask that works; from inside it is restitution. (10; jul23 t52)
- 🔒 They lean on pact magic as the most open road left to them, not the only one. Orc rites are "everyone's religion with a poorer patron." (10)
- ✅ No society to fracture; the fracture lives inside each orc. They never had kings, so "Orc King" is a symbolic title. (t522–t524)
- ✅ They enslave goblins and trolls as trauma replication. (10)
- ✅ **Far future:** the galaxy's cattle, bought by the trillion as cathedral-ship material: bulk material (hulls, cores, living metal), never transplant stock, because "their organs would be wrong"; left out of the organ trade. (07 t278–t280; R144; t343)

### DWARVES
- ✅ **Body:** low Remnant and naturally short-lived (alongside orcs), but **hardy**. (R093)
- ✅ **Skill:** "the highest skill of all races"; they "built systems for building tools that used remnant" and desperately extend their lives through craft, tech and magic. (R093; BANK 11-16)
- ✅ **Root:** independence at all costs, with greed as the strategy they chose. Selling to both sides is business; selling to your ally's enemy is betrayal. (10 jul23 t54)
- 🚫 Never "dwarves are neutral" as a trait: neutrality was learned and bought by being too costly to conquer. (10 tripwire; t148)
- ✅ **Culture:** a loud, rowdy, fiercely loyal family, ruthless to outsiders, with rigid hierarchical family trees. Yet they sold the dwarves they considered weak to an in-betweener to save themselves. (t590–t594, t146)
- ✅ **Government:** several emperors, each running his own empire as a bureaucratic autocracy tied to business; abroad, a merciless trading empire. (t594–t596, t146)
- ✅ They keep a district in every race's cities, the elven city included. Show it; never tell it. (jul08 t223)
- 🔒 Their empire never fell: a managed plateau, outside the giants → dragons → humans apex-fall pattern. The race that thrived most. (10)
- ✅ In the coup they were arms dealers to both sides, present but uninvolved. (10 Darkest Age stance)
- ✅ **Tech:** computing was born from their magic-infused metalwork, and humans coerced it out of them. They hold the focusing-chamber monopoly and build drives and cannons. Dwarven make is the seal of mastery. "Giants are builders and Dwarves make machines." (07 ASI; R043, R041)
- ✅ Book 2 era: deep-flung mine worlds, missile cruisers, old trade networks; true dragons fund dwarven ventures. (R082, R052)
- ✅ **Toward others:** they use the weaker, other dwarves included. Gnomes are their indentured class, sold to anyone. They run the organ trade with humans and traffic dark elves. (t566–t568, t341; wk W68)

### THE RETURNED (sold dwarves; placeholder name)
- ✅ Born inside the in-betweener's maze-land, a real place with rivers and birds, where empires are born and die without leaving. (07 t150–t154)
- ✅ Their magic is new Remnant magic learned from their environment (phasing, re-forming, compressing warped space), not stolen Rule magic. They reverse-engineer captured in-betweener trinkets. (07 t148; wk W37)
- ✅ They cannot go back once out. A depot confiscates items at exit; the first out hold the monopoly and tax later arrivals. (R059)
- ✅ Grateful and resentful at once, "consumed by the drive never to be bound again," and absolutely unscrupulous. No one hunts them; ordinary dwarves see them as distant relatives. (t622–t624)
- ✅ None exist in Book 3's age; they belong to the far future. Returned people and goods can appear anywhere in Book 1. (R147; BANK 11-18)

### GNOMES, GOBLINS, TROLLS
- ✅ **Gnomes (medieval):** the one happy people, bright, loud, indifferent to wealth and rank; a gnome town "is bright and smells good." Seen as the lowliest but power users, Remnant roughly a dwarf's, second only to changelings at assassination. (t284; jul23 t50–t62)
- ✅ Their safety is self-deception: they hold their homes only because no one sees them as a threat. Their villages are likely gone by the end. "Noone is just tolerated." (jul23 t62; R142)
- ✅ **Gnomes (far future):** the dwarves' indentured class. (t568)
- ✅ **Goblins:** the orcs' underclass, abused until they act like the orcs' animals, yet no different from anyone else, just weaker. Far future: "a small orc." No goblin city survives. (jul23 t64–t68, t566)
- ✅ **Trolls:** smaller than an orc, larger than a goblin, "not the large towering trolls. In this world thats a giant." A few whisper in their ghettos. (R065; jul23 t66)

### CHANGELINGS (a crime, not a race)
- ✅ Anyone of any race who masters forbidden soul magic and binds stolen souls to their own. Possessors, never shapeshifters; no changeling is ever born. How they work (two roads, one driver, 1–2 souls, the tell): `magic.md`. (t116, t680, t120)
- ✅ They live about as long as dragons and giants; when the souls leave, the held-back years arrive at once. (t630, t774)
- ✅ Largely monsters now; only a few clever ones remain at court. (wk W18–W19)
- 🔒 **Pre-write check:** ask "can ANYONE see them?" If so, no transfer happens on the page. (07 mandatory check)
- ✅ Everyone knows souls can be stolen ("your father, your uncle, the priest"), and no one guards against it: no folk tests or wards. (t680; R037)
- ✅ Utterly alone, with no family, attached to institutions that outlive bodies; their aim is to quietly become the system. (jul23 t161; jul08 t133)
- 🚫 Never a born race, shapeshifters or "usually human." (01)

### PLACES
- ✅ **The city never moved.** The giants' capital became the dragons' seat, then the human capital; only the ruler changed. The Tower stands at its heart (`cosmos.md`). (t64)
- ✅ The capital was radioactive for hundreds of years. Dragons and enslaved orcs went back first; orc teams rebuilt for thousands of years. Leftovers cause cancers, unexplained illness and deformation. (R087, R137, R061)
- ✅ Who comes: psychics to commune with spirits, the gifted to study, the greedy to exploit, the ill-intentioned to hunt the powerful. "Almost Noone here is nobody and if they are they will be kidnapped and used quickly." (R062, R071)
- ✅ The main city holds the first and largest rift; rifts are reservoirs every race fights over (`cosmos.md`). Remnant also pools like oil reserves. (t66, t188; R025)
- ✅ The last giant city is huge to small folk: a caravan needs a day just to get from the edge to the delivery point, and an orchestra plays constantly so the small aren't crushed. To a giant, "a back water." (R040)
- ✅ **Far-future Earth:** a planet-wide city, food imported by convoy, the Tower at its heart. The human capital, not the only one; the Galactic Council sits in human buildings. (07 Earth; wk W58)
- 🔒 The world is ancient by the medieval era, full of ruins no one understands; the giants' age was erased from memory by the dragons. (10; wk W61)

- ✅ Chips, chambers, implants, flesh augments and drones: `magic.md` · devices.

## B. FLAVOR / AURA

**The whole world**
- Grimdark with no clean hands: "All races in this world are greedy prideful jealous beings. This is a deeply broken place. All races are wrathful bringing down vengeance upon those who wronged them." (jul23 t52)
- **"Its grimdark. So were doing haunting evil world with a kind hearted light we follow."** "A light in a dark world." The light comes from the protagonists, never from the world. (BANK 11-21; 10 texture)
- "It doesn't matter in the grand scale, but everything matters to someone" (t466; see `cosmos.md`).
- His signature move is irony: heaven is empty and hell is full ("one of our trademark ironies," t604); the "Darkest Age" was named for dragons but ruled by humans (jul08 t347); the weakest race produces one of the strongest beings (t772); the race that suffered longest keeps the shortest record (orcs).
- Every race's age is monument-scale: later powers live inside the bones of earlier ones. "Dragons sit in halls physically inside the remains of the giant family who built them." (t32)
- Moral murk is designed in: giants against dragons "should be able to root for either and feel the weight." (t474) Duality, grading scales (`cosmos.md`).
- The reader-trap: let the reader slide toward "maybe they deserve it," then pull the camera back. The slide itself is the indictment. (10 orcs)

**Giants:** cold stone patience, grief turned to spite on a timescale "even the dragons dont match" (jul23 t44). Grown, seamless walls; a city so big an orchestra plays nonstop. The forge-world image is dry and cold: a giant reads a report of two cathedral ships destroying each other, sets it down and takes the next commission (t99). "Hateful, spite-filled, vengeful giants making gross amalgamations of their ancestors." (t276)

**Dragons:** brilliant, witty, ancient academics with castles, schools, firms and fleets. A dragon senses your dwarven shield, is *impressed*, and grants you "the privilege" of its transmutation breath (07). "Just build me a big beautiful ship." (R045) "Not the dragon. He sees everything." (t286) A master screams about incompetence, then straps himself into the chamber. (t442)

**Elves:** calm, beautiful, totalitarian: "a totalitarian juggernaut because of rigid laws and harsh punishment." (jul23 t132) Beacon homeworlds, pinprick exiles, grief for the never-born. "You can build a grove in the mountains but you are always part of the collective." (t598) Hell's "angry noise."

**Humans:** a ravenous, zealous, numberless throng. "The ever-grinding human maw" (t334). "They solved need and found they were never actually driven by need at all and it was always WANT." (t480) Their Earth decays in smog while "the elite continue pumping out new devices." (R089) Far future: anesthetized by AI and VR, "even clones and slaves barely register." (CU §3)

**Orcs:** the heaviest moral weight in the world, "a heavy moral weight clinging to the world" (jul08 t181). Bodies "processed into food or luggage or space craft" (t524). Orcs "literally walked on" as ship floors (t280). Hate as inheritance, a bruise known "by the ache, not the blow."

**Dwarves:** loud family feasts, ruthless ledgers. "Hey mate, you were the bottom of the ledger, you'd have done the same." (t622) Long patient hours over a blade, then very hot when it's time to put it in you. (10) The one race that thrived: the control group that proves brutality is a choice.

**Gnomes:** a bright contrast. A gnome town is "bright and smells good, loud and happy people bustling" (jul23 t60), and it is doomed.

**Changelings:** paranoia without folk remedies. "It's your father, it's your uncle, it's the priest at the church." (t680) "We started off with a shape shifter and it became a soul stealing wizard." (t780)

**The far future:** fixed and brutal. Mastery fixed nothing in beings (t480; "the brightness is the horror" is Claude's line, pending F128). Extraction, not chaos: "Theres really no wild west. These pirates no longer exist. They were smashed." (R088)

**The capital:** wrongness. Radiation sickness, deformation, animals avoiding the Tower, and everyone powerful feeling it "like the sun." (R061, R129)

**What makes it Patrick's (inferred):** body horror as economics (flesh hulls, organ markets, living metal, grafted kings); the old races' rage outlasting human memory; every utopia carrying its own sister-hell; races as webs of entanglement rather than stat blocks; grand scale undercut by one small, personal cost.
